## Astro World: the access audit, and four PRs on the comment area

Sam asked for permissions to be double and triple checked before the sign-in
links went out to the 42 people in the Entra group. The answer Zac had already
given him was true for the app as you click through it, and not true underneath:
`GET /api/travel/hub` returned the staff directory and every person's trips to
any signed-in viewer, needing no id at all, because its routes were written on
the reasoning that the coordinator-gated PAGE stood in front of them. A typed URL
never passes through the page. Four content and course byte routes had the same
shape, needing an item id.

Fixed in #74, merged. Every API route now takes the gate its page takes, and
`test:viewer-scope` classifies every route file and every HTTP handler so a new
route fails until somebody says out loud which gate it takes. It caught my own
reactions route later the same day.

Then, in order: #75 (drafts that survive the card closing, an emoji picker,
reactions on comments) merged; #76 (a reaction to your note reaches your inbox)
open; #77 (the bell - watch a card or a list, plus a real notifications page)
open and stacked on #76.

## Decisions Zac made

- Watching is **manual only**. Commenting does not subscribe you, unlike Slack
  and Trello. A full inbox should always be somebody's decision.
- A watched card sends notes **and** changes; a watched list covers every card
  in it, including ones added later.
- The bell goes on the card front **and** the card back.
- The **envelope stays** in the top bar. The bell is for cards and lists.
- Coordinators opening the whole content library: left as is, because everyone
  stays a viewer for now.

## Worth remembering

- The local rig is what made this a real day's work rather than four claims:
  embedded-postgres on 5434 plus `next start` on two ports with different
  `DEV_ACT_AS_EMAIL`, which is **two signed-in people on one database**. The
  watch and reaction features are meaningless to test as one person.
- Three of my own checks were wrong before they were right, and each was the
  same family of mistake: a check that could not fail. One read zero keys from
  storage and passed a `<= 2` test. One checked per FILE while the promise was
  per HTTP verb. One read the whole inbox and passed only against an empty
  database, then failed on its own leftovers.
- Peter asked what Graph needs. Zac added `Mail.Send` (application) to the app
  registration himself; consent, a shared mailbox and an Application Access
  Policy are Peter's. `Sites.Read.All` has sat unconsented since July, is used
  by nothing, and should come off first - Grant admin consent applies to every
  configured permission at once.
