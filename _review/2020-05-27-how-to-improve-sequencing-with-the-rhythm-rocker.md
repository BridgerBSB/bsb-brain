# How to Improve Sequencing with the Rhythm Rocker

## TL;DR
- The Rhythm Rocker is a three-level drill progression that isolates lower-half weight shift and pelvis-to-torso sequencing, sitting between arm-action drills and full linear delivery work.
- Which of the three foot-position variations to use depends on the athlete's mobility: open-hip for mobile pitchers, closed-hip with a cheated front foot for a dominant back leg, figure-8 arm path for guys who can't sync arm and pelvis.
- Plyo ball weight stays light for this drill, from a 5 oz yellow ball up to about 1 lb; going heavier is linked to a choppy or pushy arm action.

## Claims
- [00:00](https://youtu.be/U__OwPjuKMQ?t=0) The rhythm rocker works lower-half weight shift and sits in the middle of backward chaining: after arm-action drills and basic hip-shoulder dissociation work, before rolling or walk-and-wind-up drills that add linear momentum.
- [01:01](https://youtu.be/U__OwPjuKMQ?t=61) At landing the hips should segment ahead of the shoulders, front shoulder closed, elbows up at 90 degrees, neutral wrist, driving downhill through the ball rather than pushing or rotating uphill.
- [02:02](https://youtu.be/U__OwPjuKMQ?t=122) Ball weight for this drill runs from a 5 oz yellow plyo to a 7 oz red plyo up to roughly 1 lb; going heavier than that can cause a choppy arm action or pushing.
- [02:02](https://youtu.be/U__OwPjuKMQ?t=122) Level 1 setup (feet and pelvis facing the target, hips open) fits pitchers with good hip internal rotation and thoracic rotation who are generally mobile; it is also used as a regression when an athlete can't perform level 2.
- [04:06](https://youtu.be/U__OwPjuKMQ?t=246) Level 2 setup (back foot even with the rubber, front foot cheated open, hips start closed) works better for pitchers with more hip external rotation and a dominant back leg, who get a snapping-down action from a vertical shin into landing.
- [05:09](https://youtu.be/U__OwPjuKMQ?t=309) Level 3 adds a forward-and-backward figure-8 arm path to the level 2 setup, timed to the rock back; used for athletes with a low elbow, lat-digging, uphill rotation, or hip spin, to sink the arm into the correct plane and get the elbow level with the shoulders before rotation.

## Cues
- **"driving downhill through the ball rather than pushing or rotating uphill"** - fixes pushing and uphill rotation; for pitchers doing the rocker drill
- **"smooth, controlled, fluid through these patterns... it's not about muscling the ball"** - fixes muscling/forcing the throw; for pitchers doing the rocker drill
- **"letting the hips and that front heel free up a little bit"** - fixes a stiff, feet-stuck lower half; for pitchers doing the rocker drill

## Anti-cues

## Drills
- **Rhythm Rocker Level 1 (open hip)** - feet and pelvis face the target, hips start open, rock back to the back heel then rotate down; builds downhill rotation while hips stay open; for pitchers with good hip internal rotation and thoracic mobility, or as a regression from level 2
- **Rhythm Rocker Level 2 (closed hip)** - back foot even with the rubber, front foot cheated open, hips/hands start closed, rock back coils the pelvis over the rear femur; builds pelvic load/unload without a linear move; for pitchers with more hip external rotation and a dominant back leg
- **Rhythm Rocker Level 3 (figure 8)** - same setup as level 2 plus a forward/backward figure-8 arm path synced to the rock back; builds arm-to-pelvis sequencing and timing; for pitchers with a low elbow, lat-digging, uphill rotation, or hip spin
