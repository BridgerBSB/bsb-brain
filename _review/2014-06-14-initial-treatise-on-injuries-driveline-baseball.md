# Initial Treatise on Injuries | Driveline Baseball

## TL;DR
- Kyle Boddy argues arm injury risk is probabilistic, not binary: you can minimize it, not eliminate it, and a program that does everything right can still see an injury.
- He says reducing pitcher injuries requires open collaboration across experts (arm care coaches, biomechanists, sabermetricians) and distrust of any single authority, himself included.
- Driveline had put "hundreds" of athletes through its program with no Tommy John surgeries yet as of this 2014 recording, though he frames that as a projection, not a guarantee.

## Claims
- [00:00](https://youtu.be/nzNZXSuBSuM?t=0) Driveline ran an arm care webinar with Randy Sullivan, Ron Wolforth, and Wallace, joined by Jim Wagner, whose son had undergone Tommy John surgery.
- [01:03](https://youtu.be/nzNZXSuBSuM?t=63) Driveline collects objective data through EMG sensors, a biomechanics video lab, force plates, and radar guns on weighted balls.
- [02:05](https://youtu.be/nzNZXSuBSuM?t=125) Of the "hundreds" of kids who have gone through the program, none has needed arm surgery yet, though Boddy expects that to happen eventually.
- [03:07](https://youtu.be/nzNZXSuBSuM?t=187) He argues injury risk sits somewhere between 0% and 100%, like gambling or the stock market, and that mixing luck with control is what makes it hard for people to reason about.
- [04:08](https://youtu.be/nzNZXSuBSuM?t=248) Driveline calls its outputs "projections," not predictions, based on comparing injury rates of its athletes against athletes from other organizations, without claiming causation.
- [05:09](https://youtu.be/nzNZXSuBSuM?t=309) He criticizes biomechanists and researchers who assert effectiveness claims (e.g. good pitchers vary speeds and locations) without citing sabermetric evidence, saying it undermines injury research.
- [06:09](https://youtu.be/nzNZXSuBSuM?t=369) He names Tango Tiger, MGL, Bill James, and Pete Palmer as the people most genuinely interested in open data, and predicts the sabermetrics movement will play a large role in reducing pitcher injury.
- [07:10](https://youtu.be/nzNZXSuBSuM?t=430) Driveline tests changes over predefined windows of roughly "six week," "four to six," or "eight to twelve" week periods before deciding what to keep or discard.
- [08:12](https://youtu.be/nzNZXSuBSuM?t=492) He warns against following any single authority exclusively, naming himself, Glenn Fleisig, James Andrews, and Mike Marshall among people not to trust in isolation.
