# The Rangers Signed Him After +4 MPH Bat Speed In 6 Weeks

## TL;DR
- Diego Castillo's 6-week swing overhaul added 4 mph of bat speed by replacing an arm-and-hand-dominant swing with lower-body-driven rotation and separation.
- Two named drills, offset close and pivot picks, plus cues to hinge more in the load and turn the back shoulder to the ball, were the delivery mechanism for that fix.
- After the block he hit over .500 and slugged over .800 with 2 home runs in his first six games, and signed with the Texas Rangers organization.

## Claims
- [00:00](https://youtu.be/ufh2XNcw8dA?t=0) Castillo could previously hit for power or contact but never both at once; after 6 weeks of training he was arguably the best hitter in the LMB early season and signed with the Texas Rangers organization.
- [01:00](https://youtu.be/ufh2XNcw8dA?t=60) Assessment (jump numbers, hitting mocap) showed a clear lack of physical output; he wasn't rotating efficiently, didn't separate well, and relied on his arms and hands to deliver the barrel.
- [01:00](https://youtu.be/ufh2XNcw8dA?t=60) Coaching cue given: create more hinge in the load during the stride; from there a high pitch just needs rotation, a low pitch needs added hinge and the back shoulder turned to the ball.
- [02:01](https://youtu.be/ufh2XNcw8dA?t=121) The main mechanical blocker was rotational strategy: he wasn't using his lower body and pelvis to generate force; the fix targeted faster lower-body rotation, more separation, better energy transfer up the chain, and posterior chain engagement to drive the hinge.
- [02:01](https://youtu.be/ufh2XNcw8dA?t=121) Offset close and pivot picks were named as the two drills most beneficial to him, and both became part of his pregame routine.
- [03:01](https://youtu.be/ufh2XNcw8dA?t=181) The offset close drill was run pull-side with a shorter, lighter bat, described as easier to rotate.
- [04:01](https://youtu.be/ufh2XNcw8dA?t=241) Castillo gained 4 mph of bat speed in 6 weeks, called "pretty absurd" and "a pretty unreal gain" in the video.
- [04:01](https://youtu.be/ufh2XNcw8dA?t=241) In his first six games after the block he hit two home runs, batted over .500, and slugged over .800.

## Cues
- **"Turn your back shoulder to the baseball"** - fixes handsy delivery on low pitches; for hitters relying on arms/hands instead of rotation
- **"Tilt your belt buckle down towards the ground"** - fixes a shallow, arm-driven load; for hitters who aren't engaging the posterior chain

## Drills
- **Offset close** - pull-side oriented, hit with a shorter, lighter bat for easier rotation; builds rotational speed and separation; for hitters over-relying on hands/arms
- **Pivot picks** - named alongside offset close as one of Castillo's two key drills; builds the same lower-body rotation and separation; setup not detailed in source

## Evidence cited
- Diego Castillo (named athlete): +4 mph bat speed gain in 6 weeks; 2 home runs, batting over .500, slugging over .800 in his first six games; signed with the Texas Rangers organization after the block. Internal assessment referenced but not named in detail: jump numbers, hitting mocap.

## Open questions
- The video attributes the batting-average/slugging surge and the Rangers signing to the swing changes alone, with no control for sample size (six games), opponent quality, or other training happening concurrently.
