# His bat path is costing him

## TL;DR
- A four-step drill progression (no-stride short bat, offset-open underload, handle-loader poppy, offset-open poppy) is used to raise vertical bat angle and stop a hitter from wrapping and pulling the ball.
- Raising the hand set and using implement/stance constraints forces the bat path more vertical and more toward center field, especially against low-and-inside and high pitch locations.
- Vertical bat angle went from -21 degrees to -25.6 degrees between the prior measurement and this session, the session's key number.

## Claims
- [00:00](https://youtu.be/RczpZSjZgQA?t=0) No-stride, short-bat swings against a lefty-side/oppo-angle curveball aimed low and inside are used to add posture and side bend and produce a more vertical bat angle; raising Jaylen's hands from his usual lower set increases rear shoulder adduction, which the coach says was targeted to raise vertical bat angle.
- [01:01](https://youtu.be/RczpZSjZgQA?t=61) Vertical bat angle improved from -21 degrees to -25.6 degrees between the prior measurement (transcript says "milk cap" (sic)) and this session.
- [01:01](https://youtu.be/RczpZSjZgQA?t=61) Step-in, offset-open swings with a stride use a short bat plus an underload bat; the underload makes staying vertical harder, and the offset stance forces the bat path toward center field to stay vertical, since going flat causes wrapping around the ball and pulling it.
- [01:01](https://youtu.be/RczpZSjZgQA?t=61)-[02:01](https://youtu.be/RczpZSjZgQA?t=121) A poppy drill with a handle-loader (added weight closer to the hands, similar effect to the underload) targets rear shoulder adduction; leg lift is set higher than a typical poppy to make the movement more vertical than rotational.
- [02:01](https://youtu.be/RczpZSjZgQA?t=121) Keeping the hands higher after the poppy work forces vertical tilt to reach a low pitch; if the hands stay low, the hitter can stay flat or sink into the legs, producing a flatter vertical bat angle.
- [03:01](https://youtu.be/RczpZSjZgQA?t=181) Offset-open poppies with a stride are paired with a fastball thrown higher in the zone; if the hands stay low, the flatter bat path cuts under the ball, causing popups or fouls straight back, because flatter vertical bat angles tend to miss high or low relative to the barrel.

## Drills
- **No-stride short bat** - no stride, short bat, vs. low-and-inside oppo-angle curveball; builds posture, side bend, and a more vertical bat angle; for hitters who get flat and pull
- **Offset-open stride, underload bat** - step-in offset-open stance with a stride, short bat plus underload; builds staying vertical and directing the bat path to center under added difficulty; for hitters who wrap and pull
- **Poppy with handle-loader** - poppy with a handle-loaded bat (weight near the hands) and a higher leg lift; builds rear shoulder adduction and a vertical (vs. rotational) movement pattern
- **Offset-open poppy with stride** - same vertical-focused poppy, now offset-open with a stride vs. a high fastball; builds holding a vertical bat path to center field; punishes low hands with popups or fouls

## Evidence cited
- Jaylen Smith's vertical bat angle moved from -21 degrees to -25.6 degrees between the prior measurement and this session (internal tracking, not an external study)
