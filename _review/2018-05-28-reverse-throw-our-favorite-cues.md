# Reverse Throw: Our Favorite Cues

## TL;DR
- The reverse throw trains the backside of the shoulder by reversing the throw's finish position; it isn't a backward-chaining mechanics drill, so it can be done pre- or post-throw.
- Three cues in sequence: brace the front leg, brace the front arm, then reach-scap-elbow-hand off the finish position.
- Ball weight caps at 2 lb for high school/college pitchers and 1 lb for pitchers 13 and under, done for a few sets of 10 reps a few times a week.

## Claims
- [00:00](https://youtu.be/G3RKCmTEyKs?t=0) The reverse throw trains the backside of the shoulder; it is not a backward-chaining drill, so it doesn't matter whether it's done pre-throwing or as part of post-throw arm care.
- [01:02](https://youtu.be/G3RKCmTEyKs?t=62) Cue 1, bracing the front leg: most pitchers let the front knee cave in during this drill; the fix is keeping the front leg stable on target while the thoracic spine rotates around it.
- [02:05](https://youtu.be/G3RKCmTEyKs?t=125) Cue 2, bracing the front arm: pull against the glove side rather than letting the upper half rotate as one unit; holding an object in the glove hand helps remind the pitcher to brace.
- [03:06](https://youtu.be/G3RKCmTEyKs?t=186) Cue 3: start from the throw's finish position (scap protracted, shoulder internally rotated, forearm pronated) and reverse it in sequential order, scap first, then elbow, then hand.
- [03:45](https://youtu.be/G3RKCmTEyKs?t=225) Ball weight recommendation: 2 lb maximum for high school and college pitchers, 1 lb for pitchers 13 and under; heavier weights produce compensatory patterns.
- [04:08](https://youtu.be/G3RKCmTEyKs?t=248) Volume: a few sets of 10 reps, a few times per week, either pre-throw or worked into post-throw/arm-care.

## Cues
- **"Drive that front foot into the ground to try to drive that knee out"** - fixes the front knee caving in during the brace; for pitchers doing reverse throws
- **"Think about starting a lawnmower"** - fixes the upper half rotating as one unit instead of bracing and pulling against the front side; for pitchers training the front-arm brace
- **"Reach, scap, elbow, hand"** - fixes everything rotating together instead of a sequential order off the finish position; for pitchers training arm sequencing

## Drills
- **Reverse Throw** - start from the throw's finish position (scap protracted, shoulder internally rotated, forearm pronated) and reverse the pattern with a weighted ball, 1 lb for pitchers 13 and under up to 2 lb for older pitchers, a few sets of 10 reps a few times a week; builds strength and sequencing in the backside of the shoulder; for high school and college pitchers as a pre-throw warmup or post-throw/arm-care exercise

## Open questions
- The ball weight caps and compensatory-pattern claim are based on the coach's stated observation ("I typically start to see compensatory patterns"), not a cited study or dataset.
- The claim that timing (pre- or post-throw) doesn't matter, and that there is "some mechanical carryover" but no direct transfer to the throwing pattern, is asserted without supporting data.
