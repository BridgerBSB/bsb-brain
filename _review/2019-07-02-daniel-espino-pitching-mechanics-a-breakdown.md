# Daniel Espino Pitching Mechanics | A Breakdown

## TL;DR
- Espino throws upper-90s off mechanical efficiency and mobility rather than physical maturity: listed at 6'3", 200 lb, with little strength-training history.
- Sequencing keys are a posterior pelvic tilt during the leg lift, holding the knee back to stretch the rear hip's internal rotators, then corkscrewing the knee to a fully open landing with the elbow near 90 degrees.
- He produces over 180 degrees of trunk layback and large hip-shoulder separation, letting the arm lag passively so energy transfers up the chain with minimal excess motion at release.

## Claims
- [00:20](https://youtu.be/4V8ghKiNDy0?t=20) Espino is listed at 6'3", 200 lb, without a history of heavy weight training, and already throws in the upper 90s.
- [01:01](https://youtu.be/4V8ghKiNDy0?t=61) His leg lift is rhythm-based, not a big forward stride; he uses lift height to create posterior pelvic tilt rather than to shift weight forward, unlike Nolan Ryan's use of the same move.
- [03:05](https://youtu.be/4V8ghKiNDy0?t=185) He drops into the back leg while holding a vertical shin angle, loading the rear glute before rotating.
- [04:05](https://youtu.be/4V8ghKiNDy0?t=245) Holding the knee back over the rubber as he moves forward stretches the adductors and internal rotators of the rear hip, letting him snap the knee down rather than push off the quad.
- [07:07](https://youtu.be/4V8ghKiNDy0?t=427) At landing the hips are almost fully open while the arm stays back, creating large hip-shoulder separation, scapular retraction, and horizontal abduction.
- [11:13](https://youtu.be/4V8ghKiNDy0?t=673) Elbow angle at landing is close to 90 degrees, the range the source gives (roughly 85 to 100 degrees) as efficient for upper-90s throwers.
- [12:13](https://youtu.be/4V8ghKiNDy0?t=733) Torso layback exceeds 180 degrees at this point, attributed to shoulder mobility.
- [13:15](https://youtu.be/4V8ghKiNDy0?t=795) At release there is minimal extra trunk or head motion; tying the final internal-rotation snap to a head or torso whip is called an energy leak.
- [15:16](https://youtu.be/4V8ghKiNDy0?t=916) A pitcher named in captions as "Sir Anthony Dominguez" (sic) is cited sitting 97-99 mph consistently off similar deep-stretch, knee-drive mechanics.
