# THE DOMEZONE PODCAST EP #5 - The Chase Petty Story

## TL;DR
- Petty's velocity arc: 84-86 mph as a 130 lb freshman to 94-99 (peak 100-102) within about four years, credited to BPC mechanical work plus a 65 lb weight gain.
- A new org (Twins) altering his mechanics/arm slot right after the draft coincided with a velo drop into the low 90s; returning to his own coach at the All-Star break got him back to 98.
- His stated in-game cue is "control what you can control," paired with a rule that his body language should look identical whether he's cruising or has let up six runs.

## Claims
- [06:04](https://youtu.be/xK5x85fURl8?t=364) - His agent and family concealed his unstable home life from MLB teams during pre-draft Zoom calls, believing teams would use it to justify offering less signing money.
- [07:05](https://youtu.be/xK5x85fURl8?t=425) - As a freshman he was 5'9", 130 lb, throwing 84-86 mph.
- [13:09](https://youtu.be/xK5x85fURl8?t=789) - Threw 96 mph at a junior-year pro day in front of scouts, the outing BPC says put him on the map.
- [29:27](https://youtu.be/xK5x85fURl8?t=1767) - After the Twins altered his arm slot/mechanics post-draft, his live-BP velo fell to 93-95; after being traded to the Reds and reworking with his original coach over All-Star break, his next outing hit 98, the highest he'd been all season.
- [39:35](https://youtu.be/xK5x85fURl8?t=2375) - Describes managing a season as "80 innings left" rather than tallying each start, to avoid overcorrecting after a bad stretch.
- [40:36](https://youtu.be/xK5x85fURl8?t=2436) - Broke his own knuckle punching something in frustration and pitched the rest of the year with it; says his next outing after the injury was his best of the year.
- [44:39](https://youtu.be/xK5x85fURl8?t=2679) - His slider/sweeper took "months" of work to go from breaking the wrong direction to a plus pitch; he had no changeup entering pro ball and built one afterward.
- [47:42](https://youtu.be/xK5x85fURl8?t=2862) - Gained 3-4 mph within 2 months of starting at BPC (to 90 mph) and put on 65 lb total from freshman to senior year of high school.
- [58:54](https://youtu.be/xK5x85fURl8?t=3534) - Says his strength program stays light for a starter: roughly 3 plates max on deadlift, ~175 lb on split squat, prioritizing throwing and mobility over heavy lifting.
- [59:54](https://youtu.be/xK5x85fURl8?t=3594) - Threw 100-102 mph in a filmed high-school exhibition ("BPC Challenge") despite his trainer flagging arm tightness beforehand and suggesting he skip the session.

## Cues
- **"Control what you can control"** - fixes tilt after bad-luck contact or results that don't match pitch quality; for pitchers managing outcome variance over a season
- **"It should look like I just got in that game"** - fixes visible frustration/body-language tells after adversity on the mound; for pitchers, regardless of score

## Open questions
- The Twins reportedly altered his arm slot/mechanics soon after drafting a healthy-armed prospect, which he ties to a velocity drop (94-97 sitting down to 93-95 in live BP); the source frames this as orgs wanting to "put their stamp" on a player, asserted without any data showing the change helped.
- He credits a comparatively light lifting program (deadlift ~3 plates, split squat ~175 lb) for his velocity and durability, which runs against common practice of heavier lower-body loading for pitching velocity; this is anecdotal, with no comparison group given.
