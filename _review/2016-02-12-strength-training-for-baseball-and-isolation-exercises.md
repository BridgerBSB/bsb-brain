# Strength Training for Baseball and Isolation Exercises

## TL;DR
- Pecs and lats drive arm acceleration in pitching (EMG: 88% MVC for the lat, 54% MVC for the pec) but standard compound lifts fatigue the triceps and biceps first, leaving pecs and lats under-stimulated.
- The fix is post-exhaustion training: a compound lift immediately followed by a single-joint isolation move for the same muscle; a full sample upper body session is given.
- Elbow range of motion lost to heavy pressing and rowing is not permanent; daily manual therapy restored full extension in pitchers within 1 month, even from a 110-degree restriction.

## Claims
- Intro - In a study of high-level pitchers, throwing-arm EMG during the arm acceleration phase reached 54% MVC for the pec and 88% MVC for the lat.
- Intro - The first muscle group to fail in most pressing exercises is the triceps, not the pecs, and in most rowing exercises it's the biceps, not the lats, so pecs and lats go under-fatigued in standard compound training.
- Post-exhaustion training for lat and pec growth - Post-exhaustion pairs a compound lift with a single-joint isolation move for the same muscle right after; the lat example given is Single Arm Dumbbell Row 3x6-8 followed by Kneeling Straight Arm Pulldown 3x10-12.
- Won't my chest and lats get too big (and lose flexibility)? - Pitchers with elbow extension restricted to 110 degrees regained full range of motion after 1 month of daily manual therapy; more minor restrictions improved within a couple of weeks.
- Sample Upper Body Day A - the session pairs three compound/isolation supersets: floor press with band chest flies, lat pulldown with band pullover, and push-up to 1-arm support with half-kneeling cable row.
- Final Thoughts - adding lat and pec isolation work is called a subtle change that adds mass and strength without sacrificing range of motion, to maximize their contribution to throwing velocity.

## Drills
- **Post-exhaustion superset (pec/lat)** - compound lift (dumbbell row, floor press, lat pulldown) immediately followed by a single-joint isolation move for the same muscle (straight-arm pulldown, band chest flies, band pullover, cable row); builds fuller lat and pec fiber recruitment than compound lifts alone provide; for pitchers whose pecs and lats are under-stimulated by standard pressing/rowing work.
- **Sample Upper Body Day A** - full offseason session: neutral grip DB floor press + 3-way band chest flies, neutral grip lat pulldown w/ slow eccentric + supine band pullover, push-up to 1-arm support + half-kneeling cable row; builds upper body strength and mass to support throwing velocity; for pitchers in offseason training.

## Evidence cited
- PubMed study (ref 1, https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/22958966): throwing-arm EMG in high-level pitchers reached 54% MVC (pec) and 88% MVC (lat) during the arm acceleration phase.
- Author's case observation: pitchers regaining full elbow ROM from a 110-degree restriction after 1 month of daily manual therapy; no sample size or method given.

## Open questions
- The manual-therapy ROM-recovery claim (110 degrees to full ROM in 1 month) is asserted without sample size, comparison group, or method detail.
