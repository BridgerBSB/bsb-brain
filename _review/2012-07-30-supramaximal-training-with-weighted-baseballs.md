# Supramaximal Training with Weighted Baseballs (drivelinebaseball.com)

## TL;DR
- Heavy balls and light balls train opposite things: heavy balls build arm speed by adding stretch at max external rotation; light balls build arm strength and deceleration by pushing ball velocity toward game speed.
- Weighted-ball work exists to replicate in-game arm stress (at max external rotation and at release/max internal rotation) that long toss, step-behinds, flat-mound work, and cage throwing with a standard 5oz ball cannot reach.
- Cites Joe Marsh throwing 95 mph with a 3oz ball while sitting 88-89 mph (touching 90) in games.

## Claims
- [00:04](https://youtu.be/W84Am7RjvgQ?t=4) A well-known coach the presenter respects stopped using heavy weighted baseballs; some of that coach's athletes reported not seeing the results they wanted.
- [01:05](https://youtu.be/W84Am7RjvgQ?t=65) Driveline athletes throw weighted balls up to 2 lb from a running start (rare at full speed, mostly partial throws), and throw 11oz, 9oz, and 7oz balls at full speed.
- [02:06](https://youtu.be/W84Am7RjvgQ?t=126) Claims it is a well-known biomechanical fact that pitchers throw with more intensity in games than in training, and that no standard training method (long toss, step-behinds, flat-mound, cage work with a 5oz ball) replicates the added stress at max external rotation and at release/max internal rotation that game intensity produces.
- [03:08](https://youtu.be/W84Am7RjvgQ?t=188) Throwing heavy balls at max intensity, usually via a step-behind, produces lower ball velocity and lower stress at release, but a further stretch and higher stress at max external rotation, which the source ties to training arm speed.
- [04:08](https://youtu.be/W84Am7RjvgQ?t=248) Light balls (2oz, 3oz, 4oz) give less stretch at max external rotation but raise ball velocity toward game speed, increasing deceleration stress at internal rotation; the source calls this the opposite of common belief, using light balls for arm strength/deceleration and heavy balls for arm speed.
- [04:08](https://youtu.be/W84Am7RjvgQ?t=248) Joe Marsh threw 95 mph multiple times with a 3oz ball while sitting 88-89 mph in games (touched 90 mph).
- [05:09](https://youtu.be/W84Am7RjvgQ?t=309) Recommends continuing to train with both heavy and light weighted balls rather than shifting focus entirely to deceleration training.

## Drills
- **Step-behind weighted ball throws** - throw overload balls (7-11oz, up to 2 lb partial) at max intensity using a step-behind; builds stretch and arm speed at max external rotation; for pitchers training velocity.

## Evidence cited
- Joe Marsh: threw 95 mph multiple times with a 3oz underload ball; sits 88-89 mph in games, has touched 90 mph.

## Open questions
- The "well-known biomechanical fact" about game-vs-training arm stress at max external/internal rotation is asserted with no study or dataset cited.
- Argues against a named coach's move away from heavy weighted balls and against a trend toward deceleration-only training, without presenting a head-to-head comparison.
