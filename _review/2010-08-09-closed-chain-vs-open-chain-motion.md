# Closed-Chain vs. Open-Chain Motion

## TL;DR
- Defines closed-chain (limb fixed against a surface, force driven into the ground - squat, push-up) versus open-chain (limb moves free of a surface, force applied to the load - bench press, leg press).
- For pitchers, build the lower body with closed-chain lifts (squat, deadlift) and avoid weighted open-chain lower-body work like leg extensions, which stresses the ACL and can create quad-hamstring imbalance.
- For pitchers, train the upper body open-chain since throwing is itself open-chain; use dumbbell pressing/rowing and band work with a neutral grip, and keep closed-chain upper-body lifts (chin-ups, push-ups) unweighted to preserve mobility.

## Claims
- Open-Chain Exercises: an open-chain exercise moves the limb freely against a resistance (bench press, leg press); a closed-chain exercise fixes the limb against a surface and drives force into the ground (squat, push-up).
- Open-Chain Exercises: avoid weighted open-chain lower-body exercises like leg extensions; they produce shear stress in the knee joint that can tax the ACL and can create quad-hamstring imbalances that raise injury risk and lower performance.
- Open-Chain Exercises: open-chain upper-body work suits pitchers because throwing itself is an open-chain movement of the arm around the glenohumeral joint; recommends dumbbell bench press, dumbbell rowing, and band work, with a neutral grip rather than a barbell's fixed pronated grip.
- Closed-Chain Exercises: pitchers should build the lower body with closed-chain lifts (squats, deadlifts) over leg extensions/curls, because the lower half stabilizes the throw - the glove-side leg needs a firm plant to give the hips and shoulders a base to rotate on.
- Closed-Chain Exercises: pitchers should avoid heavy weighted upper-body closed-chain work; Driveline uses chin-ups, pull-ups, and explosive push-up variants without added chains or bands, to keep upper-body mobility without added stiffness, with exceptions for athletes with excess laxity.

## Drills
- **Squat and deadlift** - closed-chain barbell lift; builds a stable lower-body base for the stride and plant leg; for pitchers
- **Chin-up, pull-up, and explosive push-up variants** - closed-chain bodyweight upper-body work done without added load; builds mobility without stiffness; for pitchers
- **Dumbbell bench press, dumbbell row, and band work** - open-chain upper-body work with a neutral grip; builds throwing-shoulder mobility; for pitchers
