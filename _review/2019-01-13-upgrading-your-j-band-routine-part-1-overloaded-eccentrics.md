# Upgrading Your J-Band Routine Part 1: Overloaded Eccentrics

## TL;DR
- Ben Brewster adds two overload variations to the standard J-band warmup: eccentric-only and assisted-concentric, across four movements (external rotation, T, Y, reverse throw pattern).
- The case for doing it: athletes are 25-50% stronger eccentrically than concentrically, so normal-resistance J-band work never overloads the negative phase.
- Dosage guidance: 2-3 sessions a week, post-throwing (not pre-throw), 2-3 sets of 8-10 reps.

## Claims
- [00:01](https://youtu.be/bJtcyANLnRA?t=1) Brewster has used J-bands as a warmup tool for 10-12 years.
- [02:04](https://youtu.be/bJtcyANLnRA?t=124) The eccentric portion of a lift is 25-50% stronger than the concentric portion; example given is squatting 225 lb concentrically but being able to lower 315 lb eccentrically.
- [02:04](https://youtu.be/bJtcyANLnRA?t=124) Says eccentric training increases hypertrophy more than concentric-only training and has been shown to reduce hamstring strain incidence in sprinters, without naming a study.
- [03:04](https://youtu.be/bJtcyANLnRA?t=184) Two overload methods: eccentric-only and assisted-concentric, applied to four movements: external rotation, T, Y, and reverse throwing pattern.
- [03:13](https://youtu.be/bJtcyANLnRA?t=193) Eccentric-only: start with no band tension at end range (90 degrees shoulder, max external rotation, good rib position), back up until tension resists you into internal rotation, then control it down; same setup logic applied to the T, Y, and reverse throw pattern.
- [05:26](https://youtu.be/bJtcyANLnRA?t=326) Assisted-concentric: back up further than the normal starting distance, use the free hand to assist the band into end range, then resist the band eccentrically back to start; done for the same four movements.
- [07:15](https://youtu.be/bJtcyANLnRA?t=435) Overloaded eccentric work causes more shoulder soreness than the standard J-band series, so it should not be done directly before throwing.
- [07:15](https://youtu.be/bJtcyANLnRA?t=435) Recommended dosage: 2-3 times a week, post-throwing (e.g., after a bullpen), 2-3 sets of 8-10 reps.

## Drills
- **J-Band Eccentric-Only (ER / T / Y / Reverse Throw)** - no band tension, back up until the band pulls you off the end-range position, then resist it down through internal rotation or scap protraction; builds eccentric strength of the rotator cuff and scapular stabilizers; for pitchers post-throwing.
- **J-Band Assisted-Concentric (ER / T / Y / Reverse Throw)** - back up past the normal working distance, use the free hand to assist the band to end range, then resist eccentrically back to start; overloads both the concentric and eccentric phases; for pitchers post-throwing.
