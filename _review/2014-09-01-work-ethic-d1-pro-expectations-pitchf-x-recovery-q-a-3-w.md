# Work Ethic, D1/Pro Expectations, PITCHf/x, Recovery | Q&A #3 w/ Driveline Baseball

## TL;DR
- Kyle Boddy argues real work ethic is daily consistency, not an inspirational one-off; he backs this with Trevor Bauer doing 200 reps a day on a two-pound ball drill and Ben Wetzler throwing 150 to 250 throws a day.
- Thumb position does not matter for most pitches because it disengages before the ball is ballistic, but for hard cutters and sliders the thumb must stay fixed off the four-seam grip to keep finger pressure offset.
- Recovery protocol: Marc Pro e-stim on the forearm, bicep, and anterior shoulder in the 20 to 40 minute window after stressful throwing, sometimes paired with Power Play cold compression to limit secondary swelling.

## Claims
- [00:01](https://youtu.be/XlLx7tYE2p8?t=1) Work ethic is defined by someone else calling you a hard worker, not by saying it yourself; a D1 pitching coach told Kyle "real dudes are real dudes all the time," not just in games.
- [01:02](https://youtu.be/XlLx7tYE2p8?t=62) Trevor Bauer, coming off a subpar 2013, did a specific drill with a two-pound ball 200 times a day at both low and high intensities; 2014 was a lot better for him.
- [02:03](https://youtu.be/XlLx7tYE2p8?t=123) Ben Wetzler, who led Division I in ERA, followed a program of 150 to 250 throws per day to raise velocity, cleared with the pitching coach.
- [03:04](https://youtu.be/XlLx7tYE2p8?t=184) Isaac, a high school junior, 6'0", 160 lb, went from throwing 75-78 mph to almost 88 mph after a full summer of training, commuting about an hour each way, and continuing work at home.
- [05:05](https://youtu.be/XlLx7tYE2p8?t=305) Thumb bend or straightness doesn't matter for most pitches since it disengages before the ball is ballistic; for hard cutters and sliders thrown off a four-seam grip, the thumb must stay put or the pitch becomes "a really bad four-seamer with no run."
- [06:06](https://youtu.be/XlLx7tYE2p8?t=366) Kyle throws his own sliders and cutters off a two-seam grip, pulling down on one side while keeping the thumb centered.
- [07:07](https://youtu.be/XlLx7tYE2p8?t=427) Driveline films pitchers throwing against an acrylic sheet with a high-speed camera to analyze pitch tunnels, then compares that to PITCHf/x data on how pitchers like Marcus Stroman sequence pitches.
- [08:07](https://youtu.be/XlLx7tYE2p8?t=487) Marc Pro is used on the forearm, bicep, and anterior shoulder, mainly in the 20 to 40 minute window after stressful throwing, to increase blood flow and capillary function.
- [09:09](https://youtu.be/XlLx7tYE2p8?t=549) Power Play cold compression sleeves are used, sometimes before and sometimes after Marc Pro, to limit secondary metabolic damage and swelling, though Kyle states fixing mechanics and training load should come first.

## Drills
- **Two-pound ball drill (unnamed, per Bauer)** - thrown 200 times a day at low and high intensities; builds better organized mechanics and velocity; for a pitcher resisting an identified deficiency

## Evidence cited
- Trevor Bauer: subpar 2013 season, added the two-pound ball drill at 200 reps/day, better 2014 season. [01:02](https://youtu.be/XlLx7tYE2p8?t=62)
- Ben Wetzler: led Division I in ERA, ran a 150-250 throws/day program to push velocity. [02:03](https://youtu.be/XlLx7tYE2p8?t=123)
- Isaac: HS junior, 75-78 mph to almost 88 mph after a summer of daily training. [03:04](https://youtu.be/XlLx7tYE2p8?t=184)

## Open questions
- Kyle says thumb position doesn't matter for most pitches but must stay fixed for cutters/sliders; this is asserted from experience with no measurement shown, and it cuts against coaching that emphasizes thumb placement more broadly. [05:05](https://youtu.be/XlLx7tYE2p8?t=305)
- Kyle says he isn't a fan of therapeutic icing and that icing slows recovery rate, yet he uses Power Play cold compression regularly to limit secondary damage; the note doesn't resolve when icing helps versus hurts. [09:09](https://youtu.be/XlLx7tYE2p8?t=549)
