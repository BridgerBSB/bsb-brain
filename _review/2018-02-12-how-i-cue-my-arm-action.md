# How I Cue My Arm Action

## TL;DR
- Ben Brewster (Tread Athletics) lays out his personal arm-action model: arm spiral, glove-arm internal torque, scap loading, tension guardrails, and syncing both arms into the plane of shoulder rotation.
- Two anchor cues carry the whole system: "figure eight" to unify the glove and throwing arm into one loop of energy, and "tennis serve" to get the 90 degree elbow angle, closed front side, and downhill finish without thinking about each piece separately.
- For remapping arm path he caps plyo ball drills at 2 lb for low-effort feel work, then moves to lighter balls as velocity increases.

## Claims
- [01:18](https://youtu.be/4GjWsy7y65I?t=78) The arm spiral ("spiral staircase") model winds the arm up dynamically into the high-cock position rather than actively pulling it there, holding roughly a 90 degree elbow bend and neutral wrist.
- [02:22](https://youtu.be/4GjWsy7y65I?t=142) The glove arm should hold internal rotation torque through the spiral so there is tension to unwind at landing; losing it lets the front shoulder open early, a fix he credits to Luke Haggerty, and says the leak costs "only a couple of miles an hour."
- [05:26](https://youtu.be/4GjWsy7y65I?t=326) Scap loading is really horizontal abduction (elbows back, scaps retracted), lengthening the arc of force application into the ball; the common teach of actively yanking the elbows back instead creates a timing issue where the arm never flips up behind the body, which he says stalled his own development for several years.
- [07:38](https://youtu.be/4GjWsy7y65I?t=447) Tension should be minimal but not zero: just enough in the bicep and scaps ("mechanical guardrails") to hold the angle and repeat the path throw to throw.
- [10:03](https://youtu.be/4GjWsy7y65I?t=637) Both arms need to sink into the plane of shoulder rotation to maximize energy transfer; when the throwing arm works in a different plane than the shoulders, pitchers compensate by forward-flexing the trunk instead of rotating.
- [12:20](https://youtu.be/4GjWsy7y65I?t=758) Arm action should come in loose and late, after the back leg/lower half does its work first; an arm-dominant delivery hijacks the kinetic chain and caps genetic velocity limit. Aaron Sanchez is named as an exception who gets away with an arm-dominant delivery.
- [13:10](https://youtu.be/4GjWsy7y65I?t=820) Plyo care drills with weight variation are his tool for remapping arm path; heaviest ball for lower-effort work is 2 lb, giving more immediate feedback on the momentum and path before progressing to lighter balls at higher velocity.
- [14:47](https://youtu.be/4GjWsy7y65I?t=944) The "figure eight" or infinity-sign cue treats glove arm and throwing arm as one unified loop of energy rather than two separate pieces, citing "Wallace Chapman" (sic) as a slow-motion example.
- [16:45](https://youtu.be/4GjWsy7y65I?t=1005) The "tennis serve" cue (imagining hitting a served ball at release point) tends to produce the 90 degree elbow angle, a closed front side, and a downhill angle without separately coaching each piece.

## Cues
- **"Arm spiral" / "spiral staircase"** - fixes actively yanking the arm up into high-cock position instead of letting it wind up dynamically; general arm-path cue.
- **"Internal torque" on the glove arm** - fixes the front shoulder opening/leaking early; credited to Luke Haggerty.
- **"Figure eight" / infinity sign** - fixes treating glove arm and throwing arm as two separate pieces instead of one loop of energy.
- **"Tennis serve"** - fixes disconnected coaching of elbow angle, front side, and downhill angle at release; general release-point cue.

## Drills
- **Plyo ball weight progression** - heavy ball (up to 2 lb) at low effort for feel, then lighter balls as velocity increases; builds remapped arm path and momentum feel; for pitchers relearning arm action.
- **PVC pipe / broomstick plane check** - pipe across the shoulders, rotate everything together; builds sinking both arms into the plane of shoulder rotation; field drill for pitchers whose arm and shoulders rotate on different planes.
