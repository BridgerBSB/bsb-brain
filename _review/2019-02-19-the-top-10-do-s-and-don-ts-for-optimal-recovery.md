# The Top 10 Do's and Don'ts for Optimal Recovery

## TL;DR
- Ranks recovery levers by impact: active recovery, sleep, and nutrition (protein around 1 g per pound of bodyweight) are the "heavy hitters"; sauna, e-stim, ART, and voodoo flossing are minor assists, roughly 10% of the benefit.
- Argues eccentric/deceleration stress, not concentric work, drives most muscle soreness, so daily active recovery should lean on sled, bike, or stair sprints rather than flat-ground sprinting.
- Argues against routine icing and NSAIDs for everyday soreness and says excess training/throwing volume, not a lack of recovery tools, is the usual real cause of poor recovery.

## Claims
- **Active Recovery**: eccentric/deceleration stress causes the most muscle damage; concentric-only work (sled push, bike sprints, stair sprints) produces far less next-day soreness than flat-ground sprinting.
- **Active Recovery**: sample protocol given is 8-12 minutes of intervals at 50-75% intensity, 15-30 second sprint duration, one sprint per minute, using bike, sled, stairs, or "deadmill."
- **Nutrition**: target protein intake of about 1 gram per pound of bodyweight.
- **Sauna**: a rat study on immobilization found heat exposure decreased muscle atrophy by up to 32%.
- **Sauna**: one cited study found time-to-exhaustion running increased 32% after sauna exposure, alongside increased blood plasma volume and red blood cell count.
- **Sauna**: cited studies used 30-40 total minutes of exposure at 176-212°F and reported growth hormone increases of 2 to 5 fold in the hours after.
- **#8: Don't Use Ice / NSAIDs**: cites Dr. Gabe Mirkin, who coined RICE, reversing his position that ice and rest delay healing by blocking release of IGF-1.
- **#10: Avoid excess training / throwing volume**: flags common overload examples of 75-85% intensity on light throwing days and 15-20 max-distance throws on long toss days as workload athletes cannot out-recover.
- **Surprise Bonus: G Flux**: two athletes at the same net calorie balance (2500 in/2500 out vs. 3500 in/3500 out) are not recovery-equivalent; higher total energy turnover is argued to improve recovery and adaptation, per Dr. John Berardi's concept.
- **My experience with G Flux**: author recovered well throwing 6 days/week in college while burning about 4,000 calories/day, versus poorer sleep and lingering soreness in a sedentary pro off-season near 3,000 calories/day.

## Drills
- **Active recovery interval protocol** - 8-12 min bike, sled, stairs, or deadmill sprints at 50-75% intensity, 15-30 sec bursts, one per minute; builds blood flow/recovery without the eccentric soreness of flat sprinting; for daily use, especially pitchers on non-lift days.

## Evidence cited
- Rat study: heat/sauna exposure reduced muscle atrophy from immobilization by up to 32%.
- Study: sauna exposure increased time-to-exhaustion running by 32%, with higher plasma volume and red blood cell count.
- Studies on sauna and growth hormone: 30-40 min at 176-212°F, GH up 2-5 fold post-exposure.
- Sleep literature cited generally linking poor sleep to increased injury risk in adolescent athletes and sleep extension to improved performance/recovery, no specific numbers given.
- Dr. Gabe Mirkin's public reversal on RICE and ice delaying IGF-1-driven healing.
- Dr. John Berardi's G Flux concept, via Precision Nutrition.
- Author's own anecdotal case: college vs. pro off-season calorie/recovery comparison.

## Open questions
- The claim that icing delays healing by blocking IGF-1 release rests on Mirkin's later blog reversal rather than a study cited directly in this piece, and it runs against decades of standard RICE practice.
- G Flux benefits (nutrient partitioning, tissue remodeling) are asserted from a secondary source (Precision Nutrition) without a study cited in this article.
