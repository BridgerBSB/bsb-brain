# Weighted Baseball Training for Pitchers - A Get Started Guide

## TL;DR
- Weighted baseballs are a velocity tool that Driveline gates behind movement and performance screens, a base of throwing, and a strength and shoulder-stability base.
- Starter progression for an 18-year-old: 39 throws per session in weeks 1-2, 52 in weeks 3-4, two sessions a week, spanning 3 oz to 7 oz.
- Driveline works outside the usual 5 oz +/- 20% window and, as of the 2017 update, is experimenting with mound throws.

## Claims
- Intro: the training toolkit also includes barbells, dumbbells, varying-weight med balls, sled push/pull intervals and bands. Weighted baseballs are the "misunderstood" piece.
- Intro: Derenne's paper concluded that either underweighted (4 oz) or overweighted (6 oz) baseballs improved velocity compared with throwing normal baseballs.
- How do you implement: athletes are not trained aggressively with weighted balls until movement and performance screens are done, to find shoulder/hip imbalances and mechanical efficiency.
- How do you implement: no weighted balls in-season, and only after a solid throwing program. No weighted throwing cold, or at 80-85% of max velocity off a mound.
- How do you implement: programs are set by age, skill level, bodyweight, fastball release velocity and mechanics. Older athletes with more refined mechanics get a more aggressive plan.
- How do you implement: a typical 18-year-old throws BP once or twice a week plus one short bullpen. Weighted balls go on bullpen days, and possibly one BP day, using a truncated motion focused on shoulder rotation.
- Throwing progression: full-mechanic flat-ground or truncated throws go from 3 oz to 7 oz, against the common advice of +/- 20% of 5 oz. The author says that advice comes from Soviet shot-put research, not baseball research.
- Throwing progression: originally no weighted balls off a mound because arm stress rises with both. The 7/22/2017 update says ASMI found mound elbow torque similar to a 5 oz ball for 4 oz, and lower for 6 and 7 oz.
- Throwing progression: weeks 1-2 are 39 throws (4x5 oz, 4x6, 4x7, 3x5, 4x4, 4x3), 2 sessions a week. Weeks 3-4 are 52 throws (5/5/5/4/5/5 in the same order), 2 sessions a week.
- The Final Scoop: build reasonable gym strength and shoulder stability first, because the program raises torque and maximum external rotation. Have a coach assess the shoulders and on-ramp the athlete.

## Drills
- **Pivot Pickoff** - 3 lb med ball, mechanics similar to a weighted-ball truncated motion; builds the out-front portion of the pitch; for pitchers using truncated weighted-ball work
- **Weighted baseball progression (3-7 oz)** - 3, 4, 5, 6 and 7 oz balls in a set order, flat ground, truncated or full motion, 2 sessions a week; builds velocity through under- and overload; for a screened 18-year-old with a throwing base

## Evidence cited
- Derenne, Effects of Under and Overweighted Implement Training on Pitching Velocity: 4 oz or 6 oz beat normal baseballs.
- ASMI weighted-ball mound study (2017 update): 6 and 7 oz gave less elbow torque than 5 oz, and 4 oz gave similar torque.
- A commenter cites Fleisig et al. 2017 and Escamilla et al. 2010 for lower shoulder and elbow forces with heavier balls, and equivocal results with underweight balls.

## Open questions
- The 3-7 oz range is justified only by calling the +/- 20% rule non-baseball research. No baseball data is given for the wider range or for the 39/52 throw volumes.
- The original "no mound throws" stance is later softened by the 2017 update. The body text and the update disagree, and the source gives no current mound protocol.
