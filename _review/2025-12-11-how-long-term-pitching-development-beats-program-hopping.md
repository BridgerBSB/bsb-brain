# How Long-Term Pitching Development Beats Program Hopping

## TL;DR
- Program hopping happens when athletes quit an approach before it has had time to work, not because the approach failed.
- The fix Brewster proposes is education: understanding the why behind a program reduces blind switching while still allowing real experimentation at the margins.
- No data or method is given for how much patience is enough; the piece is a philosophy statement, not an instructional program.

## Claims
- Thoughts on program hopping & over-tinkering: athletes who program-hop lack the patience to let an approach run long enough to judge it, so they don't let the experiment reach statistical significance.
- Thoughts on program hopping & over-tinkering: examples given are trying a new pitch grip for only a handful of pitches before dropping it, quitting a nutrition plan after stalling in week 3, and bouncing between facilities or coaches looking for a quick fix.
- Thoughts on program hopping & over-tinkering: as an athlete finds what works, mechanical/lifting/nutrition experiments should move "primarily on the fringes" rather than replacing the whole program.
- So what is the solution?: the proposed solution is education — understanding the why behind training reduces blind searching and helps athletes keep the parts of a routine that provide real utility while staying open to new ideas.
- Author bio: Brewster describes his own path from 73 mph and 150 lbs to being drafted and touching 98+ mph over roughly 20 years of tinkering.
- P.P.S.: Brewster states he recently hit 95+ mph for the first time in over 8 years, despite unspecified bodily injuries.
