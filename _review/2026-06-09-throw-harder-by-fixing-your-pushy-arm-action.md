# Throw Harder By Fixing Your Pushy Arm Action

## TL;DR
- A pushy arm action happens when the pec or lat never get stretched on the backside, so the elbow drives in front of the face instead of getting pulled through by the torso.
- The panel checks three points on video: foot strike, max external rotation, and release. At foot strike the elbow should sit on the shoulder plane at roughly 90 degrees, plus or minus 10 degrees.
- Fixing it usually means going upstream to torso sequencing and lower-half timing, not the arm itself; the panel guesses this explains the underlying issue in something like 90% of pushy throwers.

## Claims
- [01:01](https://youtu.be/zz7SwZxPQwE?t=61) A pushy arm action looks like the elbow driving in front of the face during unraveling, similar to a dart throw, visible from a side view.
- [04:05](https://youtu.be/zz7SwZxPQwE?t=245) If the lat or pec never stretch on the backside, the arm has no choice but to be pushed through to reach a consistent release point.
- [07:08](https://youtu.be/zz7SwZxPQwE?t=428) Pitchers throwing well below the average velocity for their age and level almost always have a pushy arm action.
- [12:12](https://youtu.be/zz7SwZxPQwE?t=732) At front foot strike the arm should sit on the shoulder plane at roughly 90 degrees, plus or minus 10 degrees, with the torso counter-rotated over the pelvis.
- [17:19](https://youtu.be/zz7SwZxPQwE?t=1039) Turner estimates, calling it "a gross assumption," that around 90% of pushy-arm-action pitchers have another upstream issue driving it.
- [21:22](https://youtu.be/zz7SwZxPQwE?t=1282) Elbow-driven throwers stay in internal rotation longer before a late, shallow flip; hand-driven throwers get to external rotation earlier and hold it.
- [43:40](https://youtu.be/zz7SwZxPQwE?t=2620) Training too close to a plyo wall and throwing center-cut into the net can reinforce early torso rotation and train a pushy pattern.
- [47:44](https://youtu.be/zz7SwZxPQwE?t=2864) Supinated pushy throwers tend toward cuttery four-seams and bigger, harder breaking balls; pronated pushy throwers get flatter four-seams and smaller breaking balls that are hard to make bigger.
- [58:55](https://youtu.be/zz7SwZxPQwE?t=3535) A ring-finger-pressure cue at release is offered as an in-season fix that keeps the pitcher "behind" the ball without reworking the full delivery.

## Cues
- **"Burn that ball through that net"** - fixes throwing to a spot instead of through it during plyo ball work; for pitchers whose release gets pushy when the target net is close.
- **"Hold the ball and put a ton of pressure on this ring finger"** - fixes the ball sliding to the fingertips when the torso opens early, so the pitcher stays behind it at release; for pitchers with crossfire/early-torso timing issues, as an in-season fix.
- **"Add a glove tap"** (start the hands faster) - fixes the upper half falling behind once the lower half gets faster and more efficient; for pitchers whose arm timing lags after a lower-half mechanical fix.

## Drills
- **Lasso drill** - abbreviated arm-action drill that forces the arm into position before it's allowed to lay back; builds elbow-driven timing; for pitchers being retrained toward a shorter, elbow-driven arm action.
- **Pendulum drill** - same category of abbreviated, forcing arm-action drill; builds arm positioning independent of the full delivery; for pitchers being retrained toward elbow-driven timing.
- **Plyo wall with added space** - increase the distance between release point and the net instead of throwing close-up; builds treating the target as three-dimensional rather than a single spot; for pitchers who train a pushy pattern by throwing center-cut into a nearby net.

## Evidence cited
- Mitch White: fastball movement measured at 19x8 inches (good backspin) on good days versus 15x15 (side-spin) on days his crossfire timing goes early and pushy; panel notes he hit 99 mph the week of recording. [55:50](https://youtu.be/zz7SwZxPQwE?t=3350)
- Scott Blewett: pronated pushy arm action; four-seam runs roughly 15 inches horizontal / 10 vertical break; can throw a cutter and a shorter "death ball" but can't get a big breaking-ball shape at velocity. [47:44](https://youtu.be/zz7SwZxPQwE?t=2864)
- Named athlete referred to as "Jack So" (sic, garbled in transcript): fixed a pushy arm action rooted in a self-taught "stay behind it" command cue; velocity moved into the mid-90s and breaking balls are now thrown at 84-86 mph. [50:46](https://youtu.be/zz7SwZxPQwE?t=3106)

## Open questions
- The 90% estimate for upstream-caused pushy actions is offered as a guess ("a gross assumption") with no data behind it. [17:19](https://youtu.be/zz7SwZxPQwE?t=1039)
- The panel disagrees on how directly to coach the hand-driven vs. elbow-driven timing distinction: one coach says he never teaches it because it's too hard to feel, while others build assessment and drills directly around it. [26:26](https://youtu.be/zz7SwZxPQwE?t=1586)
