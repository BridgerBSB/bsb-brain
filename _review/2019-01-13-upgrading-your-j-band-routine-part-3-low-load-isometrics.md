# Upgrading Your J-Band Routine Part 3: Low Load Isometrics

## TL;DR
- Teaches a low load isometric protocol with J-bands: find an end-range throwing position (external rotation at 90, T, Y at 130), back the band off to about a 20% contraction, hold 6 seconds, 6 reps per position.
- Frames the work as prehab borrowed from rehab reintroduction, on the reasoning that injuries occur at end-range delicate positions, not the strong mid-range.
- Recommends running the same three positions in reverse (horizontal adduction, internal rotation) so both directions of end-range stability get trained; the whole addition costs about 1 to 2 minutes in a warmup.

## Claims
- [00:00](https://youtu.be/TNOFsucvMlk?t=0) Low load isometrics train end-range delicate positions like max external rotation at low effort, with the stated goal of strengthening the neurological pathway for recruiting stabilizing muscles there, not building raw strength.
- [01:01](https://youtu.be/TNOFsucvMlk?t=61) Injuries happen at end-range delicate positions rather than the strong mid-range, so training those positions functions as prehab.
- [02:04](https://youtu.be/TNOFsucvMlk?t=124) The protocol adds only 1 to 2 minutes to a J-band warmup: about 20% effort for 6 sets of 6-second holds at each of the three positions from the part 1 routine.
- [03:05](https://youtu.be/TNOFsucvMlk?t=185) Each rep: find the end-range position unresisted, back the band off to a 20% contraction, hold 6 seconds, targeting scapula and posterior cuff without compensating.
- [04:09](https://youtu.be/TNOFsucvMlk?t=249) The Y position is 130 degrees of horizontal abduction, thumb up; the low resistance is intentional so the athlete can feel the target muscles fire at that specific joint angle.
- [05:11](https://youtu.be/TNOFsucvMlk?t=311) The same three positions should also be trained in reverse (horizontal adduction, internal rotation) with the band behind the body, same 20%-effort, 6x6-second protocol, to balance stability in both directions at the joint's extremes.

## Cues
- **"keep yourself honest and stay in good positions"** - fixes compensating out of the target end-range position during the isometric hold; for pitchers doing prehab/rehab arm care
- **"feel it in the right spots, scapula, posterior cuff"** - fixes vague or misdirected muscle recruitment during the hold; for pitchers learning the position
- **"back up until you get a 20 percent contraction and hold for six seconds"** - fixes jumping straight into loaded end-range work without first grooving low-load control; for pitchers reintroducing a joint to loading

## Anti-cues

## Drills
- **J-band low load isometric, external rotation at 90** - band set to max external rotation at 90 degrees abduction, back off to ~20% contraction, hold 6 seconds for 6 reps; builds end-range neurological recruitment and stability at the shoulder's most stressful throwing position; for throwers in prehab or rehab reintroduction
- **J-band low load isometric, T position** - straight-arm scapular retraction to end range, same 20%-contraction, 6x6-second protocol; builds end-range scapular stability
- **J-band low load isometric, Y position** - arm at 130 degrees horizontal abduction, thumb up, same 20%-contraction, 6x6-second protocol; builds end-range stability at the Y position
- **J-band low load isometric, reverse directions** - same three positions performed with the band behind the body (horizontal adduction, internal rotation), same 20%-contraction, 6x6-second protocol; builds end-range stability in the opposing direction at each joint angle
