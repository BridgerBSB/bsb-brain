# Auto-Regulation for Baseball

## TL;DR
- Proposes auto-regulating throwing volume/intensity day to day using a subjective 10-point arm-readiness scale instead of a fixed progression.
- Four session tiers keyed to readiness: max effort (8+/10), moderate (5-7/10), low (3-5/10), off (arm "hanging" or painful), inside a 4-day/week off-season long toss template.
- Argues rigid throwing programs either overload an under-recovered arm or under-load a fully recovered one; readiness-based dosing is meant to fix both.

## Claims
- **What exactly is auto-regulation?** - defines it as adjusting training stress to match the athlete's day-to-day psychological and physical readiness rather than following a fixed progression.
- **Quantifying readiness on a 10-point scale** - proposes asking "How does your arm feel today?" and scoring overall arm/body freshness 1-10 to pick the day's session.
- **Sample 4-day per week off-season long toss program with auto-regulation** - lays out a 4-day/week template with scheduled off days between throwing days, session type set by that day's readiness score.
- **Max effort day** - readiness 8/10 or higher: 97-100%+ of max long toss distance for 5-10 throws, 5-15 max-effort pull-down compression throws on the way in, optional 10-20 flat-ground throws at 75-85%.
- **Moderate effort** - readiness 5-7/10: 75-90% of max distance for 5-10 throws, optional 5-8 pull-downs at up to 90% intensity, optional 5-15 flat-ground throws at 65-75%.
- **Low effort** - readiness 3-5/10: 50-60% of max distance on a loose arc, no pull-down throws, optional 10-15 flat-ground throws at 50-60% intensity.
- **Off** - arm "hanging," painful, or extremely fatigued: skip the throwing session, do the full warm-up and arm care work, reassess next day; unscheduled off-days should be rare.
- **Conclusion** - states that powering through sessions when under-recovered raises burnout/injury risk, while easy sessions on a fully recovered day fail to use the athlete's capability.
- **References** - cites two strength-training studies as support for auto-regulation generally, then extends the logic to throwing without citing throwing-specific outcome data.

## Evidence cited
- Mann JB, Thyfault JP, Ivey PA, Sayers SP. "The Effect of Autoregulatory Progressive Resistance Exercise vs. Linear Periodization on Strength Improvement in College Athletes." J Strength Cond Res. 2010;24(7):1718-23.
- McNamara JM, Stearne DJ. "Flexible Nonlinear Periodization in a Beginner College Weight Training Class." J Strength Cond Res. 2010;24(1):17-22.

## Open questions
- Both cited studies are strength-training research; the article states "there is no reason the same concept should not be applied to throwing" without citing any throwing-specific study or outcome.
- The distance/intensity percentage table is called "quite accurate for the vast majority of pitchers" with no sample or data described to support that generalization.
