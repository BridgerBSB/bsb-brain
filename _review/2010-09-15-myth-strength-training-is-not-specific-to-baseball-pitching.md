# Myth: Strength Training is Not Specific to Baseball Pitching

## TL;DR
- Boddy argues that weight-room lifts are relevant to pitching because the body responds to force, not to the weight of the object. A 5 oz baseball does not make lifting irrelevant.
- His main number: a 135 lb bench press rep produces 135 pound-feet of torque, against the 81.35 pound-feet he cites for elbow valgus stress at release (60 N-m).
- He says strength work helps velocity and health by strengthening the segments that sequence the throw (proximal to distal) and the decelerators.

## Claims
- Opening paragraph: The belief that strength training is not specific to pitching is called a myth held at all levels of baseball.
- Paragraph 2: Elbow valgus stress in pitching can exceed 60 N-m, which he says is higher than the theoretical maximum UCL load in cadaver studies. He cites Medscape for this.
- Paragraph 2: He converts 60 N-m to 81.35 pound-feet of torque.
- Paragraph 3: One foot-pound force is one pound acting at a perpendicular distance of one foot from a pivot. He uses the bench press as the example, and says it is not perfectly perpendicular but close.
- Paragraph 4: The actual weight of the object "does not matter". The body responds to the raw force acting on it, whether the object weighs 5 oz or 200 lb.
- Paragraph 5: He assumes the bar moves 12 inches concentrically. On that assumption, a 135 lb bench rep produces 135 pound-feet of torque.
- Paragraph 6: Fewer muscle groups fire to create release velocity than in a bench press, squat, deadlift or row. He concludes the shoulder and elbow muscles carry a proportionally higher load.
- Paragraph 7: Load per contracting muscle fiber at the elbow and shoulder is said to exceed what average lifters experience in compound lifts.
- Paragraph 8: Strengthening the segments that sequence the throw, largest to smallest and proximal to distal, can raise fastball velocity.
- Paragraph 8: Strengthening the decelerators lets pitchers gain velocity more safely.

## Evidence cited
- Medscape overview page (emedicine.medscape.com/article/97451-overview), cited for elbow valgus stress above 60 N-m.
- Cadaver-based UCL loading studies, mentioned without a citation.
- The bench press torque figure is the author's own arithmetic, not a measurement.

## Open questions
- The torque comparison sets bar load times bar travel against joint torque at the elbow. These are different quantities, so the two figures are not comparable. The conversion also looks wrong: 60 N-m is about 44 pound-feet, not 81.35. The source gives no measurement showing that lifting transfers to velocity or injury rates.
- The claim that elbow and shoulder muscles carry a higher load per fiber than lifters' muscles has no data behind it. The post ends with a facility plug.
