# US vs. Korean Baseball - Are There Mechanical Differences?

## TL;DR
- Escamilla's 2002 study found American pro pitchers threw about 10% harder than Korean pros (85 mph vs 77 mph), and roughly half of that gap traces to anthropometrics (height, weight, arm length), with body mass the single most predictive variable.
- The clearest mechanical gap is external rotation at max layback: Americans reached 181 degrees vs Koreans' 167 degrees, matching Matsuo et al.'s finding that about 180 degrees of ER separates 86+ mph throwers from 75 mph throwers.
- Americans also showed more front leg knee extension after foot strike (17 degrees vs a near-isometric front leg) and more forward trunk tilt at release (36 vs 26 degrees), though the source cautions trunk tilt should not be cued directly.

## Claims
- Physical Differences - American pitchers in the study averaged 85 mph, Koreans averaged 77 mph.
- Physical Differences - Americans averaged 183 lbs and 5'11" with 25.3" arm length; Koreans averaged 177 lbs and 5'9.5" with 24.4" arm length.
- Physical Differences - anthropometric variables explained about 50% of the velocity variability between groups, with body mass the most predictive single variable.
- Mechanical Differences - at front foot contact, Americans had more horizontal abduction (-27 degrees vs -14 degrees), indicating a greater pec pre-stretch.
- Mechanical Differences - Koreans had more external rotation at front foot contact (68 degrees vs 45 degrees) but less at max layback (167 degrees vs 181 degrees).
- Mechanical Differences - cites Matsuo et al. 2001: pitchers throwing 86+ mph averaged about 180 degrees of external rotation at layback vs about 166 degrees for pitchers around 75 mph.
- Mechanical Differences - American front leg extended about 17 degrees from foot contact to release (49 to 32 degrees); the Korean front leg stayed near isometric (50 to 48 degrees).
- Mechanical Differences - Americans showed more forward trunk tilt at ball release (36 vs 26 degrees).
- Mechanical Differences - editor's note: forward trunk tilt should not be cued directly; it is a downstream result of sequencing into landing, and drills that force forward bend at the waist (towel drills) can be counterproductive.
- Final Thoughts - KBO average velocity rose from about 85 mph to about 89 mph over a decade after Korean pitchers began adopting weight training, versus MLB rising from 91.0 to 92.5 mph over the same period.

## Evidence cited
- Escamilla et al. 2002, with Fleisig and Andrews: mechanical and anthropometric comparison of American (85 mph avg) vs Korean (77 mph avg) professional pitchers.
- Matsuo et al. 2001: external rotation at layback averaged about 180 degrees for 86+ mph throwers vs about 166 degrees for about 75 mph throwers.
- Billy Wagner cited as the only pitcher averaging over 96 mph in 2002, shown with extreme layback and forward trunk tilt.
- KBO league velocity data (85 to ~89 mph over a decade) cited from a FanGraphs article on foreign players in the KBO.

## Open questions
- The source argues forward trunk tilt correlates with velocity but should not be cued directly since it is downstream of sequencing, which contradicts common towel-drill coaching that cues forward bend directly.
- The editor's note flags that the 6 lb / 1.5 inch weight and height gap between groups could reflect bone mass rather than muscle mass, meaning the "mass=gas" conclusion is asserted without enough detail in the study to confirm.
