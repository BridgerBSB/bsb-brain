# Nutrition: Pre/Post-Workout Guidelines (Alan Aragon)

## TL;DR
- Driveline reposts Alan Aragon's meal-timing protocol around training: 0.25 g/lb target bodyweight (TBW) of protein before and after, with carbs scaled to the meal.
- Total daily calories and protein matter most. The post sets protein at 1.0-1.5 g/lb TBW per day. Exact timing and glycemic index (GI) matter little.
- The post is a short repost of a forum answer, with no sources behind it.

## Claims
- Intro: Aragon's pre/post-workout advice is the one the author trusts most, and it is reposted here because athletes ask about it often.
- Pre-workout, 60-90 min before: a solid, balanced meal with protein 0.25 g/lb TBW and carbs 0.25 g/lb TBW. Fat is optional if it fits the macro goals. Skip this meal when training first thing in the morning.
- Pre-workout, 30-0 min before (or sipped during): a liquid or easily digested meal with protein 0.25 g/lb TBW and carbs 0.25 g/lb TBW. Aragon presents this as an alternative to the 60-90 min meal.
- Pre-workout, 30-0 min before: for continuous training of about 2 hours or more, this extra meal before or during training is definitely beneficial. Keep fat incidental if the athlete is prone to gastric distress.
- Post-workout, within 30 min: a liquid or solid meal with protein 0.25 g/lb TBW and carbs 0.25-0.5 g/lb TBW, depending on how carb-restricted the diet is. Fat amount does not matter if the daily target is hit.
- Post-postworkout: this is just the next scheduled meal. Whether it comes 1, 2 or 3 hours later does not matter, especially if the post-workout meal follows the guideline.
- Note: the changes from Aragon's earlier protocol are described as very insignificant and mainly meant to simplify it.
- Note: Aragon no longer cares about GI and calls it a worthless, irrelevant index. High-GI carbs before or during training are acceptable.
- Note: slight insulin elevations during and after training are good. Food amount drives this more than food type, and the insulin needed for maximal net protein balance is met without trying to spike it.
- Focus on what matters: set target calories, then set target protein at 1.0-1.5 g/lb TBW.

## Open questions
- The GI and insulin claims are asserted without data. The post calls them "proven" but cites nothing, and the 0.25 g/lb TBW and 30-minute window figures also have no stated source.
- The pre-workout section lists a 60-90 min meal and a 30-0 min meal. It is unclear whether they are alternatives or can be combined, and the post does not say how to choose for a given session.
