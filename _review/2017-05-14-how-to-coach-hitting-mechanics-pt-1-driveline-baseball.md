# How To Coach Hitting Mechanics Pt. 1 | Driveline Baseball

## TL;DR
- Diagnosing a flaw on video is the easy part; getting the hitter to change is the hard part, and the training environment does most of that work.
- Driveline prefers external cues (focus on the effect of the movement) over internal cues (focus on the body), citing studies on force and sequencing.
- Coaches change movement by manipulating constraints and overload, not by over-cueing; each athlete gets an individual program.

## Claims
- [00:00](https://youtu.be/tXKjS3iKgAk?t=0) Video diagnosis of mechanical flaws is the easy part of coaching; helping the hitter make the change is the difficult part.
- [00:00](https://youtu.be/tXKjS3iKgAk?t=0) The training environment is the most powerful influence on movement, and coaches are responsible for building one that refines the in-game skill of hitting.
- [00:00](https://youtu.be/tXKjS3iKgAk?t=0) Coaches affect the athlete's intention and focus through their cueing.
- [00:00](https://youtu.be/tXKjS3iKgAk?t=0) An internal cue directs focus to the body; an external cue directs focus to the effect of the movement. Driveline tries to use external cues and avoid internal ones.
- [00:00](https://youtu.be/tXKjS3iKgAk?t=0) Studies are said to show athletes produce more force and sequence more efficiently with an external focus than with an internal one (the caption reads "indirect" (sic) for the comparison).
- [01:01](https://youtu.be/tXKjS3iKgAk?t=61) Thinking about the body interferes with the part of the brain that handles reactions and subconscious movement.
- [01:01](https://youtu.be/tXKjS3iKgAk?t=61) A pitch at 80 to 90 mph takes less than half a second to reach the plate, so any focus not on the ball is a problem.
- [01:01](https://youtu.be/tXKjS3iKgAk?t=61) The body self-organizes to the tasks in its environment, so Driveline uses environmental and physical constraints plus overload training to drive the adaptation.
- [01:01](https://youtu.be/tXKjS3iKgAk?t=61) Over-coached, over-cued athletes become robotic without rhythm or individual style; every athlete has an individual program.
- [01:01](https://youtu.be/tXKjS3iKgAk?t=61) Challenge athletes daily and let them fail so they learn to trust their training. Ochart says this is not how he always coached, but it is the most effective method he has found.
