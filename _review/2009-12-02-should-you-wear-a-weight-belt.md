# Should you wear a weight belt?

## TL;DR
- A belt works by giving the abs something to push against, raising intra-abdominal pressure and ab muscle work, not by "supporting the back."
- Recommends a 10mm thick, no-taper belt, 4 inches tall for most lifters (3 inches for a short torso), citing EMG data showing a belt raises rectus abdominis activity versus no belt.
- Suggests adding a belt once squat progress stalls at the end of a novice program.

## Claims
- Should you wear a weight belt? - A belt gives the abs something to push against, letting the abs generate more intra-abdominal pressure and squat, deadlift, press, and bench more weight.
- Should you wear a weight belt? - Common gym belts (small in front, wide in back) give the abs almost nothing to push against, undermining the point of wearing one.
- Should you wear a weight belt? - An EMG study on sumo and conventional deadlift found the belt condition produced significantly greater rectus abdominis activity and significantly less external oblique activity than the no-belt condition.
- Should you wear a weight belt? - A proper belt is 3-4 inches tall and 10mm thick with no taper; most lifters should use 4 inches, and lifters with a short torso (less than normal room between ribcage and iliac crest) should use 3 inches.
- Should you wear a weight belt? - 13mm thick belts are for strongmen and powerlifters, take a long time to break in, and are not needed for the average athlete.
- Should you wear a weight belt? - Author recommends a single-prong APT belt, about $50 plus shipping, measured around the belt button, not the waist.
- Should you wear a weight belt? - A belt tightener tool costs about $40-50 and makes it much easier to get the belt tight without help.
- Should you wear a weight belt? - Recommends getting a belt once a lifter's squat stalls at the end of a novice program.

## Evidence cited
- EMG study, "An electromyographic analysis of sumo and conventional stance deadlift" (ACSM, 2002): belt condition produced significantly greater rectus abdominis activity and significantly less external oblique activity than no belt.
