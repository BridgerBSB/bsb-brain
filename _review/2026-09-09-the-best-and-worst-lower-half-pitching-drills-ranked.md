# The Best And Worst Lower Half Pitching Drills [Ranked]

## TL;DR
- Three Tread coaches tier-rank twelve common lower-half drills (rockers, towel drill, long toss, Hershiser wall drill, janitor throw, pulldowns/turn-and-burn, Kikuchi, med ball throws, water bag, balance point, walking windup, core velocity belt) on how well each teaches back-hip loading and rotation.
- Their recurring finding: the lowest-graded drills (Hershiser wall drill, balance point) fail for the same reason - a static or linear position where the center of mass never travels, producing a "false drift" that leaves the pitcher stuck uphill and unable to rotate.
- The highest-graded drills (janitor throw, core velocity belt) share a purpose: teaching the pitcher to load and hold back-hip tension before release instead of pushing linearly; one coach cites going from 95 to 98 mph in about three months after building his delivery around the janitor-throw feel.

## Claims
- [01:02](https://youtu.be/0IOtRAI-8qk?t=62) Rockers done linearly (front foot fixed, no rotation) reinforce a soft front leg and an extendy back leg; run as a "figure-eight" with a free front heel, the same drill teaches rotational back-hip loading instead.
- [13:15](https://youtu.be/0IOtRAI-8qk?t=795) Dry reps (e.g., towel drill) still count as throwing volume; one coach strained his brachioradialis from unaccounted dry-rep load stacked on a 30-pitch bullpen.
- [19:19](https://youtu.be/0IOtRAI-8qk?t=1159) Long toss past roughly 150 ft can push a low-slot/supinating pitcher toward more backspin than his mound throw, while a high three-quarter pitcher benefits out to 300-350 ft.
- [10:10](https://youtu.be/0IOtRAI-8qk?t=610) Getting too extended down the mound uses up available hip internal rotation, physically blocking rotation and forcing the trunk and arm to compensate.
- [33:31](https://youtu.be/0IOtRAI-8qk?t=2011) The Hershiser wall drill's static hip-load position creates a "false drift": the center of mass never moves, so there's no dynamic way in or out of it.
- [39:38](https://youtu.be/0IOtRAI-8qk?t=2378) One coach went from a career-high 95 mph to 98 mph within about three months (October to January) after building his delivery around janitor-throw back-hip tension.
- [55:54](https://youtu.be/0IOtRAI-8qk?t=3354) Pulldown velocity should ramp in small steps (90 to 92 to 94 to 96-97 mph) rather than jump to max effort; a single overload session may total only 8-12 throws.
- [1:01:58](https://youtu.be/0IOtRAI-8qk?t=3718) The step-back Kikuchi drill is credited as a key piece of Mitch Keller's velocity jump to up to 100 mph in an offseason.
- [1:18:23](https://youtu.be/0IOtRAI-8qk?t=4703) Balance point drills (pausing at peak leg lift) are graded D/F by all three coaches for the same false-drift reason as the Hershiser drill.
- [1:29:32](https://youtu.be/0IOtRAI-8qk?t=5372) A back-hip-anchored core velocity band, used at roughly 10% resistance, is graded S tier and layered onto other drills to teach holding hip rotation closed until release.

## Cues
- **"Hold tension, hold tension"** - fixes early release of back-hip tension before foot strike; for pitchers using the janitor throw.
- **"Go home with my butt leading the way"** - fixes early opening/spinning off the back hip; for pitchers with a lot of hip internal rotation in the janitor setup.
- **"Press the gas pedal late"** - fixes rushing out of leg lift into a pushy delivery; for pitchers who get stuck over the rubber.
- **"Fight it, fight it, fight it, fight it"** - fixes early pelvis rotation; for pitchers who land open with a pushy arm, using the core velocity band.
- **"Use the towel as a whip to unravel out"** - fixes an overly forward, pushy towel-drill rep; for pitchers using the towel drill as dry reps.

## Anti-cues
- **"Feel that strong powerful angle"** - taught to fix backside weight shift/loading (Hershiser wall drill); actually causes a false drift, uses up hip range of motion, and leaves the pitcher stuck uphill unable to rotate; per Tread coaches.
- **"Get to your balance point and hold it"** - taught to fix an unstable, wobbly lower half in young players; actually causes a false drift since the center of mass never travels, leading to reaching and a pushy arm; per Tread coaches.

## Drills
- **Figure-eight rocker** - open/close the pelvis rhythmically with the front heel free to lift; builds rotational back-hip loading; for pitchers whose rockers turn linear and extendy.
- **Towel drill** - throw motion holding a towel as a dry rep; builds arm-slowing feedback and sequencing feel; caution: dry reps still add throwing volume.
- **Long toss** - graduated-distance throws (120 ft+) for ball-flight/velocity feedback; builds direction, athleticism, and pitch-shape variability across arm slots.
- **Hershiser wall drill** - static hip-load against a wall at a fixed angle; builds a feel for back-leg tension but graded C/D/F for producing a false drift.
- **Janitor throw** - preset back-hip counter-rotation (foot/tibia turned in) before throwing; builds and teaches holding back-hip tension into leg lift and drive; some pitchers (e.g., Zach Britton) pitch directly from this position.
- **Kikuchi drill** (plus step-back variant) - rise onto the toe at peak leg lift, then drop to feel heel strike; builds a stacked, heel-first drop instead of toe-drift rotation.
- **Pulldowns / turn-and-burn** - overload throws above mound velocity after a multi-week on-ramp; turn-and-burn starts from a closed, coiled position; builds over-speed stimulus and diagnoses linear-momentum vs. rotational-loading bias.
- **Balance point drill** - pause the delivery at peak leg lift; graded D/F for most: center of mass never travels; a "hover" variant that still drifts afterward is rated better.
- **Walking windup** - step into the throw from a full windup at a slow tempo; builds a feel for the right amount of forward drift; paired with step-backs to calibrate from both directions.
- **Core velocity belt/band** - band anchored at the back hip pulling the pelvis open; builds resistance to early pelvis rotation and holding separation until release; layered onto other drills (janitor, rocker).

## Evidence cited
- An unnamed study ("a couple decades ago") compared torque on max-effort throws at 60 ft, 120 ft downhill, and max-distance long toss; found slightly higher torque at max distance, which one coach estimates at roughly 4-6% more stress.
- Zach Britton (Louisville) reportedly pitched directly out of a janitor-throw setup, sitting 100-102 mph.
- Mitch Keller: step-back Kikuchi drill credited as a key part of his velocity jump to up to 100 mph after an offseason.
- One Tread coach, first-person: 95 mph career-high to 98 mph within about three months of adopting janitor-throw tension work.

## Open questions
- The source disputes the cited long-toss torque study's implications without offering its own data, arguing the load difference is manageable rather than disqualifying.
- The source treats the janitor throw as directly pitchable for some athletes (e.g., Zach Britton), which sits in tension with its own general claim that no single drill is a magic bullet outside full delivery context.
