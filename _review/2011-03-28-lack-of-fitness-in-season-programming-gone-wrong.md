# Lack of Fitness: In-Season Programming Gone Wrong

## TL;DR
- In-season arm trouble is framed as a lack of fitness (underworked), not overwork: throwing continues while mobility and maintenance strength lapse.
- Training stress should be set by the pitcher's training status: the more trained the athlete, the less stress to impose in one consolidated block.
- Cressey's rule of thumb: a youth pitcher throwing fewer than three innings per week trains like a position player, with one session the day after throwing.

## Claims
- Opening: two high school two-way players on the same team missed early-season time, one to flu (systemic) and one to a left foot stress fracture (local).
- Opening: the flu athlete needs rest and minor flexibility work that does not demand systemic recovery.
- Opening: the stress fracture athlete can train from kneeling, prone, or supine positions without loading the injured area.
- Opening: both setbacks landed as they should have been finishing the skill acquisition portion of their training cycles.
- Body: an athlete's "injury" is a lack of fitness problem; scapular mobility/flexibility work was missing and maintenance strength training was neglected while practice throwing was high.
- Cressey quote: a young athlete pitching fewer than three innings per week is trained like a position player, with at least one session the day after throwing.
- Cressey quote: that placement consolidates stress into a 24-hour block for better recovery and forces mobility drills and manual stretching to normalize range of motion after an appearance.
- Body: stress tolerance is a continuum based on training status; the more skilled and trained the athlete, the less stress to impose in a consolidated block, and vice versa.
- Body: most coaches reverse this, babying youth and giving older players more workload, but a game appearance taxes the more advanced athlete more heavily.
- Closing: get maintenance or slight incremental strength/speed work in where possible, plus regular band mobility work and foam rolling.
