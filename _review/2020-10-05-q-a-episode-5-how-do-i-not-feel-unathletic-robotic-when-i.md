# Q & A Episode 5 : How Do I Not Feel Unathletic & Robotic When I Throw?

## TL;DR
- A robotic pitcher is either under-coordinated (low proprioception) or has been coached out of natural athleticism; the fix for both is moving delivery control from conscious thought to automatic.
- Tools: add a time constraint, change the movement trigger, add an external signal, and use external cues instead of internal ones.
- Limit verbal feedback to one or two things per session, and do not let video or radar become a crutch.

## Claims
- [01:00](https://youtu.be/54NQPVz1Vf8?t=60) Athleticism is body control, coordination and motor pattern adaptability that efficiently produces and transfers energy across changing conditions (mound slope, arm slot).
- [02:01](https://youtu.be/54NQPVz1Vf8?t=121) Two causes: a true lack of proprioception, or over-coaching. A catcher who throws 90 to second but 82 off the mound is the example.
- [03:01](https://youtu.be/54NQPVz1Vf8?t=181) Both causes share one fix: move from the cognitive/associative stages of motor learning to the autonomous stage.
- [04:03](https://youtu.be/54NQPVz1Vf8?t=243) A time constraint leaves less room for inefficiency and forces the body to coordinate itself.
- [05:03](https://youtu.be/54NQPVz1Vf8?t=303) Changing the movement trigger (feel a ground ball, partner toss, self-tossed fly ball, then throw) makes the throw more reactive.
- [06:04](https://youtu.be/54NQPVz1Vf8?t=364) An external signal such as "go", a hop back, or a figure-eight arm path works as a trigger. Works best for youth, but also for pros with a choppy, pushy arm action.
- [07:05](https://youtu.be/54NQPVz1Vf8?t=425) External cues tend to work better than internal ones for over-analytical pitchers.
- [08:06](https://youtu.be/54NQPVz1Vf8?t=486) Slow, deliberate drills such as balance point work give too much time to think and bring back bad habits.
- [09:08](https://youtu.be/54NQPVz1Vf8?t=548) Feedback after every pitch overloads the athlete; simplify to one, maybe two, things per session.
- [10:08](https://youtu.be/54NQPVz1Vf8?t=608) Solo throwing into a net or wall should not be the majority of throwing; throw to a partner. Solo sessions can balloon from 50 throws to two hours of over-analysis.

## Cues
- **"smash the catcher's face mask with this fastball"** - fixes internal over-focus and a robotic delivery; for over-analytical or unathletic pitchers
- **"pull this ball down through my catcher's chest protector"** - fixes internal over-focus and a robotic delivery; for over-analytical or unathletic pitchers
- **"place this ball on the corner"** - fixes internal over-focus and a robotic delivery; for over-analytical or unathletic pitchers

## Anti-cues
- **"where my hand is in space / where my front foot is landing / where is my elbow flexion angle"** - taught to fix mechanical position faults; actually causes internal focus that feels robotic and unathletic; per Ben Brewster, Tread ([07:05](https://youtu.be/54NQPVz1Vf8?t=425))
- **"getting on top of the ball", "staying back", thumb position (stacked on successive pitches)** - taught to fix mechanical faults; actually causes overload when a coach gives a new one every pitch; per Ben Brewster, Tread ([09:08](https://youtu.be/54NQPVz1Vf8?t=548))

## Drills
- **Quick pick** - drill with a time constraint and a coach "go" signal; builds automatic, reactive movement; for robotic or youth pitchers
- **Half-kneeling quick pick** - same constraint from half-kneeling; builds automatic, reactive movement; for robotic pitchers
- **Hop back pivot pick off** - hop or jump back replaces the normal pivot pick trigger; builds a more dynamic, athletic feel; for pitchers who did standard pivot pick offs
- **Figure eight rocker** - figure-eight arm path starts the rocker; builds flow and momentum
- **Bauer drill** - change of direction into the back side, then reverse; builds feel of unloading the pelvis and weight shift
- **QB drill (one shuffle)** - one shuffle back, turn and fire; builds feet moving underneath, less flat-footed
- **Turn and burn** - max-effort turn and throw; builds rotation feel and foot movement; only at the right point in the throwing progression, not daily
- **Ground ball feel, catch and throw** - feel a ground ball or self-toss a fly ball, then transition into the throw; builds a reactive trigger
