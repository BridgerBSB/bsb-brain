# Rethinking Periodization: Principles of Energy in Training Organization

## TL;DR
- Argues periodization should be organized around energy transfer (mobility, stiffness, sequencing through feet-hips-spine) rather than sets, reps, and general categories like strength or hypertrophy.
- Foot mechanics (rolling supination to pronation, force through the 4th/5th metatarsal) and hip internal rotation are the foundation; you cannot cue technique around a mobility restriction.
- Proposes a three-stage progression: build movement capacity, then integrate timing/sequencing, then integrate into sport-specific movement.

## Claims
- ## Quantitative versus Qualitative - argues sports performance overvalues quantitative measures (1RM, sets/reps, bar velocity) while treating energy transfer, rotation, and fascia as unmeasurable and therefore less important, when they are equally quantifiable, just not yet measured.
- ## Genetics - calls genetics "a blueprint, not a destiny," arguing it gets blamed by default whenever performance shortfalls aren't understood.
- ## Energy: The Critical Link - lays out kinetic energy = ½ x mass x velocity², momentum = mass x velocity, and force = mass x acceleration to argue mass, velocity, time, and collisions are the shared language underlying all movement.
- ## Feet-Hips-Spine-Fascia - the foot has three arches (medial, lateral, transverse); efficient energy transfer requires ground contact at the 4th/5th metatarsal with a roll from supination to pronation.
- ## Feet-Hips-Spine-Fascia - describes a ground-contact sequence (Achilles loading, forward-downward knee force, glute/hamstring femur retraction) that creates passive posterior chain tension without neural drive.
- ## Feet-Hips-Spine-Fascia - heel striking sends energy through bone and joint instead of fascia; limited hip internal rotation restricts hip flexion and forces QL compensation that locks the spine.
- ## Feet-Hips-Spine-Fascia - describes a spinal wave during a jump's arm swing: shoulder extension pairs with T-spine flexion (loading), shoulder flexion pairs with T-spine extension (unloading), timed with knee and hip extension for full-body elasticity.
- ## Periodizing Energy Transfer and Putting it All Together - "You can't cue your way out of mobility restrictions"; exercise selection should target the specific mechanism improving energy transfer, not general labels like strength, power, or hypertrophy.
- ## Periodizing Energy Transfer and Putting it All Together - cites Jan Zelezny, world record javelin thrower, as an example: his high running volume only reinforced good performance because his foot contact and hip internal rotation were already sound.
- ## Periodizing Energy Transfer and Putting it All Together - proposes training progress in three stages: build movement capacity (feet, hip rotation, spinal segmentation), integrate timing/sequencing into faster movement, then integrate into sport-specific movement.

## Drills

- **Spinal waves** - performed in various body positions; builds spinal segmentation and energy transfer feel
- **Dead arm circles** - hips and spine move while the arms stay relaxed through big shoulder circles; builds passive, relaxed shoulder-hip sequencing
- **Crawling exercises** - Ido Portal crawling patterns, named without setup detail; builds full-body coordinated movement

## Evidence cited

## Open questions
- The claim that force through the 4th/5th metatarsal and hip internal rotation are "the most overlooked aspects of athleticism" is asserted without a cited study or biomechanical dataset.
- The article dismisses standard periodization terms (strength, power, hypertrophy) as "too general" but does not propose a testable way to measure "energy transfer" itself.
