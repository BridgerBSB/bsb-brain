# Baseball Home Gym to Pitch Harder

## TL;DR
- Coan McAlpine trained almost exclusively in a 13'x9' room from age 12 to 18 and went from a low-70s fastball at 15 to touching 92 mph as a 6'4", 230+ pound high schooler.
- The article's argument: limited equipment forces focus on roughly 10 basic movements instead of chasing gym variety, machines, or training fads.
- Core claim is that consistent programming, coaching, and self-directed work ethic drive development more than facility quality.

## Claims
- Intro: Coan McAlpine trained almost exclusively in a 13'x9' room from age 12 to 18, going from a low-70s lefty at age 15 to 6'4", 230+ pounds and touching 92 mph in high school.
- The Equipment: he was taught about 10 key movements at age 12 and spent the following 6 years progressing and perfecting them.
- #1 It focused my efforts: limited equipment filtered out distractions such as extra machines, accessories, and gym misinformation, concentrating effort on essential lifts.
- #1 It focused my efforts: he cites his brother/mentor's guiding phrase, "Control what you can control. Believe in the process. Control your inputs; monitor your outputs."
- #2 It developed my work ethic and identity: his brother/trainer left for college when Coan was 13, after which he trained alone for most of his early career, communicating with his brother daily.
- #3 It kept me focused on progression: he recommends adding training volume when "over-recovered" and taking a deload week when "under-recovered," tracked against personal bests.
- Continuing the journey: he argues athletes should confirm mastery of basics before chasing the latest training gimmick.
