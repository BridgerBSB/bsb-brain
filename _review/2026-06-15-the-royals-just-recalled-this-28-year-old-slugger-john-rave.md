# The Royals Just Recalled This 28-Year-Old Slugger | John Rave Cage Session

## TL;DR
- Driveline hitting trainer Christian Leone runs a maintenance cage session with Royals OF John Rave, built around a leg-lift-to-toe-tap change made over the 2025-26 offseason.
- Biomech/mocap data both justified the toe-tap change and flagged that Rave still reaches for more during his stride, which shaped the drill sequence (pivot pick, underload bat, RH cutter/LH curveball).
- Goal of the session was maintenance, not new mechanics: hold the load, stay vertical through breaking pitches, keep the barrel working through the zone; Rave was recalled by Kansas City on 2026-06-14.

## Claims
- [00:01](https://youtu.be/yep_HchrEnA?t=1) Rave changed from a leg lift to a toe tap this offseason to better control his forward move.
- [00:01](https://youtu.be/yep_HchrEnA?t=1) Biomech data supported the toe tap change: it helps him control center of mass, hold his back foot, and keep his hips from sliding up underneath him.
- [00:01](https://youtu.be/yep_HchrEnA?t=1) This session's goal was maintenance of an established change; Driveline keeps a drill set in a package to reset Rave when he drifts from it.
- [00:01](https://youtu.be/yep_HchrEnA?t=1) Rave starts cage sessions with "darts" to get loose, then works handle load and barrel load (open and closed) to shape barrel path and direction.
- [06:11](https://youtu.be/yep_HchrEnA?t=371) Rave's last biomech assessment showed he reaches for more during the stride phase; the coach used a left-handed curveball and the pivot pick drill to get him loaded to end range early instead of reaching during the stride.
- [08:12](https://youtu.be/yep_HchrEnA?t=492) Coach's instruction on breaking balls: catch it as deep as possible and pull it, aimed at cleaning up misses where the barrel works through late.
- [09:13](https://youtu.be/yep_HchrEnA?t=553) Rave recalls being told "all year" that hitting the ball to the second baseman doesn't matter how hard it's hit, tied to his bat path getting too flat; the RH cutter / LH curveball pairing is meant to keep him in posture and maintain steepness in his vertical bat angle.

## Cues
- **"Catch it as deep as you can and pull it"** - fixes the barrel working through the zone late on breaking balls; for hitters working cutters and curveballs
- **"Stay in the spot"** - fixes reaching or rushing off the load before the pitch; for hitters chasing extra bat speed
- **"Keep [the back shoulder] lower than this front one"** - fixes the back shoulder rising early; for hitters who get flat and roll over

## Drills
- **Darts** - light-ball warm-up throws to start the cage session; builds feel before hitting; for warm-up
- **Handle load / barrel load (open and closed)** - hitting off a handle-loaded and barrel-loaded implement in open and closed positions, including a heavier ball; builds barrel entry angle and direction; for hitters working barrel path
- **Pivot pick** - swings starting from the end range of the load with no stride, against a left-handed curveball; builds staying in the load instead of reaching during the stride; for hitters who overreach in the stride phase
- **Underload/light bat vs. left-handed curveball** - swings a lighter bat against a LH curve, hands high with a bottom-hand elbow-up feel; builds staying vertical instead of getting flat and hooking it; for hitters who roll under breaking balls
- **RH cutter / LH curveball live BP** - live at-bats mixing a right-handed cutter and left-handed curveball; builds staying in posture and keeping vertical bat angle steep through both shapes; for hitters who flatten out and roll over when extended
- **Opposite-side short round** - a short round of swings taken left-handed; builds feel for back-side-to-front-side sequencing; for maintenance/feel work
