# Supplements for Baseball: Part Two

## TL;DR
- Quotes a T-Nation article arguing Vitamin D (via sunlight or supplementation) improves athletic performance, citing old irradiation studies with gains as high as 13%.
- Recommends a Vitamin D supplementation range from the NIH minimum of 200 IU/day up to the author's personal dose of 6000-8000 IU/day, well above the NIH's stated 2000 IU tolerable upper limit.
- Also recommends a specific whey protein product mixed with milk 3-4 times per day; both product mentions carry Amazon affiliate links.

## Claims
- **Vitamin D**: Russian studies from the 1930s found 100m dash times improved 7.4% in UV-irradiated athletes vs. 1.4% in non-irradiated athletes under the same training.
- **Vitamin D**: German studies from the 1940s found a 13% improvement in bike ergometer performance in the irradiated group vs. no improvement in controls.
- **Vitamin D**: A 1950s study at the Sports College of Cologne found a "convincing effect" on athletic performance, reported to the Olympic Committee.
- **Vitamin D**: A 1956 study found UV radiation improved reaction times by 17%.
- **Vitamin D**: A 1960s study on American college women found improvements in strength, speed, and endurance after a single UV irradiation dose.
- **Vitamin D**: Vitamin D administration improved muscle protein anabolism in Vitamin D-deficient subjects.
- **4 comments**: NIH's tolerable upper intake for Vitamin D is 2000 IU/day; some 1997 research suggested tolerance up to 10,000 IU/day; NIH's stated minimum (no sunlight assumed) is 200 IU/day.
- **4 comments**: The Vitamin D Council's research list supports dosing around 5000 IU/day; Dr. Richard Cohen of Bioletics recommends 4000 IU/day to reach a 50 ng/ml blood concentration.
- **4 comments**: The author personally doses 6000-8000 IU/day of Vitamin D with no reported ill effects.

## Evidence cited
- Russian 1930s irradiation study on 100m dash times (7.4% vs. 1.4% improvement), cited secondhand via a T-Nation article, no primary citation given.
- German 1940s bike ergometer irradiation study (13% improvement), same secondhand sourcing.
- 1956 UV reaction-time study (17% improvement), same secondhand sourcing.
- 1960s American college women irradiation study, same secondhand sourcing.
- NIH tolerable upper intake standard for Vitamin D (2000 IU/day) and a cited 1997 finding of tolerance up to 10,000 IU/day.
- Vitamin D Council research list (referenced, not summarized in detail).

## Open questions
- The author's personal 6000-8000 IU/day dose and Dr. Cohen's 4000 IU/day recommendation both exceed the NIH's stated 2000 IU tolerable upper limit, with no safety data offered beyond "no ill effects."
- All performance studies are cited secondhand through a T-Nation article with no direct links to the original 1930s-1960s papers, so their methods can't be checked.
