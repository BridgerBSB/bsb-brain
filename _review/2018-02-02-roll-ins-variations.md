# Roll Ins + Variations

## TL;DR
- Roll-ins teach hip-shoulder separation: lock the back hip forward, keep the shoulders closed at landing, then drive off the back leg so force applies over a long arc to release.
- Five variations run along one progression, from abbreviated arm action with the elbow already up, through full arm action, plus a stationary version and a step-back version for pitchers who cannot feel the position dynamically.
- A pitcher whose 80% throw matches or beats their max-effort throw has a sequencing break, not a stuff problem, caused by muscling up instead of ramping intensity evenly through the chain.

## Claims
- [00:03](https://youtu.be/v3IBryZpmjc?t=3) roll-ins build on pivot pickoffs by adding the lower half: hips face forward, shoulders stay closed at landing, creating separation instead of the hips and shoulders rotating together.
- [01:04](https://youtu.be/v3IBryZpmjc?t=64) the target position is maximum distance between the back hip/knee and the front shoulder at landing.
- [01:04](https://youtu.be/v3IBryZpmjc?t=64) a common mistake is training separation without back-leg drive, which reduces transfer to the actual delivery.
- [02:04](https://youtu.be/v3IBryZpmjc?t=124) starting with the arm already elevated removes the hand-break variable so the pitcher can focus on separation and back-leg drive.
- [03:05](https://youtu.be/v3IBryZpmjc?t=185) the progression moves from abbreviated arm action, to a flip-up into high-cock, to full arm action with the complete hand break.
- [03:59](https://youtu.be/v3IBryZpmjc?t=245) the stationary roll-in, originally called the separation drill and created in 2013, presets the back foot forward and the front shoulder closed from the c-spine, then gathers and drives off the back leg.
- [06:09](https://youtu.be/v3IBryZpmjc?t=369) the step-back roll-in is for pitchers who can't feel their center of mass out front or the angle off the back leg; they step back to load, then drive through.
- [07:10](https://youtu.be/v3IBryZpmjc?t=430) the most common mechanical fault is failing to hold the back hip forward at landing due to limited internal rotation on the back leg, a mobility limitation calling for hip IR and soft-tissue work.
- [08:14](https://youtu.be/v3IBryZpmjc?t=494) if an 80% throw is as good as or better than a max-effort throw, that signals a break in sequencing from muscling up rather than ramping intensity evenly through the kinetic chain.

## Cues
- **"Lock the back hip forward"** - fixes hips and shoulders rotating together as one unit instead of separating; for pitchers learning dissociation.
- **"Keep the glove and throwing arm loose, with the elbows syncing into the plane of shoulder rotation"** - fixes a tense, muscled arm action.
- **"Gather your weight and drive off the back leg, rather than coasting off a dead back leg"** - fixes a passive lower half that doesn't transfer to the full delivery.
- **"Preset the front shoulder as close as possible, from the c-spine, not the low back"** - fixes faking separation with lumbar rotation instead of true thoracic closure; for pitchers learning the stationary roll-in.

## Drills
- **Roll-in, abbreviated arm, high-cock start** - begin with the arm already up in high-cock, step and drive; builds separation feel without the hand-break; for pitchers new to the drill.
- **Roll-in, abbreviated arm, flip-up** - same start but flip the arm up into high-cock during the move; builds the bridge from abbreviated to full arm action.
- **Roll-in, full arm action** - full hand break from the set position; builds separation with the complete arm path, closest transfer to the delivery.
- **Stationary roll-in / separation drill** - no step; back foot planted forward, front shoulder preset closed, gather and rotate hard off a fixed back leg; builds forcing hips-open/shoulders-closed at landing; for pitchers who can't yet feel the position dynamically.
- **Step-back roll-in** - start with weight on the front leg, step back to gather and load the back leg, then drive through; builds sensing back-leg load; for pitchers who can't feel center of mass out front or the angle off the back leg.
