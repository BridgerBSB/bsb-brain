# What Your Long Toss Distance Says About Your Velocity

## TL;DR
- Long toss distance gives a rough velocity estimate: about 200 ft at a 30° arc is roughly 68-72 mph; 300 ft at 30-50° is roughly 87-90 mph.
- Progress depends on intensity dosage, not distance alone: keep max-distance (100%) days to 2-3 per week and drop to 50-60% of max distance the day after a max effort.
- Long toss should be individualized by fault (constrained kneeling drills for arm/trunk issues, rolling or quick-pick drills for early lower-body dissociation) and isn't universal - low-slot/submarine arms get little benefit and should train velocity off radar feedback instead.

## Claims
- [00:00](https://youtu.be/KDkG1se2wHI?t=0) Long toss distance correlates directly with mound velocity; pitchers with better arms throw it farther.
- [01:01](https://youtu.be/KDkG1se2wHI?t=61) Distance-to-velocity estimate: 200 ft at a 30° arc is roughly 68-72 mph; 300 ft at 30-50° is roughly 87-90 mph.
- [02:03](https://youtu.be/KDkG1se2wHI?t=123) Long toss at max distance every day risks injury; intensity needs a high-low dosage structure to allow adaptation.
- [03:03](https://youtu.be/KDkG1se2wHI?t=183) Recommended dosage: long toss 2-3 times per week; the day after a 100% max-distance session should sit around 50-60% of max distance.
- [03:31](https://youtu.be/KDkG1se2wHI?t=244) Constrained long toss (double kneeling, single kneeling, split stance) removes the lower half to fix arm/trunk-timing faults like stabby or pushy arm action.
- [05:05](https://youtu.be/KDkG1se2wHI?t=305) Rolling, quick-pick, or shuffle long toss target pitchers who peak hip-shoulder separation early and open up early at landing.
- [06:06](https://youtu.be/KDkG1se2wHI?t=366) Pitchers with efficient mechanics (cites Yamamoto) get more carryover from traditional out-of-the-stretch long toss than from constraint drills.
- [07:27](https://youtu.be/KDkG1se2wHI?t=427) Long toss is not recommended for low-slot or submarine arms: past roughly 200 ft their release point shifts to reach their partner, altering mound timing; radar-gun feedback at shorter distances (e.g. 120 ft) is preferred for these pitchers.
- [12:24](https://youtu.be/KDkG1se2wHI?t=732) Long toss (30-50° arc, working in to about 12 throws at max distance, 80-90% intensity on the way in) is distinct from pulldowns (radar-fed, run-and-throw, max effort for a velocity reading).
- [13:12](https://youtu.be/KDkG1se2wHI?t=792) A pitcher long tossing 330-340 ft (chart-implied 90-93 mph) but only throwing 86-87 mph off a mound has a timing mismatch between the long-toss arc and the slope; recommendation is less long toss, more full-delivery/bullpen work once max-distance capacity is already established.
