# Antone's return from a third TJS is providing hope and a road map for arms

## TL;DR
- Tejay Antone returned to the majors after a third Tommy John surgery, one of only two known big leaguers (with Jonny Venters) to come back from three TJS.
- Kremchek's third repair addressed a torn-off flexor mass with eight anchors, an Internal Brace, and a collagen patch; Antone paired the surgery with mechanical changes (28-degree arm angle, new pitch mix) worked out at Driveline.
- Since his May 6 return he's averaging 95.5 mph on his fastball, his best mark since 2021, with a 16.7% K-BB% through his first 14 innings.

## Claims
- [on repeat-TJS survival odds] Of 173 pitchers who've had multiple Tommy John surgeries, 59.8% have not returned to play, per Jon Roegele's database.
- [on the closest comp] Jonny Venters is the only other MLB pitcher to return from a third TJS; he threw 42.1 innings before retiring, with a 5.31 ERA/4.61 FIP.
- [on why repeats fail] Dr. Timothy Kremchek, who has performed nearly 4,000 TJ surgeries, says average longevity of a Tommy John repair at the MLB level is about six years.
- [on the third surgery itself] Kremchek reattached the UCL without a new graft, added an Internal Brace, eight anchors to reattach a torn flexor mass, and a collagen patch over the ligament.
- [on the return assessment] Driveline's Brett Cook led Antone's post-surgery evaluation and named the psychological hurdle of trusting the arm again as the biggest challenge of a multi-TJS return.
- [on mechanical changes] Driveline's Daulton Barry worked with Antone on torso movement during his throwing motion using constraint-based drills.
- [on workload monitoring] Antone tracks every throwing session with Driveline's PULSE device and a personal spreadsheet, using the numbers to decide whether to throw on back-to-back days.
- [on the new arsenal] Post-surgery, Antone dropped his arm angle to a career-low 28 degrees and added a sweeper and a cutter.
- [on 2026 performance] Since returning May 6, Antone is averaging 95.5 mph (best since 2021) with a 16.7% K-BB% through 14 innings; pitch mix is slider 51.2%, cutter 21.7%, curveball 14.3%, sinker 5.4%, fastball 7.4%.
- [on his velocity philosophy] Antone says throwing harder creates more opportunities at every level of the game, and calls "you don't need to throw hard to get outs" outdated thinking.

## Evidence cited
- Jon Roegele's Tommy John surgery database: 173 multi-TJS cases, 59.8% did not return to play.
- Jonny Venters comp: 42.1 IP post third-TJS return, 5.31 ERA/4.61 FIP, before retiring.
- Kremchek's stated career total: nearly 4,000 Tommy John surgeries performed; ~6-year average repair longevity at the MLB level.

## Open questions
- Antone's claim that "you don't need to throw hard" is outdated logic runs against ongoing industry conversation about velocity and injury risk; the article doesn't resolve this, just states his position.
- The piece credits his durability partly to mechanical changes (arm angle, torso constraint work) but gives no biomechanical measurement confirming those changes reduced elbow stress.
