# How To Fix Your Pushy Arm Action | Pushy Arm Action Part Two

## TL;DR
- Pushy (elbow-dominant) arm action raises UCL and tricep/olecranon injury risk because the same force gets applied over a longer time; fixing it starts with diagnosing whether the push is a positional compensation or an unnatural high-slot habit.
- Coaches use constraint drills (L-screen, 10 toes, abbreviated arm action) and implement swaps (heavy/light ball, open-hand work) to build awareness and get the arm pulled through instead of pushed; one case cited a 4 mph gain after cleanup.
- Timelines vary a lot: in-season mechanical changes rarely carry over to game outcomes, so coaches often lean on range-of-motion work and volume tweaks (like restoring long-toss volume) rather than major overhauls until the off-season.

## Claims
- [01:00](https://youtu.be/EOFwsj3-GEY?t=60) Pushy arm action raises injury risk because the same force gets applied over a longer time (elbow-driven mechanics), increasing total stress; the opposite fault, excessive shoulder drag, overloads the shoulder instead.
- [05:11](https://youtu.be/EOFwsj3-GEY?t=311) Repeated slamming into elbow extension is a precursor to UCL repair; the body lays down calcium (bone spurs) trying to block the extension, which may eventually need surgical removal.
- [06:19](https://youtu.be/EOFwsj3-GEY?t=379) Pushy throwers tend toward tricep/olecranon fractures and distal bicep issues rather than the proximal bicep issues seen in shoulder-dominant throwers; healthy pushy arm actions are mostly seen in established pros, rarely in healthy 16-18 year olds (survivorship bias).
- [16:48](https://youtu.be/EOFwsj3-GEY?t=1008) If a motor-learning/drill approach isn't moving the needle, the recommended fix is to restore range of motion first (reduce loading, decompress the system) before drill work can chip away at the pattern.
- [21:00](https://youtu.be/EOFwsj3-GEY?t=1260) Tall pitchers taught to throw from an unnaturally high slot without adequate shoulder flexion compensate by climbing the elbow above the shoulder plane, causing chronic high misses.
- [28:13](https://youtu.be/EOFwsj3-GEY?t=1693) After an L-screen constraint drill, one athlete's radar reading was 4 mph faster once he let the arm get pulled through instead of pushed.
- [30:16](https://youtu.be/EOFwsj3-GEY?t=1816) Ball-weight feedback is individual: some muscle-bound pushy throwers relax more with a heavier ball, others need a lighter ball; one young low-elbow pitcher got worse with a heavier ball but cleaned up immediately with a lighter ball and open-hand tennis-racket work.
- [37:38](https://youtu.be/EOFwsj3-GEY?t=2258) A grooved pushy pattern can take as long to unwind as the thousands of reps that built it; abbreviated arm action is used to build awareness before checking for carryover into the full throw.
- [46:01](https://youtu.be/EOFwsj3-GEY?t=2761) In-season, mechanical arm-action changes rarely transfer to game outcomes because pitchers are incentivized to perform, so coaches favor removing/restoring known variables (like long-toss volume) over major drilling.
- [54:25](https://youtu.be/EOFwsj3-GEY?t=3265) Self-scouting method: filter Baseball Savant by velocity to find a pro with a similar hand break, then compare where the mechanics diverge (e.g., tricep/lat extension point) to identify the gap versus your own arm action.

## Cues
- **"Throw it with your chest"** - fixes a pushy/elbow-dominant arm action by getting the arm in line with the rotation plane; for pitchers who push rather than get pulled to release.
- **"Get it up"** - feed-the-flaw cue for a low back-side elbow; for cue-receptive, more advanced athletes.

## Drills
- **L-screen constraint** - place an L-screen edge at the release line so the throwing hand must clear it; forces a pulled release instead of a push; for young pitchers and pushy arm actions.
- **10 toes drill** - throw with feet together, no lower-half stride, shoulders kept level; isolates the upper half and shoulder-plane feel; for pitchers learning to load on plane instead of climbing the elbow.
- **Abbreviated arm action** - shortened, compact arm path; builds awareness of a correctly timed lay-back feel at foot strike; for pitchers who have never felt true shoulder layback/external rotation.
- **Underload ball / open-hand tennis racket** - light implement or open-hand throws in place of a weighted ball; relaxes a tense, muscle-bound pushy arm action; for pitchers who tense up further with heavier implements.

## Evidence cited
- [28:13](https://youtu.be/EOFwsj3-GEY?t=1693) Unnamed athlete: L-screen constraint change measured 4 mph faster on radar, no further detail given.
- [47:07](https://youtu.be/EOFwsj3-GEY?t=2827) Named pro athlete "Griffin": in-season fix was restoring long-toss volume that had dropped when his arm action degraded, rather than adding new drills.

## Open questions
- The source presents abbreviated arm action as an "awareness" tool rather than a band-aid, but gives no data on how often the change holds once full-intent throwing returns.
