# Cleaner Swing Path = More Barrels vs Velo & Lefties (Swing Design ft. ABL MVP)

## TL;DR
- Full swing design session cleans up a pro hitter's bat path, replacing an out-and-around turn that lengthens the swing against high velocity and lefties.
- Space-creation work is moved from the load (which never carried over to games despite years of trying) into the stride phase, using feet-in-cement, flow, Kershaw, and hook'em drill variations.
- The player's own in-game cue of hitting everything at a 35 VBA broke down on inside pitches; the coach's fix was aiming at the top-inside part of the ball instead.

## Claims
- [00:00](https://youtu.be/xMbjlQ2v0vI?t=0) Erat's struggles vs high velocity and lefties trace to an inefficient path where he works out and turns through the zone, adding swing length and forcing contact too far out front.
- [00:00](https://youtu.be/xMbjlQ2v0vI?t=0) Feet-in-cement drill keeps both feet planted while rotating, forcing hip engagement and more pelvic tilt/space to turn.
- [02:00](https://youtu.be/xMbjlQ2v0vI?t=120) Load-based hinge work never transferred to Erat's actual swing across "several years," so the session attacks space-creation during the stride phase instead.
- [04:00](https://youtu.be/xMbjlQ2v0vI?t=240) Flow drill with a handle-loaded bat builds separation while keeping the torso closed; the added handle weight exposes any pulling of the hands across the body.
- [06:05](https://youtu.be/xMbjlQ2v0vI?t=365) On good swings, the lead shoulder must abduct into the "slot" during the downswing; failing that flattens the path, contrasted with Nolan Arenado's exaggerated turn-behind pattern.
- [07:06](https://youtu.be/xMbjlQ2v0vI?t=426) Modified offset-closed stance vs a closed-angle machine is used because the machine already presents an extreme horizontal approach angle, forcing the barrel to stay tight to the body.
- [08:07](https://youtu.be/xMbjlQ2v0vI?t=487) Erat's own in-season cue of a 35 VBA on every pitch broke down on inside pitches; alternative cue given was to turn to the top-inside part of the ball for a flatter top.
- [11:10](https://youtu.be/xMbjlQ2v0vI?t=670) Kershaw drill creates space during the forward move by letting center of mass drift slightly toward first base as it moves forward, pulling the hitter into a hinge.
- [12:11](https://youtu.be/xMbjlQ2v0vI?t=731) Kershaw-into-hook'em combo forces engaging the posterior chain and loading the rear leg, preventing the hitter from cheating by swinging the front leg over the back leg.
- [15:14](https://youtu.be/xMbjlQ2v0vI?t=914) Walk-in drill (starting further back, stepping toward the plate with a long bat) trains resisting the forward pull of center of mass so the hitter doesn't get up on his toes and early-extend the pelvis.

## Cues
- **"Let it get to a point where you think you're going to break your bat, then swing 82 miles an hour"** - fixes timid, panicked early turns against pitches that feel like they'll beat the hitter; for hitters who bail out against velocity.
- **"Turn to the top-inside part of the ball"** - fixes over-forcing a steep vertical bat angle everywhere; for hitters who need to stay flatter at the top while still reaching the pull side.
- **"Leave your top hand almost open"** - fixes excess top-hand tension into the load checkpoint; for hitters who grip too early and can't re-grip naturally.

## Drills
- **Feet-in-cement rotation** - feet stay planted through rotation; builds hip engagement and pelvic tilt to create turning space; for hitters who don't hinge well.
- **Flow drill, handle-load bat** - added backward flow toward the catcher with weight loaded at the handle; builds separation while torso stays closed; exposes hands pulling across.
- **Offset-closed vs closed-angle machine** - modified offset-closed stance against a machine set to a closed, extreme horizontal approach angle; builds staying tight and releasing out front instead of casting; for hitters facing lefties.
- **Short bat, steep VBA punch** - short bat swung with a punch-down, steep vertical bat angle; builds handling inside pitches without needing a full-size bat.
- **Kershaw** - isolates the stride phase, letting center of mass drift back as it moves forward; builds depth and separation into foot plant.
- **Kershaw into hook'em** - Kershaw entry feeding into a hook'em setup; builds a stable, powerful load off the rear leg without cheating the front leg over the back leg.
- **Walk-in, long flow bat** - starts weight far back, steps toward the plate; builds resisting the forward pull of center of mass to avoid early quad load and pelvis extension.
