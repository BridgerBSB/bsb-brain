# Elbow Injuries and What Causes Tommy John Surgery

## TL;DR
- Werner et al.'s 2002 regression on 40 pro pitchers found peak shoulder horizontal adduction angular velocity, shoulder abduction at foot strike, elbow angle at peak valgus torque, and max shoulder external rotation torque together explain 97.4% (adjusted R² 0.974) of elbow valgus stress variance.
- "Scapular loading," a velocity cue popularized by Paul Nyman/SETPRO, is implicated as a likely driver of that high angular velocity and therefore of elbow valgus stress, illustrated with Stephen Strasburg's delivery before his UCL rupture.
- Valgus stress during arm acceleration can exceed 60 Nm, above cadaver-measured UCL tensile strength, and surrounding musculature (triceps, wrist flexor-pronator, anconeus) is proposed to act as a dynamic stabilizer that offloads the ligament.

## Claims
- ### What Causes Tommy John Surgery?: elbow extension during acceleration reaches up to 2500 degrees per second, and valgus stress can exceed 60 Nm, above cadaver-measured UCL strength, per Medscape.
- ### What Causes Tommy John Surgery?: Werner et al. found triceps, wrist flexor-pronator, and anconeus activity at peak valgus stress suggests these muscles act as dynamic stabilizers assisting the UCL.
- ### Are Bad Pitching Mechanics the Cause of Elbow Injuries?: Werner et al. (2002) filmed 40 professional pitchers at high speed during 1998-1999 Cactus League spring training and built a regression model with adjusted R² of 0.974 linking mechanics to elbow valgus.
- ### Are Bad Pitching Mechanics the Cause of Elbow Injuries?: the four parameters were shoulder abduction angle at stride foot contact (positive correlation), peak shoulder horizontal adduction angular velocity (positive correlation), elbow angle at peak valgus torque (negative correlation), and max shoulder external rotation torque (negative correlation).
- ### Peak Shoulder Horizontal Adduction Angular Velocity: torque around the shoulder both increases injury risk and increases fastball velocity, so reducing a valgus-correlated variable could cost velocity.
- ### Peak Shoulder Horizontal Adduction Angular Velocity: Paul Nyman of SETPRO originally described "scapular loading" as vital to developing stretch reflex, elastic energy storage, and increased range of motion in the delivery.
- ### Bringing it Back to Stephen Strasburg: the article attributes Strasburg's pronounced scapular loading as one contributor to the elbow valgus stress behind his UCL rupture and Tommy John surgery.

## Anti-cues
- **"Scapular loading"** - taught to fix limited range of motion and low velocity (Nyman/SETPRO said it builds stretch reflex, elastic energy storage, and ROM); actually raises peak shoulder horizontal adduction angular velocity, which correlates with elbow valgus stress and UCL injury risk; per Kyle Boddy, citing Werner et al. 2002.

## Evidence cited
- Werner et al., "Relationship between throwing mechanics and elbow valgus in professional baseball pitchers" (2002): 40 professional pitchers, high-speed video, 1998-1999 Cactus League spring training; adjusted R² = 0.974.
- Sabick et al., "Humeral Torque in Professional Baseball Pitchers": cited as background on humeral loading, no numbers given in this article.
- Medscape description of the acceleration phase: extension up to 2500 deg/s, valgus stress can exceed 60 Nm.
- Stephen Strasburg's 2010 UCL rupture and Tommy John surgery, cited via a Washington Post quote from GM Mike Rizzo, used as an illustrative case.

## Open questions
- The article asserts that reducing valgus-correlated variables like scapular loading would cost fastball velocity, citing a general torque-velocity tradeoff without numbers specific to this claim.
- Strasburg's injury is attributed partly to scapular loading based on video observation alone, not on biomechanical data measured on him, while the supporting correlation comes from a separate 40-pitcher study.
