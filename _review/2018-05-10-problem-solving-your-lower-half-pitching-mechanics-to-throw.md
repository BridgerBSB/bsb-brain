# Problem Solving Your Lower Half Pitching Mechanics to Throw Harder

## TL;DR
- Drive off the mound with the rear glute and hamstring, not the quad, so the center of mass moves toward the plate instead of rising too early.
- The author went from 87-90 (t92) to 90-94 (t95) with a slide step, then to 96-97 (t98) after adding a full leg lift, crediting the change entirely to learning to shift his weight.
- Loading drills that step back before reversing (Step-Back Roll Ins, Rocker Drills, Step-Back Wind-Ups) teach the feel of lateral loading in the back leg.

## Claims
- Intro: author's velocity moved from 87-90 (t92) to 90-94 (t95) with a slide-step delivery, then to 96-97 (t98) after adding a full leg lift, from learning to shift weight properly.
- Section 1: quad extension that fires too early drives the center of mass up instead of forward; driving from the rear glute and hamstring instead keeps that motion directed at the target.
- Section 1: the quad still activates during the drive but as a secondary isometric contraction, not the primary concentric one.
- Section 2: pre-coiling the hips against the upper half at peak leg lift (cited in Roger Clemens) sets up a smooth lateral and rotational unload out of leg lift.
- Section 2: the back leg should "catch and accelerate" a center of mass that is already shifting, not actively push down the mound from a static position over the rubber.
- Section 3: step-back drills load the muscles in the opposite direction of the throwing pattern, so the movement is reversed from the back leg on the way out.
- Closing: a sequencing check: throwing low 80s during warm-up at roughly 50% perceived effort with no felt arm effort indicates good sequencing and roughly 15+ mph left in reserve that day.

## Cues
- **"Clawing" or "pulling" the rubber back towards second base, not "pushing" or "lunging" off of it** - fixes early quad drive that rises the center of mass instead of moving it forward; for pitchers learning to use the back leg
- **"The ball is throwing itself" / "the arm is along for the ride"** - fixes over-applying arm effort before the lower half has sequenced; for pitchers ramping up drill intensity
- **"Try to feel smooth and fluid going through these drills"** - fixes stomping down on the back leg and jerking forward instead of ramping up through the movement; for pitchers doing loading drills

## Anti-cues
- none

## Drills
- **Step-Back Roll Ins** - step back to shift weight into the rear leg, then reverse the movement forward; builds the feel of lateral loading in the back leg; for pitchers learning to drive off the mound
- **Properly Done Rocker Drills** - rocking motion that loads weight back before driving forward; builds the same lateral loading pattern as Step-Back Roll Ins; for pitchers learning to use the back leg
- **Step-Back Wind-Ups** - full wind-up that begins with a step back to load the rear leg; builds lateral loading of the back leg within a fuller delivery; for pitchers learning to use the back leg
