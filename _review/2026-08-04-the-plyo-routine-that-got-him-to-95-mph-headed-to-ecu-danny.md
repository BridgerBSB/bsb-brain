# The Plyo Routine That Got Him To 95 MPH & Headed To ECU | Danny Cihocki

## TL;DR
- Two-month in-house program took Danny Cihocki from sitting 90 mph (touching 93 in-game) to sitting 94, touching 95 in a bullpen, by fixing a quad-dominant crossbody pattern.
- The fix sequence was posterior weight shift first (load the glute instead of the quad), which gave lower-half direction, which gave torso timing and separation.
- Delivery method was split-stance plyo wall variations with external constraints (a hurdle in front of the knee, a mini mound under the lead foot) to force a lead-leg-block feel, since the coach's stated principle is that athletes need a position to physically get into before they can repeat the feel on their own.

## Claims
- [00:00](https://youtu.be/QznGfiTK1T0?t=0) Cihocki came in sitting 90 mph, touching 93 in-game; after roughly two months he was sitting 94, touching 95 in a bullpen.
- [00:00](https://youtu.be/QznGfiTK1T0?t=0) His fault was described as a quad-dominant, crossbody pattern that led to an open torso at front foot strike; the two priorities were posterior weight shift and torso timing.
- [00:00](https://youtu.be/QznGfiTK1T0?t=0) His first bullpen after the initial changes produced a 94 mph pitch, topping 95 in that same session.
- [02:01](https://youtu.be/QznGfiTK1T0?t=121) In-season he topped 92-93 mph; in a bullpen the week before this video he topped 95, attributed to improved lower-half direction giving better torso timing and separation.
- [03:02](https://youtu.be/QznGfiTK1T0?t=182) The coaching target is keeping the knee behind the toe line so the glute loads; letting the knee pass the toe line reverts him to a quad-driven push instead of true rotation.
- [04:11](https://youtu.be/QznGfiTK1T0?t=251) Split-stance variations were the primary drill vehicle; the stated rationale is that once an athlete can create a feel, the goal becomes returning to that same position, not re-deriving it.
- [05:12](https://youtu.be/QznGfiTK1T0?t=312) Constraint tools used: a hurdle placed in front of the lead knee to block forward leak, and mini mounds under the lead foot to create a ramp so he feels behind the front leg through it.
- [06:12](https://youtu.be/QznGfiTK1T0?t=372) The two-month focus was almost entirely back-leg loading and unloading, on the stated logic that a feel achieved in drill work will not transfer if an earlier leak (here, the back leg) prevents the same setup at front foot strike.
- [06:12](https://youtu.be/QznGfiTK1T0?t=372) A roll-in drill (building momentum into split stance, then turning sideways) was used to train staying behind the lead leg through rotation.

## Cues
- **"Heel to the sky"** - fixes riding the back leg out into a quad-dominant pattern; used to get a more athletic hip turn/flip on the plyo wall.
- **"Follow the ball with your chest"** - fixes leaking forward onto the lead leg early; keeps the torso behind the front leg through the take-back.
- **"Keep the knee behind toe line"** - fixes the knee driving past the toe, which turns rotation into a push; used to access the glute instead of the quad.
