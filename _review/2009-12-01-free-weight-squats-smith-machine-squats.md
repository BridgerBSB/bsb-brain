# Free Weight Squats > Smith Machine Squats

## TL;DR
- Boddy argues free weight squats beat Smith machine squats for performance training because the fixed bar path restricts range of motion and skips stabilizer development.
- He cites a study showing EMG activity averaged 43% higher across all measured muscles during the free weight squat versus the Smith machine squat (p < 0.05).
- Individual muscle differences ran higher still: 49% in the vastus medialis, 34% in the gastrocnemius, 26% in the biceps femoris.

## Claims
- Free Weight Squats > Smith Machine Squats: the Smith machine restricts range of motion in one or more planes, depending on the machine and exercise, and this leaves out development of stabilizing muscles, which Boddy calls inherently injurious.
- Free Weight Squats > Smith Machine Squats: cited study found EMG activity significantly higher during the free weight squat by 34% (gastrocnemius), 26% (biceps femoris), and 49% (vastus medialis), p < 0.05.
- Free Weight Squats > Smith Machine Squats: EMG averaged over all muscles was 43% higher during the free weight squat versus the Smith machine squat, p < 0.05.
- Free Weight Squats > Smith Machine Squats: no significant EMG differences were found between the two squat types for any of the other muscles measured.
- Free Weight Squats > Smith Machine Squats: the free weight squat may be superior to the Smith machine squat for long-term strength and hypertrophy development in the major leg muscle groups.

## Evidence cited
- Study (PubMed 19855308, cited in full text): free weight squat vs Smith machine squat EMG comparison; gastrocnemius +34%, biceps femoris +26%, vastus medialis +49%, averaged across all muscles +43% (all p < 0.05); no significant difference in the remaining muscles measured.

## Open questions
- The post does not state the study's subject population, sample size, or squat depth/load protocol, so how far the EMG finding generalizes to baseball athletes is asserted rather than shown.
