# Blake Snell Pitching Mechanics | A Closer Look

## TL;DR
- Breakdown of Snell's delivery as a model: a drift with a shoulder dip, a held back foot, a seamless arm loop, and a front hip that holds tension until the pelvis opens it.
- Foot position on the rubber changed his direction to target; he went from 94.7 to 96.5 mph between 2017 and 2018, though he credits consistency, not the tweak.
- Efficiency, not brute effort, is what lets a pitcher sustain velocity deep into games.

## Claims
- [00:00](https://youtu.be/Gu689qkuOfE?t=0) Snell is listed at 6'4" 225, four-seam averages 95.5 mph and is thrown about half the time; spin rates are unremarkable (2400 rpm four-seam).
- [01:01](https://youtu.be/Gu689qkuOfE?t=61) His four-seam rose from 94.7 to 96.5 mph between 2017 and 2018, around a move from the third base side of the rubber to center. Writers hypothesised the move caused it; Snell says it did not.
- [03:01](https://youtu.be/Gu689qkuOfE?t=181) Starting off the third base side put him about a foot more closed at landing, forcing him to yank the upper half around to get back online.
- [04:02](https://youtu.be/Gu689qkuOfE?t=242) Crossbody stride is not automatically a fault; fix it only if it is not working. Jake Arrieta is the example of a change that backfired.
- [05:03](https://youtu.be/Gu689qkuOfE?t=303) Crossbody striders without good t-spine and hip rotation tend to show knee bow, foot cave and a throw that breaks down at release.
- [07:04](https://youtu.be/Gu689qkuOfE?t=424) A shoulder dip or chin tuck in the drift may help keep the shoulders level in the linear move, so the pitcher does not get uphill at landing. Offered as something to try, not a rule.
- [09:06](https://youtu.be/Gu689qkuOfE?t=546) Drift is a float with little back-leg load, then a transition into a hinge on a stable back leg, described as a seesaw.
- [10:07](https://youtu.be/Gu689qkuOfE?t=607) The back foot turns over into eversion rather than pivoting or pushing off, so he holds the ground into landing. Ankle braces and high-top cleats can force an early pop out of the back side, costing perhaps 1 to 2 mph and consistency.
- [13:09](https://youtu.be/Gu689qkuOfE?t=789) Arm action is one seamless loop from hand break to acceleration; scap retraction and horizontal abduction at landing let force be applied over a longer arc. Needs pec and t-spine extension mobility.
- [16:11](https://youtu.be/Gu689qkuOfE?t=971) Glove arm should find tension and sink into the plane of torso rotation, not pull or roll aggressively. The front hip holds internal rotation tension until the pelvis opens it ([17:25](https://youtu.be/Gu689qkuOfE?t=1045)).

## Cues
- **"Add a little bit of a chin tuck, add a little bit of a dipping of the shoulders"** - fixes getting uphill at landing; for pitchers who go uphill in the linear move (try for one session)
- **"Keeping the cleats or the bottom of the foot facing the target as long as possible"** - fixes front leg swinging the gate and reaching early; for pitchers where it fits (attributed to Paul Nyman; the speaker says it is not appropriate for everybody)
- **"The back hip opens the front hip"** - fixes front hip reaching and leaking before the pelvis unloads; for pitchers with a front-side-driven lower half

## Anti-cues
- **"Aggressively pull the glove arm" / "aggressively roll the glove arm down to the hip"** - taught to fix glove arm control and rotation; actually produces a glove arm that leads aggressively instead of sinking into the plane of rotation; per Tread (Ben Brewster)

## Evidence cited
- Snell velocity: 94.7 mph (2017) to 96.5 mph (2018); 2018 Cy Young.
- Snell quote on consistency from moving left on the rubber; his walk rate fell the next year.
- Jake Arrieta: left the Orioles, who tried to remove his crossbody stride, and succeeded with the Cubs when allowed to keep it.
- Roger Clemens cited as an example of a chin tuck.

## Open questions
- The 1.8 mph gain is attributed to rubber position by writers but not by Snell, and the video does not settle it.
- The claim that efficient mechanics sustain velocity deeper into games is asserted without data.
