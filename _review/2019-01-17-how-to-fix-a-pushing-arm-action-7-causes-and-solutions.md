# How to Fix a Pushing Arm Action - 7 Causes and Solutions

## TL;DR
- Pushing arm action (elbow drifting in front of the shoulder line at max external rotation, or trunk flexing forward instead of rotating through release) trades pec/lat driven layback for tricep driven extension, and the analyst should read it as a velocity leak and an injury flag rather than just a style quirk.
- Seven distinct causes are named: overloaded plyo balls, mis-aimed plyo wall drills, the "just throw strikes" cue, flying open at landing, compensating for prior injury, tight pec minor/subclavius/upper trap/lat-tricep, and forced layback that is really elbow flexion.
- Weighted ball guidance is specific: no 4 lb ball for any athlete, 2 lb only sub-maximal for HS/college, and nothing at or above 2 lb for youth pitchers.

## Claims
- **Going too heavy on weighted ball / plyo drills**: 4 lb balls are no longer used with any athlete; 2 lb is fine for HS/college pitchers only at sub-maximal effort; nothing at or above 2 lb for youth pitchers; over 1 lb is used selectively for HS/college.
- **Yanking the ball across body on Plyo drills**: standing too close to the wall and aiming at the face causes cutting or pushing; the fix is aiming slightly arm-side of a taped center mark and backing up a few additional feet.
- **Over-cueing strike throwing**: the "just throw strikes" cue pushes youth pitchers toward a linear, forward-flexed finish; long toss is recommended because the distance constraint forces a rotational, high-intent finish while directional feedback (the ball running or cutting) keeps throws on line.
- **Flying open with upper half**: shoulders fully open at landing leave nowhere for the arm to go but a linear, pushed finish; improving hip/shoulder separation is the fix, with the roll-in drill used for some athletes (not prescribed universally due to individual hip anatomy).
- **Compensating for current or prior injury**: pushing frequently follows return from bicep tendon, labrum, rotator cuff, or medial elbow injury; the fix is not rushing rehab progressions and prioritizing throw quality over building intensity quickly.
- **Tissue restriction preventing smooth layback**: pec major/minor/subclavius, upper trap, and lat/tricep (long head) are named in that order of observed prevalence as the tightness that most often blocks layback.
- **"Fake" External Rotation**: forcing the elbow forward can produce an apparent 180+ degrees of layback that is actually elbow flexion, not true external rotation, and does not translate to performance gains.
- **Closing thoughts**: some pitchers reach high levels with an extreme pushing pattern, but it becomes exceedingly rare at the higher levels of the game.

## Cues
- **"think about rotating your back shoulder through the target"** - fixes cutting/pushing on the plyo wall; for athletes who yank the ball across their body on plyo drills

## Anti-cues
- **"just throw strikes"** - taught to fix walks/command at the youth level; actually causes a linear, forward-flexed delivery and a pushing arm action; per Tread (Ben Brewster)

## Drills
- **Plyo wall target-tape drill** - back up a few extra feet from the wall and aim slightly arm-side of a taped center mark instead of the face; builds a rotational finish that stays on line without cutting or pushing
- **Long toss** - distance-constrained throws; builds a rotational, high-intent finish while directional feedback keeps the throw on line; for pitchers over-cued into linear, "just throw strikes" mechanics
- **Roll in** - named hip/shoulder-separation drill; builds closed shoulders at landing to prevent a linear, pushed finish; not used for every athlete due to individual hip anatomy
- **Barbell pec minor release** - self-myofascial release on the pec minor using a barbell; builds tissue quality to restore layback range; for pushing tied to pec minor/subclavius tightness
