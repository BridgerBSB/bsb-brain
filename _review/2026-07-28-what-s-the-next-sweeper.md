# What's the Next Sweeper?

## TL;DR
- Driveline's pitching and data-science directors call the "vertical slider" (aka death ball) - a high-gyro slider with just enough down-tilt to drop while holding velocity - the next underused pitch shape, currently thrown consistently by only about 12 pitchers league-wide at roughly 1% usage but with about 40% whiff.
- Better ball tracking (TrackMan) and high-speed cameras (Edgertronic) have tightened pitch classification and let coaches identify and train the specific release mechanics behind a shape instead of guessing at grips.
- Reliever-to-starter conversions have gone from clearly worse than career starters to nearly break-even: the WAR gap fell from .78 in the 2000s to .6 in the 2010s to .01 in the 2020s.

## Claims
- [02:03](https://youtu.be/QPzFR4GNxdk?t=123) Connor White defines the vertical slider as within 10 mph of the fastball, more drop than sweep, and under 4 inches of glove-side sweep - a high-gyro pitch with the spin axis tilted just enough to add drop without losing velocity.
- [03:04](https://youtu.be/QPzFR4GNxdk?t=184) This shape has hovered around 1% league usage over the last few years but posts roughly a 40% whiff rate when thrown.
- [12:11](https://youtu.be/QPzFR4GNxdk?t=731) Only 12 pitchers currently throw the shape consistently, and only 10 of those average that classification; a wider group throws it occasionally with strong results, especially high three-quarter to high-slot arms with a supination bias.
- [12:11](https://youtu.be/QPzFR4GNxdk?t=731) As sweeper usage has risen league-wide, its results are starting to decline, which White frames as an opening for the vertical slider as a harder middle option in an arsenal.
- [07:08](https://youtu.be/QPzFR4GNxdk?t=428) Widespread TrackMan use tightened pitch classification (young arms often mislabel a sweeper or cutter as a curveball); pairing TrackMan with Edgertronic high-speed cameras lets teams study release dynamics directly instead of trial-and-error grip testing.
- [09:08](https://youtu.be/QPzFR4GNxdk?t=548) Elite sweepers are often built from Edgertronic footage showing a pitcher not getting fully around the ball, then finding a grip that seam-shifts it like a cutter without sacrificing velocity; the kick changeup came from a similar grip-spike insight that lets a supinator mimic pronation-style arm-side movement.
- [15:17](https://youtu.be/QPzFR4GNxdk?t=917) Jack Lambert's own pull of reliever-to-starter conversions since 2000 (defined as 80%+ relief appearances one year to 80%+ starts the next) found 2008 the peak year with 10 conversions; 2026 has had 3 so far, with no clear rising or falling trend.
- [15:17](https://youtu.be/QPzFR4GNxdk?t=917) Comparing converts to year-over-year starters, the WAR gap was .78 in the 2000s, .6 in the 2010s, and .01 in the 2020s - essentially a rounding error today.
- [16:18](https://youtu.be/QPzFR4GNxdk?t=978) Named recent reliever-to-starter converts: Garrett Crochet, Freddy Peralta, Michael King, Reynaldo Lopez, Chris Bubic, Seth Lugo.
- [23:27](https://youtu.be/QPzFR4GNxdk?t=1407) Nick Martinez, previously a reliever/swingman, converted to starter with the Reds, signed with the Rays, made his first All-Star team at age 35, and uses the Pulse wearable to track acute/chronic workload through the transition.
