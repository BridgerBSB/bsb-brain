# Stop Choosing Between Power & Contact | Tyler Jones Swing Design

## TL;DR
- Eric Kozak redesigns Oregon infielder Tyler Jones's load around two goals: get him stable into his back leg, and add pelvic tilt so he can drive the ball and lift it instead of picking one.
- The session progresses through constrained drills (offset open pivot pick, step-behind, handle-load bat, big poppy, short bat) into a harder Smashball environment, then live game swings.
- One logged game swing hit 114.

## Claims
- [00:01](https://youtu.be/7nvwOFe51c8?t=1) The redesign targets a stable back-leg load and added pelvic tilt so Jones can hit the ball hard and elevate it, rather than choosing between the two.
- [01:03](https://youtu.be/7nvwOFe51c8?t=63) The offset open pivot pick keeps both feet on the ground until the coil finishes, then the front foot lifts to swing.
- [02:04](https://youtu.be/7nvwOFe51c8?t=124) Pointing the belt buckle down as he coils creates forward bend of the pelvis and spine, producing a more vertical bat path and more lift at the bottom of the zone.
- [03:05](https://youtu.be/7nvwOFe51c8?t=185) Kozak says Jones's leg block comes from hitting max internal rotation of the lead hip too early; a slight external-rotation "pinch" before foot plant buys rotational room.
- [04:06](https://youtu.be/7nvwOFe51c8?t=246) Step-behind (foot stays down until the forward move) tilts the pelvis forward and shifts center of mass back, deepening the hinge through the stride.
- [06:08](https://youtu.be/7nvwOFe51c8?t=368) The handle-loaded implement magnifies any hands-first or barrel-around fault, rewarding a hitter who holds the barrel tight to the spine.
- [07:09](https://youtu.be/7nvwOFe51c8?t=429) The big poppy's intent is to increase hip-shoulder separation; a common fault is reaching for the ground and moving linearly instead of rotating the pelvis under a closed torso.
- [10:17](https://youtu.be/7nvwOFe51c8?t=617) Once Jones was comfortable with the new movements in an easier setup, Kozak moved him to a mini hack with Smashballs to test the same patterns in a more constrained environment.
- [18:57](https://youtu.be/7nvwOFe51c8?t=1137) More pelvic tilt and spine angle lifts the ball more consistently; Kozak frames more tilt as suited to counts where Jones is hunting damage, and staying upright/on-plane as suited to two-strike counts.
- [20:11](https://youtu.be/7nvwOFe51c8?t=1211) One game swing is logged at 114.

## Cues
- **"Belt buckle down towards the ground"** - creates forward pelvic tilt for lift on pitches at the bottom of the zone
- **"Hold space"** - keeps the barrel tight to the spine instead of coming out and around; used with the handle-loaded implement
- **"Keep that foot in the air and then rotate out of it"** - fixes reaching for the ground and popping up early out of leg lift
- **"Chest over the plate"** - keeps the hitter tight through the mini-hack drill environment

## Drills
- **Offset open pivot pick (hybrid)** - sit into an offset-open stance, load/pivot-pick with both feet down, lift the front foot only after the coil finishes; builds comfort loading into the back leg before moving forward
- **Step-behind** - step behind and keep the foot down until the forward move; builds forward pelvic tilt and a deeper hinge via a rearward center-of-mass shift
- **Handle-loaded bat** - swing with a handle-weighted implement; builds holding the barrel tight to the spine; exposes hands-first faults
- **Big poppy** - bat swung with intent to separate; builds hip-shoulder separation; common fault is reaching for the ground instead of rotating the pelvis
- **Short bat** - swung to feel pelvic tilt and posture; builds forward bend for consistent lift
- **Mini hack with Smashballs** - same movement patterns run in a smaller, more constrained hitting environment; builds transfer under difficulty
- **Game swings** - live BP-style swings closing the session; builds situational manipulation of spine angle by count

## Evidence cited
- Tyler Jones (Oregon infielder) logged one game swing at 114 during the final round [20:11](https://youtu.be/7nvwOFe51c8?t=1211).

## Open questions
- Kozak's claim that Jones's leg block comes from early max internal hip rotation is tied to this athlete's specific mobility (good external rotation, limited internal), not shown as a general pattern.
- The link between added spine angle/pelvic tilt and more consistent lift is asserted from this one session's misses and swings, not from outside measurement.
