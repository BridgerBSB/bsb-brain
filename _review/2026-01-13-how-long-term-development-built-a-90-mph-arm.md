# How Long-Term Development Built a 90+ MPH Arm

## TL;DR
- An 8-year athlete arc at Driveline Academy, ending with the player throwing 91 MPH and posting 97-98 EV as a college prospect.
- Training was structured around daily output goals with immediate, transparent feedback, which the coach contrasts with the delayed, noisy feedback of games.
- The coach frames later-stage development as many small gains rather than one big jump, and ties long-term adherence to intrinsic motivation rather than skill outcomes.

## Claims
- [Description] Danny Morgan progressed from an 8-year-old beginner to a college prospect throwing 91 MPH and hitting 97-98 EV through Driveline's Skills that Scale methodology.
- [01:03](https://youtu.be/UIPrH6_f7q4?t=63) The father was drawn to Driveline's separation of game results from skill-development results before ever training there.
- [03:05](https://youtu.be/UIPrH6_f7q4?t=185) Every training day had a specific output goal (throw harder, hit harder, hit farther) with transparent, immediate feedback the coach says isn't available in games.
- [03:05](https://youtu.be/UIPrH6_f7q4?t=185) Between ages 11-13 the athlete added significant weight room work, started understanding his mechanics, and reports his biggest gains came from that period.
- [05:12](https://youtu.be/UIPrH6_f7q4?t=312) At 14 the athlete increased weight room intensity with trainers and teammates pushing him past what he could push himself, and saw output numbers rise further.
- [04:09](https://youtu.be/UIPrH6_f7q4?t=249) The coach describes development as non-linear, with plateaus occurring even in the best players.
- [06:13](https://youtu.be/UIPrH6_f7q4?t=373) At the current stage, the coach says big beginner-level gains are no longer available and progress instead comes from many small improvements ("drilling tiny holes in the dam").
- [07:18](https://youtu.be/UIPrH6_f7q4?t=438) The program is built to reinforce intrinsic motivation by communicating daily progress to kids in a way the coach says is atypical in baseball training.
- [09:30](https://youtu.be/UIPrH6_f7q4?t=570) The coach states skill development is secondary and baseball is tertiary to the primary goal of teaching intrinsic self-improvement motivation.
