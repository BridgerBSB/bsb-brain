# Do Different Arm Slots = Different Mechanics?

## TL;DR
- Arm slot groups (overhand <40°, three-quarters 50-60°, sidearm >70°) differ in more than just arm angle: shoulder abduction at release separates them most, dropping to 81° for sidearm vs 94° for overhand.
- Sidearm throwers show more shoulder external rotation (169° vs 162-163° for the other two groups) and rotate their hips later but faster, landing with a straighter front leg.
- The study's average velocity was only ~85 mph (38 m/s) across all groups, which the author flags as likely elite sidearmers compared against average 3/4 and overhead pitchers.

## Claims
- Shoulder Abduction: at front foot strike all three groups were similar (89° overhand, 89° three-quarters, 84° sidearm), but at release overhand reached 94°, three-quarters stayed nearly flat, and sidearm dropped to 81°.
- Contralateral Tilt: identified as the single biggest driver of arm slot differences between groups, shown to differ significantly across the three.
- Hip and Shoulder Separation: sidearm pitchers rotated their hips later but their shoulders sooner than the other two groups, and rotated hips significantly faster (degrees per second) than overhand or three-quarters; overhand reached peak hip rotation earliest and peak shoulder rotation latest, giving that group the most separation time.
- Max External Rotation: sidearm averaged 169 degrees of external rotation versus 162 for overhand and 163 for three-quarters.
- Front Leg Action: sidearm landed straightest (39° at foot strike, extending only to 37° at release); three-quarters extended most (43° to 35°, an 8-degree change); overhand landed with the most knee bend (44°) and extended to 39°.
- Back Leg Action: sidearm pitchers lifted the front knee less but led with the hips more (more "pelvic drift") than overhand and three-quarters, which were nearly identical to each other.
- Forward Trunk Tilt: all three arm slot groups fell within the range separating "fast throwers" (36°) from "slow throwers" (28°) reported in a separate velocity study.
- Editor's note on the source study: average velocity across all three groups was about 38 m/s (~85 mph), which may mean the sample captured elite sidearmers against only average 3/4 and overhead pitchers.
- The study excluded pitchers in the 40-50 and 60-70 degree arm angle ranges to keep the three groups cleanly separated.

## Evidence cited
- Unnamed study comparing overhand (<40°), three-quarters (50-60°), and sidearm (>70°) pitchers on external rotation, hip-shoulder timing, front/back leg action, and trunk tilt at ~85 mph average velocity.
- "Comparison of Kinematic and Temporal Parameters between Different Pitch Velocity Groups" (ResearchGate) - fast throwers averaged 36 degrees of forward trunk tilt versus 28 degrees for slow throwers.
- Randy Johnson and Nolan Ryan cited as visual examples of differing back-leg ground-force direction (horizontal vs. vertical); no measured data given, author calls this his own extrapolation. Shohei Ohtani gif cited to illustrate front leg extension continuing past ball release.

## Open questions
- The featured study's ~85 mph average velocity may reflect elite sidearmers compared against only average 3/4 and overhead pitchers, which could bias the arm-slot comparisons.
- The author explicitly says he is "stretching" the study's hip-drift data into a theory about horizontal vs. vertical ground-force direction (Johnson vs. Ryan), without new data to support it.
