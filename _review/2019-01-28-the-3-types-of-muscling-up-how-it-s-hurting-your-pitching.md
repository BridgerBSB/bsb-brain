# The 3 Types of "Muscling Up" | How it's Hurting Your Pitching Velocity

## TL;DR
- Muscling up (excess tension that blocks the kinetic chain) shows up in three places, not just the arm: the back leg/hip at landing, the low back, and the arm action itself.
- Ben Brewster's own back injury from trying to spin his hips as fast as possible cost him 8-12 weeks; learning to relax the low back instead raised his velocity from 85-87 mph to 88-91, touching 93.
- Tensing the pecs, upper traps, and biceps at max effort produces a disproportionately small velocity gain over submax effort; he cues "throw loose and quick" instead of "throw hard" to fix it.

## Claims
- [[00:00](https://youtu.be/c5ANKyaw6hI?t=0)] Muscling up is usually thought of as an arm-action problem but can also occur in the lower half/hips.
- [[00:50](https://youtu.be/c5ANKyaw6hI?t=60)] Kinetic chain energy transfers proximal to distal; any blocked segment is an energy leak.
- [[02:32](https://youtu.be/c5ANKyaw6hI?t=122)] First muscling-up site: the back leg/hip not relaxing into landing, which blocks converting linear momentum into rotational momentum.
- [[03:03](https://youtu.be/c5ANKyaw6hI?t=183)] Effective lower half has three phases: drift (leg lift), drive/pulse (knee extension, hip extension, hip abduction), and rotation into landing.
- [[04:03](https://youtu.be/c5ANKyaw6hI?t=243)] Jake Arrieta example: after exhausting the back-leg drive, he rotates/internally rotates the back hip down into landing rather than pushing.
- [[06:06](https://youtu.be/c5ANKyaw6hI?t=366)] Second site: low back/lumbar spine. Author strained his back in college trying to spin his hips as fast as possible for velocity, was shut down 8-12 weeks.
- [[07:08](https://youtu.be/c5ANKyaw6hI?t=428)] After rehab, relaxing the back instead of forcing rotation raised his velocity from 85-87 mph to 88-91, touching 93 that summer.
- [[08:10](https://youtu.be/c5ANKyaw6hI?t=489)] Third site: arm action, either hijacking the energy flow before the lower half finishes, or tensing pecs/upper traps/biceps at max effort, which limits scap retraction and reduces velocity transfer.

## Cues
- **"Feel like a tornado" / "a wave of energy"** - fixes the back leg/hip not relaxing into landing (muscling up the lower half); for pitchers who push into landing instead of rotating down.
- **"Throw loose and quick"** - fixes tensing in the arm action (pecs, upper traps, biceps) during max-effort throws; replaces the "throw hard" cueing.

## Anti-cues
- **"Throw hard" / "throw as hard as possible"** - taught to fix low effort or low velocity; actually causes tensing in the pecs, upper traps, and biceps that limits scap retraction and arm speed; per Ben Brewster (Tread).

## Evidence cited

## Open questions
