# From 88 to 96 MPH in 10 Weeks: The Luis Patiño Story

## TL;DR
- Patiño went from a shoulder-injured 87-88 mph with a torn-down mocap profile to averaging 94 and topping 96 in pro ball over roughly 10 weeks.
- The fix was arm-path retraining, not more stuff: his pelvis/trunk speed was already elite, but his max shoulder external rotation was 165 degrees versus 180+ for Tread's top throwers.
- Coaches cut catch play for two weeks, used hand-guided slow-intent arm-path drills, then reintroduced feel-based catch play (softballs) before ramping intent.

## Claims
- [00:00](https://youtu.be/69mmb4DadAY?t=0) Patiño was the Padres' No. 3 prospect and No. 27 overall, and had touched 100 mph in the majors before this decline.
- [01:03](https://youtu.be/69mmb4DadAY?t=63) Six years later he couldn't hold 92 mph with a bad shoulder; the piece frames the turnaround as reaching 95-96 within three months.
- [01:36](https://youtu.be/69mmb4DadAY?t=63) His first mocap visit at Tread had him sitting 86-89 mph, topping out at 93.
- [02:08](https://youtu.be/69mmb4DadAY?t=128) At the WBC he touched 94 in one exhibition outing but averaged around 92 across two appearances, and got hit hard by Cuba in the first inning.
- [06:14](https://youtu.be/69mmb4DadAY?t=374) Mocap showed max shoulder external rotation at 165 degrees versus 180+ for Tread's top throwers, roughly a 15-20 degree deficit.
- [07:18](https://youtu.be/69mmb4DadAY?t=438) Before the fix, velocity was inconsistent bullpen to bullpen: 93 one day, 89 the next, because the arm relied on trunk and lower-half speed alone.
- [08:18](https://youtu.be/69mmb4DadAY?t=498) Catch play was cut entirely for two weeks in favor of heavy-constraint, hand-guided slow-intent arm-path work.
- [09:18](https://youtu.be/69mmb4DadAY?t=558) Around week 3, catch play switched to softballs for spin feedback before returning to baseballs.
- [10:19](https://youtu.be/69mmb4DadAY?t=619) By weeks 6-7 he was hitting 93-95 in bullpens, then hit 96 on mocap in a full-intent pen.
- [13:26](https://youtu.be/69mmb4DadAY?t=806) End state after 10 weeks: from lost at 87-88 to averaging 94, topping 96, in pro ball.

## Cues
- **"Go back to what you used to do"** - fixes an overcorrected early arm flip; for pitchers coming back from Tommy John who were told (wrongly, per this coach) their arm slot was too low
- **"Feel like a shortstop, feel like an infielder"** - fixes robotic, over-manipulated arm action during catch play; for pitchers who consciously steer the arm
- **"Feel yourself in front of the ball"** (paired with rolling through the inside of the baseball) - fixes an uncommandable spike/kick changeup; for pitchers who get around the side of the ball

## Drills
- **Heavy-constraint hand-guided arm path** - coach manually positions the arm at slow/light intent to get the elbow in plane before letting the pelvis and trunk take over; builds a repeatable arm path after a long layoff; for pitchers relearning mechanics post-shoulder injury
- **Softball catch play** - catch play using softballs instead of baseballs; builds spin/feel feedback (backspin cues if pushing or cutting the ball) so the arm works without conscious steering; for pitchers who over-manipulate the arm
- **Plyo movement variety** - plyo work incorporating double-play feeds, self-toss, and drive catches instead of static reps; builds natural, non-robotic arm action; for pitchers who get too mechanical when thinking about the arm

## Evidence cited
- Named-athlete case data on Luis Patiño: mocap-measured max external rotation of 165 degrees versus a 180+ benchmark for Tread's top throwers; velocity progression from 86-89 mph (initial mocap) to 96 mph (week 10 full-intent pen).

## Open questions
- The stated turnaround duration is inconsistent within the same video: the title and closing line say 10 weeks, the narration says three months at [01:03](https://youtu.be/69mmb4DadAY?t=63), and a separate estimate at [05:12](https://youtu.be/69mmb4DadAY?t=312) describes a projected 5-6 months compressed to two months.
- The coaching fix ("go back to what you used to do," arm stays lower/later) directly contradicts arm-slot advice Patiño says he received after his Tommy John surgery ([07:18](https://youtu.be/69mmb4DadAY?t=438)), with no outside source cited for either claim.
