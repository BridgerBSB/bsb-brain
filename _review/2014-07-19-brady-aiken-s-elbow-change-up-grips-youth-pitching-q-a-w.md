# Brady Aiken's Elbow, Change-Up Grips, Youth Pitching | Q&A w/ Driveline Baseball

## TL;DR
- Boddy says lead-leg knee bracing at foot contact (not full extension, just no further flexion after contact) is confirmed by force-plate and high-speed video data as a major driver of command and velocity, and torso collapse over the front leg is often the real cause when it fails.
- He walks through Driveline's changeup teaching progression (two-seam thrown hard, to a three-seam sinker grip thrown hard, to a choked-back changeup grip) and explains why they hold youth pitchers off changeups.
- He names three forearm flexor muscles (flexor carpi ulnaris, pronator teres, flexor digitorum superficialis) as key UCL protectors and pairs general grip work with sport-specific loaded-ball and wrist-weight throws to time that protection into the delivery.

## Claims
- [01:01](https://youtu.be/Bb0dUGj-wWU?t=61) Brady Aiken is reported to have a congenitally small or possibly absent UCL, comparable to R.A. Dickey's case; pitchers with damaged or missing UCLs usually don't reach pro ball because of instability and pain.
- [02:02](https://youtu.be/Bb0dUGj-wWU?t=122) A congenitally absent ligament may make Tommy John surgery an overlay rather than a repair, which could complicate the fix; asymptomatic reports don't rule out injury since pitchers' shoulders are commonly structurally abnormal ("jacked up") yet asymptomatic.
- [03:02](https://youtu.be/Bb0dUGj-wWU?t=182) Lead-leg (glove-side) knee bracing at foot contact is, per force-plate and high-speed video data cross-checked against other labs, a huge factor in command and velocity; full knee extension isn't required, but the knee must not flex further after contact.
- [04:03](https://youtu.be/Bb0dUGj-wWU?t=243) Lead-leg collapse is multivariate: it can trace to weak knee extensors, poor ankle mobility, or torso positioning collapsing over the front leg, with torso positioning called the bigger mechanical factor.
- [04:03](https://youtu.be/Bb0dUGj-wWU?t=304) Recommends squats, deadlifts, single-leg work, and eccentric/lateral plyometrics (skater jumps) for lead-leg strength; argues training only in the frontal plane (lateral med ball throws) misses force acceptance, which needs sagittal-plane work like heavy squats and deadlifts too.
- [06:05](https://youtu.be/Bb0dUGj-wWU?t=365) Names three forearm flexor muscles (flexor carpi ulnaris, pronator teres, flexor digitorum superficialis) as major elbow stabilizers that cross and protect the UCL, per unspecified outside research.
- [06:05](https://youtu.be/Bb0dUGj-wWU?t=425) Recommends both general grip work (rice bucket, farmer's walks, deadlifts, pull-ups) and sport-specific loaded work (heavy ball throws, wrist weights) so forearm flexor activation is timed into the actual delivery, not just constant grip tension.
- [07:05](https://youtu.be/Bb0dUGj-wWU?t=486) Changeup teaching progression: two-seam fastball thrown as hard as possible, to a three-seam sinker grip thrown as hard as possible, to a changeup grip choked back and pulled on the inside seam, always at full effort.
- [09:08](https://youtu.be/Bb0dUGj-wWU?t=548) States they don't teach changeups much to youth pitchers, believing early changeup work cannibalizes arm speed and makes the delivery too linear.
- [09:08](https://youtu.be/Bb0dUGj-wWU?t=608) For youth pitchers in growth spurts: watch for medial/lateral elbow pain and lateral compression at open growth plates (which can present as biceps tendinitis); recommends strict innings limits, more practice-phase training, and a video library to track mechanics through growth spurts.

## Cues
- **"keep the intents up"** (sic) - fixes deceleration/loss of arm speed while working through the two-seam to sinker to changeup grip progression; for pitchers learning the changeup grip.

## Anti-cues
- **"equal and opposite" / firm front side** - taught as a way to build a blocking front side; argued to be a poor teach, though the specific mechanism or harm isn't detailed in this clip (he points to a separate written piece); per Kyle Boddy (Driveline).
- **"shedding"** (for maximum external rotation forearm/wrist action) - taught to describe the forearm laying back at max external rotation; called "a really terrible term for it," though the specific mischaracterization isn't spelled out here; per Kyle Boddy.

## Drills
- **Changeup grip progression** - two-seam fastball thrown max effort, to a three-seam sinker grip thrown max effort, to a changeup grip choked back and pulled on the inside seam; builds feel for axis of spin while preserving arm speed and intent; for pitchers being introduced to a changeup.
- **Grip-strength circuit** - rice bucket wrist rotations, farmer's walks, deadlifts, pull-ups, Captains of Crush grippers; builds general forearm flexor mass (FCU, pronator teres, FDS) that protects the UCL; for pitchers generally.
- **Heavy ball and wrist-weight throws** - loaded-ball and wrist-weight throwing work; builds sport-specific forearm flexor activation timed into the actual delivery rather than constant grip tension; for pitchers, to translate general grip strength into arm care.

## Evidence cited
- Internal Driveline biomechanics lab data (force plates, high-speed video) on lead-leg bracing at foot contact, said to be cross-validated by other labs; no specific numbers given.
- Unnamed outside research cited for the forearm flexor mass (FCU, pronator teres, FDS) crossing and protecting the UCL; no study named.
- Named mechanical examples, not performance results: Tim Lincecum and Clayton Kershaw for lead-leg extension without full knee lockout; Marco Gonzalez for lead-leg collapse tied to torso positioning.

## Open questions
- The "equal and opposite" / firm front side critique is asserted without the mechanism given in this clip (mechanism lives in a linked article not included here).
- Argues for both frontal-plane (lateral, skater-jump) and sagittal-plane (squat/deadlift) lead-leg training against a training-community trend toward lateral-only work, but gives no split or dosing.
