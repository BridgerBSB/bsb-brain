# Max Velocity Pulldowns - 105.8 MPH | Driveline Baseball

## TL;DR
- Casey Weathers throws a pulldown series that clusters around 105 mph, with the coach crediting a 105.8 mph read and discounting a called-out 109 mph as not real.
- Per the video description, the circuit runs an overload-to-underload progression: a 7 oz ball at 105.1 mph, standard baseballs near 105 mph, and a 2 oz underload ball at 116 mph.
- The clip shows the drill itself, not a taught cue; the coach's commentary is mostly live velocity calls, not mechanical coaching.

## Claims
- [00:07](https://youtu.be/d-a0CKlF8sQ?t=7) The coach calls out readings in real time on a pulldown series, crediting 105.8 mph and openly discounting a 109 mph call as "not real," settling on a range he'll "believe... like 107."
- [00:07](https://youtu.be/d-a0CKlF8sQ?t=7) One throw reads 104.9 mph, described as "nice."
- [00:07](https://youtu.be/d-a0CKlF8sQ?t=7) The circuit moves through ball weights called "fours" and "threes," each thrown once at roughly 80 percent effort and once at 100 percent.
- [00:07](https://youtu.be/d-a0CKlF8sQ?t=7) The session finishes on the lightest "twos" ball, which the coach calls going "crazy" before the clip cuts off.

## Drills
- **Max velocity pulldowns (overload/underload ball circuit)** - crow-hop pulldown throws moving from a 7 oz overload ball through standard baseballs down to a 2 oz underload ball, each weight thrown once at roughly 80 percent and once at 100 percent effort; builds max-effort arm speed the description claims transfers to mound velocity; for an experienced pitcher (Casey Weathers) in an off-season velocity block, not touching a mound until late November.

## Evidence cited
- Casey Weathers, per the video description: 105.8 mph credited on a pulldown, a 7 oz overload ball at 105.1 mph, multiple 5 oz baseballs around 105 mph, and a 2 oz underload ball at 116 mph.
- The description links an external Driveline article ("transfer-weighted-baseball-velocity-mound," Nov 2014) as its basis for claiming this work transfers to mound velocity; that article is not part of this source and its content is not verified here.

## Open questions
- A 109 mph read is called out live and then dismissed by the coach as unreliable with no stated reason beyond disbelief; the clip does not say how the radar reading was checked.
- The description asserts transfer to mound velocity, but this video shows only pulldown throws, no mound work, so the claim is unsupported within this source.
