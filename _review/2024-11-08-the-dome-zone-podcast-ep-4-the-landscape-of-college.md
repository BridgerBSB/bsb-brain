# THE DOME ZONE PODCAST EP#4- The Landscape of College Baseball and the Draft

## TL;DR
- Scholarship and transfer rule changes have turned college rosters into year-to-year free agency, squeezing out late-developing high schoolers who used to land at mid-majors.
- Draft evaluation now runs on stuff models and per-pitch data instead of raw ERA, because minor league development windows have shrunk to two or three years, down from five or six.
- The hosts pitch group training as a business alternative to private lessons, arguing $80-100/hour one-on-one sessions bought once a week can't build skill the way daily access does.

## Claims
- [01:00](https://youtu.be/PkFCxMVwYLY?t=60) Roster and scholarship rule changes are removing the traditional path for late-developing high school players into mid-major programs.
- [02:00](https://youtu.be/PkFCxMVwYLY?t=120) The proposed 34 fully-funded scholarship rule won't help mid-majors evenly since funding is a school budget decision, not a baseball department one, favoring SEC/ACC/Big Ten programs.
- [03:03](https://youtu.be/PkFCxMVwYLY?t=183) Rosters are increasingly built through the transfer portal rather than four-year development; donors are said to influence personnel decisions at some big programs.
- [04:03](https://youtu.be/PkFCxMVwYLY?t=243) Scholarships are year-to-year rather than guaranteed four years, with the number rising for producing players to keep them from leaving for NIL money elsewhere.
- [08:11](https://youtu.be/PkFCxMVwYLY?t=491) Playing time is described as the main recruiting currency: players move up levels only if they produce, turning college into a resume-building stop.
- [15:18](https://youtu.be/PkFCxMVwYLY?t=918) Analytics on transfer-portal pitchers correct for small-sample ERA, citing an example of a pitcher with 11 scoreless innings and one bad outing landing at a 4.50 ERA despite good stuff.
- [16:18](https://youtu.be/PkFCxMVwYLY?t=978) More high school prospects are reportedly signing after being drafted rather than betting on college development, given the risk of being cut after one bad fall.
- [33:32](https://youtu.be/PkFCxMVwYLY?t=2012) Draft evaluation centers on a stuff/model score estimating whether a pitcher's stuff gets outs in the minors now; 88-92 mph is called "fairly stock," and undrafted high school arms need a plus breaking ball, command, and physical maturity.
- [34:33](https://youtu.be/PkFCxMVwYLY?t=2073) Minor league development windows have shortened to roughly two to three years, down from five or six previously.
- [41:39](https://youtu.be/PkFCxMVwYLY?t=2499) One-on-one lessons run roughly $80-100/hour and are typically bought once a week (about four hours a month), contrasted with the facility's group model offering daily access for a comparable monthly cost.
