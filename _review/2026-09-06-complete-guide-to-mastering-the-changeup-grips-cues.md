# Complete Guide To Mastering The Changeup [Grips, Cues, & Sequencing]

## TL;DR
- Changeup design starts with a diagnostic, not a grip: check spin efficiency to sort pronators from supinators (above 90-95% is pronator, 80s or below is supinator) before picking a grip family.
- Two-seam or one-seam orientation is how most elite changeups get their late bite; the design target is velocity deviation off the fastball plus vertical or horizontal shape separation.
- Shape without location is wasted: target down and arm-side, start the pitch around the catcher's chest, and let it work down rather than starting it where you want it to end.

## Claims
- [01:01](https://youtu.be/fBpo461Q7y8?t=61) High-release, north-south pitchers should lean on vertical approach angle rather than arm-side movement; Justin Verlander's changeup has unremarkable movement but a VAA around -8.5.
- [01:01](https://youtu.be/fBpo461Q7y8?t=61) Lower-slot, east-west pitchers have less access to vertical separation between fastball and changeup than true 3/4 slots, which have the most.
- [01:01](https://youtu.be/fBpo461Q7y8?t=61) Changeup design generally targets velocity deviation from the fastball plus vertical/horizontal shape separation, though firm changeups and splitters can also work.
- [02:01](https://youtu.be/fBpo461Q7y8?t=121) Most of the best MLB changeups use two-seam or one-seam orientation to maximize seam-effect movement.
- [03:01](https://youtu.be/fBpo461Q7y8?t=181) Spin efficiency above 90-95% indicates a true pronator; efficiency in the 80s or lower indicates a supinator, checked via slow-motion camera on ball flight or a Rapsodo/TrackMan spin efficiency reading.
- [03:01](https://youtu.be/fBpo461Q7y8?t=181) The default starting grip for everyone is a traditional two-seam circle change, middle and ring fingers over the seams, biased slightly outside each seam.
- [04:03](https://youtu.be/fBpo461Q7y8?t=243) Very heavy pronators (Devin Williams-style screwball) should slide the grip toward a four-seam look without a seam circle underneath, to avoid unwanted lift from inverse gyro spin.
- [06:05](https://youtu.be/fBpo461Q7y8?t=365) Supinators should drop changeup-specific cueing and throw it like a fastball, staying on the outside of the ball instead of cutting through the side of it.
- [08:08](https://youtu.be/fBpo461Q7y8?t=488) Split-change options include the Gausman grip (middle finger on the outer rail, three fingers together) for pitchers who can't get to the inside or outside of the ball; the Logan Gilbert splitter grip pulls the ring finger in to cut hand tension.
- [15:15](https://youtu.be/fBpo461Q7y8?t=915) Locate the changeup down and arm-side (bottom-right quadrant for a righty), starting around the catcher's chest; depthier, more vertical splitters thrown underneath are more platoon-neutral, citing "Scheimel" (sic) in the WBC going underneath to righties.

## Cues
- **"Turn the ball over and get to the inside"** ("pronate through") - fixes a flat, non-separating changeup; for pronators
- **"Throw it like a fastball"** - fixes overcueing/cutting the changeup; for supinators
- **"Get that middle finger away from the midline"** - fixes a changeup that breaks along the arm path like a fastball
- **"Feel like the palm of your hand is facing directly at the catcher at release"** - fixes a cutty, slider-shaped changeup
