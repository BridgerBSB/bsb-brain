# Tread Athletics - Q&A Episode 1

## TL;DR
- Ben Brewster runs through a broad Q&A on nutrition, workload, mechanics and cues, repeatedly pushing back against one-size-fits-all rules in favor of individualized assessment.
- The clearest actionable point: off-season throwing shutdown should scale with an athlete's workload and career timeline, ranging from zero time off to a full 3-4 months, not a fixed default.
- Signature cue: add internal rotation torque on the glove arm ("point that palm to the sky") to stop the front side from flying open.

## Claims
- [00:00](https://youtu.be/3uEkjxYrRgY?t=0) Lean mass gain requires a caloric surplus with roughly 1g protein per pound of bodyweight, 20-25% of calories from fat, rest from carbs; athletes far from their genetic potential can add 1-2 lb/week (mostly lean mass), intermediates should target closer to 0.5 lb/week (2-3 lb/month).
- [03:02](https://youtu.be/3uEkjxYrRgY?t=182) No established method exists for training spin rate; anecdotally, some pro pitchers who snapped the wrist more aggressively at release saw a 200-300 rpm increase.
- [05:02](https://youtu.be/3uEkjxYrRgY?t=302) Two-knee long toss isolates upper-half arm action from the lower half; Craig Kimbrel's rehab version of this drill is credited with a 5-10 mph velocity increase.
- [09:04](https://youtu.be/3uEkjxYrRgY?t=544) Pitchers train speed/conditioning about twice a week, treated as only 1-3% of total physical preparation.
- [13:07](https://youtu.be/3uEkjxYrRgY?t=787) Shoulder tilt should result from back-leg drive after a weight shift, not an artificial lean back; Tread athlete "Rowin" went from 88-91 mph to 92-96 mph in bullpens partly by removing an artificial lateral lean.
- [18:14](https://youtu.be/3uEkjxYrRgY?t=1094) At young ages the priority is movement patterns and athleticism, not throwing hard or throwing strikes; meaningful muscle-mass gain isn't possible until puberty (roughly 15-18).
- [20:17](https://youtu.be/3uEkjxYrRgY?t=1217) Off-season shutdown scales with workload and career stage: fatigued or high-workload athletes may take a full 3-4 months off with a 4-week ramp back, while a rising HS senior needing to go from 78 to 85 mph in about 6 months may take little to no time off.
- [28:27](https://youtu.be/3uEkjxYrRgY?t=1707) Sustainable fat loss targets 1-1.5 lb/week via a 500-700 calorie weekly deficit, with protein at 30-40% of intake or above 1g per pound of bodyweight.
- [33:31](https://youtu.be/3uEkjxYrRgY?t=2011) Recommended cue for pitchers who fly open: internal rotation torque on the glove arm, learned from Luke Haggerty (Tom Seaver's pitching coach).
- [39:39](https://youtu.be/3uEkjxYrRgY?t=2379) Long toss is framed as a drill, not velocity training; it rewards higher arm slot and backspin and is not recommended for submarine or sinker-reliant pitchers.

## Cues
- **"Point that palm to the sky"** - fixes the front shoulder flying open early; for pitchers who can't stay closed through the glove side, per a cue Brewster learned from Luke Haggerty

## Anti-cues
- none

## Drills
- **Two-knee long toss** - throwing from both knees, removes the lower half; builds upper-half arm-action efficiency and kinetic-chain transfer; for pitchers with an arm-action deficiency (as used in Craig Kimbrel's rehab)
- **Pivot pick / 10-toes drill** - short-range throws with the lower half taken out; builds isolated arm-action feel; for pitchers needing arm-action work without lower-half compensation
- **Long toss** - extended-distance throwing used as a drill, not velocity training; builds backspin/arm-slot efficiency through visual ball-flight feedback; for traditional 3/4-overhand pitchers, not submarine or sinker-heavy arms
- **Rolling/torque drill (Texas Baseball Ranch)** - lock front and back foot forward, counter-rotate the shoulders as far as possible, then step and throw; builds felt hip-shoulder separation; for pitchers who fly open early and have adequate internal rotation/thoracic mobility
- **Serratus wall slides** - low-load wall slide cueing the serratus anterior; builds overhead shoulder-flexion mechanics; for pitchers building overhead capacity
- **Landmine press** - angled press substituted for a true overhead press; builds upward-rotation strength without full overhead load; for pitchers with limited scapular mobility

## Evidence cited
- Craig Kimbrel: rehabbed via two-knee long toss, credited with a 5-10 mph velocity gain [05:02](https://youtu.be/3uEkjxYrRgY?t=302).
- Tread athlete "Rowin": bullpen velocity went from 88-91 mph to 92-96 mph after removing an artificial shoulder lean [13:07](https://youtu.be/3uEkjxYrRgY?t=787).
- Chris Sale: spring-training rehab outing at 96-98 mph, 13 strikeouts in 5 innings, on a low arm slot/sinker profile with no long toss in his program [40:41](https://youtu.be/3uEkjxYrRgY?t=2441).

## Open questions
- Cites "30-50 percent" of big leaguers having had Tommy John surgery to argue nobody has solved healthy high-velocity throwing, without naming a source study [08:04](https://youtu.be/3uEkjxYrRgY?t=484).
- Recommends against long toss for submarine/sinker-ball pitchers, which runs against the common practice of prescribing long toss to all pitchers regardless of arm slot [39:39](https://youtu.be/3uEkjxYrRgY?t=2379).
