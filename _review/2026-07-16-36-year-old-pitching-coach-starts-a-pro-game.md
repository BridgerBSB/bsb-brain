# 36-Year-Old Pitching Coach Starts A Pro Game

## TL;DR
- Mic'd-up start where a 36-year-old pitching coach pitched a doubleheader game plan built around protecting the bullpen, targeting 12-15 pitches an inning over strikeouts.
- Rushing his tempo under adrenaline made his front leg land early and his arm lag behind, which he fixed mid-game with the cue "delay your front leg, get it up, give this time to get down."
- Final line: about 80 pitches across 3.2 innings with 9-10 strikeouts, hurt in part by a blood blister and dry fingers affecting his grip.

## Claims
- [01:01](https://youtu.be/XiaSpklFIOs?t=61) Game plan for a doubleheader start: pitch to contact, average 12-15 pitches an inning, prioritize innings pitched over strikeouts to save the bullpen for the rest of the week.
- [00:01](https://youtu.be/XiaSpklFIOs?t=1) When he speeds up his tempo under adrenaline his pelvis opens sideways early and he gets "pushy," missing arm-side; his preferred feel is a big arm swing that floats into place before he pulls through.
- [03:17](https://youtu.be/XiaSpklFIOs?t=197) In 90-degree heat he cut his warmup 3-5 minutes shorter than usual to bring his heart rate down before the first pitch, since he pitches best calm.
- [05:18](https://youtu.be/XiaSpklFIOs?t=318) No-step roll-in drill used to prep the oblique swing, feeling a stretch through the obliques and rib cage while keeping the arm loose and untensed.
- [07:25](https://youtu.be/XiaSpklFIOs?t=445) After video review he identified that landing early made his torso rotate ahead of his arm, causing him to throw at a batter's head last outing; his fix cue is delaying the front leg.
- [11:01](https://youtu.be/XiaSpklFIOs?t=661) Team's starter warmup rule: roughly 30 minutes out, about 10 minutes of stretching then 5-6 minutes of long toss capped at 90-120 feet, not a max-distance long toss.
- [23:43](https://youtu.be/XiaSpklFIOs?t=1423) Discussion of rubber positioning: standing third-base side of the rubber points the target more centrally and can help rotation, while first-base side encourages more crossfire.
- [43:57](https://youtu.be/XiaSpklFIOs?t=2637) A blood blister from a too-short-cut fingernail, plus sweat-dried fingers, made the ball slip and come out low in the middle innings, so he raised his target to compensate.
- [48:14](https://youtu.be/XiaSpklFIOs?t=2894) Final line: about 3 and 2/3 innings, roughly 80 pitches, 9-10 strikeouts, several walks and a hit batter; he attributed the command trouble to excess movement in his delivery that day.

## Cues
- **"Let it float into place and then just pull it through"** - fixes rushing tempo and going arm-side under adrenaline; for the pitcher's own in-game timing.
- **"Delay your front leg, get it up, give this time to get down"** - fixes landing too early and going under the pitch (previously caused a pitch thrown at a batter's head); for pitchers whose arm lags behind an early-landing front leg.
- **"Arms just along for the ride"** - fixes tensing/overusing the arm during warmup; cued while the pelvis and torso do the work in the roll-in plyo drill.

## Drills
- **No-step roll-in (plyo)** - a stationary roll-in plyo ball throw with no step; builds oblique/rib-cage stretch and free pelvis-driven energy while keeping the arm loose; used in pregame warmup.

## Open questions
- The team's starter warmup protocol (10 min stretch, 5-6 min long toss capped at 90-120 ft) is shorter and more distance-limited than many programs use; the source gives no reasoning or season-long outcome data for it.
- He attributes mid-game command loss to a too-short-cut fingernail and sweat-dried fingers rather than a mechanical cause, asserted without further support.
