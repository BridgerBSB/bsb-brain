# Q & A Episode 4

## TL;DR
- Broad pitcher-development Q&A: outside programming and feel-based training beat self-analysis and heavy video review; Ben says he went from 85 to 95 mph over four years partly by watching less of his own mechanics.
- Gives absolute-strength benchmarks for diminishing returns (deadlift low-to-mid 400s, squat 315-335, DB bench 100 lb, all x3-5) and a power-phase intensity range of 30-70% 1RM once that base is built.
- Argues follow-through faults are downstream of earlier kinetic-chain errors, so cueing the finish (e.g. telling a pitcher to "get on top of it more") misses the actual cause.

## Claims
- [00:48](https://youtu.be/1nMbr9IoQrg?t=48) Having someone else write your program is preferable to self-programming because self-coaches tend to over-focus on what they're already good at and lack outside perspective.
- [02:57](https://youtu.be/1nMbr9IoQrg?t=177) About 15-20% of the pitchers Tread coaches are "over-analytical," fixating on internal cues (hand position, elbow, foot landing) and unable to shift out of that mode to compete.
- [04:14](https://youtu.be/1nMbr9IoQrg?t=254) Ben says he went from 85 to 95 mph over four years in college partly by cutting back on watching his own mechanics on video and training more by feel.
- [06:15](https://youtu.be/1nMbr9IoQrg?t=375) Watching full-speed video of elite throwers transfers better to a viewer's own movement pattern than slow-motion video, which he reserves for grip and pitch-design detail.
- [08:28](https://youtu.be/1nMbr9IoQrg?t=508) Recommends an "athletically lean" body fat range of roughly 10-20%, with most Tread athletes landing 10-16%; rule of thumb is being able to see top abs.
- [16:56](https://youtu.be/1nMbr9IoQrg?t=1016) For hip impingement, check adductor tightness and pelvic control (RDLs, single-leg RDL, hip airplanes) before assuming a bony abnormality requiring imaging.
- [19:41](https://youtu.be/1nMbr9IoQrg?t=1181) A flat-back, extended follow-through is a downstream result of getting lateral into the back leg during weight shift, not something to fix by cueing the finish directly.
- [22:08](https://youtu.be/1nMbr9IoQrg?t=1328) Strength benchmarks where returns diminish: deadlift low-to-mid 400s lb, squat 315-335 lb, DB bench 100 lb, DB row ~100 lb/arm, all for 3-5 clean reps; judged on absolute numbers, not bodyweight ratios.
- [24:12](https://youtu.be/1nMbr9IoQrg?t=1477) Once a strength base is built, shift training to 30-70% of 1RM (30-50% speed-strength, 50-60% strength-speed) plus lighter med ball and bodyweight plyos to raise rate of force development.
- [39:18](https://youtu.be/1nMbr9IoQrg?t=2394) When repatterning a new movement, floor intensity should be the slowest speed that still lets the athlete feel the movement (he cites roughly 30% as workable); max-effort throwing should be capped around twice a week, 15-25 throws, modeled on javelin training.

## Anti-cues
- **"get on top of it more"** - taught to fix a pitch left up; actually addresses a symptom rather than the cause, since arm slot and release are downstream of earlier kinetic-chain errors like opening the hips or shoulders early or not staying on the backside long enough; per Ben Brewster (Tread).

## Drills
- **Elevated push-up progression** - push-ups regressed onto a stair or couch to keep form clean before progressing to the floor; builds a proper pressing pattern; for young kids training without gym access.
- **Inverted row / doorway pull-up** - TRX or blast straps off a pull-up bar, or a doorway pull-up bar; builds the main pulling pattern; for young kids training without gym access.
- **Goblet squat to box** - dumbbell held goblet-style, squatting to a box for depth; builds the main squat pattern; for young kids training without gym access.
- **Goblet reverse lunge** - dumbbell goblet hold, reverse lunge (with a lateral variation); builds single-leg strength; for young kids training without gym access.
- **Band pull-apart** - resistance band pulled apart through different planes; builds scapular control; for young kids training without gym access.

## Evidence cited
- Tread's free internal metrics tool ranks athletes against absolute-strength benchmarks (deadlift, squat, DB bench, DB row) rather than bodyweight ratios [22:08](https://youtu.be/1nMbr9IoQrg?t=1328).
- Ben cites his own case: velocity dropped 6-7 mph with no pain before a labrum injury surfaced, traced to an unintentionally raised arm slot [31:30](https://youtu.be/1nMbr9IoQrg?t=1905).

## Open questions
- Claims full-speed video transfers movement patterns better than slow-motion review, without citing a study [06:15](https://youtu.be/1nMbr9IoQrg?t=375).
- Argues in-season med ball work should be reduced because throwing itself already provides enough absolute-speed volume, which cuts against programs that maintain med ball work year-round for power maintenance [18:27](https://youtu.be/1nMbr9IoQrg?t=1109).
