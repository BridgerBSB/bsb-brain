# The Tennis Racket Drill | A Simple Arm Action Fix?

## TL;DR
- Uses the tennis serve as a cue to fix a pushy, tricep-dominant arm action by teaching pitchers to accelerate the arm from behind the body instead of out front.
- Lays out a three-step progression (cue only, then a rhythm rocker with a figure-8, then a full delivery with step-back or step-in) reserved for high three-quarter arm slot pitchers, not low-slot arms.
- Estimates the cue only clicks for about 3 to 5 pitchers out of every 12 to 15 on a staff.

## Claims
- [01:09](https://youtu.be/XEMqA0BTuzo?t=69) A tennis serve requires creating acceleration from behind the body to contact the ball overhead, which prevents cheating the elbow forward.
- [02:11](https://youtu.be/XEMqA0BTuzo?t=131) High-velocity throwers pull the arm into release with the pec, matching the serve's sequencing of elbow extension then internal rotation, rather than pushing with the tricep out front.
- [03:11](https://youtu.be/XEMqA0BTuzo?t=191) Elevating the glove arm, as in a tennis serve, is needed to deliver the throwing arm; the drill targets pitchers with a lazy or overly rotational glove side.
- [04:14](https://youtu.be/XEMqA0BTuzo?t=254) Citing Paul Nyman's rotational-plane framework (ferris wheel, merry-go-round, flatbed truck), the drill is said to apply to three-quarter and high three-quarter slot pitchers, not lower-slot arms.
- [06:16](https://youtu.be/XEMqA0BTuzo?t=376) Recommends testing the cue across a staff of 12 to 15 pitchers, focusing on higher-slot guys with particularly bad arm paths.
- [07:16](https://youtu.be/XEMqA0BTuzo?t=436) The rhythm rocker drill adds a figure-8 hand pattern to connect weight shift and hip rotation to the arm swing at low intensity, not max effort.
- [09:20](https://youtu.be/XEMqA0BTuzo?t=560) Once the cue and rocker drill click, progresses to step-back or step-in versions of the full delivery.
- [11:23](https://youtu.be/XEMqA0BTuzo?t=683) Warns that using an actual tennis racket risks over-supinating or chopping into release; the wrist should stay neutral and the elbow extended at release.
- [12:23](https://youtu.be/XEMqA0BTuzo?t=743) Estimates the cue will apply to roughly 3 to 5 pitchers out of a 12 to 15 man staff, not everyone.

## Cues
- **"Step and throw, hands together, imagine you got to hit a ball above your head"** - fixes a pushy, tricep-dominant arm path with elbow climb at release; for high three-quarter arm slot pitchers with inefficient or long arm paths.
- **"Close the figure 8, open the figure 8"** - fixes a glove arm and throwing arm that feel dissociated from each other; for pitchers who need to integrate glove-side torque with arm swing.

## Anti-cues
- none

## Drills
- **Tennis racket step-and-throw** - step and throw with hands together, imagining a tennis racket serving a ball overhead to a partner 60 feet away; builds acceleration from behind the body and glove-arm elevation; for high three-quarter slot pitchers with a pushy or long arm path.
- **Rhythm rocker with tennis racket and figure-8** - low-intensity rocker drill, hands together, adding a figure-8 hand pattern while rocking back and directing the "ball" toward a target; builds the connection between weight shift, hip rotation, and arm delivery.
- **Tennis racket full delivery (step-back / step-in)** - once the cue and rocker feel click, repeat with step-back or step-in versions of the normal delivery, optionally holding an actual tennis racket for auditory feedback; builds carryover of the accelerate-from-behind feel into a game delivery; caution against chopping or over-supinating into release.
