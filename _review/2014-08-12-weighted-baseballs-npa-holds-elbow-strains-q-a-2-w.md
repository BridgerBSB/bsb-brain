# Weighted Baseballs, NPA Holds, Elbow Strains | Q&A #2 w/ Driveline Baseball

## TL;DR
- Boddy argues Tom House's NPA "holds" are mechanically inefficient and unsupported by published research, despite EMG data Driveline collected showing them "pretty ineffective."
- He lays out in-season pitcher management: weighted balls cut to about once a week for advanced (90+ mph) arms, long toss 2 to 7 times a week as the main stimulus, and 3 to 5 months off pitching per year.
- He gives a fix for a cutter that was causing ulnar nerve and cartilage irritation: pronate the release instead of supinating/pulling off it.

## Claims
- [00:00](https://youtu.be/MJJg9zZOo5M?t=0) His forthcoming book draws on 7 years of biomechanics lab and empirical work with 10+ colleges and 20+ pro athletes, plus hundreds of high school and youth pitchers.
- [01:02](https://youtu.be/MJJg9zZOo5M?t=62) Pronator flexor strains are often misdiagnosed as UCL tears; the forearm flexor mass (pronator teres, FCU, FDS) dynamically stabilizes the elbow alongside the UCL, and either injury can occur with or without the other.
- [02:04](https://youtu.be/MJJg9zZOo5M?t=124) A sidearm slot combined with early torso rotation produces the highest valgus stress on the UCL and pronator flexor mass, per research he cites but does not name in this segment.
- [04:08](https://youtu.be/MJJg9zZOo5M?t=248) Recommends heavy/med ball throws, wrist weight work, and farmer's-carry-style deadlifts to build the pronator flexor mass; treats existing strains with myofascial release, e-stim, and ice combined with compression.
- [05:09](https://youtu.be/MJJg9zZOo5M?t=309)/[08:13](https://youtu.be/MJJg9zZOo5M?t=493) Argues against NPA holds: Driveline's own EMG data shows them "pretty ineffective"; the grip-and-hold activates the pronator flexor mass early, blocks external rotation layback, and engages the hand before the proximal segment, violating the kinetic chain. No peer-reviewed or even non-peer-reviewed research on the holds' claims exists; one athlete he knows had a "significant decrease of internal rotation" after the program.
- [10:18](https://youtu.be/MJJg9zZOo5M?t=618) Driveline built a 5-camera 3D DLT motion capture system after studying Feltner and Dapena's biomechanics papers; uses it to compare pre/post training technique, not routine diagnostics, because marker-based lab throws run 10 to 15 mph slower than game velocity and vary by athlete.
- [11:19](https://youtu.be/MJJg9zZOo5M?t=679) Uses the Marc Pro e-stim device pre- and post-throwing; says 27 of 30 MLB teams use it.
- [12:19](https://youtu.be/MJJg9zZOo5M?t=739) In-season plan for advanced (90+ mph) pitchers: weighted-ball throwing cut to about once a week at most, with long toss 2 to 7 times a week as the primary training stimulus, plus arm care and foam rolling.
- [14:21](https://youtu.be/MJJg9zZOo5M?t=861) Recommends 3 to 5 months off pitching per year and calls winter ball a poor practice for arm health, though acknowledges pros often take it for pay (roughly $10,000/month) versus a minor league salary of $1,600 to $2,000/month.
- [15:22](https://youtu.be/MJJg9zZOo5M?t=922) A college pitcher's cutter grip (supinating, pulling off the pitch) caused ulnar nerve irritation and cartilage damage; fixed by having him pronate the release instead, which maintained or increased spin rate while keeping elbow flexion.

## Cues
- **"think about pronating, pulling the side down"** - fixes a supinated, pulled-off cutter release that causes ulnar nerve and elbow cartilage irritation; for pitchers who supinate their cutter

## Anti-cues
- none

## Drills
- **Heavy ball / med ball throws** - overload implement thrown for reps; builds pronator flexor mass and dynamic elbow stabilization; for pitchers with (or at risk of) pronator flexor strain
- **Wrist weight and rebounder throws** - wrist weights or a rebounder net used without a gripped, held-back position; builds posterior shoulder strength for deceleration without blocking external rotation; alternative to NPA Holds
- **Long toss** - thrown at increasing distance, 2 to 7 sessions per week in-season; builds/maintains arm strength as the primary in-season stimulus; for advanced (90+ mph) pitchers

## Evidence cited
- Feltner and Dapena's biomechanics papers on quantifying pitching kinematics and kinetics, cited as foundational to Driveline's motion capture build
- Driveline's internal EMG data on NPA holds, described as showing the holds are "pretty ineffective"
- Marc Pro adoption figure: 27 of 30 MLB teams, per Boddy
- One unnamed athlete with a "significant decrease of internal rotation" after the NPA holds program, cited anecdotally

## Open questions
- Boddy calls the NPA holds mechanically inefficient and unsupported by any published research, yet says college programs (he names Scott Brown at Vanderbilt) still use them as a warm-up; unresolved against common practice.
- The claim that a sidearm slot with early torso rotation produces the highest UCL/pronator valgus stress is asserted as "research" without naming the study in this segment.
