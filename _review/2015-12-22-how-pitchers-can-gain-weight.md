# How Pitchers Can Gain Weight

## TL;DR
- Consistency on a few core lifts with tracked progressive overload beats constant exercise variety: Christian Meister gained 10 lbs and added 4-8 mph on pull-downs in 6 weeks using four primary exercises.
- Weight gain comes from a tracked caloric surplus, not from eating "a lot" or "clean" by feel; caloric density of food matters more than perceived cleanliness.
- Practical target: at least one meal per 800-1,000 calories of the daily goal, with roughly 80% whole foods and 20% flexible foods to hit the number consistently.

## Claims
- **#6 They ignore the principle of progressive overload** - most programs aren't tracked, so overload (volume/intensity) isn't actually applied; gives a 4-week weighted front plank progression from 45 lbs to 90 lbs.
- **#5 They vary up their training too much** - Christian Meister went from 205 to 215 lbs in ~6 weeks and from 96 to 100 mph on pull-downs, using four primary exercises 3 days/week; box squat 185x5 to 275x5, DB bench 60x8 to 80x8, DB row 70x8 to 100x8, barbell lunge 125x6 to 185x6.
- **#4 They have no idea what they are training towards** - argues for individualized goal numbers per lift, e.g. moving a squat max from 275 lbs to 315 lbs across a 12-week phase.
- **#3 They lie to themselves that they eat a lot** - subjective fullness cues are unreliable for hardgainers; recommends tracking intake and bodyweight/strength response instead.
- **#2 They focus on eating only "clean" foods** - caloric density matters more than food volume; recommends chicken thighs over breast, full-fat dairy over low-fat, to hit a 4,000+ calorie target.
- **The 80-20 rule** - recommends 80% of intake from whole foods, up to 20% from otherwise "not allowed" foods, to help hit daily calorie/macro goals.
- **#1 They don't bring food with them wherever they go** - recommends at least one meal per 800-1,000 calories of the daily goal (e.g. 5 meals for a 5,000-calorie goal); sample portable meals: 2.5 PB sandwiches (~900 cal), 2 granola bars + 2 tortillas + trail mix (~1,000 cal), 16 oz chocolate milk + 3 handfuls trail mix (~1,000 cal).
- Intro - author states his own transformation from 6'3", 155 lbs to 65 lbs heavier (220 lbs), and says skinny pitchers he's worked with have put on 15-25 lbs in their first 3 months.
