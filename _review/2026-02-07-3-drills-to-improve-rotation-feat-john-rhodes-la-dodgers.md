# 3 Drills To Improve Rotation (feat. John Rhodes, LA Dodgers)

## TL;DR
- Driveline's Eric Kozak runs a swing design for Dodgers minor leaguer John Rhodes, whose root problem was an arm-dominant swing that didn't rotate.
- The progression: offset-open plus a handle-loaded bat, then a knob-to-hip constraint, then overload bat work; a bazooka-bat rep hit 110 mph off a 50 oz bat.
- The hard part is transfer to the game swing; it clicked once Rhodes stopped presetting his load and instead worked a small hinge through the stride, using his front leg to hold himself back into rotation.

## Claims
- [00:03](https://youtu.be/sKTs4WImtp4?t=3) Rhodes' root issue was that he didn't rotate and relied on his arms to deliver the barrel.
- [01:04](https://youtu.be/sKTs4WImtp4?t=64) Offset-open plus a handle-loaded bat was used because pulling the hands/lead arm across gets punished by the extra handle weight, forcing him to stop cutting balls oppo.
- [02:06](https://youtu.be/sKTs4WImtp4?t=126) Starting the knob near his hip gave runway to slot the barrel deeper, raising hip-shoulder separation and tightening the oppo-side bat path.
- [03:06](https://youtu.be/sKTs4WImtp4?t=186) A "let it soak" freedom progression was added and removed from hookup reps to rebuild rhythm without losing the new path.
- [06:11](https://youtu.be/sKTs4WImtp4?t=371) A bazooka bat (50 oz) rep produced a 110 mph reading; the overload weight is framed as forcing rotation because getting stuck back makes it un-turnable.
- [09:15](https://youtu.be/sKTs4WImtp4?t=555) Staying in place and rotating, instead of reaching out, was flagged as the target feel versus earlier sliding/reaching reps.
- [10:16](https://youtu.be/sKTs4WImtp4?t=616) The best game-style swing came when the leg lift blended with the forward move: the front leg holds him back while gravity pulls him forward, so the back hip stays loaded into rotation.
- [12:18](https://youtu.be/sKTs4WImtp4?t=738) Drills either open new motor patterns (example given: "big poppy," for hip-shoulder separation) or challenge an already-learned pattern under game-like conditions.
- [14:20](https://youtu.be/sKTs4WImtp4?t=860) Presetting and holding the load through the stride caused him to lose position; the fix is a small hinge, not maxed out, worked through the stride so he's set at foot plant.

## Cues
- **"Keep this closed a tiny bit longer"** - fixes catching the ball too shallow/early; for hitters working the offset-open hookup drill
- **"Turn behind it at second base"** (not center) - fixes losing opposite-field direction; for hitters learning rotation-driven direction
- **"Let it soak"** - fixes a rigid, stop-start swing during drill reps; used when adding freedom back into a constrained drill
- **"Your front leg is how you stay back"** - fixes losing the back hip/rushing forward during the leg lift

## Drills
- **Offset-open with handle-loaded bat** - offset-open stance, bat weighted at the handle, front toss; builds deeper barrel turn and oppo direction; for hitters whose hands/lead arm pull across
- **Knob-to-hip upper body constraint (hookup)** - start the knob near the hip before slotting the barrel; builds hip-shoulder separation and a tighter, deeper bat path
- **Bazooka bat hookup** - 50 oz overload bat in a hookup rep; builds forced rotation and rotational bat speed; produced a 110 mph rep
- **Big Poppy** (named, not shown in this session) - given as an example drill for building hip-shoulder separation

## Evidence cited
- Named athlete: John Rhodes, LA Dodgers minor leaguer, 110 mph exit velocity off a 50 oz bazooka bat rep

## Open questions
- The coach tells Rhodes not to preset/max his load and hold it through the stride, instead creating a small hinge worked through the stride; this runs against a common "hold your load" cue, and no data is given for why the smaller hinge transfers better.
- The video doesn't state how long the first training block lasted before this session, so the timeline behind the reported rotation change isn't verifiable from this source.
