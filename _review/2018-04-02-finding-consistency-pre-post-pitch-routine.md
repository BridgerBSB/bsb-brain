# Finding Consistency: Pre & Post-Pitch Routine

## TL;DR
- Teaches pitchers to build a fixed, repeatable pre-pitch routine (setup, breath, mantra, dry rep) as an anchor for the delivery, spending 5 to 10 minutes on it per session.
- Teaches a post-pitch routine that minimizes time off the mound so the pitcher isn't rushing against the roughly 20 to 25 second pitch clock.
- Teaches an "oh shit" reset routine using a fixed visual focal point to flush emotion after a bad play before getting back on the rubber.

## Claims
- [00:03](https://youtu.be/lpv80cCnNLs?t=3) A consistent, individualized pre-shot routine, borrowed from the NBA free-throw shooter model, increases the ability to repeat a motor pattern; it can serve as an anchor when mechanics get inconsistent, at any level from high school to the big leagues.
- [01:03](https://youtu.be/lpv80cCnNLs?t=63) Recommends spending about 5, at most 10, minutes on the pre-pitch routine using light dry reps; the setup on the rubber (foot position, body posture, picking up the target) should be identical every time.
- [02:05](https://youtu.be/lpv80cCnNLs?t=125) Recommends at least one deep breath after coming set, two is his own preference; the breath calms the nervous system and clears the mind.
- [03:07](https://youtu.be/lpv80cCnNLs?t=187) An optional one or two word mantra locks in mentality right before the dry rep; the dry throw itself scales from about 10 percent effort when not warmed up up to 50 percent effort when warmed up.
- [04:09](https://youtu.be/lpv80cCnNLs?t=249) On the post-pitch side, receive the ball near the front of the mound and get back on the rubber quickly, otherwise the pitcher is pushing close to the roughly 20 to 25 second pitch clock and has to rush.
- [05:11](https://youtu.be/lpv80cCnNLs?t=311) Recommends rehearsing situational variations (different holds, 1-2-3 looks with runners on) as mental reps, and spending 5 to 10 minutes per practice on this instead of running up pitch counts, since it doesn't add arm stress.
- [06:12](https://youtu.be/lpv80cCnNLs?t=372) The "oh shit" routine: after a bad play, step off the mound, turn away from the plate, pick a fixed focal point that exists in every park (top of the foul pole, corner of the scoreboard), take a deep breath and hold focus 5 to 10 seconds until the emotion is flushed, then return to the rubber.

## Cues
- **"Attack" / "Relax"** (transcribed "a tack," sic) - fixes loss of focus and nerves right before the pitch; for pitchers building a repeatable pre-pitch routine, any level

## Anti-cues
- none

## Drills
- **Pre-pitch dry-rep routine** - fixed rubber setup, one or two deep breaths, optional one or two word mantra, then a dry throw at roughly 10 to 50 percent effort depending on warmup; builds a repeatable delivery and release point; for pitchers whose mechanics drift under pressure
- **"Oh shit" reset routine** - step off the mound, turn back to the plate, fix eyes on a stable ballpark landmark, hold deep breaths for 5 to 10 seconds before returning to the rubber; builds emotional reset after a bad pitch or play; for pitchers who get rattled after failure
