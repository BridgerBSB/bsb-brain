# Advanced Pitching Mechanics: Pronation in the Delivery

## TL;DR
- Pronation is not an isolated wrist action; it is inexorably linked to the shoulder, and forearm layback (max external rotation plus supination) should unwind into internal rotation plus pronation, not be actively initiated late.
- Common breaking-ball teaching cues that manually turn the ball over cap the amount of spin and stress the elbow; Boddy argues for an axial (straight-to-target) arm path trained with wrist weights and overload/underload balls.
- Case study: Trevor Bauer remapped his arm path over 2 months in 2013 after a postural change wrecked his command, producing a curveball with visibly more depth on 420 fps video.

## Claims
- [01:02](https://youtu.be/5vo0SRBxjd0?t=62) Boddy owns Driveline Baseball, then based in Seattle/Puyallup, with "several hundred" past and present clients across high school, college, and pro levels.
- [02:05](https://youtu.be/5vo0SRBxjd0?t=125) Dr. Mike Marshall theorized roughly 30 years ago, from high-speed video alone, that pronation creates a gap in the elbow, engages the medial forearm to protect the UCL, and reduces olecranon/cartilage collisions; Boddy says peer-reviewed research now backs this.
- [04:07](https://youtu.be/5vo0SRBxjd0?t=247) The commonly taught curveball/slider cues that supinate through release limit how much rotation and break a pitcher can generate and are unhealthy for the elbow.
- [07:11](https://youtu.be/5vo0SRBxjd0?t=431) Bauer's mid-2013 shift from an upright, Lincecum-style posture to a more classic posture changed how he applied force to the ball and disrupted his command, despite command never being a prior issue for him.
- [09:14](https://youtu.be/5vo0SRBxjd0?t=554) Over roughly 2 months, Bauer remapped his arm path using wrist weights and overload/underload implements; his curveball gained significantly more depth, visible in 420 fps footage.
- [17:21](https://youtu.be/5vo0SRBxjd0?t=1041) Cites research out of the San Diego motion analysis lab (Dr. Arnel(sic) Aguinaldo) showing elbow flexion at release correlates with reduced elbow valgus stress, and early trunk rotation onset in sidearm-type deliveries correlates with increased valgus stress.
- [18:22](https://youtu.be/5vo0SRBxjd0?t=1102) Driveline runs 11 high-speed cameras and had just purchased an EMG kit to measure muscle firing timing rather than biomechanics alone.

## Cues
- **"driving the hand over the elbow"** - fixes disconnected pronation/internal rotation timing during forearm layback; drilled with wrist weights; cue credited to Chris Holtz
- **"driving the back of the hand to the plate"** - fixes early or passive pronation that limits curveball rotation; for pitchers building an axial (straight-line) arm path to the target

## Anti-cues
- **"turning the doorknob"** / **"pulling a lampshade down"** / **"karate chopping the ball"** - taught to fix curveball/slider spin at release; actually caps the amount of rotation and break achievable and adds stress to the elbow; per Kyle Boddy (Driveline)

## Drills
- **Wrist-weight pronation drill** - 10 lb wrist weights on the throwing hand; builds the hand-driving-over-the-elbow feel for pronation into internal rotation; cue from Chris Holtz
- **Overload/underload plyo ball throws** - two- and four-"PB" balls (unit unclear, auto-caption, sic); builds glove separation, scapular separation, and arm-path remapping; used in Bauer's 2-month curveball remap

## Evidence cited
- [02:05](https://youtu.be/5vo0SRBxjd0?t=125) Dr. Mike Marshall's elbow-gap/UCL-protection pronation theory, proposed from high-speed video roughly 30 years before this video; Boddy says it is now supported by peer-reviewed research (studies not named).
- [17:21](https://youtu.be/5vo0SRBxjd0?t=1041) Dr. Arnel(sic) Aguinaldo / San Diego motion analysis lab: elbow flexion at release vs. reduced elbow valgus stress; trunk rotation timing vs. valgus stress in sidearm-type deliveries.
- [09:14](https://youtu.be/5vo0SRBxjd0?t=554) Trevor Bauer, named athlete example: 2-month arm-path remap, curveball depth compared via 420 fps video before/after.

## Open questions
- Boddy argues pronation should unwind passively from max external rotation/supination rather than be actively initiated, which he frames as a refinement of Dr. Marshall's model, but no study is cited that directly compares active vs. this "unwinding" pattern for injury or performance outcomes.
- Boddy states passive pronation ("it's natural, everyone does it after release") is also incorrect, without giving a number or study for what the correct balance of forearm strength and timing should look like beyond wrist-weight and overload/underload training.
