# A Look Into One of the World's Hardest Throwers : Part 2

## TL;DR
- Tread breaks down Nate Pearson's mechanics from video, without coaching him: stacked linear move, rotational hinge, compact arm action, intent timed late, trunk extension as a byproduct.
- Intent should build from the lower half and be added to the arm late; telling a thrower to just throw with intent or to reach out front disrupts sequencing.
- Pearson's frame and genetics matter: the author guesses the same mechanics on a smaller, less powerful athlete top out near 92 mph instead of 104.

## Claims
- **1. Staying Stacked during the Linear Move:** Pearson stays stacked and centered over his pelvis and back heel in the initial weight shift. Tipping the torso toward third base and leaking over the toes is a common fault.
- **1. Staying Stacked during the Linear Move:** He loads a rotational hinge (pelvis coiled over the rear femur into hip IR), holds the closed pelvis as long as possible down the slope, and releases it just before FFS. Hips, torso and arm unload in the same plane of rotation.
- **No vertical shin?:** No vertical shin appears in his forward move; he shows an internal rotation dominant back leg. The author says the preference may depend on hip anatomy and trial and error.
- **2. Compact Arm Action:** Taller, hard-throwing athletes often benefit from a more compact arm action. For very long wingspans, keeping the arm nearer 90 degrees in the swing helps repeat the release point and reach the plane of rotation.
- **2. Compact Arm Action:** Compact does not mean tensing or yanking the hand to the ear. Pearson allows scapular retraction behind the body and lets the arm float into position. Tread tests changes and keeps them only if split-testing trends positive and there is off-season time.
- **3. Properly Timed Intent:** Pearson throws at a true 100% but applies intent so it does not break the flow of energy from the ground. The author calls this "rotational violence".
- **3. Properly Timed Intent:** The throw builds from the lower half, the upper half stays relaxed through the linear move, then the pec stretch at FFS is followed by active contraction (front side obliques, throwing arm pec and lat).
- **4. Trunk Extension:** Extension is a byproduct of a well sequenced load and unload and of pelvis-torso dissociation, not of reaching with the arm. Bucket and towel reach drills fake "getting out front".
- **4. Trunk Extension:** Longer-armed throwers (Caminero named) often show more trunk extension at release. The author says he has nothing concrete to back this, only a correlation.
- **5. Genetics:** Throwing 104 mph takes frame, power output and mobility as well as mechanics. "If you throw like this guy you will throw 100 mph" is not true.

## Cues
- **"let the throw build"** - fixes the arm being added early and muscling up the throw; for hard throwers generally
- **"rotating down into landing"** (also "landing from above") - fixes spinning the pelvis open into landing; for throwers whose pelvis opens off the plane of the shoulders

## Anti-cues
- **"get on top of the ball" / "get out front"** - taught to fix lack of extension or getting out front; actually causes the front side to yank open and the athlete to reach and create extension artificially; per Ben Brewster (Tread)
- **"throw with intent"** (said bare) - taught to fix low effort; actually causes the arm to be added to the throw prematurely and ruins sequencing, "muscling up"; per Ben Brewster (Tread)

## Evidence cited
- Pearson: 99 mph over 5 scoreless innings in his MLB debut, 104 mph career high; 6'6", 245 lb. Observed from video only, Tread does not coach him.
- Part one case study of his power metrics at the College of Central Florida (by Graham Lehman, not reproduced here).
- Arquimedes Caminero cited as an example of long-armed extension.

## Open questions
- The vertical shin is called a common teach, and Pearson lacks it, but the source offers no data on whether it helps or hurts other throwers; it only ties the preference to hip anatomy.
- The extension-by-limb-length link is an admitted hunch with no evidence.
