# Are all novices the same?

## TL;DR
- Novices, whatever their training background, recover from workout stress within 48 hours, so nearly all of them can run the same basic program.
- Driveline puts novice trainees on a program modeled on Rippetoe's Starting Strength, with baseball-specific swaps like dumbbell bench press for barbell bench press.
- The philosophy: don't over-individualize a novice's programming; the compound lifts drive rapid gains and wasting that window is a mistake.

## Claims
- Are all novices the same? - training theory splits trainees into four levels (Novice, Intermediate, Advanced, Elite) based on how fast they recover from and adapt to training stress.
- Are all novices the same? - a novice can recover from a workout within 48 hours, which is why novice programming can be basic and progress workout to workout.
- Are all novices the same? - all Driveline novices follow a program similar to Rippetoe's Starting Strength, adapted for baseball (example given: dumbbell bench press substituted for barbell bench press).
- Are all novices the same? - all trainees perform some variant of squat, overhead press, bench press, deadlift, row, and chin-ups/pull-ups.
- Are all novices the same? - most pitchers use the front squat instead of the back squat because it is easier on the shoulders.
- Are all novices the same? - all trainees also perform a Rate of Force Development exercise, such as speed squats, deadlifts, power cleans, power snatches, medball cleans, or medball slams.
- Are all novices the same? - individual concessions (sport-specific or anatomical, e.g. front squat over back squat) are allowed, but programming should stay centered on compound lifts.
