# The Difference Between "Working Out" and Training

## TL;DR
- Driveline draws a line between "working out" (casual, social, unstructured) and "training" (goal-directed, programmed, progress-tracked).
- Training requires accepting failure as routine: missed reps, deload weeks, bad starts, or striking out six times in a week of games.
- The post gives no cues, drills, or data; it is a mission-statement piece defining the org's identity around training versus working out.

## Claims
- **"The Difference Between 'Working Out' and Training"** - working out is defined as the casual gym-goer pattern: benching the same weight for three sets of 5-10 reps, plus leg extensions, lat pulldowns, and bicep curls, about three times a week.
- **"The Difference Between 'Working Out' and Training"** - training is defined as a short-term, medium-term, and long-term goal plan combining nutrition and physical exertion with incremental progress markers.
- **"The Difference Between 'Working Out' and Training"** - training necessarily includes failure: missing a rep, needing a full deload week, getting shelled in a scheduled start, or striking out six times across a week of tournament games.
