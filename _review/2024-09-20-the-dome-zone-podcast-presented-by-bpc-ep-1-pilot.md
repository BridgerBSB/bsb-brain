# THE DOME ZONE PODCAST: Presented by BPC EP#1- Pilot

## TL;DR
- BPC's founders trace their growth from a converted oil garage with one tee and one net to a full facility, crediting three early decisions: a free first-day trial, an individual-development-first model instead of team-first training, and an honest, no-hype coaching culture.
- They frame development as a multi-year process starting around 7th grade, and claim every athlete who trained there from 7th grade through high school graduation has gone on to play college baseball, most at Division I.
- For coordinating with high school coaches they use a "pilot vs. mechanic" split (coach runs the team, BPC handles mechanical/physical development) and cite two-plus local players drafted every year since the facility opened.

## Claims
- [02:23](https://youtu.be/DpGxwKPhflk?t=143) Before founding BPC, the co-founders paid $40/day to rent a cage in the offseason; the lack of a South Jersey training facility drove them to convert co-founder Mike Adams' family oil garage into their first space.
- [04:25](https://youtu.be/DpGxwKPhflk?t=265) Their early credibility came from one of their first clients, a left-handed pitcher (Dan Noonan), getting drafted about two years after he started training there.
- [12:37](https://youtu.be/DpGxwKPhflk?t=757) They credit three early practices for growth: a free first-day trial for every new client, building the model around individual player development rather than team-based training, and a culture of telling athletes the truth about their level rather than praising them.
- [20:47](https://youtu.be/DpGxwKPhflk?t=1247) They state South Jersey has produced two-plus draft picks every year since BPC started.
- [28:56](https://youtu.be/DpGxwKPhflk?t=1736) Stated philosophy: they take players as young as 7th grade and work on a roughly six-year horizon toward college or pro readiness, deprioritizing short-term results.
- [29:57](https://youtu.be/DpGxwKPhflk?t=1797) They claim every athlete who has gone through the program from 7th grade through high school graduation has played college baseball, and most at the Division I level.
- [39:05](https://youtu.be/DpGxwKPhflk?t=2345) For their travel teams, player development takes priority over winning: no situational bunting or pulling a struggling pitcher just to win, on the belief that better individual players eventually make better teams.
- [51:19](https://youtu.be/DpGxwKPhflk?t=3079) They use a "pilot vs. mechanic" framing with high school coaches: the coach runs games and lineups (pilot) while BPC handles mechanical and physical development (mechanic), which they say New Jersey rules permit them to do concurrently with the school season.
- [58:33](https://youtu.be/DpGxwKPhflk?t=3513) Their coaching approach changed over time: early on, hitters had to perform a "perfect dry swing" before being allowed to hit off a tee, which produced good-looking swings that didn't always produce results; they moved toward testing hitters against live velocity daily instead.
- [1:03:49](https://youtu.be/DpGxwKPhflk?t=3829) The new facility added in-house PT and strength staff, which they say lets them catch and manage arm soreness in days rather than defaulting to generic multi-week rest prescribed by outside doctors.
