# Weight Gain Shake - I Drank This Every Night for 4 Years

## TL;DR
- A meal-replacement shake the author drank nightly for about 4 years to close the gap between whole-food meals and a caloric surplus target, part of a 160 lb to 215 lb gain by the end of his freshman year of college.
- The same base ingredients (milk, whey protein, a carb source, a fat source, spinach, cinnamon) scale from 250 to 1,100+ calories just by adjusting quantities, so the same recipe works for gain, maintenance, or a cut.
- Behavioral tip for hitting caloric targets during a bulk: don't let yourself sleep until the shake is finished.

## Claims
- [00:10](https://youtu.be/qtXjHhIDa5A?t=10) The author drank a version of this shake nightly for about 4 years while going from 160 lb to roughly 215 lb by the end of his freshman year of college.
- [00:10](https://youtu.be/qtXjHhIDa5A?t=10) The same recipe can be adjusted for weight gain, maintenance, or fat loss by changing ingredient quantities, not ingredients.
- [00:10](https://youtu.be/qtXjHhIDa5A?t=10) Base recipe: 1-2 cups milk (fat-free for lower calories, whole or chocolate for higher), 1-2 scoops whey protein (chocolate Optimum Nutrition preferred), a carb source (berries, oats, or frozen banana), and a fat source (peanut butter or another nut butter, with optional light-tasting olive oil).
- [01:11](https://youtu.be/qtXjHhIDa5A?t=71) A handful of frozen spinach is added for micronutrients (author states the taste isn't noticeable), plus cinnamon for flavor and additional micronutrients.
- Description: Three demonstrated variants: low calorie (250 cal), moderate (650 cal), and high (1,100 cal); the author says he has made versions exceeding 1,500 calories on days he fell behind on his caloric target.
- [02:11](https://youtu.be/qtXjHhIDa5A?t=131) Tip for a weight-gain phase: don't let yourself go to sleep until the shake is finished, as a way to guarantee hitting the day's caloric surplus; the author calls this "probably not the best strategy."
