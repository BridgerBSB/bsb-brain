# Progress: How to Measure It

## TL;DR
- Progress is measured against the competition you will face next, not the one you're beating now; dominating your current level is not the goal.
- Uses Wade LeBlanc (about 150 innings across High-A and AA, almost 200 in AAA) to argue his gaudy minor league numbers were built on an advanced changeup and didn't predict big-league readiness until he was forced to command his fastball.
- Sets a concrete bar for pitchers: you aren't ready to move up until you can throw a complete-game shutout using fastballs alone.

## Claims
- Progress: How to Measure It: true competitors aren't satisfied with dominating their current level of competition, even after a hot stretch (cites a hypothetical .400 average with home runs, or two strikeouts per inning over 20 games).
- Progress: How to Measure It: quoting Paul DePodesta on Wade LeBlanc, he pitched about 150 innings between High-A and AA and almost 200 innings in AAA before reaching the majors.
- Progress: How to Measure It: DePodesta argues that leaving LeBlanc at AA would have let him keep getting hitters out on his changeup alone, without being forced to command his fastball.
- Progress: How to Measure It: LeBlanc's solid minor league numbers were a mirage; major league hitters lay off changeups and look for below-average fastballs to drive, which is what separates the levels.
- Progress: How to Measure It: scouts evaluating a high school sophomore want to see fastball velocity and command, not a good curveball fooling weak high school hitters; the author lists cutting, running, sinking, and locating the fastball as the specific things scouts look for.
- Progress: How to Measure It: the author's stated bar for his own pitchers is throwing a complete-game shutout using only fastballs before they're ready to move to the next level.
