# Elite Velo Isn’t Enough—Here’s What You’re Missing

## TL;DR
- Scouts now weight a complete arsenal and in-zone whiffs over pure velocity; the 13th-fastest pitch at a recent spring training was already 103 mph.
- Command problems are usually pitch-selection decisions in the wrong count, not a permanent mechanical ceiling.
- A good arsenal assigns each pitch its own shape and role rather than maxing movement on every pitch.

## Claims
- [00:00](https://youtu.be/mpmMAMKsQnc?t=0) Predicts pitchers will start changing arm slots specifically to attack holes in a hitter's swing plane, and expects this trend to come from Japan first.
- [01:01](https://youtu.be/mpmMAMKsQnc?t=61) Points to Nestor Cortez as an existing example of changing slots and timing to work against hitters' expectations.
- [01:01](https://youtu.be/mpmMAMKsQnc?t=61) References Kyle Boddy's earlier prediction that pitchers would eventually throw 105 mph, and notes that at a recent spring training the 13th-fastest pitch was already 103 mph, meaning 12+ pitches exceeded it.
- [02:04](https://youtu.be/mpmMAMKsQnc?t=124) Argues lowering or moving back the mound could make things worse for hitters, since fastball ride would lose its downhill plane and pitchers would gain more time to create movement.
- [02:04](https://youtu.be/mpmMAMKsQnc?t=124) Discusses a Mississippi State switch pitcher drafted by Seattle: right-handed stuff was "electric," but left-handed opponents hit .310 off him.
- [03:04](https://youtu.be/mpmMAMKsQnc?t=184) Scouting checklist for a young arm: elite athleticism, a clean/efficient mover (tied to durability), velocity, and swing-and-miss in the strike zone as the best predictor of stuff quality, better than chase-driven whiffs.
- [04:07](https://youtu.be/mpmMAMKsQnc?t=247) Additional scouting priorities: strike percentage, race to two strikes, getting ahead early; says stuff+ matters less than having pitches that each fill a distinct role (tunneling, platoon coverage) rather than five "plus" pitches.
- [05:07](https://youtu.be/mpmMAMKsQnc?t=307) Argues most command problems are decision-making problems: throwing the wrong pitch type in a given count, e.g. a four-seam/splitter pitcher throwing the splitter in a 2-0 count and drawing too many takes.
- [06:08](https://youtu.be/mpmMAMKsQnc?t=368) Contrasts pitchers who chase maximum movement on every pitch with "KC," who has a fastball, a hard cutter he can throw for strikes in any count, a put-away curveball, and a slider, each built for a specific situation.
- [07:08](https://youtu.be/mpmMAMKsQnc?t=428) Targeting strategy: a pitcher who consistently misses arm-side should aim glove-side so the miss comes back over the plate, and the reverse for pull-side misses.
