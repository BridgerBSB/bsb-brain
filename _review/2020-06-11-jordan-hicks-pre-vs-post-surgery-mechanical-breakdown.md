# Jordan Hicks: Pre vs Post Surgery Mechanical Breakdown

## TL;DR
- Brewster compares Jordan Hicks's post-Tommy-John rehab bullpen to his pre-surgery mechanics (103-104 mph) and traces the differences to one root fault: an early, quad-dominant load into the back leg.
- That root fault chains forward: pelvis unloads gradually instead of late and explosively, elbow climbs above the shoulder line at release, torso stays upright and forward-flexes instead of rotating, lead knee fails to stabilize.
- Brewster's stated fix is one cue (hold weight over the heel longer) plus more full-intensity throwing, and his broader claim is that these post-injury compensations are common at every level and must be caught during rehab, before they solidify.

## Claims
- [01:00](https://youtu.be/DnezZtFTKY8?t=60) Post-surgery, Hicks's back knee sits significantly over his toe at load versus not over the toe pre-surgery, a shift from a glute/hip-dominant load (box squat pattern, weight centered on the heel) to a quad-dominant one (front squat pattern).
- [02:00](https://youtu.be/DnezZtFTKY8?t=120) Post-surgery the pelvis "leaks" down gradually into landing instead of holding and unloading late and explosively, which Brewster attributes to losing the heel-centered load.
- [04:00](https://youtu.be/DnezZtFTKY8?t=240) Pre-surgery, ball, elbow, and shoulders line up at release (elbow straight, wrist neutral, through the axis of rotation); post-surgery the elbow visibly climbs above the shoulder line, which Brewster calls elbow climb and links to getting linear and pushy, something he says is very common in Tommy John rehab.
- [05:02](https://youtu.be/DnezZtFTKY8?t=302) Post-surgery the torso is nearly fully upright at release versus extended and catapulting over the front leg pre-surgery, which Brewster says leaves few options but a linear finish.
- [06:02](https://youtu.be/DnezZtFTKY8?t=362) Post-surgery follow-through shows the back knee shooting forward and the trunk forward-flexing (rectus abdominis-driven) with the arm pushing downhill, versus pre-surgery where the back foot stays down and the torso keeps rotating about its axis into a spiral arm path.
- [08:05](https://youtu.be/DnezZtFTKY8?t=485) Post-surgery the lead knee supinates and shoots laterally toward first base at landing (poor transverse-plane stabilization) versus a stable lead-leg block pre-surgery.
- [09:07](https://youtu.be/DnezZtFTKY8?t=547) Brewster states these post-injury mechanical compensations are very common across high school, college, and pro pitchers, and get harder to correct after thousands of reps, so they need to be hunted during rehab, not after clearance.
- Description: Brewster writes he believes 99% of Hicks's issues would clean up if he kept his weight over the heel a tad longer and threw at full game intensity more regularly.

## Cues
- **"keep that weight over the heel a tad longer"** - fixes an early quad-dominant back-leg load that cascades into elbow climb and a linear follow-through; for pitchers returning from major arm surgery/rehab
