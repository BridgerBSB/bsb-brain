# Drills to Fix Your Timing and Hinge | Swing Design

## TL;DR
- Eric Kozak works one-on-one with Kyler Meccage on a swing redesign built around loading into the back leg and hinging, so the pelvis leads rotation instead of the upper body spinning off early.
- The session runs a drill progression (step-back, step-behind, Kershaw, Big Poppy, handle-loaded bat) that isolates hinge, timing, separation, and barrel release in order.
- No numbers are given; the teaching is entirely qualitative, built on feel and external cues rather than measured targets.

## Claims
- [00:01](https://youtu.be/CxeP7Hikgks?t=1) Getting into the back leg and into the ground cleans up the hinge, creates more space, and sets up a better rotation.
- [01:07](https://youtu.be/CxeP7Hikgks?t=67) The step-back drill's goal is to sink and put force down through the back heel.
- [01:07](https://youtu.be/CxeP7Hikgks?t=67) When the pelvis rises early ("early extension"), the torso has no choice but to come up and spin off with it.
- [02:11](https://youtu.be/CxeP7Hikgks?t=131) Rotation is pelvis-led: wherever the pelvis goes, the torso follows, so generating rotation from the upper body first fights the ability to hold posture.
- [03:12](https://youtu.be/CxeP7Hikgks?t=192) If the pelvis doesn't open, the upper body has no clean way to open, and if it does, the upper half tends to pop up rather than rotate well.
- [04:13](https://youtu.be/CxeP7Hikgks?t=253) Starting the stride/load early is cued as "if you're gonna miss, miss early," since trying to control a long forward move causes late, panicked timing.
- [05:15](https://youtu.be/CxeP7Hikgks?t=315) Holding the ground with the back foot, rather than pushing off it, lets the pelvis do the rotating.
- [06:17](https://youtu.be/CxeP7Hikgks?t=377) The Kershaw drill builds a stable hinge: sink down, let the pelvis shift, then ride it out; going straight down and squatty is stable but loses the backside on the forward move.
- [10:22](https://youtu.be/CxeP7Hikgks?t=622) "Catch it deep and pull a homer" is framed as an external cue meant to increase separation, requiring the pelvis to open while the torso stays closed and side-bends.
- [12:22](https://youtu.be/CxeP7Hikgks?t=742) The handle-loaded bat (Redbat) is used because all the weight sits in the handle, so any early push or pull with the upper body causes the bat to take over and keep rotating on its own.

## Cues
- **"Keep your front foot down"** - fixes the front foot popping up during the sink into the back leg; for Meccage during the step-back drill
- **"Keep it [the pelvis] down as you turn"** - fixes early extension, where the pelvis rises up and toward the plate and pulls the torso up with it
- **"Try to hold the ground with that back foot"** - fixes pushing/lunging forward off the back hip instead of letting the pelvis rotate
- **"Pull your back hip away from home plate when you stride"** - fixes losing the hinge and rushing forward on the stride
- **"Catch it deep and pull a homer"** - external cue to increase separation and rotation by pulling the barrel deep and to the pull side
- **"Hold, hold, hold... and then just let go"** - cue for building and releasing the barrel whip off the handle-loaded bat

## Drills
- **Step-back (offset open)** - step back from an offset/open stance into the swing; builds sinking into the back leg and putting force into the ground to clean up the hinge; for Meccage early in the session
- **Step-behind** - stride steps behind the back foot instead of straight back; builds continuing to add force into the ground while starting the turn
- **Longer bat timing walk-through** - walk-through swings with a longer bat as an external constraint; builds starting the load earlier instead of rushing/panicking late
- **Kershaw** - start tall, sink/hinge down (named for Clayton Kershaw's leg lift), then ride the hinge out into rotation; builds a stable hinge position without losing the backside
- **Big Poppy** - set up closed with the lower body and open with the upper body (David Ortiz-style setup), weight kept in the back heel; builds separation and rotation timing
- **Redbat / handle-loaded bat pull-back-and-release** - weighted-handle bat where the hitter holds the upper body back against pelvis rotation, then releases; builds the barrel whip/lag release
