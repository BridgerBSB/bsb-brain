# In-Season Training: A Difficult Task

## TL;DR
- Argues that players with real college/pro aspirations should narrow to baseball so they get a true off-season, since multi-sport athletes never get a dedicated block for general strength work.
- Prescribes a split: high volume/high intensity lifting off-season, deload into skill work as the season nears, then one heavy low-volume lifting day per week during the season.
- Only concrete number given: a high schooler's in-season load is roughly 20 games plus playoffs, five team practices a week, plus weekend travel-team work, which is why heavy squat/deadlift work can't be recovered from more than once a week.

## Claims
- **In-Season Training: A Difficult Task** - multi-sport participation raises overall fitness but caps how great an athlete gets at any one sport because there's no offseason dedicated to general strength and conditioning.
- **In-Season Training: A Difficult Task** - players with a real shot at college or pro baseball should consider dropping other sports to get a clearly defined off-season program targeting SPARQ Baseball metrics, bat speed, fastball velocity, fat loss, or general strength.
- **In-Season Training: A Difficult Task** - off-season work should be high volume and high intensity, since there's enough time to recover from it.
- **In-Season Training: A Difficult Task** - as the competitive season approaches, athletes should deload and shift to skill work: grounders/fly balls, long toss, bullpens, mechanical changes for velocity/effort, and swing evaluation.
- **In-Season Training: A Difficult Task** - a high school in-season schedule (20 games plus playoffs, five practices a week, plus weekend travel-team practices or workouts) makes recovering from heavy squats or deadlifts within the week very difficult.
- **In-Season Training: A Difficult Task** - recommends finding one day per week to lift heavy (e.g. heavy triples in the squat and deadlift) to maintain off-season strength gains without hurting recovery.
- **In-Season Training: A Difficult Task** - sprint work and med ball throws are safe in-season goals because they pair well with conditioning the team is already doing and don't add much extra fatigue.
- **In-Season Training: A Difficult Task** - kettlebell swings are recommended in-season for developing rapid hip extension power and a conditioning stimulus that's easy to schedule.

## Drills
- **Heavy triples (squat/deadlift)** - one day per week, high intensity/low volume; maintains off-season strength gains without overtaxing recovery; for in-season high school players
- **Kettlebell swings** - explosive hip-hinge implement work; builds rapid hip extension power plus a conditioning stimulus; for in-season maintenance
- **Med ball throws** - implement-based power work alongside sprint training; builds power output without heavy recovery cost; for in-season conditioning

## Open questions
- Recommends single-sport specialization for elite-track youth players without citing developmental data; this runs against common LTAD guidance favoring continued multi-sport participation through high school.
- Prescribes exactly one heavy lifting day per week in-season as the right frequency, with no data or citation behind the number.
