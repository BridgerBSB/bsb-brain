# The Definitive Guide to Belt Squatting (without a machine)

## TL;DR
- Belt squat hangs the load from the pelvis instead of the spine, so athletes with low back pain or in-season fatigue can keep loading the lower body without compressive or shear spinal stress.
- Gives a DIY rig (loading pin, squat rack, hip belt) as a substitute for a $3,000 machine, with rep ranges for strength (4-6 reps at >75% 1RM), hypertrophy (8-12 reps at 60-75%), and power (2-3 reps at 50-70%).
- Argues coaches under-load injured athletes with bodyweight squats or light dumbbells when they could be progressively loading the belt squat instead.

## Claims
- How Belt Squats Work: hanging the weight from the pelvis bypasses compressive spinal loading; even healthy athletes can use it periodically for deload during high-stress mesocycles such as in-season.
- No Belt Squat Machine? No Problem: a 12" loading pin holds about 300 lb of plates; a 15" pin holds 405-450 lb.
- Set-Up Tips: 45 lb plates may require too wide a stance for shorter athletes, who should use 35 lb plates instead.
- Best Rep Ranges: Strength = 3-4 sets of 4-6 reps at >75% 1RM; Hypertrophy = 3-5 sets of 8-12 reps at 60-75% 1RM; Power = 4-8 sets of 2-3 reps at 50-70% 1RM.
- Best Rep Ranges (quick tip): treat submaximal warm-up sets on heavy strength days as power sets, accelerating the bar rather than coasting up to working weight.
- Biggest Low Back Injury Mistakes: once a medical professional clears an athlete for bodyweight lunges and squats, he should begin progressively loading the belt squat rather than staying on bodyweight-only work.

## Cues
- **"Sit back deep into the squat, driving your hips back and aiming to keep a more vertical shin-angle"** - fixes excess forward knee travel/knee stress; for lifters using the box-squat-style setup
- **"Drive explosively through the heels, aiming to accelerate the weight as fast as possible like you're trying to jump out of the bottom of the lift"** - fixes coasting through reps with no power output; for power-phase and submaximal warm-up sets
- **"Use as little assistance from your arms as possible"** - fixes pulling the rep up with the arms instead of the legs doing the work; for all belt squat lifters

## Drills
- **Belt Squat (DIY loading pin setup)** - weight hung from a hip belt via a loading pin, squat rack used as a stabilizing counterbalance, standing on boxes to clear the stack; builds lower body strength and power without spinal loading; for athletes with low back pain or those needing an in-season deload

## Open questions
- Claims the belt squat protects the spine and speeds return from low back injury, but cites no study or outcome data, only reasoning from load placement.
- States bodyweight squats and light-dumbbell goblet squats "will not help you throw harder" without giving any comparison numbers.
