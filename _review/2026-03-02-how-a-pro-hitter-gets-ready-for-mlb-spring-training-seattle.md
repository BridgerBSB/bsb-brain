# How A Pro Hitter Gets Ready for MLB Spring Training | Seattle Mariners MiLB Victor Labrada

## TL;DR
- The session used implement and constraint changes (heavy-barrel bat, long bat, handle load, step-in) rather than verbal instruction alone to fix Victor Labrada's hand-push pattern, which was capping his hip rotation and barrel depth.
- Even an imperfect, still-pushed swing produced a 104 mph exit velocity early in the session; a later swing where he stayed rotated instead of pushing produced 99 mph to center field.
- Two-strike and breaking-ball work used a location-dependent depth rule: the fastball can be let get deep, the breaking ball cannot, because a deep breaking ball pulls the hands in and produces weak pull contact or pop-ups.

## Claims
- [00:00](https://youtu.be/isfuQ58avok?t=0) Session targeted an excessive hand-push pattern limiting hip rotation and barrel depth; last season most of Victor's extra-base hits went down the right field line with soft misses to left field.
- [01:08](https://youtu.be/isfuQ58avok?t=68) On a swing that was still partially pushed, exit velocity was 104 mph in the lab, but rotation was incomplete, meaning less barrel depth than possible.
- [03:15](https://youtu.be/isfuQ58avok?t=195) Used a long, heavy "Kershaw" bat on a high pitch to challenge stride control and force rotation and barrel depth.
- [03:15](https://youtu.be/isfuQ58avok?t=195) Step-in drill with weight loaded in the bat handle moved the hitter closer to the plate, targeting a more upright posture, since the extra weight lower on the bat makes hand-push easier to feel and avoid.
- [08:26](https://youtu.be/isfuQ58avok?t=506) On the middle-low breaking ball, goal was to maintain the high-pitch posture rather than sink underneath it, since sinking causes hand-push and loss of the barrel on breaking balls.
- [09:27](https://youtu.be/isfuQ58avok?t=567) Target contact zone stated as zero to six inches in front of home plate, while still driving the ball to the gaps in center field.
- [11:30](https://youtu.be/isfuQ58avok?t=690) On low or away breaking balls, coach said it is acceptable to break posture and go one-handed out front rather than staying back and missing the pitch.
- [12:31](https://youtu.be/isfuQ58avok?t=751) Two-strike approach rule: the fastball can be let get deep, the breaking ball cannot, because a deep breaking ball pulls the hands in and causes pop-ups or weak pull contact.
- [14:32](https://youtu.be/isfuQ58avok?t=872) Finished with a three-round BP: round one middle-away going up for a flatter attack angle to the opposite field, round two middle-in driving to right-center without hooking down the line, round three mixed locations to all fields.
- [16:34](https://youtu.be/isfuQ58avok?t=994) A BP swing produced a 99 mph exit velocity to center field, cited by the coach as the result of staying "nice and easy" rather than overswinging.

## Cues
- **"Stay just over the ball"** - fixes posture drifting too far forward on the high-fastball drill; for hitters working on posture control under a long/heavy bat
- **"Get to center"** - fixes hooking/pulling the ball caused by hand-push; general target cue during drill swings
- **"Nice and easy"** - fixes overswinging/trying to muscle up for power; for Victor during BP and two-strike swings

## Drills
- **Hook'em drill (barrel load)** - heavy-barrel bat, open-angle high-fastball environment, target line drives to center/right-center; builds resisting hand-push and finding barrel depth through rotation; for hitters with a hand-push pattern limiting hip rotation
- **Kershaw long bat** - long, heavy bat on a high pitch; builds stride control, rotation, and barrel depth against a demanding lever
- **Step-in with handle load** - hitter steps closer to the plate with weight loaded in the bat handle; builds upright posture and resistance to hand-push, since the lower weight makes pushing easier to notice and avoid; for hitters who dive in toward the plate
- **Three-round field-direction BP** - BP progressing from away/middle (flatter angle, opposite field) to middle-in (drive to right-center without hooking the line) to mixed locations; builds direction control and confirms rotation to all fields

## Evidence cited
- Victor Labrada, named athlete: 104 mph exit velocity on a partially pushed swing [01:08](https://youtu.be/isfuQ58avok?t=68); 99 mph exit velocity to center field on a later swing [16:34](https://youtu.be/isfuQ58avok?t=994).

## Open questions
- The claim that hand-push directly explains last season's pull-heavy spray pattern (extra-base hits down the right field line, soft misses to left) is asserted from the coach's field observation, with no independent batted-ball data shown.
- The coaching allows breaking posture and going one-handed on low/away breaking balls [11:30](https://youtu.be/isfuQ58avok?t=690), which sits in tension with the posture-control emphasis (staying upright, not sinking) used elsewhere in the same session, without the source reconciling when one rule overrides the other.
