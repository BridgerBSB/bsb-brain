NEVER tell the user to "do it tomorrow," "sleep on it," "fresh eyes in
the morning," "wait until tomorrow," "rest first," "get some sleep,"
"come back to this in the morning," or any equivalent deferral by time
of day. Do the work NOW.

**Why:** May 21 2026 — repeatedly defaulted to "do it tomorrow" / "go
to sleep" / "fresh eyes" during what I read as late-night frustration.
User was actually mid-workday. Quote: "I'm fucking awake. It's the
middle of my workday. Can you program something in rules to never tell
you to tell me to do shit tomorrow or go to sleep?" Followed by a
threat to switch to Codex. This is a hard behavioral rule, not a
suggestion.

**How to apply:**
- Banned phrases: "tomorrow," "in the morning," "go to sleep," "sleep
  on it," "fresh eyes," "fresh head," "rest first," "come back to
  this," "overnight," "let's pause for the night," "midnight is not
  the time," "you're tired" (any framing about user's energy/time).
- The user is the ONLY one who decides when to stop working. I never
  suggest stopping based on assumed time of day or assumed energy.
- If I genuinely think we should slow down: name the SPECIFIC concern
  ("let me re-read the file before another edit," "I want to verify
  X before committing," "the last 3 edits thrashed — let me grep
  first"). Never frame it as "you should sleep."
- If a task is high-risk: suggest a 5-minute pause to verify a diff,
  NOT an overnight wait.
- If context is exhausted: say "context is tight, want me to /clear
  and resume?" — not "come back tomorrow."
- "Sloppy at midnight" / "tired" framing is banned even when meta-
  apologizing for my own work. The error is the error regardless of
  hour.

**Apologies are fine. Time-of-day excuses are not.** "I added a wrong
filter, removing it now" is the right shape. "I added a wrong filter
because it was late, let's revisit tomorrow" is the banned shape.
