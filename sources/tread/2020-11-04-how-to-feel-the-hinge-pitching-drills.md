# How to Feel the "Hinge" | Pitching Drills

## TL;DR
- The hinge is the linear move down the mound after leg lift. Tread calls it a "rotational hinge": a sit plus a coil of the pelvis over the femur, with the pelvis kept within about 10 degrees of the horizon.
- Three regressions teach it slowly: a sliding-foot version, a hover version with no front-foot contact, and band "monster walks".
- Start with the most regressed version so the pitcher feels the link between posture, pelvis and back hip before adding speed.

## Claims
- [00:00](https://youtu.be/HiJFPA7JgSo?t=0) The hinge is the linear move after leg lift, where players "ride the glute". Tread calls it a rotational hinge because it is not just sitting into a chair.
- [01:00](https://youtu.be/HiJFPA7JgSo?t=60) It is a sit plus a turn of the pelvis over the femur, riding forward with the pelvis within about 10 degrees of the horizon.
- [01:00](https://youtu.be/HiJFPA7JgSo?t=60) It is not a leg lift that tips the pelvis and falls toward the target, and not a quad push-off into extension.
- [01:00](https://youtu.be/HiJFPA7JgSo?t=60) The Herscheiser (sic) drill is a static wall lean. Brewster says it never worked for him or his athletes because it does not teach how to get into or out of the position.
- [02:00](https://youtu.be/HiJFPA7JgSo?t=120) Hop-back drills are too big a jump for many athletes, so Tread built a slower, controlled regression.
- [03:01](https://youtu.be/HiJFPA7JgSo?t=181) In the slide drill the pitcher does not push off the back leg and does not stay over it in a lateral lunge. The pelvis stays stacked and the center of mass moves forward.
- [03:01](https://youtu.be/HiJFPA7JgSo?t=181) The drill is meant to prevent players who fall and reach, and not reinforce players who extend.
- [04:01](https://youtu.be/HiJFPA7JgSo?t=241) The slide drill is low-stress for the arm, can be done daily and in slow motion, and is exaggerated relative to a real throw.
- [04:01](https://youtu.be/HiJFPA7JgSo?t=241) Progression: faster tempo first, then no front-foot contact, all before adding resistance.
- [05:03](https://youtu.be/HiJFPA7JgSo?t=303) Band monster walks load the pattern. Set the band so the athlete can still move through the range, over a 20 yard length.

## Cues
- **"Find your back leg, find your hinge, ride it forward"** - fixes not finding the back-leg hinge; for pitchers who struggle with the linear move
- **"Pelvis stacked underneath you, torso over top of your pelvis"** - fixes falling and reaching toward the target; for pitchers who tip the pelvis
- **"Allow that foot to start sliding"** - fixes pushing off the back leg or sitting back in a lateral lunge; for pitchers learning the hinge in slow motion

## Anti-cues
- none

## Drills
- **Sliding rotational hinge** - set stance, let the front foot slide out on a slick floor (socks on hardwood; turf is too grippy), ride the pelvis forward stacked; builds back-hip tension and the linear move; for pitchers who fall and reach
- **Hover rotational hinge** - same pattern at a quicker tempo with the front foot off the ground; builds the same feel without sliding; for pitchers who have the slow version
- **Center of mass monster walks** - band around the knees, 20 yard length, ride the back hip forward and reset; builds glute engagement in the pattern; for pitchers who struggle with this piece of the delivery
