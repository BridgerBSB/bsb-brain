# Overlooked Concepts in Shoulder Pain for Pitchers | #AsktheDoc Episode 5

## TL;DR
- Dr. Nevin Markel says most thrower shoulder pain comes from overuse or misuse, and misuse usually means the rest of the kinetic chain is not doing its job, so the shoulder absorbs the force.
- A mobile shoulder needs stability. When the ball loses centration in the socket, the rotator cuff tendons, capsule and labrum get overloaded.
- Workload counts alone do not explain injury. A pitcher with better body organization may tolerate far more throws, though no number is given.

## Claims
- [01:01](https://youtu.be/rlsbKyE3Xns?t=61) Almost all shoulder pain in throwers is overuse or misuse. Misuse is a bad allocation of forces that adds up at the joint.
- [01:01](https://youtu.be/rlsbKyE3Xns?t=61) Compression and friction on tendons, ligaments and bursa irritate the shoulder, especially with high throw volume.
- [01:01](https://youtu.be/rlsbKyE3Xns?t=61) Function should be judged from the ground up (ankle, knee, hip, pelvis, spine, scapula, shoulder) because force travels through the whole chain.
- [02:03](https://youtu.be/rlsbKyE3Xns?t=123) Mobility needs stability. The shoulder is the most versatile and most unstable joint, and even high-level throwers can hide instability.
- [02:03](https://youtu.be/rlsbKyE3Xns?t=123) Centration is the ball sitting centered in the socket. Losing it, for example with a forward-rotated shoulder or anterior scapular plane, is a precursor to pain. Markel says the body usually creates this because of lacking function at other joints.
- [03:03](https://youtu.be/rlsbKyE3Xns?t=183) Throwing for a long time without centration risks overloading rotator cuff tendons, capsule and labrum. Improving joint position usually changes pain and function.
- [04:04](https://youtu.be/rlsbKyE3Xns?t=244) Muscles have a stabilizing role as well as a push-pull role. When the pec fires, the rotator cuff and deep stabilizers keep the shoulder centrated so it can rotate through usable range.
- [05:04](https://youtu.be/rlsbKyE3Xns?t=304) A pec that is overtoned from pressing without rowing pulls the shoulder into an internally rotated, anteriorly tilted position.
- [05:04](https://youtu.be/rlsbKyE3Xns?t=304) Missing ankle flexion, hip or pelvic rotation, thoracic extension or scapular control means the athlete still gets the ball out on time, but the shoulder takes up the force.
- [06:05](https://youtu.be/rlsbKyE3Xns?t=365) Workload is over-blamed. Mechanical efficiency, joint mobility and tissue quality are part of the answer, not just cueing.
