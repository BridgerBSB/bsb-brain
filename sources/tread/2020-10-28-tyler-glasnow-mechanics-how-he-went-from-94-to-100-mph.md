# Tyler Glasnow Mechanics | How He Went From 94 to 100+ mph

## TL;DR
- Brewster attributes Glasnow's gain to the lower half: a stacked, level pelvis in the leg lift, then a held hinge that rides the back glute instead of tipping and falling at the target.
- Four-seam average went from 93.9 to 97.6 mph (over 3 mph) with similar pitch usage.
- A longer arm swing is not a flaw if it syncs with the lower half. "Shorter is always better" is called a fad.

## Claims
- [01:01](https://youtu.be/fPXBDaavfUA?t=61) K/9 went from 9.3 (2016) to 14.3 (2020); BB/9 from 5.0 (2016) to 2.1 (2019) and 3.5 (2020). In 2018 he walked 5.5 per 9 with the Pirates and 3.1 with the Rays.
- [03:01](https://youtu.be/fPXBDaavfUA?t=181) Four-seam average rose from 93.9 to 97.6 mph. Changeup up a couple mph, curveball up over 3. Pitch usage about the same.
- [04:02](https://youtu.be/fPXBDaavfUA?t=242) Leg lift: 2020 Glasnow drifts slightly and stays stacked over a level pelvis. 2016 Glasnow tips the pelvis first, "the first domino" that is hard to recover from.
- [06:03](https://youtu.be/fPXBDaavfUA?t=363) Linear move is called the biggest before/after difference. He loads the back glute as he comes down out of the lift, holds hip and knee flexion, rides forward without pushing off, and keeps the head back.
- [08:06](https://youtu.be/fPXBDaavfUA?t=486) Tipping the pelvis uses up back-hip abduction range (about 25-30 degrees in the demo), so the 2016 version falls at the target. This is what "not using his legs" means.
- [11:07](https://youtu.be/fPXBDaavfUA?t=667) A "greater than" lateral angle in ground reaction force comes from holding the drive. An upright, mostly vertical force is a symptom of an earlier fault.
- [12:08](https://youtu.be/fPXBDaavfUA?t=728) Back foot: 2020 holds firm on the inside of the heel, then flips from inversion to eversion as the pelvis unwinds. 2016 pivots up and over.
- [14:09](https://youtu.be/fPXBDaavfUA?t=849) Glasnow's stated goal is to "get rid of all thought" and be athletic. Brewster says a subconscious delivery beats an internal focus on arm, foot and hips.
- [19:13](https://youtu.be/fPXBDaavfUA?t=1153) Glasnow says quad-dominant lifting kept him falling forward; he added heavy deadlifts, hip single-leg work and hip mobility. Brewster says balance posterior chain against quad work for the mind-muscle connection.
- [23:17](https://youtu.be/fPXBDaavfUA?t=1397) The 2020 arm swing is longer, with more scap retraction and loading, and matches his lower half. The 2016 short arm path gives less loading. How the arm gets into position matters, not just the position.

## Cues
- **"dancing down the mountain"** - fixes over-analytical, robotic delivery; for pitchers who think about arm, foot and hip positions
- **"keeping that stacked pelvis as you move forward"** - fixes tipping the pelvis and falling at the target; for pitchers who lose the hinge
- **"sitting in a chair"** - fixes coming across the body (Glasnow's feel); add a coil for pitchers who still open early
- **"weight on the inside of the back heel"** - fixes failing to find the glute and pelvic load; for remote athletes who lose the backside

## Anti-cues
- **"shorten the arm action"** - taught to fix arm or timing problems; actually can reduce scap retraction and loading and hurt sequencing; per Ben Brewster, who says it helps some pitchers and hurts "two or three guys"
