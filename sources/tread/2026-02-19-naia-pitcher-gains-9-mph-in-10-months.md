# NAIA Pitcher Gains 9 MPH in 10 Months

## TL;DR
- Tread took an NAIA pitcher (Ethan Hochendoner, 6'7", 260 lbs) from 88-90 mph to 98 mph indoors over ten months, mostly through remote coaching with daily video exchange.
- The mechanical fix sequence was arm action first (hand-driven to elbow-driven flip), lateral tilt in the lower half last, introduced only once the arm action had cleaned up.
- The author credits the gain to three things beyond mechanics: daily video, full program execution (weight room, mobility, nutrition), and refusing to fix everything at once.

## Claims
- The Intake Form That Made Me Laugh: at 6'7" and 260 lbs, the pitcher had physical tools but wasn't topping 90 mph before training began.
- What the Assessment Showed: the initial delivery lacked drift at peak leg lift, the back leg dumped toward third, and the front leg swung open to compensate, forcing the upper half to correct late.
- In-Season: Don't Overcomplicate It: with only two in-season feels (a pendulum drill for the arm, a "get off the quad" cue for the lower half), he threw a complete game and a new PR of 91 mph.
- Remote Training: Trial and Error: with a 24-hour-plus remote feedback loop of daily video, the cue shifted his arm action from hand-driven to elbow-driven, and the lower half was cued to internally rather than externally rotate.
- First Velocity Phase: pulldowns hit 94 mph on the first attempt and reached 97 mph by the end of June; bullpen PR of 92 mph.
- The Pivot: plyo testing hit 95 mph with a 3.5 oz ball, and he sat 90 mph in a fall pen for the first time, which the author calls more important than any ceiling number.
- The Breakthrough: lateral tilt was introduced in October; the following overload/underload mound velo phase used 6, 5, and 4 oz balls paired with "1 mph pens" starting at 85 mph.
- The Life-Changing Moment: he hit 94.8 mph in a 1 mph pen, 95.4 mph the next velo day, then 95.9 mph the week after, before a deload.
- The Trip to Tread HQ: on his second of two bullpens at Tread HQ he sat 96 mph and topped out at 98 mph indoors, throwing strikes throughout.
- What Made This Work: the author names three factors for the gain — daily video, full execution of the weight room/mobility/nutrition program, and patience instead of changing multiple things at once.

## Cues
- **"Don't worry about getting your hand up, worry about getting your elbow up"** - fixes a hand-driven, long arm action; for a pitcher who took the hand way behind him on the break.
- **"Try to dip into the velo bucket"** - cue for a rocker drill with lateral tilt, used to add linear direction for a pitcher who was too rotational.

## Evidence cited
- Single named athlete (Ethan Hochendoner), with a self-reported velocity progression from 88-90 mph in NAIA play to 98 mph indoors at Tread HQ over roughly ten months. No comparison group or controlled test; the account is a coach's narrative of one client.

## Open questions
- The lateral tilt cue is said to help pitchers who are "too rotational," but no criteria are given for identifying that population before applying it.
- Mechanical changes (arm action, lateral tilt) and the weighted-ball overload/underload work ran concurrently, so the piece doesn't separate how much of the 92-to-98 mph gain came from each.
