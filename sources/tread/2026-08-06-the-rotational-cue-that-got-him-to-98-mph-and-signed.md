# The Rotational Cue That Got Him To 98 MPH And Signed

## TL;DR
- Craig Stem's case study on Jack Weisenburger: a released, injury-prone reliever fixed a counter-rotation flaw and finished the offseason at a career-high 98 mph.
- The plan combined a mechanical diagnosis (excessive counter-rotation at leg lift, corrected by a pushy back leg and a draggy arm) with overload/underload mound velos (6 oz / 4 oz balls) and a "connected throws" drill that biases rotation over linear drive.
- One verbal cue ("rotate a little bit") added 2 mph on the next throw at pro day (96-97 to 99 mph); after a deload and a block of connected throws, Weisenburger PR'd at 98 mph.

## Claims
- [01:01](https://youtu.be/1Apgr0f0bgA?t=61) Weisenburger spent 6 years as a reliever in the A's system, had 4 surgeries, and was released in 2025 out of spring training.
- [02:01](https://youtu.be/1Apgr0f0bgA?t=121) Mechanical assessment found a "move backwards before forwards" pattern at peak leg lift that produces excessive counter-rotation, corrected by an overly aggressive back-leg push and a "draggy" arm action - flagged as a likely contributor to his elbow issues.
- [03:03](https://youtu.be/1Apgr0f0bgA?t=183) First month of the plan was a plyo routine to keep the arm from getting stuck behind the body, reduce counter-rotation, and find a lower-stress arm path.
- [04:03](https://youtu.be/1Apgr0f0bgA?t=243) Overload/underload mound velos followed: a 6 oz ball (overload) to feel arm position and engage the pec so the elbow doesn't drop, then a 4 oz ball (underload) to repeat the same movement faster.
- [04:03](https://youtu.be/1Apgr0f0bgA?t=243) Bullpen velocity trended to 94-96 mph heading into pro day.
- [06:04](https://youtu.be/1Apgr0f0bgA?t=364) On the mound before pro day, given the cue "if you would just rotate a little bit you'd probably throw this 100 miles an hour," his next shuffle jumped from 96-97 mph to 99 mph.
- [06:04](https://youtu.be/1Apgr0f0bgA?t=364) Pro day itself (Jan 9-11): 94-96 mph, no MLB offers, interest from a Mexican League team.
- [07:05](https://youtu.be/1Apgr0f0bgA?t=425) After pro day: a deload week, then a "connected throws" drill block (keep back foot on the ground to force rotation instead of leaping forward); his first bullpen after the deload was a career-high 98 mph.
- [09:07](https://youtu.be/1Apgr0f0bgA?t=547) Signed with the Mexican League team as a starter rather than a reliever, on the reasoning that innings-eating starters are more valuable in affiliated ball and can move to relief later, not the reverse.
- [10:08](https://youtu.be/1Apgr0f0bgA?t=608) Second start: 8 innings, 1 hit, 10 strikeouts, 0 walks - signed with the Mets within a week and assigned to Triple-A, his first time there in his career.

## Cues
- **"If you would just rotate a little bit you'd probably throw this 100 miles an hour"** - fixes stopping rotation too early / bleeding forward instead of rotating; for an experienced pitcher whose delivery is dominated by linear drive.
- **"Keep your back foot on the ground"** (connected throws) - fixes leaping off the back foot / drifting forward instead of rotating; for pitchers who understand the linear part of the throw but not the rotational part.

## Evidence cited
- Named athlete: Jack Weisenburger. Numbers given: 94-96 mph bullpens pre-pro day, +2 mph (96/97 to 99) off one verbal cue, career-high 98 mph bullpen post-deload, 8 IP / 1 H / 10 K / 0 BB Mexican League start, Mets signing, Triple-A assignment.

## Open questions
- The pro-day cue's +2 mph jump is a single-throw anecdote in a high-adrenaline setting (throwing in front of scouts); the source doesn't separate the cue's effect from that context.
- The claim that counter-rotation plus a "pushy back leg" contributed to Weisenburger's elbow issues is Stem's mechanical read, not tied to any measured biomechanics or study.
