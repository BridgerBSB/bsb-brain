# What is the TRUE Role of Soft Tissue Work? | #AsktheDoc Episode 6

## TL;DR
- Dr. Nevin Markel treats foam rollers, lacrosse balls and similar tools as an extension of the dynamic warm-up, meant to stimulate tissue and wake up the brain-body link before throwing.
- The purpose is not arbitrary range-of-motion gains. He expects a temporary increase in function, "maybe not permanently."
- He argues warm-ups should be individualized from an evaluation (for example hip internal rotation or ankle dorsiflexion limits) rather than one generic routine.

## Claims
- [01:00](https://youtu.be/T8nQANoNjUY?t=60) The research on soft tissue tools is varied; some studies show an outcome and some do not. His view comes from observation and experience.
- [01:00](https://youtu.be/T8nQANoNjUY?t=60) Tissue tools are an extension of the dynamic warm-up, which he says has been validated in track and field and other sports.
- [02:02](https://youtu.be/T8nQANoNjUY?t=122) He defines tissue quality as connective tissue and the muscle-fascia interface being hydrated, with blood flow and proprioceptive receptors stimulated for sport.
- [02:02](https://youtu.be/T8nQANoNjUY?t=122) Throwing is a total-body event, so the whole system should be stimulated, not one shoulder in isolation. At 100 mph the force through the tissues is large, and he does not want athletes going in cold.
- [03:03](https://youtu.be/T8nQANoNjUY?t=183) The intent of tissue work is to have joints at their maximum for that session, not to make arbitrary range-of-motion gains.
- [04:05](https://youtu.be/T8nQANoNjUY?t=245) Warm-ups should be tailored to the individual from an existing evaluation. He names hip internal rotation and ankle dorsiflexion as examples. He admits individualizing is hard for coaches of large groups.
- [05:06](https://youtu.be/T8nQANoNjUY?t=306) With better tissue quality he expects "at least for a time" an increase in function, using a squat limited by dorsiflexion as the example. You do not force the ankle to move more.
- [05:06](https://youtu.be/T8nQANoNjUY?t=306) Fascia is delicate, water-based tissue, "not a piece of steak that needs to be tenderized." Forcing range of motion does not work in his experience.
- [06:06](https://youtu.be/T8nQANoNjUY?t=366) The nervous system is in charge, so implements and exercises are an interface to the brain to improve proprioception.

## Open questions
- He concedes the research is mixed and rests the case on personal observation. No study or measurement is cited for the temporary function gain.
- The view that tissue work is not about range of motion differs from the common coaching use of rolling to gain mobility. The video does not test either.
