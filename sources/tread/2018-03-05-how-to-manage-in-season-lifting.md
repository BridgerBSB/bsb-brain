# How To Manage In-Season Lifting

## TL;DR
- Across four studies (beginners x2, endurance athletes, pro soccer players), cutting training volume by roughly a third to two-thirds while in season was enough to maintain strength and hypertrophy gained in the off-season.
- Tread's own rule of thumb: cut volume by 50% and intensity by only about 10%, never cut both hard at once.
- Total detraining doesn't cost trained lifters strength or muscle for 2-3 weeks; early apparent mass loss is glycogen, not tissue, which matters most for tapering the final weeks of a season.

## Claims
- [01:18](https://youtu.be/Pjbxp2kwpiU?t=78) In previously untrained lifters, cutting a 27-set/week lower body program down to 1/3 volume (9 sets) still slightly increased strength and fully maintained hypertrophy; cutting to 1/9 volume (3 sets) maintained strength but not hypertrophy.
- [02:18](https://youtu.be/Pjbxp2kwpiU?t=138) In moderately trained endurance athletes, a 50-70% reduction in training volume was sufficient to maintain strength and performance gains.
- [03:21](https://youtu.be/Pjbxp2kwpiU?t=201) A second beginner study cut 18-24 sets/week down to 6-8 sets/week (about 1/3) and fully maintained strength and hypertrophy.
- [04:22](https://youtu.be/Pjbxp2kwpiU?t=262) In pro soccer players over a 12-week season, training once per week maintained strength, jump height, and 40-meter sprint performance; training once every other week did not.
- [04:22](https://youtu.be/Pjbxp2kwpiU?t=262) In trained lifters, total detraining for 2-3 weeks showed no significant loss of strength or muscle mass; early apparent mass loss is muscle glycogen depletion, not lost tissue.
- [05:24](https://youtu.be/Pjbxp2kwpiU?t=324) Tread's applied recommendation: about 50% volume reduction in season, e.g. an off-season 9-12 sets of 5-10 reps twice a week becomes 6 sets one day and 3-6 sets the other day (9-12 sets/week total, down from 18-24).
- [06:30](https://youtu.be/Pjbxp2kwpiU?t=390) Example set/rep conversions: off-season 3x6 becomes 1-2 sets of 6 in season; 4x8 becomes 2x8; 3x10 becomes 1-2x10; 3x12 becomes 1-2x12.
- [07:32](https://youtu.be/Pjbxp2kwpiU?t=452) Recommend cutting volume roughly in half while dropping intensity only about 10% (e.g. 100 lb becomes 90 lb); dropping both volume and intensity is called a common mistake that leads to losing 15-20 lb or a large drop in body composition and strength over a season.
- [08:32](https://youtu.be/Pjbxp2kwpiU?t=512) In the final 2-3 weeks of a season, recommend dropping volume further still, since the detraining research shows no meaningful strength or mass loss over that window.

## Evidence cited
- Study 1: untrained lifters, 16-week lower body program (27 sets/week), tested 1/3 and 1/9 volume reduction for maintenance. No authors or citation given.
- Study 2: moderately trained endurance athletes, 50-70% volume reduction maintained strength/performance. No authors or citation given.
- Study 3: beginners, 18-24 sets/week reduced to 6-8 sets/week, maintained strength and hypertrophy. No authors or citation given.
- Study 4: pro soccer players, 12-week season, once-weekly vs once-every-other-week maintenance training compared for strength, jump height, 40m sprint. No authors or citation given.

## Open questions
- The video presents four studies as the basis for its recommendations but names no authors, journals, or years, so the claims can't be independently verified against the source research.
- Once-per-week training maintained gains in the soccer study but once-every-other-week did not, while beginner studies found even 1/9 of original volume maintained strength; the video doesn't reconcile why frequency matters more in trained/team-sport populations than raw volume does in beginners.
