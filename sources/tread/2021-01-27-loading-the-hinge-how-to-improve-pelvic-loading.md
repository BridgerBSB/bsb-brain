# Loading the Hinge: How to Improve Pelvic Loading

## TL;DR
- Ben Brewster teaches a band-resisted step back drill that exaggerates early pelvic opening so the pitcher feels holding the hinge, then releasing into landing.
- The band is anchored about 15 degrees toward the glove side; three small bands looped together mimic a Core Velocity Belt.
- Use the minimum effective dose: one external constraint instead of ten internal cues.

## Claims
- [01:01](https://youtu.be/5qJB9tIWXIw?t=61) Minimum effective dose: a simple external constraint or feel is often better than many internal cues, which risk over-analysis.
- [02:02](https://youtu.be/5qJB9tIWXIw?t=122) Feeding the flaw: set the band to encourage early pelvic opening so the body learns to stay in the hinge. Compared to throwing off the back of the mound for uphill pitchers.
- [02:02](https://youtu.be/5qJB9tIWXIw?t=122) The flaw targeted is pitchers who lose tension and coil over the back hip and open the pelvis early toward landing.
- [03:03](https://youtu.be/5qJB9tIWXIw?t=183) Setup: step into the band with the back leg, turn 360 degrees away from the anchor, keep the band at belt height, and anchor in front offset about 15 degrees to the glove side.
- [04:03](https://youtu.be/5qJB9tIWXIw?t=243) Distance from the anchor needs tuning: too far pulls you out of the leg lift, too close gives no feedback.
- [04:03](https://youtu.be/5qJB9tIWXIw?t=243) The step back is the preferred drill because it handles the initial weight shift and is dynamic enough that there is no time to overthink.
- [05:04](https://youtu.be/5qJB9tIWXIw?t=304) The pelvis should be released into rotation, not powered through into landing; the band pops the hips open at the last second.
- [05:04](https://youtu.be/5qJB9tIWXIw?t=304) The band can be added to 10 toes, rhythm rocker, walking windup, or full delivery.
- [06:05](https://youtu.be/5qJB9tIWXIw?t=365) Progression: move closer to the anchor, then remove the band and keep the feel.
- [06:05](https://youtu.be/5qJB9tIWXIw?t=365) Two flaws: over-rotating away from the anchor, and leaking open immediately and letting the band take over.

## Cues
- **"hold their pelvis over their back leg and resist the pull of the band as long as they can"** - fixes pelvis opening early, loss of coil over the back hip; for pitchers who do not stay in the hinge

## Anti-cues
- none

## Drills
- **Banded step back** - three small bands looped at belt height, anchored in front about 15 degrees glove side, step back drill; builds pelvic loading and tension-free release into landing; for pitchers who open the pelvis early
