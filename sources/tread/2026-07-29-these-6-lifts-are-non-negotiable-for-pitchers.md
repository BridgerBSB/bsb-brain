# These 6 Lifts are Non-Negotiable For Pitchers

## TL;DR
- Six strength exercises are each tied to a specific position or quality from the delivery: the split-squat landing, whole-foot/hip-extension drive, lateral force output, posterior chain length and stiffness, and trunk flexion/rotation control.
- Reverse lunge load of 1.5-1.8x bodyweight is flagged as the point where carryover is greatest; pushing past that shows diminishing returns.
- Repeated framing: exercises don't need to look like the throw, they need to reinforce the same physical quality (position, force direction, tissue length) the throw uses.

## Claims
- [00:42](https://youtu.be/sBOTw365OIk?t=42) Safety bar reverse lunge trains the split-squat position pitchers land in; the safety bar is used instead of a straight bar to avoid extra shoulder stress from external rotation.
- [01:00](https://youtu.be/sBOTw365OIk?t=60) Players who reach 1.5-1.8x bodyweight on the reverse lunge see the biggest carryover; loading past that point gives diminishing returns.
- [02:39](https://youtu.be/sBOTw365OIk?t=159) Sled work loads the lower half with minimal spinal compression, contrasted with high-volume heavy squatting/deadlifting, which can stiffen the spine over time.
- [03:03](https://youtu.be/sBOTw365OIk?t=183) Sled push drives through the whole foot to the big toe with hip extension, mirroring toe-off and hip extension at foot strike.
- [05:05](https://youtu.be/sBOTw365OIk?t=305) Sled lateral drag adds a frontal-plane push absent from standard sagittal-plane lifting, matching the lateral push during load and rotation.
- [06:05](https://youtu.be/sBOTw365OIk?t=365) Sled reverse backpedal trains quad-driven knee extension into the block, complementing hamstring-dominant posterior chain work.
- [07:26](https://youtu.be/sBOTw365OIk?t=446) Single-leg DB RDL trains the lead-leg block: lengthening hamstring/glute with minimal forward knee travel, described as how high-velocity throwers stop momentum.
- [10:19](https://youtu.be/sBOTw365OIk?t=619) Nordic ISO / GHR hamstring hold at roughly 45 degrees of knee flexion trains front-leg stiffness before landing; prescribed as 3-second holds for about 5 reps to start.
- [11:42](https://youtu.be/sBOTw365OIk?t=702) Reverse crunch trains resisting spinal extension so the trunk stays flexed through rotation; low-velocity throwers are described as extending the trunk early and losing separation.
- [13:36](https://youtu.be/sBOTw365OIk?t=816) Trunk rotation drill isolates rotational output at the landing position, loaded moderately-heavy for useful feedback, with a straight-bar variation given for those without the rotational tool.

## Cues
- **"Big toe"** - fixes flat-footed sled pushing and incomplete hip extension; for pitchers doing sled work
- **"Hold flexion"** - fixes early trunk extension that causes loss of separation; for low-velocity throwers
- **"Extend the elbows, let the trunk move"** - fixes elbows bending into a press instead of the trunk rotating; for pitchers doing the cable trunk rotation drill

## Drills
- **Safety bar reverse lunge** - reverse lunge into split-squat position with a safety bar; builds strength in the throwing landing position; benchmark 1.5-1.8x bodyweight
- **Sled push** - straight-arm sled push, drive through the whole foot to the big toe; builds the toe-off/hip-extension pattern with low spinal load
- **Sled lateral drag** - crossover-step lateral sled drag, loaded heavier than the push; builds lateral/frontal-plane force output for load and rotation
- **Sled reverse backpedal** - low stance, drive heel down, pull sled backward; builds quad-driven knee drive into the block
- **Single-leg DB RDL** - single-leg RDL with support, foot set to landing angle; builds lead-leg posterior chain length with minimal knee travel; for the block
- **Nordic ISO (GHR hamstring hold)** - partner-anchored hamstring isometric at roughly 45 degrees of knee flexion, 3-second holds for 5 reps; builds front-leg stiffness before landing
- **Reverse crunch** - loaded reverse crunch using a dumbbell or rack for support, progressed by straightening the legs; builds resisted spinal extension/trunk flexion through rotation
- **Trunk rotation (cable or straight bar)** - rotate from the landing position against moderate-heavy resistance, elbows locked; builds rotational force output at foot strike; straight-bar variation given for those without a rotational tool

## Open questions

- States that reverse lunge load above 1.5-1.8x bodyweight gives diminishing returns without describing how this was measured or tracked.
