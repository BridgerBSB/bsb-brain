# Fixing a Late Arm for Pitchers | Pitching Drills

## TL;DR
- A late arm is defined here as under 25 degrees of external rotation when the front foot accepts weight; on time is 45 to 90 degrees.
- Find the cause first (rushing, early torso rotation, forced scap retraction, early back leg extension, arm path direction, tension), then fix it with slightly heavier implements, abbreviated drills, and slow-to-fast reps.
- Do not force scap retraction or shorten the arm to a hand-to-ear catcher action; keep the spiral.

## Claims
- [00:00](https://youtu.be/cDEYVs_wLAw?t=0) Timing is measured at force acceptance of the front foot, not first heel touch. On time is 45 to 90 degrees of external rotation; slightly late is 25 to 45; late is below 25.
- [03:02](https://youtu.be/cDEYVs_wLAw?t=182) A late arm forces an aggressive transition into layback with the scap still anteriorly tilted, which may stress the shoulder.
- [05:05](https://youtu.be/cDEYVs_wLAw?t=305) A late arm resembles early torso rotation: the shoulders are open by the time the forearm is vertical. It tends to give a linear, dart-throw finish and limited layback.
- [09:31](https://youtu.be/cDEYVs_wLAw?t=571) Change it faster for repeat-injury, struggling, or youth pitchers. Use a slower trigger for historically healthy high-level pitchers, and keep it to one or two core issues in the offseason.
- [11:25](https://youtu.be/cDEYVs_wLAw?t=685) Arm lateness can be injury compensation, so ease into intensity after fixing it. Other causes: rushing, a long arm action against a fast lower half, over-vertical or over-rotational arm path, upper trap or pec tension, late hand break, early torso rotation.
- [16:12](https://youtu.be/cDEYVs_wLAw?t=972) Forcing scap retraction by yanking the elbows back makes the arm late. Retraction happens naturally from the momentum of the hand break.
- [18:14](https://youtu.be/cDEYVs_wLAw?t=1094) Early back leg extension drops the torso, produces uphill throwing, and leaves the elbow down. Fixing it often cleans up the arm.
- [19:15](https://youtu.be/cDEYVs_wLAw?t=1155) Use implements from 7 oz to 1 lb (a 20 oz ball at most) for patterning. A 4 lb ball or wrist weight is too heavy, and light balls are for speeding up the arm after the guard rails are in place.
- [22:18](https://youtu.be/cDEYVs_wLAw?t=1338) Work back to front (start with the arm up) and front to back (the spiral). Run slow to fast: 5 to 15 reps, one or two sets, then ramp up.
- [29:18](https://youtu.be/cDEYVs_wLAw?t=1758) Challenge the full pattern every session, for example 90% arm action drills and 10% stretch or wind-dip work.

## Cues
- **"Let the arm pendulum"** - fixes arm that does not flip up on time; for late-arm pitchers
- **"Capture the momentum of the downswing"** - fixes arm that does not flip up on time; for late-arm pitchers
- **"Float the arm up the spiral"** - fixes tense, forced arm path to the up position; for late-arm pitchers
- **"Arm on a cloud"** - fixes tension in abbreviated drills that blocks scap and torso segmenting; for late-arm pitchers
- **"Leave the arm behind"** - fixes tense arm while turning the torso; for late-arm pitchers
- **"Keep the back ear over the back hip"** - fixes rushing, where the upper half gets ahead of the lower half; for late-arm pitchers (also "head over the pelvis", "stay stacked")

## Anti-cues
- **"Yank the elbows back behind the body"** - taught to fix scap retraction and separation; actually causes a forced, late arm at landing; per Ben Brewster
- **"Break the hands and go hand to ear like a catcher"** - taught to fix arm timing by shortening the arm; actually loses the spiral and the figure-eight energy; per Ben Brewster

## Drills
- **Lasso drill** - abbreviated start with the arm already up at landing; builds feel of the landing position and acceleration from it; for diagnosing mobility vs patterning in late-arm pitchers
- **Figure eight trigger** - trace a figure eight through the release point in the initial arm swing; builds a loop of energy on the downswing; for late-arm pitchers
- **Pendulum starting trigger** - start with the hand up or just think "pendulum" in a split stance; builds the arm flip-up on time; for late-arm pitchers
- **Rhythm rocker** - split-stance rocker, with Indian club, plyo or weighted ball, slow to fast; builds timing and body control
- **10 toes drill** - modified pivot pick off, slow to fast; builds arm up and on time
- **Indian club pre-throw warm-up** - 1 lb club at 10 to 30% effort; builds the arm pattern as a shoulder warm-up
- **Step-back slide step progression** - slide steps without leg lift, then step back with leg lift, then wind-up; blends upper half with lower half
- **Hands over the head wind-up** - wind-up with hands overhead; builds pendulum energy capture; source reports 1 to 3 mph jumps

## Evidence cited
- [01:00](https://youtu.be/cDEYVs_wLAw?t=60) Korean vs American pitcher study: faster Americans had about 45 degrees of external rotation at landing; slower Koreans had 68 degrees.
- [30:03](https://youtu.be/cDEYVs_wLAw?t=1803) Three coach-case examples: Johnny (early back leg extension plus old shoulder injury, re-aggravated when fixed), Tyler (rotational arm swing), and Brewster himself (forced scap retraction).

## Open questions
- Injury links are explicitly speculation ([06:06](https://youtu.be/cDEYVs_wLAw?t=366)); no data is cited that a late arm causes injury.
- Brewster calls shortening the arm a 3 to 6 month, high-risk change, which contradicts the common pro-coach practice of shortening first. Treat this as one coach's opinion.
