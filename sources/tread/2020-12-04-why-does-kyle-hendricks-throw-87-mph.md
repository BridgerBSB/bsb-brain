# Why Does Kyle Hendricks Throw 87 MPH?

## TL;DR
- Side-by-side of Kyle Hendricks (about 87 mph, third percentile) and Walker Buehler (a hair over 97 mph) shows four movement patterns that cost velocity.
- Hendricks tips forward at peak leg lift, goes quad and toe driven, lifts the heel early, and opens the pelvis early.
- Arm timing and layback differ: Hendricks lifts the arm early and does not reach full layback, so he pushes through the finish.

## Claims
- [00:00](https://youtu.be/2DtRBCP_j4M?t=0) Hendricks averages about 87 mph, third percentile in the big leagues; Buehler averages a hair over 97 mph. The speaker believes Hendricks could theoretically throw significantly harder, but not that he should change anything.
- [01:00](https://youtu.be/2DtRBCP_j4M?t=60) At peak leg lift Hendricks is already hunched toward the third base side. Buehler stays tall and stacked over the pelvis and heel, with a slight backward lean.
- [01:00](https://youtu.be/2DtRBCP_j4M?t=60) The early tip presets a toe and quad driven linear move. Escaping it is possible but less likely.
- [02:02](https://youtu.be/2DtRBCP_j4M?t=122) In the forward move Hendricks' knee goes well over the toe and his back heel comes off the ground early. Buehler keeps the whole back foot in contact, which gives a back foot anchor and single leg stability.
- [02:02](https://youtu.be/2DtRBCP_j4M?t=122) Hendricks begins opening the pelvis over many frames, early. Buehler keeps it closed and snaps it open at the last second.
- [03:02](https://youtu.be/2DtRBCP_j4M?t=182) Ideal is to stay loaded and in contact with the back heel as long as possible, ideally through the back glute, while keeping the stack.
- [03:02](https://youtu.be/2DtRBCP_j4M?t=182) An arm that lifts too early (pie-throwing arm action) lags and captures less stretch reflex through the pec.
- [04:03](https://youtu.be/2DtRBCP_j4M?t=243) Buehler's arm gets up at the last second, two frames before landing, in a smooth loop (scapular fish hook, credited to Paul Nyman). Hendricks' arm action is hand driven.
- [04:03](https://youtu.be/2DtRBCP_j4M?t=243) Buehler has close to 180 degrees of layback (not exactly measurable from a front view); Hendricks does not quite reach full layback.
- [05:04](https://youtu.be/2DtRBCP_j4M?t=304) Less layback means a shorter arc and less elastic stretch reflex at the shoulder, and a linear, crunching follow-through over the front leg instead of late torso rotation over a firm lead leg. Hendricks also shows a soft front leg.
