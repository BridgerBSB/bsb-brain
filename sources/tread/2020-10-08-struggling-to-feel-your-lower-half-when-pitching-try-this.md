# Struggling to Feel Your Lower Half When Pitching? Try This! | TRX Drift Drill

## TL;DR
- A suspension-strap drill that teaches the "drift": a small forward shift of the center of mass during the leg lift, so the pitcher drops into the glutes and the pelvis accelerates forward.
- Use 5 to 10 percent of body weight (5 to 10 pounds) on the strap, felt on the inside of the back heel. It is a subtle lean, not a balance point.
- A second variation guides the descent down the mound to teach the linear move, with the pelvis closed and the head stacked over the pelvis.

## Claims
- [00:00](https://youtu.be/XSWNr5BgTzk?t=0) The drill targets pitchers who cannot transition from the initial weight shift into the linear move and cannot feel the back leg underneath them or load the pelvis.
- [00:00](https://youtu.be/XSWNr5BgTzk?t=0) High-level throwers do not come to a balance point and stay over the rubber. They have aggressive tempos, and the leg lift carries them slightly forward.
- [01:01](https://youtu.be/XSWNr5BgTzk?t=61) Because the center of mass has shifted forward, dropping into the glutes accelerates the pelvis forward. That is the essence of the drift.
- [01:01](https://youtu.be/XSWNr5BgTzk?t=61) The drill is aimed at pitchers stuck in a balance point or a hip hike, whose weight stays too far back for the whole delivery.
- [01:01](https://youtu.be/XSWNr5BgTzk?t=61) The strap should carry about 5 to 10 percent of body weight, "maybe five to ten pounds", so it is not an aggressive lean.
- [02:01](https://youtu.be/XSWNr5BgTzk?t=121) The position is a dynamic balance point. It cannot be held without the strap because the weight is slightly out front.
- [02:01](https://youtu.be/XSWNr5BgTzk?t=121) The old routine was to feel the position for a couple of reps, let go, and throw. Some athletes still lose contact with the back leg at release of the strap.
- [03:01](https://youtu.be/XSWNr5BgTzk?t=181) Peak leg lift has no real tension in the back glute. Tension arrives once the pitcher sits on the way down, and the strap needs much more weight there because the weight is moving down the mound.
- [04:03](https://youtu.be/XSWNr5BgTzk?t=243) The loaded position should not feel unstable or quad dominant. Legs and pelvis sit underneath, and the upper half is back. They call this "the stack".
- [05:04](https://youtu.be/XSWNr5BgTzk?t=304) The drill can be used as a primer, with plyos thrown into a net, or with a partner in catch. It can regress pitchers who have no feel for the drift or the linear move.

## Cues
- **"the inside of the back heel"** - fixes weight leaking to the toes, falling backward, or sitting on the outside of the foot during the initial shift; for pitchers stuck in a balance point or hip hike
- **"lean and turn"** - fixes an initial weight shift that is too big or absent; for pitchers learning the drift
- **"feel my head stay stacked over my pelvis"** - fixes losing the stack on the way down the mound; for pitchers who cannot connect the leg lift to the linear move
- **"feel that back foot stay anchored and stable into the ground"** - fixes losing contact with the back leg after the leg lift; for pitchers who lose the back leg when they let go of the strap

## Anti-cues

## Drills
- **TRX drift drill, leg lift shift** - hang suspension straps and take 5 to 10 percent of weight on them at peak leg lift; builds the feel of a subtle forward shift onto the inside of the back heel; for pitchers stuck in a balance point or hip hike
- **TRX drift drill, guided descent** - hold the strap and lower slowly out of the leg lift down the mound, keeping the pelvis closed and direction to target; builds the feel of the linear move and loaded back glute; for pitchers who lose the back leg after the shift

## Open questions
- The coach says the drill is "really useful" but gives no data or athlete results. The claim rests on coaching experience.
- The drift contradicts balance-point teaching. It is described as a dynamic balance point, so the line between the two is a matter of how much shift the athlete uses.
