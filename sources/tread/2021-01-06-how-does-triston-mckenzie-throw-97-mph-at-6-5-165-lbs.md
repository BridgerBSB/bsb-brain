# How Does Triston McKenzie throw 97 MPH at 6'5" 165 lbs?

## TL;DR
- McKenzie (6'5", 165 lbs, 93 mph average, 97 peak) gets velocity from sequencing: a held hinge, a relaxed hip release, a lead leg block, and an elbow kept in the shoulder plane.
- Arm speed "comes from the body," not the arm; hips open by relaxing out of stored tension rather than being forced.
- Ben Brewster classes him as fascia driven, with long levers and fast-twitch fibers, and says 99 out of 100 athletes who say they can't gain weight can, once a calorie surplus is tracked.

## Claims
- [00:31](https://youtu.be/vti59tFtQy8?t=62) Fastball averages 93 mph, peaks at 97, about 2,300 rpm, thrown about 53% of the time; top-10 vertical break in the league.
- [01:02](https://youtu.be/vti59tFtQy8?t=62) Intent is high but sequenced: he delays bringing the upper half and arm in, then explodes at the last second.
- [03:04](https://youtu.be/vti59tFtQy8?t=184) High leg lift with a pelvic tuck shows the move starts from the center of the body and shifts weight to the heel, setting up the hinge. Torso stays stacked and quiet.
- [06:05](https://youtu.be/vti59tFtQy8?t=365) He drops aggressively into the back leg but keeps level pelvis and shoulders, with a stable back foot and no leak off the rubber.
- [09:07](https://youtu.be/vti59tFtQy8?t=547) He holds the hinge about six frames before the pelvis triggers; lower-level throwers hold it a split second and open early.
- [11:11](https://youtu.be/vti59tFtQy8?t=671) Level shoulders matching the pelvic angle get him downhill; leaning back to "load" leaves a low elbow at landing and an energy leak. Elevated glove arm cues the downhill unload.
- [12:13](https://youtu.be/vti59tFtQy8?t=733) Hip rotation is a relaxing release of stored tension, not forced; forcing it creates less separation and spinal shear.
- [18:21](https://youtu.be/vti59tFtQy8?t=1101) Hips are roughly 45 to 55 degrees open at landing (Brewster's estimate, no mocap); the lead leg block finishes pelvic rotation and peaks hip-shoulder separation just after foot plant.
- [20:22](https://youtu.be/vti59tFtQy8?t=1222) About 45 degrees of external rotation at landing, in the 45 to 90 target range; elbow stays in the shoulder plane through rotation.
- [26:04](https://youtu.be/vti59tFtQy8?t=1586) He has gained 5 lbs since the draft; the likely cause is no true calorie surplus. Tracking intake and body weight daily, with consistency, is the fix.
