# The Plane of Rotation: How Elite Throwers Use It To Throw Hard

## TL;DR
- Defines the plane of rotation as the arm in line with the glove-side shoulder at release, and names three ways pitchers break it: shoulder climb, elbow drag, and dart/push arm action.
- Ties arm slot to posture and hip/spine range of motion rather than the arm itself; per Tread's internal mocap data, most pitchers who throw above 94 mph work from a side or three-quarter slot with hips parallel to the ground.
- Gives five drills (mobility stick, abbreviated throws, rollings, off-balance throws, wall/net target throws) to train and assess whether a pitcher is respecting the plane.

## Claims
- [00:14](https://youtu.be/-KSOIC0K6oM?t=14) The plane of rotation is the arm in line with the opposite (glove-side) shoulder at release; respecting it maximizes angular velocity of the trunk and arm.
- [00:14](https://youtu.be/-KSOIC0K6oM?t=14) Where the glove aims predicts rotation direction, with about a 5 to 20 degree bandwidth of variance.
- [03:10](https://youtu.be/-KSOIC0K6oM?t=190) Three patterns break the plane: shoulder climb, elbow drag below, and dart/push arm action (triceps extension).
- [04:01](https://youtu.be/-KSOIC0K6oM?t=241) Shoulder climb is often taught to 13-14 year olds as "get over the top" without detail, adding shoulder flexion stress linked to labrum issues.
- [06:03](https://youtu.be/-KSOIC0K6oM?t=363) Elbow drag comes from restricted pecs/lats, poor scap movement, or shoulder fatigue; it increases ball velocity into the elbow during layback and extension.
- [07:04](https://youtu.be/-KSOIC0K6oM?t=424) Dart/push arm action, driven by cues like "get behind the ball," forces the bicep to decelerate the arm more aggressively and is linked to triceps tendinitis and bone spurs over time.
- [10:06](https://youtu.be/-KSOIC0K6oM?t=606) Per Tread's mocap data, most pitchers throwing above 94 mph use a side or three-quarter slot with hips parallel to the ground.
- [13:08](https://youtu.be/-KSOIC0K6oM?t=788) Submarine pitchers get lower shoulder angular velocities because the plane fights the downhill mound slope.
- [16:10](https://youtu.be/-KSOIC0K6oM?t=970) Side-flexing before the pelvis rotates reduces velocity, causes spray, or raises injury risk.
- [20:14](https://youtu.be/-KSOIC0K6oM?t=1214) Wall/net target throws give feedback: missing high/in the face signals pushy/dart action, missing low/scoopy signals a dragging elbow.

## Cues
- **"Unravel out"** - fixes disrespecting the plane of rotation (climbing, dragging, or darting); for pitchers generally
- **"Let the pelvis just do the work, imagine your arm is basically dead"** - fixes forcing or muscling the arm up; used across arm slots in abbreviated throwing drills

## Drills
- **Mobility stick plane feel** - hold a PVC pipe/mobility stick along the spine-arm line and rotate through side, three-quarter, and high-slot postures; builds proprioception of the plane across arm slots; for pitchers assessing slot bias
- **Abbreviated throws** - split stance, back foot at a slight 45 degree angle, arm "dead," pelvis initiates rotation; builds a pelvis-driven unravel without forcing the arm; for pitchers learning the plane at their own slot
- **Rollings** - abbreviated or half-abbreviated setup with momentum, pelvis stays still while spine and arm do the work; builds identification of a pitcher's natural arm-path bias; used as an assessment tool
- **Athletic off-balance throws** - jump throws, one-foot throws, double-play-style throws from an unbalanced position; builds natural unraveling without the ability to cheat the arm; used for assessment
- **Wall/net target throws** - throw to a fixed point on a net or wall from a set slot; builds feedback on plane disrespect via miss direction

## Evidence cited
- [10:06](https://youtu.be/-KSOIC0K6oM?t=606) Tread's internal mocap data: most pitchers throwing above 94 mph use a side or three-quarter slot with hips parallel to the ground
- [13:08](https://youtu.be/-KSOIC0K6oM?t=788) Named example: Tyler Rogers, submarine slot, throws 82-83 mph and gets outs on angle/deception rather than velocity
- [05:02](https://youtu.be/-KSOIC0K6oM?t=302) Named examples: Bautista and Sabathia cited as "sidearm throwers" by spine-tilt logic despite over-the-top arm slots

## Open questions
- The claim that "everybody is a sidearm thrower, the only difference is the angle of the spine" contradicts conventional slot-based coaching and is asserted without study data.
- The 5 to 20 degree glove-aim-to-rotation bandwidth is stated without a measurement source.
