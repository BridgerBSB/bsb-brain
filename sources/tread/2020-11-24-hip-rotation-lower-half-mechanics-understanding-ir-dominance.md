# Hip Rotation & Lower Half Mechanics | Understanding IR Dominance

## TL;DR
- Internal rotation (IR) dominant lower halves are not all the same. Three MLB relievers load the hip in different ways and with different timing.
- A heel-through-knee line is the visual check for whether the back ankle and shin stay connected through the linear portion of the delivery.
- Ankle eversion should come at the last moment, as the back foot turns over into landing. Early eversion is linked to a leap off the back leg.

## Claims
- [00:00](https://youtu.be/IpMhApgnqBo?t=0) There is a misconception that all IR-dominant lower halves share the same timing and positions. Castillo, Williams and Rodney are all IR dominant but look different.
- [00:00](https://youtu.be/IpMhApgnqBo?t=0) Castillo is a vertical shin, IR-dominant pitcher, which is relatively uncommon. His heel-through-patella line does not intersect the pelvis. Williams' line points close to the front hip. Rodney's line points even further than the front hip.
- [01:00](https://youtu.be/IpMhApgnqBo?t=60) Castillo builds IR load from a combination of pelvis and femur motion. He uses a vertical shin and a lot of pelvic counter-rotation, with the belt buckle facing back and his back to the target.
- [01:00](https://youtu.be/IpMhApgnqBo?t=60) Rodney builds the load mostly from the femur side of the hip joint, with some pelvic rotation but far less than Castillo.
- [02:01](https://youtu.be/IpMhApgnqBo?t=121) Williams has early IR like Rodney, but his ankle caves early (early eversion). The heel points down while the shin angle is still in the other direction, which creates a disconnect at the base of the delivery.
- [02:01](https://youtu.be/IpMhApgnqBo?t=121) Rodney is stable through the ankle, and the ankle angle matches the shin as the shin angle changes.
- [03:02](https://youtu.be/IpMhApgnqBo?t=182) Eversion should not appear until the last second, as the back foot turns over into landing. Castillo shows this by holding the ground with a stable back ankle.
- [03:02](https://youtu.be/IpMhApgnqBo?t=182) The heel-through-knee line is an indicator of ankle stability through the linear portion of the throw.
- [03:02](https://youtu.be/IpMhApgnqBo?t=182) Williams' early eversion seems to lead to a bit of a leaping action off the back leg. Athletes often try to recover from an earlier disconnect with a push-off or leap.
- [04:02](https://youtu.be/IpMhApgnqBo?t=242) Rodney and Castillo are more rotational into landing and do not leap. There are areas of gray within ER and IR dominant lower halves.
