# Improving Shoulder Stability For Pitchers : Kettlebell Waiter Walk

## TL;DR
- The kettlebell waiter walk trains the upper trap, lower trap and serratus to co-contract overhead so the scapula can upwardly rotate and posteriorly tilt for safe layback.
- Progress by adding instability, not load: once you reach a 12 to 18 lb kettlebell, hang a 5 to 10 lb weight from a band instead of using a 30+ lb kettlebell.
- Heavy kettlebells turn the drill into a grip or anterior delt exercise and lose the point, which is scapular motor control.

## Claims
- [00:00](https://youtu.be/xBX70V15bnk?t=0) The scapula must upwardly rotate and posteriorly tilt to clear the way for layback. If it stays pinned in anterior tilt, the shoulder is more likely to see impingement, "labor issues (sic)", biceps tendon and supraspinatus problems.
- [01:00](https://youtu.be/xBX70V15bnk?t=60) The scapula also needs to sit flush on the rib cage, not winging, in overhead work.
- [01:00](https://youtu.be/xBX70V15bnk?t=60) Upper trap pulls upward rotation, lower trap pulls upward rotation and posterior tilt, serratus holds the scapula flush and drives upward rotation. Opposing tight muscles are addressed elsewhere in the program.
- [01:00](https://youtu.be/xBX70V15bnk?t=60) Purpose of the drill: add instability in upward rotation and posterior tilt under light load so these muscles fire in coordination.
- [02:00](https://youtu.be/xBX70V15bnk?t=120) The kettlebell's center of mass sits away from the handle, which gives built-in instability and forces co-contraction around the shoulder and scapula.
- [02:00](https://youtu.be/xBX70V15bnk?t=120) Elbow position: scapular plane with slight elevation and protraction, because serratus is most active above 90 degrees and in protraction. In line with the shoulders it turns on less; too far in front, the anterior delt takes over. Some individual freedom is allowed.
- [03:02](https://youtu.be/xBX70V15bnk?t=182) Basic version: grip the kettlebell very hard, walk, keep the rib cage down, go until form is lost.
- [03:02](https://youtu.be/xBX70V15bnk?t=182) Problem with the traditional heavier-kettlebell progression: at 35 lb with sweaty hands it becomes a grip exercise, and at 30+ lb the anterior delt and biceps take over.
- [04:02](https://youtu.be/xBX70V15bnk?t=242) Once at about 12 to 18 lb, add instability instead of weight: loop a band around a 5 to 10 lb weight, wrap the band around the hand, then grip the kettlebell. The plate bounces and demands stabilization in three dimensions.
- [05:04](https://youtu.be/xBX70V15bnk?t=304) Next step is a subtle skip while walking. A hanging weight of 20 to 30 lb starts shifting load to the anterior delt, so skipping adds demand without heavy load. Use it in the general routine or as pre- or post-throw activation, at some point in the week.

## Drills
- **Kettlebell waiter walk** - kettlebell held overhead in the scapular plane with slight elevation and protraction, walk with rib cage down; builds scapular stabilizer co-contraction and overhead stability; for pitchers
- **Banded plate kettlebell waiter walk** - 5 to 10 lb weight hung from a band wrapped around the hand holding a 12 to 18 lb kettlebell; builds 3D stability without heavy load; for pitchers who have outgrown the basic version
- **Skipping banded waiter walk** - same banded setup with a subtle skip while walking; builds a larger stability demand without more weight; for when the banded walk gets easy
