# Bulking on a Budget

## TL;DR
- Bulking cheaply comes down to tracking macros and calories instead of eating out, since eating out is the biggest cost driver for athletes trying to gain weight.
- Protein should land around 1 gram per pound of bodyweight per day; going higher just raises cost without adding muscle gain.
- The article closes with a full 5,000-calorie sample day (180g fat, 625g carbs, 220g protein target) built for $8.72.

## Claims
- **2. Avoid the "dirty" bulk** - recommends targeting about 1 lb of weight gain per week rather than chasing large surpluses like 6,000 calories, since excess intake mostly adds fat, not muscle.
- **3. Limit eating out** - cites athletes who were spending $25-30 a day on food, roughly double their prior spend, once given more robust calorie and macro targets.
- **4. Don't over consume protein** - recommends about 1 gram of protein per lb of bodyweight per day (200g for a 200 lb male); more than that gives no added benefit for bulking. Suggests staying under $4/lb for meat and $2.50/lb for chicken.
- **6. Take advantage of healthy fats** - fat provides 9 calories per gram versus 4 for carbs or protein; 3 tbsp of peanut butter adds about 600 calories for under $1.
- **9. Substitute higher calorie variations** - simple swaps (2% milk to whole milk, 96% lean beef to 85-90%) can add 1,000+ calories a day without more meals or larger portions.
- **Putting it all together** - builds a 5,000-calorie sample day (target: 180g fat, 625g carbs, 220g protein) that actually lands at 178g fat, 613g carbs, 228g protein, 4,990 calories, for a total grocery cost of $8.72.
