# How Does Sixto Sanchez Throw 100 MPH? A Closer Look

## TL;DR
- Ben Brewster breaks down Sixto Sanchez (99 mph four-seam average) on tempo, front-leg block, pelvic coil, arm action and back foot.
- Tempo of 22-23 frames from peak leg lift sits inside the 19-25 frame range he reports for hard throwers.
- The pelvis loads closed over the back hip, keeps direction, then unloads "down into landing" rather than spinning open.

## Claims
- [00:00](https://youtu.be/ZUqO_Xqr4rc?t=0) Sixto averages 99 mph four-seam, 97 sinker, low 90s changeup; usage 31% four-seam, 20% sinker, 26% change, 23% slider; 74% four-seams in his first postseason start.
- [02:02](https://youtu.be/ZUqO_Xqr4rc?t=122) Tempo is counted in frames from peak leg lift; Sixto is 22-23. Hard throwers run about 19 (deGrom) to 24-25 (Ryan).
- [03:02](https://youtu.be/ZUqO_Xqr4rc?t=182) Tempo correlates with velocity but is not a direct cause. Lower-level throwers rush (16-17 frames) or stall over the rubber. 26-28 frame hard throwers are rare, tall, long-armed athletes.
- [04:02](https://youtu.be/ZUqO_Xqr4rc?t=242) On every fastball he blocks the front leg. On every off-speed pitch the author checked, he breaks the front knee into release.
- [07:07](https://youtu.be/ZUqO_Xqr4rc?t=427) His changeup arm slot is about 10-15 degrees lower than the fastball slot, which hitters may eventually read.
- [08:07](https://youtu.be/ZUqO_Xqr4rc?t=487) In the leg lift the pelvis coils over the back hip (belt buckle from third base toward left center) while the upper half stays stacked and quiet.
- [10:08](https://youtu.be/ZUqO_Xqr4rc?t=608) Direction is kept by the back hip, stride direction and center of mass over the back foot, so it is not coil-and-fire like a shot putter.
- [11:09](https://youtu.be/ZUqO_Xqr4rc?t=669) The pelvis should unload down into landing, not open like a merry-go-round. Pelvic rotation angle should match arm slot and torso rotation angle.
- [13:11](https://youtu.be/ZUqO_Xqr4rc?t=791) His arm action is simple and tension-free, with the arm flipped up behind the head at landing. Anterior shoulder restriction or bad timing can prevent this.
- [16:14](https://youtu.be/ZUqO_Xqr4rc?t=974) Weight sits on the inside of the back heel, with no push-off or triple extension. The back foot everts as the pelvis goes. Missing ankle mobility makes the heel pop up instead.

## Cues
- **"rotating the pelvis down into landing"** - fixes hips spinning open flat in the transverse plane; for throwers who need to unload from a closed pelvis
- **"think of it much more like a ferris wheel"** - fixes merry-go-round hip opening and out-of-sync planes of rotation; for throwers who need to unload from a closed pelvis
- **"throw on the line"** - fixes loss of direction, arm slot repeatability and strike-throwing in long toss and partner throwing; for throwers working rotational loading
