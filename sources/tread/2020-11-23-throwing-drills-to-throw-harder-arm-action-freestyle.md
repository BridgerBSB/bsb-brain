# Throwing Drills to Throw Harder | Arm Action Freestyle

## TL;DR
- A low-intensity warm-up series that teaches the arm to move independently of the neck, torso and hips, so the arm path stays compact and fluid.
- Run it at about 20-30% effort with a medium to light ball (7-inch, 5 oz or 1 lb), from varied body positions and directions.
- Adapted from Dave Coggin (PFA) and Joe Kelly, who shortened a long arm path and gained velocity.

## Claims
- [[00:00](https://youtu.be/0bMk0-Qb3aY?t=0)] Joe Kelly had a long arm path early in his career and saw a large velocity bump after Dave Coggin shortened and compacted it.
- [[00:00](https://youtu.be/0bMk0-Qb3aY?t=0)] These are not high-intent drills; they are among the first things done in a day, aiming for an athletic, "infielder type" arm path.
- [[01:00](https://youtu.be/0bMk0-Qb3aY?t=60)] Many pitchers, two-ball relievers for example, yank neck, head, chest and arm together; the drills teach the arm to dissociate from the neck, torso and hips.
- [[01:00](https://youtu.be/0bMk0-Qb3aY?t=60)] Brewster calls these the most effective piece of the series, if done properly (his testing and that of staff).
- [[02:02](https://youtu.be/0bMk0-Qb3aY?t=122)] Forward momentum, as an infielder throwing to first, forces the arm to work independently of the body.
- [[02:02](https://youtu.be/0bMk0-Qb3aY?t=122)] Figure-eight analogy: glove arm tracks the top of the infinity sign, throwing arm tracks the bottom; close the loop, open the loop.
- [[03:04](https://youtu.be/0bMk0-Qb3aY?t=184)] Alternate forward throws and walk-back throws at roughly 20-30% effort.
- [[03:04](https://youtu.be/0bMk0-Qb3aY?t=184)] Use a 7-inch ball, 1 lb ball or 5 oz ball so the arm moves free and easy.
- [[04:06](https://youtu.be/0bMk0-Qb3aY?t=246)] Jump-back pivot pickoffs and a hop-back lasso drill can be added; let the arm float, spiral and pull into release.
- [[05:07](https://youtu.be/0bMk0-Qb3aY?t=307)] Timing and positions can be customized to the individual arm action.

## Cues
- **"Loose arm spiral"** - fixes a stiff or forced arm path; for pitchers who need a free, efficient arm (from the description)
- **"Stay tall"** - fixes drifting or collapsing posture while the arm works (from the description)
- **"Capture the loop of energy"** - fixes a long, disconnected arm path; for pitchers needing a compact loop (from the description)
- **"Float and strike"** - fixes forcing the throw; arm floats, spirals and pulls into release (from the description)

## Anti-cues
- none

## Drills
- **Walk/jog-toward-target throw** - jog toward the target like an infielder throwing to first, arm working independently of the body; builds a compact fluid arm loop; for pitchers whose arm moves with the whole body
- **Walk-back throw** - momentum moving backward, throw to the target, alternate with the forward throw; builds arm independence from varied lower-body positions
- **Jump-back pivot pickoff** - jump back into a pivot pickoff throw at low effort; builds a free, athletic arm path
- **Hop-back lasso drill** - hop back, compact loop of energy, let the throw unfold with little effort; builds a relaxed arm loop

## Evidence cited
