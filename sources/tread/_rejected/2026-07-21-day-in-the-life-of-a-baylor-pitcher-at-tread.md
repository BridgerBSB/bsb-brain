# Day In The Life Of A Baylor Pitcher At Tread

## TL;DR
- Full training day for Baylor-bound pitcher Tate DeRias at Tread's Charlotte facility: mobility work, plyos, catch play, a bullpen, live ABs, and a lift.
- Two numbers anchor the video: he's described as a 95+ mph arm, and he claims a pre-throw scalene release opens 10-15 degrees of internal rotation.
- Bullpen coaching is mostly about pitch sequencing off the fastball and a two-knee drill meant to delay rotation from the front side.

## Claims
- [00:03](https://youtu.be/uyn9iqFavTw?t=3) DeRias is filming a day in his life at Tread with live ABs scheduled about an hour out.
- [01:04](https://youtu.be/uyn9iqFavTw?t=64) Posting a live-AB video got Baylor's staff to reach out; a campus visit led to his commitment.
- [02:04](https://youtu.be/uyn9iqFavTw?t=124) A nerve release on the scalenes before throwing is claimed to open 10-15 degrees of internal rotation.
- [03:11](https://youtu.be/uyn9iqFavTw?t=191) He does internal-rotation/throwing-motion band work before throwing and saves external-rotation band work for after, calling that post-throw arm care.
- [04:12](https://youtu.be/uyn9iqFavTw?t=252) Med ball throws, done three times a week, are described as a big part of his training for rotating fast enough to throw hard.
- [05:13](https://youtu.be/uyn9iqFavTw?t=313)-[06:14](https://youtu.be/uyn9iqFavTw?t=374) Coach Paul Hall runs a two-knee drill with the glove taken behind the back, intended to delay rotation and stop him opening early from the front side.
- [10:28](https://youtu.be/uyn9iqFavTw?t=628)-[13:36](https://youtu.be/uyn9iqFavTw?t=816) In the bullpen, coaches talk sequencing off a poorly tunneled pitch: go to a sinker/two-seam for a barrel-side miss, or a firm cutter, depending on the hitter's timing.
- [14:36](https://youtu.be/uyn9iqFavTw?t=876) Live AB result: no walks, no hits allowed, one ball hit hard but caught.
- [16:38](https://youtu.be/uyn9iqFavTw?t=998) He normally lifts sets of 80s for six reps, but the weight felt heavier after throwing that day.
- Description: he's cited as a 95+ mph arm.

## Cues
- **"Get back"** - fixes opening/rotating too early from the front side; for pitchers who get "too excited" with the glove side.
- **"Be convicted"** - fixes forcing a pitch into the zone instead of trusting it; for pitchers overthrowing a location.

## Drills
- **Nerve release on scalenes** - hold pressure on a spot on the scalenes and breathe, done pre-throw; builds internal rotation (claimed 10-15 degrees); for pitchers who lack IR.
- **Pre-throw internal-rotation band work** - light bands, throwing-motion movements before throwing, with external-rotation band work saved for after; builds throwing-specific mobility; for use before a bullpen or live outing.
- **Two-knee glove-behind-back throws** - kneeling constraint with the glove taken behind the back; builds delayed rotation from behind the lead leg; for pitchers who open early from the front side.
- **Med ball rotational throws** - thrown three times a week; builds fast rotation to support velocity.

## Evidence cited
- DeRias is described as a 95+ mph arm (video description, no method given).
- Self-reported live AB line: no walks, no hits allowed, one hard-hit ball caught.

## Open questions
- DeRias says he's "not fully there" in confidence on one bullpen pitch despite the stated velo and clean AB line; the gap between stuff and command feel isn't resolved.
- The 10-15 degree internal-rotation gain from the scalene release is self-reported with no measurement method stated.
