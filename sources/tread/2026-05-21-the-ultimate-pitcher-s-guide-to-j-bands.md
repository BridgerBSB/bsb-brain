# The Ultimate Pitcher's Guide To J-Bands

## TL;DR
- Breaks the J-band routine into a joint-by-joint sequence (wrist/forearm, elbow, scapula, shoulder) aimed at driving blood flow and motor control, not strength, before throwing.
- Names three faults he says nearly every pitcher makes: rushing the routine, humeral anterior glide on rows/presses/flies, and finishing scapular movements with the ribcage or spine instead of the scapula.
- Key number: the routine should take 3-5 minutes, not the 1-2 minutes most pitchers spend on it.

## Claims
- [02:06](https://youtu.be/IOCWJKUNoJk?t=126) He estimates 98-99% of pitchers at every level make these band-routine execution mistakes.
- [03:09](https://youtu.be/IOCWJKUNoJk?t=189) Slowing down turns a 1-2 minute routine into 3-5 minutes; that extra time is where the motor-control and activation benefit comes from.
- [04:12](https://youtu.be/IOCWJKUNoJk?t=252) Humeral anterior glide (front of shoulder dumping forward on rows/presses) risks bicep tendon irritation, SLAP tears, and internal impingement when combined with layback.
- [08:22](https://youtu.be/IOCWJKUNoJk?t=502) Wrist/forearm warm-up covers 6 directions (flexion, extension, radial/ulnar deviation, pronation, supination), 5-10 reps each.
- [10:23](https://youtu.be/IOCWJKUNoJk?t=623) The FCU muscle contracts isometrically during layback to stabilize the UCL and offload throwing torque.
- [12:31](https://youtu.be/IOCWJKUNoJk?t=751) Elbow extension in split stance with forward torso lean gets a fuller stretch through the long head of the tricep; 5-10 reps, then 5-10 bicep curls (biceps doubles as a supinator).
- [18:53](https://youtu.be/IOCWJKUNoJk?t=1133) The rotator cuff is 4 muscles (3 posterior: supraspinatus, infraspinatus, teres minor; 1 anterior: subscapularis); elbow extension exceeds 2,000 deg/s and shoulder internal rotation exceeds 4,000 deg/s during the throw.
- [21:00](https://youtu.be/IOCWJKUNoJk?t=1260) He rejects the rule of never taking internal rotation stretches past 90 degrees for a healthy pitcher, arguing you should train the range you actually have.
- [24:11](https://youtu.be/IOCWJKUNoJk?t=1451) Y-raises target the low trap best at 120-135 degrees of scapular upward rotation, matching that muscle's fiber orientation.
- [26:18](https://youtu.be/IOCWJKUNoJk?t=1578) The serratus anterior drill (front raise with reach) is framed as building proximal scapular stability so the arm can move fast distally.

## Cues
- **"Scaps apart, scaps together"** - fixes finishing rows, flies, and presses by extending the ribcage or spine instead of moving the scapula; for pitchers doing a band routine.

## Drills
- **J-band wrist/forearm sequence** - 5-10 reps each of flexion, extension, radial/ulnar deviation, pronation, supination without bands on yet; builds activation of UCL-stabilizing forearm muscles (FCU, pronator teres); for pitchers pre-throw.
- **Scapular CARs** - band-resisted controlled articular rotation of the scapula through full protraction/elevation/retraction/depression; builds scapular control and awareness through its complete range.
- **Shoulder CARs** - ball-and-socket rotation from full flexion into internal rotation and back, controlled at every step; builds full-range control of the glenohumeral joint.
- **Row/fly/press with scap reach-and-squeeze** - split stance, elbows off the body about 45 degrees, scapula reaches out then retracts before the ribcage moves; builds mid/low trap activation and prevents humeral anterior glide.
- **Three-way shoulder internal rotation** - IR trained at three arm angles (outer, middle, inner arc) with control, no 90-degree ceiling for healthy arms; builds subscapularis and posterior cuff strength through full layback range.
- **Y-raise and front-raise-with-reach at knee height** - Y-raise trains low trap at 120-135 degrees scapular upward rotation; front raise with a forward reach and hinge targets serratus anterior; builds the deceleration and scapula-to-ribcage stabilizers.
