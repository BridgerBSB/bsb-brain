# James Karinchak | What Makes Him So Unhittable? | Mechanical Breakdown

## TL;DR
- Karinchak's deception comes from a toe-first, hunched leg lift that looks like a low slot, then a shift to the heel and a near over-the-top slot (6.7 ft release height).
- The high slot is built from trunk tilt with the arm in plane with the shoulders, not from climbing the elbow. It demands hip extension, hip internal rotation, lumbar stability and a strong anterior core.
- Pitch results: 95-97 mph four-seam with about 20.5 in of vertical break, and a curveball with a 57% whiff rate against a 25% league average.

## Claims
- [00:00](https://youtu.be/QRlWLPORH30?t=0) 6'3", 215 lb, drafted 2019. 2020 line as captioned: "267 era" (sic) in 27 innings, 14 hits, 53 strikeouts.
- [01:00](https://youtu.be/QRlWLPORH30?t=60) Throws fastball and curveball about 50% each. Four-seam sits 95-97 with about 20.5 in vertical break. Curveball averages 83 mph (82-85) at about 2357 rpm (captioned "23 57"), so its value is tunneling off the fastball, not spin.
- [02:02](https://youtu.be/QRlWLPORH30?t=122) Uses two locations per pitch, not in and out. He gets strikes low with either pitch, then elevates the fastball or bounces the curveball for swing and miss. Combined whiff rate is 45.5%; curveball alone is 57%.
- [02:02](https://youtu.be/QRlWLPORH30?t=122) Average release height is 6.7 ft, against 6.4 ft for Kershaw.
- [03:02](https://youtu.be/QRlWLPORH30?t=182) In college he threw about 90-94 from a high three-quarter slot with a milder hunch. Since the draft he has exaggerated both the hunched leg lift and the near over-the-top slot.
- [05:05](https://youtu.be/QRlWLPORH30?t=305) His leg lift shifts the center of mass forward onto the toe and quad. The speaker would not recommend this for a normal pitcher; the usual preference is stacked over the back heel. That posture usually leads to a low slot, as with Adam Cimber (captioned "adam simber", sic).
- [06:06](https://youtu.be/QRlWLPORH30?t=366) At the hinge he moves from toe to midfoot/heel and ends in the stacked head-pelvis-foot position. The speaker reads the first move as a fake that sets up hitters for a low slot.
- [07:09](https://youtu.be/QRlWLPORH30?t=429) Athletes often think a high slot means climbing the elbow. The slot should come from contralateral trunk tilt with the arm staying in plane with the shoulders.
- [08:11](https://youtu.be/QRlWLPORH30?t=491) That position needs hip extension, hip internal rotation, lumbar stability, a strong anterior core, and thoracic extension and rotation. Athletes lacking spinal lateral flexion or rotation hike the elbow instead. Tread uses a spinal screen and looks for a smooth low-back curve.
- [10:12](https://youtu.be/QRlWLPORH30?t=612) He holds the hinge on the back leg and keeps pelvis and shoulders closed while moving to the target. The speaker says the mobility and stability cost is why over-the-top slots are rare. At 92-93 without the vertical break, tunneling and slot he would be less effective.
