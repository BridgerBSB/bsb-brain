# Diego Castillo Mechanics | How Does He Throw 100 mph?

## TL;DR
- Castillo's velocity comes from extreme hip internal rotation in the load, a late pelvis release into landing, and an arm that finishes the throw after the body goes quiet.
- Pitchers with about 50-60 degrees of hip IR may do better with the back toe turned in 10-15 degrees, so the pelvis does not over-rotate.
- Separation comes from loading and then releasing the pelvis, not from forcing hips open against closed shoulders.

## Claims
- [[02:02](https://youtu.be/V0TsXflOFd8?t=122)] Many 100 mph throwers stay tall with a quiet head at the leg-lift peak while the pelvis tucks into posterior tilt. The coach's theory is that this pre-loads the hinge into anterior tilt.
- [[03:02](https://youtu.be/V0TsXflOFd8?t=182)] Castillo tips the pelvis about 20-25 degrees at the leg lift. The coach prefers 10-15 degrees of the horizon, but says he makes it up with deep positions in the linear move.
- [[05:03](https://youtu.be/V0TsXflOFd8?t=303)] The load shows extreme hip IR: the pelvis goes from posterior to anterior tilt and counter-rotates over the back femur. The t-spine stays in line with the pelvis. Back view hides the coil.
- [[07:07](https://youtu.be/V0TsXflOFd8?t=427)] With 50-60 degrees of table hip IR, the coach sets the back foot even with the rubber or toe-in 10-15 degrees. This keeps the pelvis more online with the target. His own velocity rose 2-3 mph after the change.
- [[09:09](https://youtu.be/V0TsXflOFd8?t=549)] IR or ER is read from pelvis position relative to the femur, not from shin angle ("vertical shin" teaching).
- [[12:11](https://youtu.be/V0TsXflOFd8?t=731)] The pelvis triggers only a couple of frames before landing. The pelvis unloads and pulls the back hip with it, rather than the knee driving down first. The coach calls this his opinion.
- [[16:17](https://youtu.be/V0TsXflOFd8?t=977)] Forced separation (rollins (sic) style) produces less separation in lab tests than loading and unloading the pelvis, per unnamed biomechanics experts.
- [[18:58](https://youtu.be/V0TsXflOFd8?t=1138)] Thoracic extension and rotation, and 80-90 degrees of cervical rotation, are needed to hold the torso back. Without cervical rotation, players fly open to keep their eyes on target.
- [[20:50](https://youtu.be/V0TsXflOFd8?t=1250)] Glove arm and throwing arm should work in opposition and pair in length (long with long, short with short). They need not mirror.
- [[23:23](https://youtu.be/V0TsXflOFd8?t=1403)] Judge arm timing when the front foot accepts weight, not at first touch. The arm should be the last thing to move.

## Cues
- **"Rotate down into landing"** - fixes inability to get out of the load, forcing hips open, or spinning into a bowing front knee; for pitchers who muscle the hips open
- **"Directing intensity through the tip of the whip"** - fixes a muscled-up finish where torso, head and arm yank together; for pitchers who bring the arm in too soon

## Anti-cues
- **"Force the hips all the way open, force the shoulders all the way closed, hold that"** - taught to fix lack of hip-shoulder separation; actually produces less separation and a robotic delivery; per Tread (Ben Brewster), citing unnamed lab biomechanists

## Drills
- **Back-foot toe-in split test** - turn the back toe in 10-15 degrees and compare with foot even to the rubber; builds a pelvis load that stays more online with the target; for pitchers with 50-60 degrees of hip IR
- **Standing hip IR belt-buckle feel** - stand five feet wide, hinge, rotate the belt buckle about 45 degrees over the back femur; builds understanding of relative hip IR; for athletes confused by shin angle

## Evidence cited
- Castillo 2020: sinker 96.8 mph at 37% usage, four-seam 97 mph at 1.2%, slider 87 mph at 60%+ with 51.2% whiff and .087 opponent average.
- Unnamed lab biomechanics experts on forced vs elastic separation (no data shown).
- The coach's own 2-3 mph jump from a toe-position change.

## Open questions
- The "vertical shin means ER" teaching is contradicted here, with no measured data shown, only a demonstration.
- The late pelvic trigger and hitting-like unload are stated as opinion and observation, not evidence.
