# Struggling to Throw Strikes? | 5 Tips to Improve Command in Pitchers

## TL;DR
- Control (throwing strikes) is mostly a direction problem: a good linear move (the hinge) removes side-to-side misses; arm plane and arm timing are the next two mechanical factors.
- Use at most one internal cue, and make it proximal (hold the hinge). Otherwise go external: blow up the center of the catcher.
- ASMI range for elite arm timing is 36 to 82 degrees of external rotation at front foot contact; a late arm is under about 25 degrees.

## Claims
- [00:13](https://youtu.be/pNT7f_JYIQU?t=13) Control is putting pitches somewhere in the zone; command is locating to a specific spot. The video targets control.
- [02:02](https://youtu.be/pNT7f_JYIQU?t=122) The linear move (hinge) gives direction to the throw and to the rotation that follows. It is the most important mechanical factor for control.
- [04:03](https://youtu.be/pNT7f_JYIQU?t=243) Staying in the hinge keeps the move backside driven and prevents early pelvic and torso rotation (leaking early).
- [05:04](https://youtu.be/pNT7f_JYIQU?t=304) Poor direction shows up as side-to-side misses: low glove side or high arm side. Good direction leaves mostly up/down misses.
- [08:07](https://youtu.be/pNT7f_JYIQU?t=487) Late arm is under about 25 degrees of external rotation at foot contact. Early is about 90 degrees more than two to three frames before contact. ASMI elite range is 36 to 82 degrees.
- [09:08](https://youtu.be/pNT7f_JYIQU?t=548) Elbow should sit near 90 degrees abduction. ASMI elite range is 78 to 98 degrees at foot contact and 80 to 94 at release.
- [10:10](https://youtu.be/pNT7f_JYIQU?t=610) Arm slot is set more by contralateral tilt than by abduction angle.
- [12:11](https://youtu.be/pNT7f_JYIQU?t=731) An early arm misses low and glove side; a late arm misses high and arm side. A 4 oz ball shows the first, a 6 oz ball the second, at the same intent.
- [14:12](https://youtu.be/pNT7f_JYIQU?t=852) Muscling up (torso or arm entering early) breaks the kinematic sequence, costs velocity and repeatability. The D1 example peaked torso before pelvis.
- [20:18](https://youtu.be/pNT7f_JYIQU?t=1218) Three to five internal cues ruin in-season control. Use one proximal internal cue at most; focus externally in games.

## Cues
- **"Set your angle, laser pointer on the front hip"** - fixes direction loss and side-to-side misses; for pitchers who open up out of the leg lift
- **"Ride the hinge as long as you can"** - fixes leaking early and early pelvic rotation; for pitchers with early rotation
- **"Let the rest of the throw unfurl and unfold downhill"** - fixes over-thinking arm and foot position; for pitchers who over-control the delivery
- **"Hold the hinge"** - fixes early opening; for in-season pitchers needing a single internal cue
- **"Stay back over the back foot"** - fixes early weight shift forward; for in-season pitchers needing a single internal cue
- **"Blow up the center of the catcher"** - fixes aiming and fine-spot tension after walks; for pitchers struggling with control
- **"Throw hard easy"** - fixes muscling up and poor sequencing; for daily catch play at 30 to 60 percent effort

## Anti-cues
- **"Where's my front shoulder, glove arm, throwing arm"** - taught to fix mechanical faults in season; actually causes loss of control when three to five internal cues stack; per Ben Brewster
- **"Hit that exact spot / don't walk this guy"** - taught to sharpen command; actually causes the ball to go less where intended as control tightens; per Ben Brewster

## Drills
- **4 oz vs 6 oz ball catch play check** - same intent with a lighter then heavier ball; builds diagnosis of early vs late arm from miss pattern; for pitchers suspecting arm timing issues
- **Hard-easy catch play** - throw hard with ease at 30 to 60 percent effort daily; builds sequencing and repeatability; for pitchers working on muscling up
