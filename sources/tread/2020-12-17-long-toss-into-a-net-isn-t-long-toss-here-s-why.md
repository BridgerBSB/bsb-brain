# Long Toss into a Net Isn't Long Toss | Here's Why

## TL;DR
- Ben Brewster argues that Jaeger-style long toss works because of outdoor feedback and an external focus. Thrown indoors into a net, most of the benefits are lost and all of the cons stay.
- Long toss is a drill, not a core pillar, and it is not right for every pitcher.
- Throw to a person each session if possible, or several times a week at minimum, even at 60 or 90 feet.

## Claims
- [00:00](https://youtu.be/hgFHVHLAjpo?t=0) Indoor long toss alone into a net is not long toss. Jaeger-style means loose extension outward, then lower-trajectory compression throws on the way in, from a crow hop.
- [01:00](https://youtu.be/hgFHVHLAjpo?t=60) Long toss is a drill, like any other throw that is not a full delivery off the mound. It is not for every athlete in their program.
- [02:37](https://youtu.be/hgFHVHLAjpo?t=157) The main pro is loud, instant feedback. At 250 to 350 feet, ball flight, carry and run show what changed from throw to throw, with no radar needed.
- [05:03](https://youtu.be/hgFHVHLAjpo?t=303) Max distance long toss selects for high axis, high spin, efficient throws. Compression throws are a built-in transfer to downhill throwing.
- [06:04](https://youtu.be/hgFHVHLAjpo?t=364) It keeps linear direction without losing rotational intensity, and it gives an external focus.
- [08:06](https://youtu.be/hgFHVHLAjpo?t=486) Cons: uphill, non-specific release angle; may reinforce flaws (uphill landing, low elbow, late arm); crow hop lacks specificity; works against low-axis, low-spin or low-slot pitchers; needs a partner and outdoor space.
- [10:08](https://youtu.be/hgFHVHLAjpo?t=608) Indoors, the feedback, organic ramping, selection, direction and external focus are gone. Compression throws and varied release angles remain.
- [14:10](https://youtu.be/hgFHVHLAjpo?t=850) Pitcher A, who drills indoors and then long tosses outdoors, should make more progress than Pitcher B, who stays indoors with an internal focus. Feedback is critical to motor learning.
- [17:11](https://youtu.be/hgFHVHLAjpo?t=1031) An external focus increases power output. Once the mechanical guard rails are in place, the performance focus should be external.
- [21:18](https://youtu.be/hgFHVHLAjpo?t=1278) Net throwing has a place for patterning early in a session. Indoors, still throw to a partner at 60 to 90 feet.

## Cues
- **"Throw the crap out of the ball as far as you can get it to that target"** - fixes internal focus that limits intensity and power; for athletes who already have their mechanical guard rails in place

## Anti-cues
- none

## Drills
- **Jaeger-style long toss** - outdoors with a partner, ramping out to max distance, then back in; builds feedback, efficiency and intensity; for pitchers whose profile fits high axis and spin, not every pitcher
- **Compression throws (pull downs)** - lower-trajectory throws on the way in from a crow hop; builds transfer from uphill to downhill release; for long toss users
- **Net patterning with internal focus** - plyos or weighted balls into a net for 5 to 10 minutes before outdoor throwing; builds a specific mechanical feel (arm up on time, shoulders closed); for flaws such as flying open or a late arm
- **Broad jump to tape target** - jump, then jump again to tape two inches past that mark; shows external focus beats internal focus; for any athlete

## Evidence cited
- Nolan Ryan said he built his fastball with long toss (cited second-hand).
- Cal "deets" (sic), Minnesota strength coach, bar speed groups: the "move the bar fast" group beat the form-cue groups, "something like 20 to 40 percent" (paraphrased, exact numbers not given).
- Broad jump claim: "99 times out of 100" the external focus wins. No data given.

## Open questions
- The pro and con lists are asserted without data, and Pitcher A against Pitcher B is a hypothetical, not a comparison of real pitchers.
- Long toss is said to work against sinker and low-slot pitchers, which contradicts the common practice of long tossing everyone.
