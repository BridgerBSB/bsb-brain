# How does Walker Buehler throw 100 MPH? | Mechanics Breakdown

## TL;DR
- Ben Brewster breaks down Buehler's delivery as a model of efficiency: level pelvis through the drift and hinge, rotation released rather than forced, and an arm path with no lag.
- Hands over the head is offered as a tool for pitchers whose arm comes to the throw too soon; Tread sees a 1 to 3 mph bump in a good share of athletes who try it.
- Buehler passes 53 of Tread's 54 high-velocity checkpoints; the miss is a front leg that does not extend ("paw back") at release.

## Claims
- [03:02](https://youtu.be/6HalpY3JXlM?t=182) Hands over the head helps athletes who feel flat-footed, tense the arm, or rotate pelvis and torso together. Tread bases this on nearly 1,000 athletes coached over five years.
- [04:03](https://youtu.be/6HalpY3JXlM?t=243) With hands rising as the leg lifts, the hands move opposite the leg. This breaks the tie between leg lift and hand break, which otherwise brings the arm into the throw too soon.
- [05:03](https://youtu.be/6HalpY3JXlM?t=303) Many athletes see a 1 to 3 mph bump (not 5 to 10). Split test it: 10 throws, or a couple of days of catch play, not an in-season overhaul.
- [06:03](https://youtu.be/6HalpY3JXlM?t=363) In the drift he keeps contact with the back leg and the pelvis stays level. Tipping the pelvis uphill early makes the back-leg load and hinge impossible and the pitcher pushes off into landing.
- [07:05](https://youtu.be/6HalpY3JXlM?t=425) The drop is the hinge: the pelvis locks into the back hip and holds tension ("finding the glute").
- [08:07](https://youtu.be/6HalpY3JXlM?t=487) The pelvis stays within about 10 degrees of the horizon down the mound, giving a stable position to unload from.
- [08:07](https://youtu.be/6HalpY3JXlM?t=487) Muscling the pelvis into rotation lumps the spine and pelvis together and gives a choppy move. Hold tension, then release at the last second. The speaker saw a velocity bump from this in his own career.
- [10:08](https://youtu.be/6HalpY3JXlM?t=608) From hand break through flip up, layback, acceleration and release, the arm should never lag or stop moving.
- [12:11](https://youtu.be/6HalpY3JXlM?t=731) Buehler passes 53 of 54 checkpoints; the only gap is front leg block.
- [13:12](https://youtu.be/6HalpY3JXlM?t=792) He sinks a couple of degrees into the front leg and holds that angle without the usual knee extension at release. Hard throwers usually extend.

## Cues
- **"hands over the head"** - fixes arm coming into the throw too soon and pelvis and torso rotating together; for flat-footed or less athletic pitchers who tense the arm
- **"capturing the momentum of the downswing"** - fixes lags or hitches in the arm path from hand break to release; for pitchers with a tense or hitchy arm action
- **"release into rotation"** - fixes forcing the pelvis through rotation; for pitchers who muscle the turn (framing: hold the tension, let it go at landing)

## Anti-cues
- none

## Drills
- **Hands-over-the-head walking wind-up** - walking or full wind-up with hands overhead, 10 throws in a pen or a few days of catch play to split test; builds segmentation of arm from lower half and rhythm; for athletes who tie hand break to leg lift
- **Figure-eight arm path variant** - add a figure-eight path as the movement trigger in rocker, rolling or walking wind-up drills; builds sync of arm swing with torso and lower half without early arm entry; for pitchers with a lag or hitch in the arm action

## Evidence cited
- Buehler: 97 mph average fastball, 62% usage, 6'2" 185 lb, 3.15 ERA over 365 IP. Came back from Tommy John 3 to 4 mph harder than his pre-draft 92 to 96 range.
- Tread's 54-checkpoint model: Buehler meets 53.
- Bartolo Colon cited as another triple-digit thrower without the paw back.
- The 1 to 3 mph bump is Tread's coaching experience; the speaker says no paper exists.

## Open questions
- The 1 to 3 mph bump from hands over the head is asserted from coaching experience only, with no data or sample size.
- Buehler and Colon throw very hard without the paw back, which sits against the claim that high-level throwers usually show it.
