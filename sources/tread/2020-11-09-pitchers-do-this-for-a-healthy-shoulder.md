# Pitchers... Do THIS for a Healthy Shoulder

## TL;DR
- Ben Brewster teaches ball-based tissue work for the posterior rotator cuff (supraspinatus, infraspinatus, teres minor) to improve extensibility and shoulder internal rotation.
- Do it 2-3 times per week for 2-5 minutes; a tender spot should drop from 7-9/10 to 2-4/10 pain when it has been worked.
- Keep it light (about 1 minute, 5/10) before high-intensity throwing, and save aggressive work for the day after a start or hard bullpen.

## Claims
- [00:00](https://youtu.be/DlbkH2aFLac?t=0) The posterior cuff externally rotates the shoulder and decelerates internal rotation, so its tissues lengthen during the deceleration path.
- [00:00](https://youtu.be/DlbkH2aFLac?t=0) It also acts as a dynamic stabilizer, keeping the humeral head centered in the socket as the arm goes overhead into layback. It needs to be strong, stable and able to lengthen.
- [01:01](https://youtu.be/DlbkH2aFLac?t=61) Better tissue and fascial quality is said to improve muscle fiber contraction, so it matters for both cuff strength and internal rotation.
- [01:01](https://youtu.be/DlbkH2aFLac?t=61) Start with a tennis ball or other soft ball. Progress to a lacrosse ball, then a golf ball, after a few weeks or a couple of months. Baseball and softball are too unforgiving to start.
- [02:01](https://youtu.be/DlbkH2aFLac?t=121) Lie on your side and roll the groove of the posterior shoulder, hunting for a tender or dense spot. Teres major can be explored lower down.
- [03:02](https://youtu.be/DlbkH2aFLac?t=182) Friction a spot with micro movements. A drop from 7-9/10 to 2-4/10 pain over 2-5 minutes means that area is done.
- [03:02](https://youtu.be/DlbkH2aFLac?t=182) Pin and stretch: pin the spot, then pull cross-body with small mobilizations. Yanking the elbow high impinges the front of the shoulder.
- [04:03](https://youtu.be/DlbkH2aFLac?t=243) Third option: keep the spot pinned while going through external and internal rotation. If the spot slips away, you have to find it again. If the shoulder pinches, open the body up a little for more room.
- [04:03](https://youtu.be/DlbkH2aFLac?t=243) All three can fit in one 2-3 minute set.
- [05:03](https://youtu.be/DlbkH2aFLac?t=303) Avoid heavy work right before a pull-down day, mound velo or a game, especially if new to it. Pre-throw, stay near 1 minute at 5/10. Do aggressive work the day after a start or high-intensity bullpen.

## Cues
- **"sink into it as deep as you can tolerate"** - fixes dense, tender spots in the posterior cuff; for pitchers limited in shoulder internal rotation
- **"don't obliterate that tissue"** - fixes over-aggressive tissue work right before high-intensity throwing; for pitchers new to tissue work

## Anti-cues
- none

## Drills
- **Posterior cuff ball friction** - side-lying on a tennis or soft ball, micro movements across a dense spot; builds tissue hydration and extensibility; for limited internal rotation or gritty cuff adhesions
- **Posterior cuff pin and cross-body stretch** - pin the spot on the ball, small cross-body pulls; builds lengthening under load; for tight posterior cuff
- **Posterior cuff pin with ER/IR** - hold the spot while rotating externally and internally; builds cuff extensibility through rotation; for tight posterior cuff

## Open questions
- The claim that better tissue quality improves muscle fiber contraction is asserted without a study.
- The pre-throw limit (1 minute at 5/10) is a subjective rule of thumb with no data behind it.
