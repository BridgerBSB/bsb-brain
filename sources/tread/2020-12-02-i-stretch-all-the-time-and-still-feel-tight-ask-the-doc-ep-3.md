# I stretch all the time and still feel tight! | Ask The Doc Ep. 3

## TL;DR
- A muscle that feels tight is often being over-recruited to cover for missing joint stability elsewhere, so smashing or stretching it treats the symptom.
- The nervous system will not authorize more mobility until the joint feels stable and controlled.
- If tightness keeps coming back, get a whole-body evaluation to find the source instead of repeating local tissue work. No numbers are given.

## Claims
- [00:00](https://youtu.be/2ZRoBr9yWVc?t=0) The common reflex for a tight trap, hamstring or hip is to mash it with a lacrosse ball or static stretch it. The host asks how an athlete knows the muscle is actually tight, whether that matters, and whether the cause is local.
- [01:00](https://youtu.be/2ZRoBr9yWVc?t=60) The doc does not dispute that the muscle feels tight to the athlete. His guiding question is "why is that muscle tight".
- [01:00](https://youtu.be/2ZRoBr9yWVc?t=60) The brain and nervous system are built to protect joints and tissues, and will not authorize more mobility unless the joint is stable and under control through that range ("joint stability over mobility").
- [02:01](https://youtu.be/2ZRoBr9yWVc?t=121) In his experience a tight trap is being recruited excessively to make up for another tissue's stability role, for example weak rotator cuff stability or activation.
- [02:01](https://youtu.be/2ZRoBr9yWVc?t=121) The brain has built an alternative strategy that is symptomatic, but it is not a mistake and the muscle is not an enemy to be overpowered.
- [02:01](https://youtu.be/2ZRoBr9yWVc?t=121) The felt discomfort is a byproduct of a lack of function or stability in the tissues and joints that muscle crosses.
- [03:02](https://youtu.be/2ZRoBr9yWVc?t=182) In athletics the body works as a continuum of tissues and joints, not one muscle or joint at a time. The doc compares it to a symphony orchestra.
- [03:02](https://youtu.be/2ZRoBr9yWVc?t=182) A qualified coach or therapist can give an objective read, such as "this hip problem is where the issue developed because of other dysfunction".
- [03:02](https://youtu.be/2ZRoBr9yWVc?t=182) Smashing feels good at the time but may not change function, and changing function is what ends the cycle of repeated intervention.
