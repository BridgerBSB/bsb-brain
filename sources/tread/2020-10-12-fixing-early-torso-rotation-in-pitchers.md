# Fixing Early Torso Rotation in Pitchers

## TL;DR
- Early torso rotation is trunk rotation that starts before foot plant. It raises elbow and shoulder torque and cuts hip-shoulder separation.
- Back knee drive, front knee collapse and a pushing elbow are usually downstream of it, so fix the upstream flaw first.
- Four causes to sort: muscling up, mobility (hip internal rotation, t-spine rotation), early glove arm supination, and a patterning or awareness gap.

## Claims
- [00:00](https://youtu.be/CGawW4bnssU?t=0) Early torso rotation is trunk rotation starting before foot plant. The ideal is to hold the torso until after foot plant, then bring it in.
- [01:01](https://youtu.be/CGawW4bnssU?t=61) Pitchers who rotate before foot plant show a large increase in elbow and shoulder torque (studies not named). It also limits dynamic hip-shoulder separation, so velocity is lost.
- [03:02](https://youtu.be/CGawW4bnssU?t=182) Command suffers because the pitcher either becomes a pusher/dart thrower or yanks off and over-rotates.
- [05:04](https://youtu.be/CGawW4bnssU?t=304) Back knee drive is a symptom of the forward-flexing, rectus abdominis-driven follow-through. Reverse lunges will not fix it.
- [06:04](https://youtu.be/CGawW4bnssU?t=364) Lead leg block comes from the pelvis finishing its rotation and pulling the femur into extension, not from slamming the quad. Landing open collapses the front knee.
- [08:07](https://youtu.be/CGawW4bnssU?t=487) A pushing elbow can come from wrist weights or heavy plyo care (sic) drills, but often clears up once the linear follow-through is fixed.
- [09:08](https://youtu.be/CGawW4bnssU?t=548) Muscling up means throwing too early. It is one of four contributing factors, along with mobility, early glove arm supination and patterning.
- [12:09](https://youtu.be/CGawW4bnssU?t=729) Forcing hip-shoulder separation tends to produce less of it. Cues that pull the athlete down into release teach forward flexing instead of rotating around the spine.
- [17:13](https://youtu.be/CGawW4bnssU?t=1033) Tread screens each athlete with 40+ movement videos and pairs each mechanical flaw with a mobility, stability or strength cause.
- [21:16](https://youtu.be/CGawW4bnssU?t=1276) Relaxing the hips and low back let the speaker throw 2-4 mph harder at lower effort.

## Cues
- **"Allow the arm to work"** - fixes muscling up and throwing too soon; for pitchers who tense the arm and pull the glove side
- **"Puppet master"** - fixes arm-driven early arm swing; scapula leads and the arm follows; no population stated
- **"Show your numbers to the target as long as possible"** - fixes early torso opening; also phrased as holding the glove-side scap to the target; for athletes with enough counter rotation
- **"Holding internal rotation torque"** - fixes early glove arm supination; for athletes with adequate shoulder internal rotation
- **"Break with the thumb down"** - fixes early glove arm supination; simpler wording for younger athletes

## Anti-cues
- **"Pull down and into ball release" / "pull the lampshade"** - taught to fix a loose finish through release; actually causes forward flexing with the abs instead of rotating around the spine, feeding early rotation; per Ben Brewster, Tread

## Drills
- **Dissociation feels** - stand and move the pelvis without moving the torso; builds pelvis-torso separation; for athletes with early torso rotation, also used as an assessment
- **Swiss ball rotations** - upper half held back against a Swiss ball to a cable stack or band; builds the feel of the pelvis firing under a held-back torso; for throwers and hitters
- **Medicine ball throws holding torso back** - hold the torso closed as the pelvis goes, then fire; builds the same sequencing at speed

## Evidence cited
- Unnamed studies linking pre-foot-plant trunk rotation to higher elbow and shoulder torque.
- The speaker's own case: about 73 mph in high school, 97 mph flat ground years later, which he attributes to patterning, not mobility.
- Edwin Diaz as an example of extreme glove-arm supination tension.

## Open questions
- The torque studies are not named, so the size of the effect is unverified.
- The claim that forcing separation reduces it rests on one athlete's experience (the speaker's).
