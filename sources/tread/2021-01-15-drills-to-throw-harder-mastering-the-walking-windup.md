# Drills to Throw Harder | Mastering the Walking Windup

## TL;DR
- The walking windup is a normal windup with about 5% more forward momentum from a step in from behind. It is the last drill before throwing, tying the back-chaining drill work to the full delivery.
- It is used to find the right amount of drift: too much and the pitcher falls forward and loses the back leg, too little and the pitcher stalls over the rubber.
- Keep the same leg lift height and tempo as in the delivery. Rushing the drill can raise the radar number without transferring to the mound.

## Claims
- [00:00](https://youtu.be/1bwvha23-Fc?t=0) The drill is the most sport-specific final step after upper half, torso rotation and pelvic rotation drill work, before catch play or a bullpen.
- [00:00](https://youtu.be/1bwvha23-Fc?t=0) Added momentum helps players who feel flat-footed or less athletic out of the delivery.
- [00:00](https://youtu.be/1bwvha23-Fc?t=0) Too much momentum causes falling forward during the leg lift and losing the back leg. Too little causes stalling over the rubber.
- [01:00](https://youtu.be/1bwvha23-Fc?t=60) The step in from behind adds about 5% more forward motion than a normal delivery. The athlete gathers the center of mass, stays tall and stacked, closes off the pelvis and rides the back leg hinge forward.
- [01:00](https://youtu.be/1bwvha23-Fc?t=60) Common mistake: too much momentum, so the pitcher skips the full leg lift and loses contact with the back foot. This may raise the gun reading but does not mean more velocity from the mound.
- [02:00](https://youtu.be/1bwvha23-Fc?t=120) Pitchers with more coil in the leg lift (pelvis rotates) may find entry from behind turns them almost 180 degrees. A 45 degree entry can make closing off the pelvis easier. No right or wrong answer; choose by comfort, repeatability, back-leg stability and radar numbers.
- [03:02](https://youtu.be/1bwvha23-Fc?t=182) Worth trying even for stretch-only pitchers. Not every athlete keeps it in the program, but those who do not use it in games report feeling less flat-footed and carry over the rhythm and weight shift.
- [04:03](https://youtu.be/1bwvha23-Fc?t=243) Starting with hands over the head can give a slight velo uptick for many athletes and acts as a bridge between upper and lower half.
- [04:03](https://youtu.be/1bwvha23-Fc?t=243) Split test variants with 5 to 10 throws each way and compare radar numbers, then check transfer to the delivery.

## Drills
- **Walking windup** - windup with an extra step in from behind, same leg lift height and tempo; builds rhythm, sequencing and the right amount of drift; for pitchers who feel flat-footed
- **Walking windup, 45 degree entry** - step in from a 45 degree angle instead of directly behind; builds a controlled pelvis close-off; for pitchers with a coiled leg lift
- **Walking windup, hands over head** - start with hands over the head, old-school windup; builds connection between upper and lower half; for pitchers who struggle to link the two
