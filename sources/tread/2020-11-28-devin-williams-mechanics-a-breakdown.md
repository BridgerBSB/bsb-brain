# Devin Williams Mechanics | A Breakdown

## TL;DR
- Williams' changeup works because of a 2827 rpm spin rate, a 12.2 mph velocity gap off his fastball, and a side-spin axis near 3:15 to 3:30 on the clock.
- His lower half has minor inefficiencies (early back-foot eversion, a leap off the back leg), offset by a very good lead leg block and a release about a foot closer to the plate.
- Efficiency is not the end-all: pitchers who throw hard enough can be elite compensators.

## Claims
- [00:00](https://youtu.be/68Jbpp43mCs?t=0) 2020 line: 0.33 ERA, 27 IP, 53 K, 53.4% strikeout rate; fastball averages 96.4 mph at 2383 rpm, used 44%.
- [02:01](https://youtu.be/68Jbpp43mCs?t=121) Changeup: 84 mph, used a little over 50%, hitters .032; 12.2 mph gap off the fastball (97th percentile); 40.2 in vertical and 18.1 in horizontal movement.
- [03:02](https://youtu.be/68Jbpp43mCs?t=182) Changeup spin is 2827 rpm, about 1000 rpm above the MLB changeup average and 400 above the next closest pitcher.
- [04:03](https://youtu.be/68Jbpp43mCs?t=243) Ben speculates his changeup arm slot is about 2.4 inches lower than his fastball slot, and lower than in high school, giving more side spin. Williams says it should spin "flat like a saucer" with pronation through the ball; grip matters less.
- [07:04](https://youtu.be/68Jbpp43mCs?t=424) Tanner (pitch design): about 10 in arm-side and about -2 in vertical break, a 3:15 to 3:30 spin direction. Most good changeups he saw with the Angels sat between 12:30 and 3:00.
- [09:19](https://youtu.be/68Jbpp43mCs?t=559) Leg lift: slight forward move, posterior pelvic tilt, stays stacked over the back heel, then a "seesaw" into the hinge. Graded A plus.
- [10:06](https://youtu.be/68Jbpp43mCs?t=606) Early back-foot eversion and internal rotation from the femur moving under a still pelvis, rather than the pelvis turning over a stable leg. Fixing this often gives 1 to 3 mph.
- [13:11](https://youtu.be/68Jbpp43mCs?t=791) The back foot comes off the rubber by about a foot, which costs perhaps 1 to 2 mph. A release a foot closer is worth about 3 mph of perceived velocity.
- [15:14](https://youtu.be/68Jbpp43mCs?t=914) Front foot lands over-rotated open, possibly from the back-leg leap or limited tibial internal rotation. Lead leg block is very efficient: the knee stabilizes in both planes, with a "paw back" pulled into extension.
- [18:00](https://youtu.be/68Jbpp43mCs?t=1080) Longer arm swing with the glove arm working in opposition. Shoulder tilt is matched by the glove elbow and returns near level at landing. Flip-up elbow is near 90 degrees (plus or minus 10 to 15). Layback comes from thoracic extension and upright posture.

## Cues
- **"Throw the chest at the target"** - fixes limited thoracic extension that restricts layback; for certain athletes (the source calls it and "use the sternum to deliver the arm" helpful cues)

## Anti-cues
- **"Hook the rubber"** - intended fault not stated; actually pairs with early back-foot eversion and an early disconnect, which leads to a lunge or push late; per Tanner (Tread), who says "not huge fans" though it works for some pitchers
