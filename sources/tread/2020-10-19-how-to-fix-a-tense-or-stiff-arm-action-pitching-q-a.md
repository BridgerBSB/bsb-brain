# How to Fix a Tense or "Stiff" Arm Action | Pitching Q&A

## TL;DR
- A tense arm action is excess or badly timed muscle tone anywhere from handbrake to acceleration. It has several distinct causes (stab/hook, upper trap shrug, bicep yank, limited scap load, pushing), so it needs a diagnosis, not one drill.
- Diagnose with a movement screen plus mechanics review first: mobility limit versus patterning habit. Heavy weighted balls are named as a common cause of a sudden push pattern.
- Fixes shown: lasso drill, figure eight, abbreviated arm action starts, slow-motion ten toes. One athlete went from 85-87 to sitting 90 mph after a relax cue.

## Claims
- [00:00](https://youtu.be/QrjBZdwFFbI?t=0) Tense arm action is excess or improperly timed tone in any phase. It only needs fixing if the athlete is not where they want to be; Eovaldi stabs and throws 100.
- [03:01](https://youtu.be/QrjBZdwFFbI?t=181) The cause differs by athlete: a mobility limit or a learned pattern. Tread screens about 400 remote athletes and treats the movement screen as non-negotiable.
- [05:03](https://youtu.be/QrjBZdwFFbI?t=303) Stabbing or hooking leaves the arm late and down at landing, so it cannot accept layback.
- [06:03](https://youtu.be/QrjBZdwFFbI?t=363) Stabbing often builds in time for a slow tempo or a stalling lower half; the arm action does not work in isolation from the lower half.
- [07:04](https://youtu.be/QrjBZdwFFbI?t=424) Upper trap dominance is self-perpetuating through throwing, rows and push-ups, so it needs throwing, lifting and mobility work together.
- [08:05](https://youtu.be/QrjBZdwFFbI?t=485) Case: a 6'3", 175 lb pitcher sat 90-91 in the first inning and fell to 85-87 as the trap took over. After strength work and a relaxed arm swing he reached 96 and held velocity deeper into starts.
- [10:08](https://youtu.be/QrjBZdwFFbI?t=608) Yanking into elbow flexion uses effort instead of free gravity and momentum, and "nine times out of ten" is followed by elbow forward, tricep push.
- [11:08](https://youtu.be/QrjBZdwFFbI?t=668) Without scap retraction the pec stretch-shortening effect is never used. Snell is shown floating into deep scap load.
- [13:10](https://youtu.be/QrjBZdwFFbI?t=790) Case: slowing a hurried athlete and cueing relax and float took him from 85-87 to sitting easy 90 with deeper positions. A tensing face is an early-effort sign.
- [13:37](https://youtu.be/QrjBZdwFFbI?t=817) A pushing pattern that appears suddenly is often linked to too-heavy balls (4 lb pivot pick, 2 lb pull-down). Without a mobility issue it can change within a week or two.

## Cues
- **"capturing the momentum of the downswing"** - fixes yanking or stabbing that loses the handbrake energy; for tense arm swings
- **"floating the arm up the spiral staircase"** - fixes upper trap, bicep or tricep effort lifting the arm; for athletes who muscle the flip up
- **"the infinity loop"** - fixes stabbing and lifting instead of one fluid loop of glove and throwing arm; for timing and pushing issues
- **"relax the face, relax the tongue"** - fixes upper trap tension and early effort; for athletes who tighten as the arm lifts
- **"relax into layback"** - fixes pushing into acceleration; for athletes with a bad habit and no mobility limit

## Anti-cues
- none

## Drills
- **Lasso drill** - start at landing with shoulders closed and arm up, rotate and let the arm lag then whip; builds relaxed layback and acceleration; for pushing or tense acceleration
- **Figure eight drill** - add a figure eight downswing before the throw; builds feel for arm swing fluidity and momentum; for stab, hook or yank
- **Abbreviated arm action variants** - start at 90 degrees elbow flexion and 90 abduction, then rocker, pivot pick or ten toes; builds arm on time at landing; for late arm at landing
- **Ten toes slow-motion build** - slow motion first, then 20%, 30%, 40%, 50% up to full speed, with 20 throws into the session; builds arm and torso timing; for tension in the arm path
- **Modified starting position with backwards chain** - skip the handbrake, start up at landing, then add the swing back in; builds the feel of elbow in line at landing; for stabbers and hookers

## Evidence cited
- Nathan Eovaldi: stabbing arm action, 100 mph, works for him.
- Unnamed drafted pro: 90-91 falling to 85-87, later 96 after trap and relax work.
- Remote athlete: 85-87 to sitting 90 after a relax and float cue; another changed in 15 minutes.
- Blake Snell shown as an example of free scap load.

## Open questions
- The "nine times out of ten" yank-to-push link and the claim that heavy balls trigger a protective push pattern are asserted without data.
- Presented as a Q&A overview, with the full fix sold through screens and remote coaching; the case studies are single athletes.
