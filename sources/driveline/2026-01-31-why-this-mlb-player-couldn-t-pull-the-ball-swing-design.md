# Why This MLB Player Couldn't Pull the Ball (Swing Design)

## TL;DR
- Trey Lipscomb's 2024 trouble pulling the ball in the air came from a lack of space and rotation, not bat-to-ball skill; he compensated by manipulating the barrel with his hands.
- The session builds space and coil through a drill progression (offset closed, step behind, pivot pick, hookum poppy) so the barrel can turn around the spine and work uphill instead of down.
- Central cue: stop turning up to add lift; turn the body down like hitting a ground ball, or the pelvis rises and the bat path has to work down to stay on plane.

## Claims
- [00:02](https://youtu.be/loijBoVRwg8?t=2) Trey's 2024 struggles came from a lack of space and improper rotation, not hand-eye skill; he leaned on his hands to deliver the barrel, which made pulling the ball in the air inconsistent.
- [00:02](https://youtu.be/loijBoVRwg8?t=2) Offset closed starts Trey closed so he has to rotate further to reach a good position before contact, building comfort with getting open.
- [02:03](https://youtu.be/loijBoVRwg8?t=123) A common mistake when hitters chase lift is turning up with the body, which early-extends the pelvis and loses posture, forcing the bat path down to stay on plane since the body is rising.
- [04:04](https://youtu.be/loijBoVRwg8?t=244) Step behind gets hitters into a hinge and shifts center of mass into the heels, creating space; the drill's angled pitch exposes any drift toward the plate immediately.
- [07:06](https://youtu.be/loijBoVRwg8?t=426) When late, Trey leaned his torso back toward the catcher to buy time, which shifted his spine angle and rotation and forced a hands-driven, flat bat path with low attack angles.
- [09:12](https://youtu.be/loijBoVRwg8?t=552) Pivot pick starts with the back foot normal and the front side fully open, forcing an active coil into the load and separating the load/coil phase from the stride.
- [11:14](https://youtu.be/loijBoVRwg8?t=674) Trey's felt "wrap" behind his head wasn't an upper-body issue; it came from continuing to coil his pelvis (and torso) during the stride to stay stable, a compensation for an unstable hinge/coil in the load.
- [14:25](https://youtu.be/loijBoVRwg8?t=865) Trey reports that being early kept results good, but being late is when the swing broke down; the plan is to intentionally practice missing out in front to retrain timing.

## Cues
- **"Turn your body like you're going to hit a ground ball"** - fixes turning up / early pelvis extension when chasing lift; for hitters whose pelvis rises and flattens their bat path.
- **"Throw that ass back"** - fixes rocking forward instead of coiling into the hinge; used during the pivot pick drill.
- **"Stay stacked"** - fixes hanging back as a compensation for drifting; for a hitter overcorrecting a drift habit by leaning back.
- **"Miss by over rotating like a [ __ ] madman"** - fixes tentative, undercommitted reps; used during the hookum poppy drill to encourage full rotation.

## Drills
- **Offset closed** - hitter starts closed and must rotate further to reach a good position before contact; builds comfort rotating and creating space; for hitters who lack space/rotation and over-rely on hands.
- **Step behind** - step-behind into a no-stride swing, heel free to lift; builds hinge feel and shifting center of mass into the heels; for hitters needing more space in the load.
- **Pivot pick** - back foot set normal, front side starts fully open, forcing a coil into the load; builds separating the coil/load phase from the stride; for hitters who wrap the bat by continuing to coil during the stride.
- **Hookum poppy** - lower body does a hookum (get down at setup, don't stand tall), upper body does a poppy (hands start low, pull into space, swing open); builds blending drill-built separation back into a fuller, more game-like rotation.
- **Long bat** - long bat used for game swings off the tee/flips after the drill series; builds transferring the session's space and rotation work into a live swing feel.
