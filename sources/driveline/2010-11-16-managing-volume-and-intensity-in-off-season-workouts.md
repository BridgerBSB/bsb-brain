# Managing Volume and Intensity in Off-Season Workouts

## TL;DR
- Volume and intensity in an off-season program depend on each athlete's training history, age, skill level and goals, so one group can run very different programs.
- The post gives one sample: a 6-week, 4-day lifting cycle for a reasonably strong high school track-and-field athlete who came from baseball.
- Athletes tolerate more volume and intensity as they add bar weight, add fastball velocity and cut sprint times, and the coach has to know when to add work and when to deload.

## Claims
- Intro: Athletes in the same semi-private group have different training history, age, skill level and goals, so their volume and intensity vary greatly.
- Sample program: The cycle is 6 weeks, written as sets x reps, and was the athlete's first modification from baseball training to field athletics. The author says it is no longer current.
- Tuesday, pressing strength and power: bench press 3x5, overhead press 3x5, power snatch 6x2, power clean 5x3.
- Wednesday, lower body strength: front squat 5x3, Pallof press 2x5 each side with a 5 second hold, depth shock jumps 5x3, single-leg DB lunge 2x5 each side.
- Friday, mixed strength and power: overhead press 3x5, power snatch 6x2, power clean 5x3, deadlift 2x3.
- Sunday, lower body strength and dynamic effort: back squat 3x5, speed deadlift with bands 8x2 on the minute, speed bench press 8x3 on the minute with varied grips, depth rebound jump 5x3.
- All days: mobility and flexibility work (bands, foam rolling, stretches), sled push or pull for conditioning, medicine ball work, and chin-ups or pull-ups scattered through the day in a Grease the Groove fashion.
- Progression: As clients add weight to the bar, add velocity to fastballs and cut sprint times, they can tolerate more volume and intensity, in season and off-season.
- Progression: Knowing when a client can take on more work matters, as does knowing when to deload. Athletes nearing intermediate status need more assistance exercises to modulate intensity.
- Sample program: Exercise selection has since changed because his bench is stalling while his Olympic lifts rise quickly.
