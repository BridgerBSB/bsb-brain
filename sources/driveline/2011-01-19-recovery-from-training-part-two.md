# Recovery From Training: Part Two

## TL;DR
- Novice lifters recover quickly from heavy compound lifts. As they get stronger they become intermediates and must spread intensity across the week instead of maxing out every session.
- For baseball players, the principle is to consolidate stressors: put max-intensity throwing on the same day as med ball volume and bench, and put heavy lower-body work on light throwing days.
- The one concrete template is the Texas Method week: 5x5@85% Monday, 2x7@55% Wednesday, 1x5@95% Friday.

## Claims
- **Attenuation of Intensity for Intermediate Lifters:** Novices can do compound lifts often without systemic overtraining, but this quick recovery fades as they get stronger.
- **Attenuation of Intensity for Intermediate Lifters:** Novices on Starting Strength find early sessions too short, but within a few months a three-lift session takes over 75 minutes.
- **Attenuation of Intensity for Intermediate Lifters:** Becoming an intermediate is not tied to a particular lift total. The author calls the threshold "largely congenital in nature."
- **Attenuation of Intensity for Intermediate Lifters:** An intermediate cannot recover from squatting heavy three times a week and deadlifting every other workout day. Intensity has to be attenuated with accessory lifts and changes to rep/set schemes.
- **Attenuation of Intensity for Intermediate Lifters:** The Texas Method example is squat 5x5@85% (volume), 2x7@55% (recovery), 1x5@95% (intensity). Wendler's 5/3/1 is the other example: one lift per day, four days per week, with added accessory work.
- **Attenuation of Intensity for Intermediate Lifters:** The aim is a positive training stimulus spread over the week rather than one workout. Done correctly this phase can last years, and for most casual lifters a lifetime.
- **Relating it to Baseball:** Pitching and hitting are largely ballistic/anaerobic and need speed-strength, so anything that affects power development affects the player.
- **Relating it to Baseball:** Knowing what the body can and cannot adapt to usually requires "a fair amount of experimentation."
- **Relating it to Baseball:** Consolidate rapid elbow extension stress into one workout, for example max-intensity bullpen, then volume med balls, then bench press.
- **Relating it to Baseball:** On light throwing days, do heavy squat/deadlift worksets and vertical leap efforts, leaving recovery time before max-intensity long toss or the weighted ball program.

## Open questions
- The claim that the intermediate threshold is "largely congenital" is asserted without evidence, and the post gives no way to tell when a player has crossed it.
- The consolidation scheme for throwers is presented as a good idea with no data, and the author says it needs experimentation. It is not tested against alternative arrangements.
