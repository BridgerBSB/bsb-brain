# Why Swinging Faster Doesn't Mean Missing More | How The Blue Jays Fixed Their Hitters

## TL;DR
- Driveline's Director of Hitting argues that training bat speed does not trade away contact, pointing to the 2025 Blue Jays, who jumped to a 71.9 mph average postseason bat speed (5th among playoff teams) while posting MLB's lowest strikeout rate.
- 2025 league data shows bat speed correlates weakly with whiff rate (r²=0.27) but moderately with exit velocity (r²=0.46) and barreled-ball rate (r²=0.48), meaning faster swings mostly help rather than hurt.
- The best batted-ball outcomes come from swinging at roughly 90% of max bat speed in games; all-out reps are a training tool, not the in-game approach critics assume.

## Evidence cited
- 2025 MLB-wide bat tracking data: bat speed vs. whiff rate r²=0.27, vs. exit velocity r²=0.46, vs. barreled-ball rate r²=0.48.
- Blue Jays 2025 postseason: 71.9 mph average bat speed (5th among playoff teams), lowest strikeout rate in MLB.
- 17 of MLB's top 20 hitters by "Wolber" (sic) had above-average bat speed this season.
- Named athletes: Brent Rooker (Driveline Arizona assessment), Shohei Otani (4th MVP in 5 years after training with Driveline), Mookie Betts and Nolan Arenado (worked with Driveline to extend peak years).

## Open questions
- The r-squared figures are stated without naming the sample, season scope, or methodology behind them.
- The video asserts "swinging out of your shoes" builds transferable bat speed but gives no criteria for how much max-effort work is appropriate versus when it risks mechanical breakdown.
