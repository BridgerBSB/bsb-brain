# Mechanics: The Effects of Loading Rate

## TL;DR
- Argues that injury risk in the pitching arm depends on how fast bone and connective tissue are loaded, not just the peak stress value they reach.
- Cites Sabick et al.: pitchers with a more extended elbow at stride foot contact tended to have lower peak humeral torque.
- Cites Aguinaldo et al.: increased elbow flexion correlates with reduced elbow valgus torque, so full elbow extension is not necessarily the safer position overall.

## Claims
- **Mechanics: The Effects of Loading Rate** - Sabick et al. found pitchers whose elbows were more extended at stride foot contact tended to have lower peak humeral torque.
- **Mechanics: The Effects of Loading Rate** - Aguinaldo et al. found increased elbow flexion is closely related to reduced elbow valgus torque, so an extended elbow throughout the motion is not necessarily ideal.
- **Mechanics: The Effects of Loading Rate** - bone is sensitive to loading rate, not just peak stress; the faster and harder the humerus and connective tissue are loaded, the greater the injury danger.
- **Mechanics: The Effects of Loading Rate** - Sabick et al. caution that comparing dynamic pitching biomechanics data directly to in vitro bone-strength data oversimplifies the problem, since lab and in-game loading rates differ and multiple force and torque components act on the bone at once.

## Evidence cited
- Sabick et al., "Humeral Torque in Professional Baseball Pitchers" - lower peak humeral torque associated with more elbow extension at stride foot contact.
- Aguinaldo et al., "Correlation of Throwing Mechanics With Elbow Valgus Load in Adult Baseball Pitchers" - increased elbow flexion associated with reduced elbow valgus torque.

## Open questions
- Sabick et al. favor elbow extension at foot contact (lower humeral torque) while Aguinaldo et al. favor elbow flexion (lower valgus torque); the post does not reconcile which elbow position carries less overall injury risk.
- A reader comment flags a typo in the Aguinaldo abstract's sidearm-pitcher finding ("reduced" should read "higher" elbow valgus torque), a correction not reflected in the original post.
