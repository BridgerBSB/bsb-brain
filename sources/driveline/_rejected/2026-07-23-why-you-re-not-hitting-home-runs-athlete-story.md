# Why You're Not Hitting Home Runs (Athlete Story)

## TL;DR
- Teagan Scott's swing was rebuilt at Driveline around posture timing and pull-side power; he went from 2 home runs to 11 with a 1.890 OPS in one year.
- Staff identify the pre-Driveline fault as setting posture too late, then diving in and making a big move back out, which capped his ability to drive the ball to the pull side.
- The rebuild caused a short-term consistency dip in his first year before the payoff showed up the following season.

## Claims
- [00:00](https://youtu.be/Ury29iTiTxo?t=0) Teagan says his swing had been shaped entirely by his parents' coaching before Driveline and he needed an outside diagnosis of "what's really wrong with it."
- [00:00](https://youtu.be/Ury29iTiTxo?t=0) He recalls facing a pitcher throwing 92 mph as a sophomore while swinging the bat at "63 miles an hour" (sic); this year he describes "seeing 92 down the middle" differently.
- [01:00](https://youtu.be/Ury29iTiTxo?t=60) His first assessment included deadlift-for-power testing; he compares his number unfavorably to teammate Wilson Webber (Oregon State), no figures given.
- [01:00](https://youtu.be/Ury29iTiTxo?t=60) Staff describe his pre-Driveline approach as "hit a line drive up the middle" regardless of exit velocity or bat speed, a philosophy from his parents.
- [01:00](https://youtu.be/Ury29iTiTxo?t=60) Staff name the fault: he set his posture too late, "dive in and then make a big move back out," which distorted his path and limited pull-side power.
- [02:02](https://youtu.be/Ury29iTiTxo?t=122) During his first full offseason of training (junior year), his power numbers rose but swing consistency suffered through the change.
- [03:05](https://youtu.be/Ury29iTiTxo?t=185) After one year training at Driveline, his senior year line was .568/.694/1.200/1.890 with 11 home runs, up from 2 the prior year.
- [04:05](https://youtu.be/Ury29iTiTxo?t=245) He credits increased bat speed with balls now carrying to right field "400 feet," which he calls new territory for his game.

## Open questions
- The improvement is a single-subject before/after story with no control for physical maturity, competition level, or other coaching; the claim that the swing rebuild alone drove the OPS jump is asserted, not demonstrated.
- Staff frame the junior-year consistency dip as an expected cost of "breaking ingrained movement patterns," but give no data on how common or how long that dip typically runs.
