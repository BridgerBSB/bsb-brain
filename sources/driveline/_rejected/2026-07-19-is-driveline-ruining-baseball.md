# Is Driveline RUINING Baseball?

## TL;DR
- Kyle Boddy says "Driveline" has become a generic industry term, comparing it to Xerox or Kleenex, used to label both practices and entire teams.
- Internal counts: all 30 MLB teams employ at least one former Driveline staffer, and 80-100 former employees now work in pro baseball, with the Red Sox having the most at roughly 19-20.
- Driveline says it stays rigid about data collection (hit tracker always on, mandatory Blast use, six-week coach check-ins) but flexible about which drills and cues coaches actually use.

## Claims
- [00:00](https://youtu.be/ebnUZmdfqQI?t=0) A Braves scouting director over 10 years ago said he couldn't visit a field without seeing plyo balls and joked about teams' "comfort level with Driveline," an early sign of the brand's spread into pro ball.
- [01:02](https://youtu.be/ebnUZmdfqQI?t=62) Boddy compares "Driveline" to Xerox or Kleenex, a brand name that became the generic term; he says this gets Driveline-influenced competitors labeled "Driveline clones."
- [01:02](https://youtu.be/ebnUZmdfqQI?t=62) He says the "Driveline team" label is usually applied to losing teams as a convenient explanation (selection bias), and that all 30 MLB teams now employ at least one former Driveline employee.
- [02:02](https://youtu.be/ebnUZmdfqQI?t=122) He credits Driveline-connected players with a first overall draft pick and multiple Cy Young and MVP winners, without naming them or giving counts.
- [02:02](https://youtu.be/ebnUZmdfqQI?t=122) Between 80 and 100 former Driveline employees now work in professional baseball; the Red Sox have the most, roughly 19-20 as of a recent count, with the Dodgers and Nationals also having "quite a few."
- [03:05](https://youtu.be/ebnUZmdfqQI?t=185) Boddy names two constant principles: data drives every decision, and a strong work-ethic culture; personal opinions among staff otherwise vary.
- [03:05](https://youtu.be/ebnUZmdfqQI?t=185) Driveline is "very rigid" about hitting data: the hit tracker is never turned off, athletes must use Blast, players are assessed periodically, and coaches meet with each athlete every six weeks with results logged for longitudinal review.
- [04:07](https://youtu.be/ebnUZmdfqQI?t=247) Coaching cues and specific drills are flexible and left to individual coaches; Boddy says "we haven't solved player development."
