# Strength Training for Youth Athletes

## TL;DR
- Youth strength training is safe when properly designed; the "it stunts growth" myth has no research backing.
- ACSM, AAP, and NSCA all endorse structured, supervised youth strength programs, and a kid ready for organized sports at 7 or 8 is ready for some form of it.
- Pre-puberty athletes (under biological age 13-14) should use a linear progression model, add load sparingly, and get closer recovery supervision than post-puberty athletes.

## Claims
- **Strength Training for Youth Athletes**: no research study has corroborated the claim that weight lifting stunts growth in youth.
- **Strength Training for Youth Athletes**: per Bergfeld (Cleveland Clinic), ACSM, AAP, and NSCA now support children's participation in "appropriately designed and competently supervised" strength training programs.
- **Strength Training for Youth Athletes**: rule of thumb given: if a 7- or 8-year-old is ready for organized sports or activities like little league or gymnastics, they are ready for some type of strength training program.
- **Strength Training for Youth Athletes**: best results come once an athlete starts producing testosterone (puberty); training before then does not endanger growth plates or bones, it just produces results more slowly.
- **Strength Training for Youth Athletes**: for youth athletes under biological age 13-14, use a linear progression model, add weight sparingly, and closely supervise recovery cycles and novice training protocols.
