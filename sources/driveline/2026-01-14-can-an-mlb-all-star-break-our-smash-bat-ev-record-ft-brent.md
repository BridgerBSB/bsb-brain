# Can an MLB All-Star Break Our Smash Bat EV Record? (ft. Brent Rooker)

## TL;DR
- Six weeks of offseason work with Brent Rooker built around one goal: challenge him without changing the swing that already makes him good.
- The session runs a fixed progression: step-back no-stride, then hook 'em (stride phase), then underload back-shoulder work, then pivot pick, then a smash bat chase.
- Rooker chased Driveline's smash bat exit-velo record and topped out around 119 mph, never confirmed to have broken it.

## Claims
- [00:00](https://youtu.be/tjb9eqleBpc?t=0) Coach's stated goal for the six weeks: challenge Rooker as much as possible without changing the mechanics that already make him effective.
- [01:01](https://youtu.be/tjb9eqleBpc?t=61) On short-bat/no-stride work, the coaching target is barrel entering the zone sooner and staying on plane longer; a held-finish, poolside-flight result is read as a good turn with good posture.
- [02:03](https://youtu.be/tjb9eqleBpc?t=123) The hook 'em drill is used to carry a load-phase fix into the stride phase, testing whether the position holds once he moves forward.
- [03:04](https://youtu.be/tjb9eqleBpc?t=184) Adding more lower-half coil made the drill harder and initially reduced separation, with the upper half dragging along instead of staying back.
- [03:04](https://youtu.be/tjb9eqleBpc?t=184) A step-back no-stride variant from an oppo angle is used because it forces the hitter to open the front side while still staying back.
- [04:04](https://youtu.be/tjb9eqleBpc?t=244) Underload back-shoulder work is used specifically to stop Rooker from swaying over his back knee; he hit 112 mph and topped out at 115 mph on that bat.
- [05:05](https://youtu.be/tjb9eqleBpc?t=305) Pivot pick's most common fault: hitters start the first rep open, then creep the front side forward on later reps; falling forward makes hands take over and lose the turn.
- [05:05](https://youtu.be/tjb9eqleBpc?t=305) The oppo-angle pivot pick is called the most challenging drill in the session because it demands creating separation while holding position behind the pitch, and "messes up almost every guy at first."
- [07:08](https://youtu.be/tjb9eqleBpc?t=428) Smash bat work shifts the emphasis from early-offseason overload training to refining bat-to-ball skill and swing path closer to spring training.
- [08:08](https://youtu.be/tjb9eqleBpc?t=488) Rooker's smash bat exit velocities across attempts: 117, a facility-best reference of 1194 (sic, likely 119.4), then 128, 130, and 119 on later swings, chasing but not confirmed to beat the standing record.

## Cues
- **"Push your back hip back as your first move"** - fixes turning into the pitch too early in the pivot pick drill, to create more space; coach's cue to Rooker.
- **"Hold the ball and catch myself"** - Rooker's self-check for a good turn and good posture during step-back no-stride work.

## Drills
- **Step-back no stride** - step back with no stride; builds using the backside efficiently in the load; first drill of the session.
- **Step-back no stride, oppo angle** - same drill worked to an oppo pitch angle; builds opening the front side while still staying back.
- **Hook 'em** - transitions a load-phase drill into the stride phase; builds carrying load-phase positions into forward movement and timing.
- **Underload back-shoulder work** - underload bat with back-shoulder emphasis; builds staying behind the ball; fixes swaying over the back knee.
- **Pivot pick, underload long bat** - starts from a pivoted/open position; builds creating separation while holding position; fixes early opening and front-side drift.
- **Smash bat** - overload/underload implement swung for max exit velocity; builds bat-to-ball skill and swing path refinement late in the offseason.
