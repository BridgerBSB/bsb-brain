# Swing Design: Fixing an FSU Infielder's Bat Path

## TL;DR
- FSU hitting coordinator Eric Kozak works D1 infielder Cal Fisher through a swing design session to fix a bat path that was working out and around too early and catching the ball deep.
- A drill progression (step-back feet-in-cement, step-behind, Big Papi separation drill) is used to get the hitter to hold ground with the back foot so the pelvis drives rotation, creating room to turn and catch the ball further out front.
- Keeping the barrel tight to the spine reduces its moment of inertia, making it easier to accelerate and release late instead of working out and around early.

## Claims
- [00:00](https://youtu.be/DRGDN8u1EKA?t=0) The target fault: the barrel works out and around too early; the fix drill is an isolated barrel face-off, stepping back with no stride, to keep the hands back and turn tighter before releasing out front.
- [03:05](https://youtu.be/DRGDN8u1EKA?t=185) "Feet in cement" drill: keep the entire foot on the ground on both feet through the swing so the pelvis is the engine of rotation instead of releasing the back foot early to spin.
- [05:11](https://youtu.be/DRGDN8u1EKA?t=311) Step-behind drill (roughly a 45-degree stagger) increases coil, hinges the pelvis down, and shifts the center of mass to create more room to turn through.
- [08:17](https://youtu.be/DRGDN8u1EKA?t=497) "Big Papi" drill: set up closed with the lower body and open with the upper body, then reverse; forces genuine separation and use of the stretch-shortening cycle, which the coach says can't be babied.
- [09:17](https://youtu.be/DRGDN8u1EKA?t=557) The coach reframes "hit the inside part of the ball" as a sight cue, not a literal contact target; turning to the outside part of the ball with this new path causes hooked balls to go foul.
- [11:17](https://youtu.be/DRGDN8u1EKA?t=677) Cue given: keep the back heel on the back of the hitting mat and get the barrel to the front of the mat, to build the intent of getting comically out in front.
- [13:19](https://youtu.be/DRGDN8u1EKA?t=799) Moment of inertia explanation: when the barrel works away from the body early, MOI increases and more force is needed to accelerate it; keeping the barrel tight to the spine (short lever arm) lets the barrel "take over" once already up to speed.
- [14:25](https://youtu.be/DRGDN8u1EKA?t=865) With the long bat (about a foot and a half longer than the short bat used earlier), the target contact point moves roughly two feet further out front than with the short bat.
- [16:26](https://youtu.be/DRGDN8u1EKA?t=986) In-game progression: after the move is dialed in with drills, intentionally seek an out-front miss in batting practice so the body self-corrects timing without conscious swing adjustment.

## Cues
- **"Feet in cement"** - fixes releasing the back foot too early and losing ground force before rotation; for a hitter who pops up or loses the back side during the load.
- **"Keep your back heel on the back of the mat and get the barrel to the front of the mat"** - fixes catching the ball too deep; for a hitter working to extend contact further out over the plate.
- **"Turn to the inside part of the ball"** - reframes the traditional "hit the inside part of the ball" cue as a visual target rather than a literal contact point, to keep the path tight and avoid hooking foul.
