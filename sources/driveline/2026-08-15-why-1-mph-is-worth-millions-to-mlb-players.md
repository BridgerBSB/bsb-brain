# Why 1 MPH Is Worth Millions to MLB Players

## TL;DR
- Driveline treats fastball velocity and bat speed as "the engine": the two base metrics that command, swing decisions, and pitch shape get built on top of.
- One mph of fastball velocity is worth about $3.4M (0.3 WAR/season at $11.2M per WAR); one mph of bat speed is worth about $2.2M (0.2 WAR).
- Four case studies (Cobb, Lopez, Nootbaar, Betts) show real players beating their WAR projections after moving one of these two numbers, in Betts's case by just 1 mph paired with a cleaner bat path.

## Claims
- [00:00](https://youtu.be/gTYcNgBpR0U?t=0) 17 of the top 25 starting pitchers by WAR last year threw with above-average velocity; 19 of the top 25 hitters by wRC+ swung with above-average bat speed.
- [00:00](https://youtu.be/gTYcNgBpR0U?t=0) Adding 1 mph to a fastball raises whiff rate by about 1%, chase rate by about 1%, and drops opponent batting average by about 8 points.
- [00:00](https://youtu.be/gTYcNgBpR0U?t=0) 1 mph of fastball velocity is worth about 0.3 WAR/season for a starting pitcher; 1 mph of bat speed is worth about 0.2 WAR; 1 WAR sells for about $11.2M on the open market.
- [01:04](https://youtu.be/gTYcNgBpR0U?t=64) That prices 1 mph of fastball velocity at about $3.4M and 1 mph of bat speed at about $2.2M.
- [01:04](https://youtu.be/gTYcNgBpR0U?t=64) League-average fastball velocity rose from 93 mph in 2015 to nearly 95 mph now; the gain from 2023 to now equals the previous eight years combined.
- [02:07](https://youtu.be/gTYcNgBpR0U?t=127) Alex Cobb took his sinker from 93 to nearly 95 mph (2021-2022) and beat his projected WAR by almost two wins; Pablo Lopez added 1.5 mph to his four-seam (2022-2023) with a similar result.
- [02:07](https://youtu.be/gTYcNgBpR0U?t=127) Lars Nootbaar raised his bat speed from 66-69 mph to 71-74 mph in a month, then to 74-77 mph over the next couple of years, using weighted bats and a cleaned-up hip-to-shoulder turn; that tracked to about 10 mph more exit velocity and close to 60 feet more carry.
- [03:08](https://youtu.be/gTYcNgBpR0U?t=188) Mookie Betts needed only about 1 mph more bat speed plus a refined bat path (2022 offseason) to add 1.9 mph of average exit velocity and over 2% barrel rate, beating his projected WAR by 2.4 wins.

## Evidence cited
- Driveline internal model: fastball velocity ≈ 0.3 WAR/mph/season ≈ $3.4M/mph; bat speed ≈ 0.2 WAR/mph ≈ $2.2M/mph, based on $11.2M per WAR.
- Alex Cobb (2021-2022): sinker 93 → ~95 mph, beat WAR projection by ~2 wins.
- Pablo Lopez (2022-2023): four-seam +1.5 mph, beat WAR projection by ~2 wins.
- Lars Nootbaar: bat speed 66-69 → 74-77 mph over ~2 years, +~10 mph exit velocity, +~60 ft carry.
- Mookie Betts (2022 offseason): bat speed +1 mph, exit velocity +1.9 mph, barrel rate +2%+, beat WAR projection by 2.4 wins.

## Open questions
- The whiff/chase/batting-average-per-mph figures and the WAR-per-mph conversions are stated without showing the underlying method or sample, so they can't be independently checked.
- "Last year" for the 17-of-25 and 19-of-25 WAR/wRC+ figures isn't dated against the 2026-08 publish date, so the season referenced is ambiguous.
