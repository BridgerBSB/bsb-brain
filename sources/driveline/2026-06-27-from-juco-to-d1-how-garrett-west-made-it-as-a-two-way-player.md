# From JUCO to D1: How Garrett West Made It as a Two-Way Player

## TL;DR
- Case study of two-way player Garrett West, who went from a 3-45 junior college season to a D1 commitment after one offseason at Driveline.
- The value here is workload and cue detail: Pulse band data drove a recovery-day throwing change, and a rotation cue produced two home runs in one round of BP.
- Headline number: 14 home runs and 93 mph off the mound in one summer, up from a career high of 3 home runs before.

## Claims
- [00:04](https://youtu.be/kVGXM7z5lZE?t=4) West went 3-45 as a two-way freshman with no home field, which pushed him to seek more development.
- [01:07](https://youtu.be/kVGXM7z5lZE?t=67) A one-week mocap visit to Driveline in high school put his bat speed at 69-71 mph, below the college and pro comps he was measured against.
- [02:09](https://youtu.be/kVGXM7z5lZE?t=129) Hitting coaches gave him a cue to rotate the chest toward the target and stop rather than over- or under-rotate; in that same session he hit a 112 mph batted ball, then a 114 mph one on the next swing.
- [03:10](https://youtu.be/kVGXM7z5lZE?t=190) Pulse band data showed his recovery-day throwing intensity climbing to 9-13, capping his higher-intent B and A days; the fix was throwing a football instead of a baseball or plyo ball on recovery days.
- [03:10](https://youtu.be/kVGXM7z5lZE?t=190) He was given two cues: "put the tongue in between the teeth" for tensing up, and "the tilt" for staying too neutral, aimed at adding shoulder side bend and mound velocity.
- [04:12](https://youtu.be/kVGXM7z5lZE?t=252) That fall he threw 10 innings with 22 strikeouts and touched 93 mph.
- [04:12](https://youtu.be/kVGXM7z5lZE?t=252) In two months of the season he hit 5 home runs, having never hit more than 3 in a full season before.
- [05:13](https://youtu.be/kVGXM7z5lZE?t=313) He finished with 14 home runs total, including several off breaking balls after targeted work on hitting spin.
- [06:15](https://youtu.be/kVGXM7z5lZE?t=375) In a sophomore-year outing described as a standout, he struck out 15 over 5 innings.
- [07:17](https://youtu.be/kVGXM7z5lZE?t=437) West said the training gave him more confidence on both sides of the ball and helped him separate results from failure; he committed to a D1 program.

## Cues
- **"Put the tongue in between the teeth"** - fixes tensing up in the box and on the mound; for hitters and pitchers who overgrip
- **"The tilt"** - fixes staying too neutral in the delivery (lack of shoulder side bend); for pitchers chasing more mound velocity
- **"Rotate the chest to the traject (sic) and stop it there"** - fixes over-rotating and under-rotating; for hitters

## Drills
- **Football recovery-day throws** - throw a football instead of a baseball or plyo ball on recovery days, workload tracked with a Pulse band; builds recovery capacity so higher-intent B and A days aren't capped; for pitchers managing forearm tightness or overloaded recovery days

## Evidence cited
- Garrett West case: freshman year record 3-45; Driveline mocap on a one-week high school visit measured batspeed at 69-71 mph against college/pro comps; Pulse band showed recovery-day workload at 9-13; that fall he threw 10 IP with 22 K and touched 93 mph, and hit 14 home runs (previous career high 3) while earning a D1 commitment.

## Open questions
- The football-on-recovery-days workload change is presented as effective from Pulse numbers and season outcomes alone, with no comparison group or arm-health follow-up cited.
- The 112-114 mph home run pair is attributed to one rotation cue in a single round of BP, treated as the turning point without ruling out pitch type, count, or fatigue.
