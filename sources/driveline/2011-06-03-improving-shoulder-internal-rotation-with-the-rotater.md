# Improving Shoulder Internal Rotation with The Rotater

## TL;DR
- Most older pitchers show GIRD in the throwing shoulder, but the goal is not equal IR and ER on both sides. The goal is stabilizing total motion between the shoulders.
- Driveline uses the side-lying sleeper stretch and a device called The Rotater to improve IR in the throwing shoulder.
- The Rotater is also used for gentle ER stretching before a bullpen and for the non-dominant shoulder to restore total motion. The post gives no numbers, and it is partly a product recommendation.

## Claims
- Intro: The vast majority of older baseball pitchers show glenohumeral internal rotation deficit (GIRD), citing a JSSM paper on asymptomatic professional pitchers and humeral retroversion.
- Intro: GIRD is described as the dominant shoulder having much higher ER than the glove-side shoulder. This does not necessarily need to be restored to equality.
- Intro: Humeral retroversion and other osseous changes result from throwing stress and may be beneficial overall.
- Intro: Baseball is asymmetrical, so restoring equal ER and IR on both sides is "misguided at best."
- Intro: Stabilizing total motion between the shoulders can have immense benefit (points to Eric Cressey's Optimal Shoulder Performance DVD set).
- Improving Dominant Shoulder Internal Rotation: The side-lying sleeper stretch for the dominant shoulder is a generic stretch they like.
- Improving Dominant Shoulder Internal Rotation: The Rotater is used for IR in the throwing shoulder. Shot put athletes have similar shoulder demands (rapid elbow extension with heavier implements).
- Improving Dominant Shoulder Internal Rotation: The Rotater is used gently for ER stretching of the throwing shoulder before a bullpen, and more aggressively on the non-dominant shoulder to restore total motion.
- Closing: Boddy used the tool for a few months before recommending it and judged it effective and durable.

## Drills
- **Sleeper stretch** - side-lying stretch of the dominant shoulder; builds internal rotation; for throwers with an IR deficit
- **Rotater IR stretch (dominant shoulder)** - IR stretch using The Rotater, demonstrated by Jack; builds throwing-shoulder IR; for throwers with an IR deficit
- **Rotater ER stretch before bullpen** - gentle ER stretch of the throwing shoulder with The Rotater; prepares the shoulder before a bullpen
- **Rotater stretch of non-dominant shoulder** - more aggressive stretch of the glove-side shoulder; builds total motion balance between shoulders

## Evidence cited
- Study: Glenohumeral internal rotation deficit in the asymptomatic professional pitcher and its relationship to humeral retroversion (JSSM, linked in the post).
- Eric Cressey, Optimal Shoulder Performance DVD set, referenced for total motion.

## Open questions
- The post cites the GIRD paper but gives no numbers, so the degree of deficit and any benefit from the stretches are asserted, not shown.
- The recommendation is a product review with affiliate links and no outcome data. It does not say how long or how often to stretch.
