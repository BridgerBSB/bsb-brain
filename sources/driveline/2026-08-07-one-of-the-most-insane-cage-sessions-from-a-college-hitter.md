# One of The Most Insane Cage Sessions From a College Hitter

## TL;DR
- Florida hitting coordinator Eric Kozak runs college hitter Collin Priest through a swing design progression (long bat, underload bat, step-behind/step-in stride constraints, pivot picks) organized around one idea: keep the center of mass in front of the back foot so gravity, not an active shift, pulls the swing forward.
- The core cue is "load the body, save the hands for the forward move" - separation should come from the lower half moving away, not from the hands firing early.
- A small, glute-driven hinge in the load beats a big, hip-flexor-driven one; Kozak grades swings on point-of-contact depth and quality of miss, not just outcome.

## Claims
- [00:00](https://youtu.be/TCkfOmonu_s?t=0) Kozak reframes a deep, out-front point of contact as normal for Priest's pull side and coaches him to "buy time" and get to the pitch rather than defaulting to an early finish.
- [01:00](https://youtu.be/TCkfOmonu_s?t=60) The cleaner path came from treating an early-feeling swing as something to correct in the moment, not as the goal state.
- [01:00](https://youtu.be/TCkfOmonu_s?t=60) Priest reports feeling glute activation and a hinge in his back instead of a squat; Kozak ties this to a hinge-based load versus a hip-flexor-dominant squat load.
- [02:00](https://youtu.be/TCkfOmonu_s?t=120) Kozak says a hitter only needs "a little hinge" in the load, not a massive one, so more hinge can be added later based on the pitch and stride rather than adding squat.
- [03:01](https://youtu.be/TCkfOmonu_s?t=181) The long bat drill produces Priest's deepest pulled contact point of the session, called good ball flight post-stride.
- [05:02](https://youtu.be/TCkfOmonu_s?t=302)-[06:02](https://youtu.be/TCkfOmonu_s?t=362) Switching to an underload bat, the focus cue is to swing the back hip "as far back as possible" every swing, resisting the urge to let the back side shoot and separate early.
- [07:03](https://youtu.be/TCkfOmonu_s?t=423) Kozak swaps the step-behind constraint for step-in because step-behind let Priest's lower body work well but let his chest lean too far forward.
- [08:04](https://youtu.be/TCkfOmonu_s?t=484)-[09:05](https://youtu.be/TCkfOmonu_s?t=545) Repeating long bat with the step-in constraint, Kozak coaches keeping center of mass in front of, never over, the back foot: over the back foot forces an active forward shift; in front of it lets gravity pull the swing forward once the front foot lifts.
- [10:08](https://youtu.be/TCkfOmonu_s?t=608) Even on an early-timed swing, holding the back hip kept Priest from drifting or sliding his hip early, producing a foul-away miss instead of a mishit.
- [11:11](https://youtu.be/TCkfOmonu_s?t=671) Kozak layers a pivot pick constraint, including a lower-grip variant, saying added constraints are meant to make the movement harder while still building the same feel, even when pivot picks have been a struggle for Priest before.

## Cues
- **"Load your body, but save your hands for the forward move"** - fixes early hand release during separation; for hitters building lower-to-upper sequencing.
- **"Keep your center mass in front of your back foot, never over it"** - fixes hip slide/drift that forces an active forward shift; for hitters who get stuck over their back side.
- **"Push your back hip as far back as possible"** - fixes early separation/shooting the back side; for hitters using an underload bat to feel a longer load.
- **"Create a little hinge in your load, not a massive one"** - fixes hip-flexor-dominant squatting in the load; for hitters who need glute engagement instead.

## Drills
- **Long bat hold-and-turn** - swings with a long bat; builds holding space in the load and a tighter turn to contact; for hitters working on holding the load longer before rotating.
- **Underload bat back-hip pull** - same swing pattern with an underload bat; builds resisting early separation and holding the back side longer; for hitters who shoot the back side early.
- **Step-behind load** - stride steps behind at load; builds a strong lower-body load but can let the chest lean too far forward; for hitters with a compact lower half who drift up top.
- **Step-in load** - stride steps in instead of behind; builds staying centered over the back foot instead of leaning the chest forward; swapped in after step-behind caused chest lean.
- **Pivot pick constraint (incl. lower grip)** - pivot-pick stride constraint, sometimes with a lower bat grip; builds holding space and a tight turn under added difficulty; for hitters who need pivot-pick work even when it has been a struggle.

## Open questions
- Kozak treats a deep, "early" point of contact as a good miss and a coaching win rather than a timing flaw to eliminate, which departs from approach coaching that treats being fooled as a problem; asserted from this session only, not checked against outcome data.
- The claim that staying in front of the back foot lets "gravity pull you forward" is given as the mechanical reason for the drill, with no force-plate or motion-capture measurement cited to support it.
