# Olympic Lifts for Baseball Players - Is Power Planar?

## TL;DR
- Driveline argues that Olympic lifts like the power clean stay in a baseball program as general physiological training, even though they are sagittal-plane movements and hitting and pitching are largely transverse and coronal.
- Training should separate general physiological work from sport-specific work, and the two sit on a continuum. The risk is where they overlap, because that is where mechanics get confused.
- Key number: overload baseballs within 10-20% of the 5 oz ball do not meaningfully change throwing mechanics.

## Claims
- **Intro:** Some coaches argue power is bound to the plane of the movement, so Olympic lifts are poor choices for baseball players, especially pitchers. Driveline's older athletes use the power clean as a barbell staple.
- **Planes:** The power clean and maximal vertical jumps are almost entirely sagittal. Pitching and hitting are largely transverse and coronal (trunk and pelvic rotation).
- **General vs sport-specific:** Taking sport-specificity too far is the error. A program should separate general physiological training from sport-specific training.
- **General vs sport-specific:** Squats and deadlifts are also mostly sagittal, and the source says they should not be dropped for that reason.
- **General vs sport-specific:** Power cleans train rate of force development and give the athlete a sense of producing ground reaction force through the "jump" in the second pull.
- **General vs sport-specific:** Swapping cleans for lateral bounds (coronal) and broad jumps (sagittal) ignores the goal of being generally more powerful, strong and athletic.
- **Overload baseballs:** Within 10-20% of the 5 oz baseball, standard throwing mechanics are not meaningfully affected, so the stimulus is largely sport-specific.
- **Overload baseballs:** Beyond that range the throw drifts toward a general stimulus but is still a "throw," so the body may learn something less efficient. Very heavy throws (2 lb, for example) are done as partial movements or in constrained positions such as kneeling.
- **Continuum:** Altering a movement pattern on purpose is fine. An overload ball in limited action can expose an arm action flaw. It is not used on a pitcher with a generally decent arm action, for fear of changing the motion negatively.

## Drills
- **Power clean** - barbell second-pull "jump" in the older athletes' barbell program; builds rate of force development and ground reaction force sense; general physiological stimulus, not sport-specific
- **Kneeling heavy-ball throw** - roughly 2 lb ball, partial or constrained movement such as pitcher kneeling; builds overload stimulus without disturbing standard throwing mechanics
- **Overload ball in limited action** - overload baseball in a limited-action throw; exposes an arm action flaw so the pitcher feels the needed change; for pitchers with a flawed arm action only

## Open questions

- The post argues against "power is planar" but gives no transfer evidence that cleans improve rotational performance. The case rests on a general-versus-specific framing.
