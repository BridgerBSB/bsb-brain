# How a struggling JUCO player worked with Driveline to reach his dream

## TL;DR
- Clarkson had plus bat speed (70.4 mph, 93rd percentile) but a 3-degree attack angle; Driveline's read was that he wasn't using his existing strength efficiently, not that he lacked it.
- A drill progression, a swing-design session, and regular Blast Motion feedback let him retrain his path and monitor it himself once he left the facility.
- After the change, his attack angle rose to 12.5 degrees (bat speed to 74.5 mph, 98th percentile) and he hit .357/.550/.833 in 42 JUCO at-bats, which led to a Division I offer from Stephen F. Austin.

## Claims
- (initial biomechanics assessment) Clarkson posted a 70.4 mph bat speed, 93rd percentile among motion-captured hitters, with a 3-degree attack angle.
- (initial assessment) Robertson attributed the flat path to how Clarkson used the strength he already had, not a lack of it.
- (feel vs. data) Clarkson had modeled his swing on Mike Trout and believed he was replicating it; the data showed he got steep on the way down and only swung up in a brief window, without staying on plane.
- (early drill work) Trainer Jacob Hirsh assigned the Big Papi drill, Pivot Picks, and Hook 'Ems to build hip-shoulder separation and a repeatable load position.
- (return trip, swing-design session) On a later visit, a swing-design session produced what Clarkson called "a moment of clarity," describing a feeling in his hamstring; Robertson frames the fix as steepening the path earlier in the chain with an external target of missing pitches in the air.
- (stress test) Driveline trained Clarkson on the iPitch with high spin and big shapes, intentionally harder than game at-bats.
- (remote training) Robertson says the goal for athletes who leave the facility is that they know what's being changed, why, and how, since coaches aren't in the box with them; Clarkson used Blast Motion regularly to track bat speed, attack angle, and swing path on his own.
- (Paris Junior College, spring season) In 18 games and 42 at-bats, Clarkson hit .357/.550/.833 with 4 home runs, a triple, and six doubles.
- (follow-up assessment) Bat speed rose to 74.5 mph (98th percentile) and average attack angle rose to 12.5 degrees, more than four times his original 3-degree mark; Robertson cites a target range of roughly 6-12 to 8-12 degrees.
- (recruitment) Stephen F. Austin, a Division I program, called Clarkson after his JUCO coach told him to keep his ringer on, and he received a D1 offer.
