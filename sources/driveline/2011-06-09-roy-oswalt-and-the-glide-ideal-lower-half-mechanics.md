# Roy Oswalt and The Glide: Ideal Lower Half Mechanics

## TL;DR
- Boddy holds up Roy Oswalt's "glide" to the plate as the model lower half: the pitcher drifts forward early and does not stop at a balance point.
- Linear momentum helps, but ASMI data and Driveline's preliminary biomechanics say pelvic and shoulder rotational velocity correlate most with fastball velocity.
- Teaching a balance point is called a worthless exercise that turns pitchers linear and costs velocity.

## Claims
- Intro: Oswalt, listed at 6'0" and 190 lbs, is presented as an example of excellent mechanics, and his glide is his best attribute for throwing 90+ mph consistently.
- Intro: "getting the butt down the hill quickly" is a National Pitching Association (NPA) tenet that Boddy agrees with as a cue.
- Intro: the NPA's other concepts tend to produce a very linear pitcher with simple mechanics, which does not produce elite velocities.
- Intro: ASMI research, confirmed by Driveline's preliminary biomechanical work, finds high pelvic and shoulder rotational velocities correlate most with fastball velocity.
- Intro: linear momentum to the plate is good but ranks well below those two rotational variables.
- The Balance Point: teaching pitchers to reach the balance point is a worthless exercise that destroys velocity and makes them linear.
- The Balance Point: it is often used to cure rushing and improve control in youth pitchers, which Boddy calls misguided.
- The Balance Point: focusing on control and command is described as one of the best ways to destroy a youth pitcher's future fastball velocity (aside, no support given).
- The Balance Point: Oswalt and Lincecum "drift" through the balance point and move toward the plate before maximum knee height; Dan Haren is the noted exception.
- The Balance Point: drifting adds linear momentum and lets the pitcher rotate the hips faster and later.

## Cues
- **"getting the butt down the hill quickly"** - fixes slow or absent linear momentum to the plate; for population not stated

## Anti-cues
- **"get to the balance point"** - taught to fix rushing to the plate and poor control in youth pitchers; actually causes loss of velocity and a linear delivery; per Kyle Boddy
