# Is Grip Strength Important for Hitters?

## TL;DR
- Grip strength does not change contact quality, because the bat is ballistic at collision and the hands cannot affect the outcome.
- Bat speed comes mostly from the legs, hips and core working through the kinetic chain; forearm and grip strength matter only as a link that must not break.
- Train grip by getting stronger overall, with heavy deadlifts, not with isolated squeezing like a tennis ball.

## Claims
- Intro: the common belief is that a stronger grip at ball/bat collision gives more solid contact. The post calls this flawed.
- Intro: the bat is ballistic at contact. Releasing the bat just before collision would not change the hit, and contact reverberations do not reach the hands before the ball leaves the bat.
- Intro: the post says swing researchers and the show Sport Science have debunked the grip-at-contact view.
- So Grip Strength is NOT Important: optimal swinging relies on the kinetic chain, ground up and proximal to distal (legs, hips, shoulders, arms, wrists, hands, bat).
- So Grip Strength is NOT Important: leg, hip and core strength contribute the vast majority of the bat's launch speed when timed correctly.
- So Grip Strength is NOT Important: a weak link in the chain causes inefficient transfer between segments, which is where forearm strength matters.
- So Grip Strength is NOT Important: at the extreme, an arm-wrestling champion could transfer close to 100% of leg and core power to the bat, and someone with a broken ulna could not.
- So Grip Strength is NOT Important: the engine/brakes/transmission analogy puts legs and core as the engine, posterior shoulder as the brakes (throwing), and forearms as the transmission (swinging).
- Grip Strength IS Important: small muscles that stabilize the bat and carry power down the barrel are worth training, but less than the engine muscles.
- Grip Strength IS Important: repeated tennis-ball squeezing is called an inefficient use of time; heavy deadlifts are named as the best way to train grip and bat-speed muscles.

## Drills
- **Heavy deadlifts** - heavy barbell deadlift, no load or sets given; builds grip strength, the muscles that contribute bat speed, and transfer of power to the bat; for hitters

## Evidence cited
- Dr. Chris Yeager, Jack Mankin, Dr. Robert Adair and the ESPN show Sport Science are named as having debunked the grip-at-contact view. No data or study is given.
- Arm-wrestling champion versus broken ulna is a thought experiment, not data.

## Open questions
- Asserts without evidence that deadlifts raise bat speed and that tennis-ball squeezing does not help. No numbers or study are given.
- A reader comment argues that bat inertia and the need for tight hand-bat coupling mean the ballistic condition depends on strong hands. The post does not answer this.
