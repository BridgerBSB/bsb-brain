# Back Squat vs. Front Squat for Baseball Pitchers

## TL;DR
- Boddy argues pitchers should back squat rather than front squat, as long as the athlete's shoulder asymmetries (GIRD, scapular instability) are managed first.
- Cressey's counter-position: back squat set-up stresses the superior labrum in throwers, so use front squat, safety bar or cambered bar instead. Boddy frames the split as risk vs. reward, not right vs. wrong.
- The only hard number: Smith machine squats show 30-40% higher shear forces than free-weight squats.

## Claims
- **Intro:** A pitcher's off-season program needs a squat variation. Lunges and Smith machine "squats" do not replace it.
- **Intro:** Smith machine squats have 30-40% higher knee shear forces than free-weight squats (Andrews, Hay, Vaughan).
- **Who's right?:** Cressey avoids back squats because 74% of his clientele are baseball players, many with structural shoulder changes. In the externally rotated, abducted position the biceps tendon peels back on the superior labrum, worse with GIRD and scapular instability.
- **Who's right?:** Driveline evaluates each pitcher's shoulder and injury history first. Badly compromised shoulders do not back squat at the start, but omitting the lift entirely loses a training effect.
- **Who's right?:** The front squat loads the posterior chain less and allows less weight (shortened hamstring position, upright torso, no back extension, lever arm out front). It is safer because a bar that drifts forward gets dumped, while a back squat can drift into lumbar flexion under load.
- **Risk vs. Reward:** Cressey trains mostly pros who need health managed. Driveline's HS and college athletes are trying to break out, so Boddy accepts more risk.
- **Risk vs. Reward:** Back squats train glutes and hamstrings harder and move more weight. Front squats are quad-dominant, which can worsen the quad bias of athletes coming off heavy distance running.
- **Forced shoulder ER:** The back squat's external rotation stretch may help untrained and novice pitchers. A large gap between static ER and dynamic MER is linked to medial elbow injury (Miyashita et al.).
- **Bar position:** High-bar is easier on the shoulders. Low-bar moves more weight but forces more shoulder ER and raises the risk of losing the bar forward.
- **Conclusion:** Back squat is preferable if the population is managed: more IR static stretching, soft tissue work for pre-existing conditions, and limited back squatting for gross asymmetries.

## Evidence cited
- Andrews JG, Hay JG, Vaughan CL, knee shear forces in barbell vs. machine squat (30-40% higher on the machine).
- Miyashita et al., "The role of shoulder maximum external rotation during throwing for elbow injury prevention in baseball players" (J Sports Sci Med).
- Cressey's newsletter 154: 74% of his clientele are baseball players (his figure, at "last check").

## Open questions
- No outcome data compares back vs. front squat injury or velocity results in pitchers. The risk tolerance for younger athletes is asserted, not shown.
- Miyashita is about the static ER vs. MER gap, not squatting. The step from that gap to "back squat ER stretch is beneficial" is Boddy's inference.
