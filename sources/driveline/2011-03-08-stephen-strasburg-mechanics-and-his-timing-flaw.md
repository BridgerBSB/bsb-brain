# Stephen Strasburg, Mechanics, and his "Timing Flaw"

## TL;DR
- Boddy relays a Verducci/Fleisig argument that arm stress depends on timing: the ball should be in the loaded (late cocking) position at the moment the stride foot lands.
- Boddy adds that the rate at which maximum shoulder external rotation (MER) is reached matters more for bone and tissue stress than the MER angle itself (Sabick et al.).
- Fleisig calls arm-not-ready-at-foot-strike a red flag for injury: "Totally. It is risky and dangerous."

## Claims
- Verducci excerpt (opening): Strasburg's injury may trace to mechanics, and he will keep bearing risky elbow and shoulder loads unless he changes how he throws. This is a hypothesis, not a finding.
- Verducci excerpt (late cocking phase): the late cocking phase, where the shoulder reaches maximum external rotation with the ball raised, is the most important link in the kinetic chain.
- Verducci excerpt (timing): the ball should be loaded precisely when the stride foot lands.
- Fleisig quote: if the arm is too early or too late, force on the shoulder and elbow rises because "the energy gets passed to the arm before it was ready, or after."
- Fleisig quote: the issue is not too much armpit angle when the elbows are raised, "it's that the arm hasn't rotated yet."
- Fleisig quote: asked whether the raised-elbow timing glitch raises injury risk, he said "Totally... That's a red flag. Definitely."
- Sabick et al. (Humeral Torque in Professional Baseball Pitchers), as read by Boddy: loading rate is an important factor in bone stress, and the rate at which MER is reached may matter more than the absolute MER value.
- Boddy's example: two pitchers both at 170 degrees MER, one with twice the shoulder external rotation angular velocity, makes the faster one the likelier to injure shoulder or elbow connective tissue. This is a hypothetical, not data.
- Boddy (lab note): Driveline had been taking these kinds of measurements in its biomechanics lab for more than a year.
- Verducci excerpt (club quote): one anonymous club decision maker said the club would not consider any pitcher, by draft, trade or free agency, who lacks the ball in the loaded position at foot strike.
