# The incredible development gains of Parker Eastwood Lilles

## TL;DR
- One D3 freshman's year at Driveline: average bat speed went from 63 mph to 73 mph, a 10 mph gain, alongside a 14 mph jump in max exit velocity.
- Strength came first (170 lbs to nearly 200 lbs) before the bat speed and swing-path work; trainer credits more force into the ground for the bat speed gain.
- Single-subject story with real numbers (OPS +258 points last season) but no comparison group or isolated variable.

## Claims
- (intro): average bat speed rose from about 63 mph to 73 mph over one year of training, a 10 mph gain, per trainer Carson Evans.
- (intro): max exit velocity increased by 14 mph over the same period.
- (strength phase): body weight went from 170 lbs to nearly 200 lbs, which Evans says translated into more force into the ground and then bat speed.
- (bat speed training): Monday/Wednesday/Friday were designated bat-speed days, with "swinging faster" as the stated focus.
- (drills): Kershaw drills used to improve balance, stability, and timing during the forward stride move.
- (drills): "Hook 'Em" drills used to promote an even coil of torso and pelvis at the loaded position, then rotate hips open into foot plant.
- (swing path): a short-bat implement was used to promote better posture and more consistent backspin, especially to the pull side, targeting attack angle specifically.
- (results, self-reported): exit velocities above 100 mph and average bat speed "creeping toward the mid-to-high 70s" in the cage.
- (results): OPS increased by 258 points in the season following the training.

## Cues
- **"Just focus on moving as fast as you can"** - fixes underloading effort on designated bat-speed training days; for hitters in a bat-speed program.

## Evidence cited
- Named athlete Parker Eastwood Lilles: bat speed 63 mph to 73 mph, max exit velo +14 mph, weight 170 lbs to ~200 lbs, OPS +258 points. All self- or trainer-reported, no external verification given.

## Open questions
- Three simultaneous interventions (strength gain, dedicated bat-speed days, swing-path drills) ran together; the piece attributes the bat speed gain to added strength but gives no way to isolate which intervention drove how much of the change.
- "Mid-to-high 70s" bat speed and "above 100 mph" exit velo are Eastwood Lilles' own recollections, not the measured 73 mph/max figures Evans gives; the two numbers aren't reconciled.
