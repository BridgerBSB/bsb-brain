# Flat curve ball? Stop throwing strikes!

## TL;DR
- A flat or inconsistent curveball comes from aiming for a strike instead of focusing on pitch quality.
- Fix: in some bullpens, deliberately land the curveball about a foot in front of the catcher so movement is the only goal.
- Then blend that feel back into normal strike-throwing sessions over a few bullpens.

## Claims
- Intro: Many pitchers of all ages struggle to control the curveball; it is sometimes good and sometimes comes out flat.
- Intro: The usual cause is throwing to the target and not focusing on the quality of the pitch.
- Intro: Pitchers think too much about throwing the curveball for a strike.
- Intro: Bullpens should still be mostly strikes, but also include work on pitch quality, such as max-intensity fastballs without care for location.
- Drill setup: Throwing the curve to land a foot in front of the catcher removes the pressure of throwing strikes so the pitcher can work on movement.
- Progression: Over a few bullpen sessions, blend the feel back into regular strike-throwing sessions; curveball control and confidence come back.

## Drills
- **Spike curveball in front of catcher** - catcher set up normally mid-plate (glove side for a slurve), or a net with tape marking the target; throw curves to land a foot in front, not for strikes; builds pitch quality and curveball movement without strike pressure; for pitchers with a flat or inconsistent curveball
