# Appropriate Energy System Development - Why Running Poles is a Bad Idea

## TL;DR
- Running poles or 30-minute distance runs does not build baseball-specific conditioning for pitchers and can blunt strength and power gains.
- Concurrent endurance work only interferes with strength/power when its intensity exceeds 75% of heart-rate reserve (HRR); the fix is to stay near-max on sprint work or below 70% HRR, never in between.
- Tabata's 1996 study found a 4-minute protocol of 20 seconds max effort / 10 seconds rest improved both aerobic and anaerobic capacity, and did it better than moderate continuous aerobic training.

## Claims
- [Appropriate Energy System Development](https://drivelinebaseball.com/blogs/blog/appropriate-energy-system-development): coaches commonly have pitchers run poles or a 30-minute run after long toss or a bullpen, partly to gauge "fitness."
- [Appropriate Energy System Development](https://drivelinebaseball.com/blogs/blog/appropriate-energy-system-development): citing McCarthy et al. (1995), strength and power loss from concurrent endurance training only occurs when the endurance work exceeds 75% of heart-rate reserve (HRR).
- [Appropriate Energy System Development](https://drivelinebaseball.com/blogs/blog/appropriate-energy-system-development): the recommendation is to train either near-maximal (circa-sprint) intensity or below 70% HRR, avoiding the moderate middle zone where athletes "get slow."
- [Appropriate Energy System Development](https://drivelinebaseball.com/blogs/blog/appropriate-energy-system-development): baseball actions (throwing a fastball, a set of 5 squats, a 40-yard dash) are short and intense, powered by the ATP-PC or glycolytic systems, not the aerobic system used in distance running.
- [Appropriate Energy System Development](https://drivelinebaseball.com/blogs/blog/appropriate-energy-system-development): Driveline instead uses plyometric jump boxes, short ladder sprints, interval work on an AirDyne or Concept2 rower, sprint intervals, and the weight sled.
- [Appropriate Energy System Development](https://drivelinebaseball.com/blogs/blog/appropriate-energy-system-development): Tabata (1996) found moderate-intensity aerobic training raises maximal aerobic power but does not change anaerobic capacity, while high-intensity intermittent training (20s max effort, 10s rest, repeated for 4 minutes) improves both systems.
- [Appropriate Energy System Development](https://drivelinebaseball.com/blogs/blog/appropriate-energy-system-development): the Tabata protocol is claimed to raise VO2max more than moderate-intensity continuous training does.

## Evidence cited
- McCarthy et al. (1995), cited via Eric Cressey's article on concurrent training: strength/power loss from endurance work only above 75% HRR.
- Tabata et al. (1996), published study (PubMed): 20s-on/10s-off interval protocol for 4 minutes improved both aerobic and anaerobic capacity, outperforming moderate continuous aerobic training on VO2max.

## Open questions
- The source treats the Tabata protocol as directly transferable to pitcher conditioning without citing any baseball-specific trial of the method.
- The 75% HRR threshold comes from a general strength-training population study (McCarthy et al.), not from pitchers, but is applied here as a firm rule for arm care programming.
