# Strength for Baseball Hitters: Why It's Important

## TL;DR
- Driveline argues that hitters need real strength training (full squats, Olympic lifts, free weights), not only med balls, batting practice and bodyweight work.
- The case rests on a DeRenne quote and one anecdote: a 210 lb former player with a 335 lb back squat, who hit hard after months off the bat.
- The post gives no study data of its own and ends with a program sales pitch, so treat it as a position statement.

## Claims
- Intro: the same rotational power training that raises fastball velocity also raises bat speed, so Driveline trains hitters with its pitcher base program.
- Intro: the author opposes the view that med balls plus lots of batting practice, or bodyweight exercises only, are enough, and objects to avoiding full squats and power cleans.
- DeRenne quotes: Dr. Coop DeRenne, in *The Scientific Approach to Hitting*, says the "Big Three" exercises belong in the Base Cycle of periodization for strength development.
- DeRenne quotes: a second point is introduced with "after a base of strength is established"; the quoted text is cut off in the raw, so the exercise it refers to is not recoverable.
- DeRenne quotes: DeRenne states that free weights and dumbbells are the best equipment for strength development.
- Intro: the author's thesis is that strength leading to rotational power gives bat speed, and so more doubles and home runs.
- Anecdote: Jack, about a year in training, has approximate 1RMs of back squat 335, bench 210, deadlift 405, power clean 210, snatch 165, at 210 lb bodyweight.
- Anecdote: Jack had not swung since May, took batting practice in October, and hit hard and centered the ball often with "tons of lift". He credits the Olympic lifts.
- Comments: a reply says most Driveline pitchers use DB bench rather than straight bar bench, for a better effect on the scapulothoracic muscles, because the barbell press fixes the hands in pronation.
- Comments: the reply says pitchers tend not to power clean, using weighted jumps or other plyometric motions for explosive work instead, and that no cambered bar is used for squats.
