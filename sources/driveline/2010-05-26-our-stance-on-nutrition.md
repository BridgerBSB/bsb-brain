# Our Stance on Nutrition

## TL;DR
- Reduces athlete nutrition to two numbers: hit a caloric target, then eat about 1 gram of protein per pound of lean body mass. Everything else is treated as secondary.
- Sets the caloric target from a Basal Metabolic Rate calculation multiplied by 1.5 for an athlete's activity level, then adjusts up or down 15-20% to gain or lose weight.
- Dismisses carb quality, fat quality, and diet trends (glycemic index, keto, HFCS) as not worth attention until the basics have been held consistent for six months.

## Claims
- **Our Stance on Nutrition**: Estimate maintenance calories with a BMR calculator, then multiply by 1.5 for an athlete (a modified Harris Benedict equation). Example given: a 2000-calorie BMR becomes a 3000-calorie maintenance level.
- **Our Stance on Nutrition**: To gain weight, eat 15-20% above maintenance level; to lose weight, eat 15-20% below it.
- **What About Macronutrients?**: Eat roughly 1 gram of protein per pound of lean body mass per day. Example given: a 200-pound athlete at 25% body fat has about 150 pounds of lean mass and should eat 150 grams of protein daily, spaced out and weighted toward workouts.
- **What About Macronutrients?**: States carbs and dietary fat quality "don't care," i.e. are not prioritized.
- **What else?**: Compresses the plan to two diet rules (calories, protein) and four exercise rules (squat, press, pull, repeat), with optional high- or low-intensity cardio.
- **"But what about the Glycemic Index..."**: Claims the caloric-and-protein rules capture 90-95% of an "optimal" nutrition plan for the average athlete, and that finer details (glycemic index, ketogenic diets, HFCS) only matter once an athlete has held the basic plan for six months.
