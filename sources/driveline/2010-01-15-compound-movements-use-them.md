# Compound Movements: Use Them!

## TL;DR
- Argues that off-season and in-season strength work should be built on compound, free-weight movements rather than machines or isolation exercises.
- Gives a full example off-season week for a high school varsity infielder, including back squat 3x5, deadlift 1x5, and power clean 5x3.
- Recommends dropping midweek lifting near tryouts/season in favor of sprint work and a 2+ mile run.

## Claims
- Compound Movements: Use Them! - Strength training should prioritize full-body compound lifts (squat, deadlift, clean, snatch, bench press, press, pull-ups, chin-ups, rows) over machine-based work.
- Compound Movements: Use Them! - Machines remove work from stabilizer muscles, produce less central nervous system response, and transfer poorly to athletic competition.
- Compound Movements: Use Them! - Isolation exercises (curls, leg extensions, lateral raises, tricep kickbacks) have no place in an athlete's program, with few exceptions for intermediate-to-advanced lifters.
- Compound Movements: Use Them! - Example off-season week for a HS varsity infielder: Monday back squat 3x5, DB neutral-grip bench press 3x5, deadlift 1x5, chin-ups 3 sets to failure; Wednesday DE box squat 8x3, DB push press 3x5, power clean 5x3, push-ups 3 sets to failure, light medball work; Friday back squat 3x5, DB neutral-grip bench press 3x5, DB one-arm rows 2x10, one-arm suitcase deadlift 2x5, pull-ups 3 sets to failure.
- Compound Movements: Use Them! - As tryouts/season approach, Wednesday's lifting session is cut in favor of sprint-based training, skills work, and a 2+ mile run.
