# THE DOME ZONE PODCAST: Presented by BPC EP#2- Analytics in Baseball W/ MLB Catcher Nick Dini

## TL;DR
- Former MLB catcher Nick Dini says withholding metrics from players stunts them: the Royals never shared his receiving grades in the minors, but once the Mets told him his bottom-of-zone grade was poor and let him fix his setup, it jumped to a 70 on the 20-80 scale.
- Roster-building philosophy separates orgs: he says the Guardians and Rays have led MLB in wins since 2016 mostly with homegrown players, while the Yankees keep roughly 50 minor leaguers stashed as trade capital.
- His clearest mental-game example is process over outcome: he went from hitting .130 through two-thirds of a college summer season to finishing at .300 and ranked the league's top position player after he stopped tracking personal stats and focused on winning.

## Claims
- [01:01](https://youtu.be/a6Z6vJz0ql0?t=61) The Royals had no analytics infrastructure for advance scouting when he reached the majors in 2019, so he learned game-planning on the job.
- [34:31](https://youtu.be/a6Z6vJz0ql0?t=2071) The Yankees keep roughly 50 minor leaguers stashed across other orgs and trade them for established big leaguers to restock their own roster.
- [35:32](https://youtu.be/a6Z6vJz0ql0?t=2132) He says the Guardians (and separately the Rays) have the most MLB wins since 2016 while building primarily through the draft and player development rather than trades or free agency.
- [1:13:10](https://youtu.be/a6Z6vJz0ql0?t=4390) A player claimed off waivers onto the 40-man roster must stay on the active MLB roster the entire season and cannot be optioned, which shapes which prospects teams are willing to expose.
- [42:39](https://youtu.be/a6Z6vJz0ql0?t=2559) He argues sunk-cost thinking drives promotion decisions: a $1M signing bonus creates pressure to keep developing or promoting that player over one who signed for $5,000, regardless of performance.
- [1:36:33](https://youtu.be/a6Z6vJz0ql0?t=5793) The Royals never told him his receiving metrics through the minors; the Mets later graded his bottom-of-zone receiving a 70 on the 20-80 scale after he adjusted his setup, up from a grade he was told was poor.
- [56:52](https://youtu.be/a6Z6vJz0ql0?t=3412) He says worrying about his draft stock and the next four or five years of his career actively hurt his in-season performance.
- [1:00:57](https://youtu.be/a6Z6vJz0ql0?t=3657) After shifting focus from personal stats to team wins mid-season, he went from hitting .130 to finishing at .300 and was ranked the league's top position player.
- [1:08:04](https://youtu.be/a6Z6vJz0ql0?t=4084) He describes feeling relieved rather than devastated when released, framing it as regaining control rather than pure failure.
- [1:18:14](https://youtu.be/a6Z6vJz0ql0?t=4694) He pulled a 15-year-old aside for goofing off in the cage, telling him players get fewer real reps to improve than they think, especially as they age.

## Cues
- **"You gotta learn to love to walk"** - fixes hitters treating a walk as a failure instead of a good outcome; for young players who only want to hit.
- **"Flip the switch"** - fixes drifting focus between pitches during games; for high school and travel-ball players who can't lock in when it matters.
- **"Did you really empty the tank there?"** - fixes low effort during practice reps; for youth and high school players who goof off during BP or cage work instead of getting real reads.

## Evidence cited
- Nick Dini's own stat line: .392 with 7 home runs his senior year of college, and a documented in-season turnaround from .130 to .300 after a mental/approach shift, finishing ranked the league's number-one position player.
- Named example: catcher Max McDougal, signed and started in Double-A specifically because of elite receiving metrics despite being unable to hit.

## Open questions
- He says his receiving grade jumped from poor to a 70 on the 20-80 scale largely from being told the number and adjusting his setup; this is a single unverified case, not a study.
- He states the Guardians and Rays lead MLB in wins since 2016 through homegrown rosters but gives no exact figures, calling it "something like that" from memory.
