# 1st Round Draft Pick Talks About OVERCOMING EARLY STRUGGLES In Professional Baseball

## TL;DR
- Chase Petty's velocity dropped into the low 90s after his organization tried to standardize his arm action early in pro ball, despite no history of arm injury.
- Returning to his private coach for even a few days repeatedly restored his velocity, most dramatically going from sub-96 to 100 mph within days of an All-Star break visit.
- The throughline is self-advocacy: knowing his own recurring fault (drifting off the back leg on foot strike) let him give org coaches specific feedback instead of accepting generic mechanical tinkering.

## Claims
- [00:00](https://youtu.be/zT2YttYB5IM?t=0) Early in pro ball he felt the organization wanted to "put their stamp" on him and adjust his arm action, despite never having had an arm injury.
- [01:00](https://youtu.be/zT2YttYB5IM?t=60) His velocity dropped after the org began changing his mechanics; he had been sitting 94-97 in instructs and hit 99 once.
- [01:00](https://youtu.be/zT2YttYB5IM?t=60) He went home to work with his private coach and, after about a week, asked to throw a live BP to check where he stood.
- [01:00](https://youtu.be/zT2YttYB5IM?t=60) He had thrown 50 pitches a day for four straight days before that live BP, where he sat 93-95, which he called low for him.
- [02:01](https://youtu.be/zT2YttYB5IM?t=121) Hitters barreled several pitches during that live BP session.
- [02:01](https://youtu.be/zT2YttYB5IM?t=121) After being traded to the Reds, his velocity stayed down for most of his first full season.
- [02:01](https://youtu.be/zT2YttYB5IM?t=121) After returning to his private coach over the All-Star break, his first outing back hit 98, the highest he'd been all season (he hadn't topped 96).
- [03:02](https://youtu.be/zT2YttYB5IM?t=182) He and his coach identified a recurring fault: getting more forward on the front foot strike and drifting off the back leg, which precedes his velocity drops.
- [04:02](https://youtu.be/zT2YttYB5IM?t=242) The following year he had a bad first month and his velocity dropped again before the All-Star break; after two to three days home, his first outing back hit 100/99, and his final outing of the season hit 100 again.
- [04:02](https://youtu.be/zT2YttYB5IM?t=242) He credits the Reds pitching staff with trusting his relationship with his private coach and letting him self-manage adjustments during the season.

## Evidence cited
- Chase Petty (1st round pick, BPC-trained): velocity dropped to 93-95 after org intervention on his arm action, then rebounded to 98-100 within days of returning to private coaching, on two separate occasions (initial pro debut and the following season's All-Star break).
