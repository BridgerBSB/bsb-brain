# Training Implements (Hitting Warm-Up / Routine)

The hitting routine (≈ warm-up) is a consistent, meaningful series of drills with **varying
bats + balls** that warms the body **and** patterns proper movement, so the hitter can compete
in an intensely focused, **external** state without conscious body thought. Start each day at
**low-impact intensity** (constraint drills + weighted implements) — three reasons: (1) **prime**
muscles/pathways for accurate high-speed swings; (2) take **more reps without fatiguing** for the
day's skill work; (3) **build "swing fitness"** so high-quality swings survive deep into a workout
/ game. Five primary tools.

## 1. Speed Trainers (Weighted Bats)
The flagship **bat-speed** tool: a **Barrel Load + Handle Load + Underload**. Barrel/Handle =
**+20%** of a standard bat; Underload = **−20%.** Used for **high-intent bat-speed training** first,
also for **potentiation** + within/between-set variability.

**Weighted-bat research (the 20% rule):** implement weight drives effectiveness. **Too heavy/too
light → bat speed stagnates or drops**; only **~20% over/under** improves it (so the swing happens
at a velocity that transfers). **DeRenne** classic study: dry-swing group **+6%**, BP (live-arm)
group **+10%**, control no gain after 12 wks × 4/wk → **specificity drives transfer** (the more the
exercise resembles the activity, the more transfer). Mechanisms: **muscle-fiber recruitment**
(fast-twitch peak power ~4× slow-twitch — high-velo loading trains better motor-unit patterns);
**variability** (challenges the CNS to adopt new patterns — basic skill is learned fast, then
plateaus without new stimulus); **intent** (clearly defining the problem = bat speed, not contact
quality). **Specificity continuum:** barbell lift (general) → live AB (specific); overload/underload
swung fast is *very* specific (kinematic/kinetic similarity to a game swing). Hence: **no more than
20% over/under** for bat-speed transfer — extreme heavy/light bats may be useful otherwise, but
won't transfer *bat speed*.

## 2. Training Bats (constraints, NOT direct bat speed)
Short bats, various overload bats, **Smash Bats** (skinny barrel) target **mechanical efficiency,
adjustability, bat path** — they act as **constraints** encouraging desired movement solutions.
Three driving concepts: **overload, feedback, specificity.** (Overload tools = Long Lat / Bazooka;
feedback tools = Short / Smash bats + plyo/smash balls.)

### Long Bat — **37 in / 37 oz**
Preferred for **patterning correct kinematic sequence** in **load + stride + launch.** The overload
amount is the point: **just heavy enough** to keep game-like kinematics/kinetics (esp. **hip-shoulder
separation**) — heavier → minimal separation + a "one-piece" swing (no SSC); lighter → not enough
awareness for timing/sequencing. Hitters say it "cleans up timing": the added weight forces them to
**rotate the pelvis independently into launch + sequence properly**, hitting peak separation **~front-
foot plant**, then recoil + rotate the torso powerfully with the upper-body segments.
- **Common mistake:** "cheating" the overload by **contacting too far out front.** Monitor the **tee
  location** (down the middle, ~the depth of the front foot at launch) to enforce proper contact point
  + sequencing.

### Bazooka Bat — **45–50 oz** (most extreme overload)
Unmistakable feedback → awareness of movement execution. **Will NOT elicit a game-like swing** (too
heavy to sequence the SSC) → used to pattern **upper-body sequencing, connection, bat path.** Requires
significant **upper-body isometric strength** (the upper segments hold isometrically until just before
contact). Pairs well with a bat-path focus.
- **Built to attack the "push pattern" + "bat drag."** **Push pattern** = arms/hands accelerate before
  + faster than the torso (lost speed + compensations). **Bat drag** (its inverse) = too much lead-
  shoulder stretch as the torso rotates; the body turns but hands/arms/barrel don't turn with it
  (disconnected, "drag"), often **dropping the barrel below plane** ("dumping/losing the barrel").

### Short Bat — **~28 in**
Preferred for **patterning postural adjustments** to varying pitch heights/locations — encourages
adjusting via **knee flexion, hip flexion, torso forward bend + side bend** at launch / swing start.
Gives feedback on mishits where posture can't reach (esp. **low + away**). Leads hitters to discover
the **"top-down" approach**: look top-of-zone, add side bend for lower pitches. A hitter who
**"maintains posture"** has ≥ as much **side bend at contact** as **forward bend at launch.**
- **Common mistakes:** landing with **too much forward bend** at launch (forces standing up → "pulling
  off"); and **not hitting with a ball-flight intention** — must set an external ball-flight/field
  goal so mishits teach the postural solution (a low-pitch ground ball is *not* a successful rep if a
  line drive was the goal).

### Smash Bat (skinny barrel)
Paired with **Hitting PlyoCare / Smash Factor balls**, attacks **bat path + feel for flush contact** —
it **overloads the precision** needed to square the ball. After a few reps hitters adjust rhythm/timing/
path; like the bazooka bat, they sequence the upper body more efficiently (connected torso + lead
shoulder + hands) → a **higher-margin-for-error path** that squares the ball at many locations + depths
(huge for in-game adjustability).
- **Common mistake:** when struggling, hitters **slow down + reduce load/stride movement** to "make
  contact" — but reducing speed/motion → **hands/arms take over early = push pattern** → less depth/
  margin, more dependence on perfect timing. The advantage comes from a path whose AA ≈ the pitch
  descent angle; a hand-dominant push limits that. Stay focused on **center-of-ball / specific ball
  flight** + adjust path after misses.

## 3. Hitting PlyoCare Balls (Hitting Plyos)
**Rubber-encased, sand-filled** balls giving **instantaneous flush-vs-glancing feedback** — hitters
self-correct after mishits faster than with a regulation ball (especially in a cage, where backspin/
topspin/side-spin are hard to read). **Hit flush → "floats"/"knuckles" on a line-drive trajectory;
hit poorly → "pancakes"/flattens + spins rapidly in the mishit direction.** A **Mini** version (**2.5"
vs 2.9"**) is harder to square (esp. with the Smash Bat). They make near-perfect collisions required to
square up — the core tool for training **plane-matching** (the swing-flaw bat-path drills use the
**offset-closed** plyo drill).

**Common mishit reads (huge feedback):**
- **Hook / Top Spin** — AA **higher** than pitch approach angle, barrel angled toward pull at contact →
  sinking LDs / pull-side grounders / foul. Plyo flattens + **dips sharply** mid-flight. Fix: **back up
  the contact point** or **flatter path** (lower AA longer).
- **Uphill Cut / Back Spin** — normal contact point but pop-up/lazy fly: bat glances the **bottom** with
  AA ≥ approach angle (swinging under the plane). Plyo flattens, **shoots up, travels a few feet.** Fix:
  **set sights top-of-zone, start the swing "above the ball."**
- **Downhill Cut / Back Spin** — similar flight but lower LA + more spin: contact at **lower** AA than
  approach angle (chopping down vs driving through center). Plyo flattens + **backspins hard** (less
  distance, lower LA from the ball's weight). Fix: **contact more out front**, or change plane to higher
  AA deeper in the zone. (Plyos expose a choppy bat path.)

## 4. Smash Factor Balls
The PlyoCare concept **for the pitching machine** (usable on tee/flips/normal + mixed BP): **same
diameter as a baseball but lighter (3 oz vs 5 oz), spongy foam.** Same instantaneous flight/contact-
quality feedback. **Added benefits:** usable in **game-like, higher-speed** environments (higher
transfer); lighter ball **exaggerates pitch shape/movement** (slightly nastier than a real ball);
**eliminates fear of pain** (jam, end-of-bat, HBP) that throttles swing speed/intent off high-velo
machines → the hitter takes a **game swing every time.** Softer on hands, won't break the bat; mishits
spin extremely (obvious), flush balls go very far. Great for making any machine/skill work harder.

## 5. Warm-Up / Patterning Drills
The constraint/warm-up drills woven through the routine (the low-intensity, high-rep priming work that
opens each day) — the vehicle for the swing-flaw drill packages.

## How used
Maps **implement → flaw**: Long Bat (sequence/separation, load+stride+launch); Bazooka (push pattern +
bat drag, upper-body connection); Short Bat (postural adjustability, top-down); Smash Bat + Plyo/Smash
balls (bat path + flush contact + plane matching). The **20% over/under rule** is the canonical
bat-speed-training constraint and the empirical reason extreme implements don't transfer. The plyo/smash
ball mishit reads are a field-portable proxy for the bat-path diagnostics that the Astros do with sensor
AA + outcome data.
