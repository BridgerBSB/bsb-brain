# Swing Flaws & Drill-Package Programming

After a **general program**, the next layer of individualization is a **drill package**
chosen at the start of each workout. Driveline maintains **8 drill packages**, each
tied to a common **swing flaw/characteristic** within one or more phases of the swing.
Unlike day-type warmups (different drills by scheduled workout type), a flaw-focused
hitter **attacks the same focus *every day*** — **focused reps re-pattern movement**,
then the hitter **stabilizes the skill via variability** (game-like pitch types →
progressively more specific random environments). The flaws map directly onto the
phases/checkpoints in [[hitting-biomechanics]].

## Program-progression context (the fragment that precedes the flaws)
The Programming chapter sequences focus areas; two examples it ends on:
- **(4) Swing Decision / Strategy Development** — progresses like the bat-to-ball program
  but the *emphasis* shifts: build strike-zone awareness on **A days**, then transfer +
  practice strategies → a **2:1 B:A day ratio during the "Blend to Competition" phase.**
- **(5) Bat Speed / Swing Decision Hybrid** — for the common college/pro hitter who climbed
  levels on above-avg bat-to-ball: tends to **swing at low-leverage pitches when ahead** +
  **expand drastically with 2 strikes**, and the lower-level "make contact / get hits"
  positive-feedback loop leaves **bat speed underdeveloped.** Program = decision-making +
  strategy **and** overall bat-speed development.

(The general program uses **A days** / **B days** + a phased ramp toward competition; the
flaw packages run *on top* of whatever day-type is scheduled.)

## The 8 flaws (by phase) — diagnosis + cues

### Load — **Undercoil**
Load preloads the lower half for powerful rotation + hip extension. Concerned with pelvis/
torso in the **sagittal** (forward bend / hip-hinge) + **transverse** (coil) planes (grouped
here). **Proper coil:** pelvis + torso turned **~10–30° toward the catcher.** **Proper
forward bend:** torso **10–30° over the plate**, pelvis anterior tilt roughly matching, plus
some knee flexion. Stay **tall / high-pitch posture** during load (add bend later via SSC +
to adjust to low pitches after reading the pitch).
- **Diagnosis:** transverse → **open-side-view** video; sagittal → **CF / behind-plate**
  video. Track **belt buckle** + a **shirt logo**: each segment should noticeably turn toward
  the catcher in load + most of stride; if not coiling (or only slightly), add coil. Sagittal:
  draw a vertical line up from just **behind the back pocket** in the stance — if glutes/rear
  pocket don't reach the line during load, the hitter lacks hip flexion / forward bend.
- **Cues:** "Turn your **back pocket / numbers** toward the pitcher"; "Turn your **belt
  buckle / belly button** toward the catcher."

### Stride/Launch — **Closed Pelvis**
A launch checkpoint is landing with an **open pelvis relative to the torso** — pelvis rotates
independently late in stride ("a running start" prestretch) → space + quickness to deliver
the barrel to an inside FB while delaying upper-half rotation for offspeed. Closed/non-separated
= less force/power + adjustability problems.
- **Diagnosis:** open-side video → **no crease/stretch marks** in the jersey along the waist =
  no upper/lower stretch. CF view → belt buckle should rotate toward the pitcher by foot plant;
  if it stays under the head (side) / invisible (CF), pelvis is closed.
- **Cues:** "**Stride open**" — RHH stride toward the shortstop, LHH toward the second baseman;
  "**Pull-side ball flight**" — line drive w/ backspin to pull, expect an inside FB.

### Stride/Launch (or swing start) — **Early Extension**
Extending the rear hip + spine angle early (losing posture / forward bend) removes the most
powerful hip extensors (**glutes, hamstrings, adductors**) from the swing → big rotational-power
/ bat-speed loss. Think of getting **"buckled" on a breaking ball** (instinct = rise out of the
rear-hip load). A hitter who instead **delays the swing while maintaining hip + spine angle**
keeps a competitive swing. Early extension also causes **"losing space"** to the inside pitch
(pelvis shifts toward the plate instead of rotating away + holding flexion → rear elbow runs
into the torso → bat-path issues).
- **Diagnosis:** CF / behind video, vertical line up the **back pocket** — if the back pocket/
  pelvis comes off the line **toward home plate** as the swing starts, early extension.
- **Cues:** "**Stay on the wall**" (keep the back pocket against an imaginary wall from loaded
  position through swing); "**Rotate around the steel rod**" (imaginary rod through the head →
  spine → back pocket).

### Upper body — **Push Pattern**
Hands initiate the swing + accelerate faster than the lead shoulder/torso — distal outpacing
proximal → inefficient kinematic sequence, bat-path + adjustability issues, lost bat speed. A
primary marker is a **significantly internally-rotated rear shoulder** at launch (limits delaying
the barrel). In good connection, **rear-shoulder *external* rotation** happens just as the torso
starts forward rotation → the bat flattens. **Early rear-shoulder internal rotation** forces the
hands to take over (push) + removes an adjustability solution (IR + scapular upward rotation can
be saved to hit out-front pitches just before contact).
- **Diagnosis:** mark of a hand-dominant swing = **vertical bat maintained past launch + well
  into swing.** Open-side view → if the **hands pass the rear shoulder before the torso center is
  significantly open** (bat still vertical, rear shoulder IR/"hunched"), it's a push pattern.
- **Cues:** **Flatter bat path** ("top-down" — hunt the top of the zone, hit a low line drive),
  "**Swing across your shoulders**" ("across your face" for exaggeration), "**Turn behind the
  ball**"; **Overload** ("pretend the bat weighs 100 lbs," "use your whole body").

### Upper body — **Torso Overcoil**
Torso coiled excessively relative to pelvis coil (loaded position / stride / launch). Muscle-tendon
units only store elastic energy for a few tenths of a second, so **separating too soon** wastes the
SSC → the torso must rotate **further + faster** on concentric contraction; under a 95 mph FB time
constraint, hitters compensate by **initiating with the hands** (push pattern) + lose connection.
- **Diagnosis:** open-side video, belt buckle + chest logo — if the **chest logo is turned back
  toward the catcher significantly more than the belt buckle**, overcoil.
- **Cues:** "**Stay / start more open**" (keep the chest logo pointed at the pitcher), "**Expect
  an inside fastball**."

### Swing posture — **Losing Posture**
Failing to **replace torso forward bend (at swing start) with torso side bend (at contact).**
Critical for line-drive contact on pitches **low / away** (need extra side bend to "turn behind").
Visualized as **"pulling off the ball"** — head visibly moves **up and away.** *Misconception:*
pulling off is NOT over-rotation/"spinning" — it's the failure to swap forward bend → side bend.
Can also be a **timing** symptom (late → pull off to create time/space).
- **Diagnosis:** CF / behind video, line from top of head → spine → back pocket (the launch
  forward-bend). Adding side bend through the swing keeps the *appearance* of the original spine
  angle even though the spine is actually extending away from the pitch.
- **Cues:** "**Rotate around a steel rod through the top of your head**," "**Turn behind the
  ball**," **top-down** ("expect a pitch at the top, adjust down").

### Bat path — **High Attack Angle** (>12° + poor bat-to-ball)
- **Diagnosis:** **bat sensor** in-game / competitive BP for AA, paired with **K%, in-zone
  contact rate, or smash factor.** If **avg AA > 12°** + game signs of poor bat-to-ball, lowering
  AA provides more room for error matching the pitch plane → more flush contact.
- **Cues:** **Flatter bat path** ("expect a pitch at the top of the zone," "swing across your
  shoulders"); **Hit the top of the ball** ("aim a ball above the ball," "swing down and through
  the ball"); **Lower ball flight** ("hit the ball at the pitcher's feet," "hit the top of the
  plate").

### Bat path — **Low Attack Angle** (<6° + GB-heavy)
- **Diagnosis:** bat-sensor AA paired with **ground-ball rate** (or barrel%, **xwOBACON**, LA of
  hard-hit). **AA < 6°** → candidate for a swing-plane change to square up at higher AA → higher LA
  on hard-hit. *Caveat:* low AA can be a **timing / contact-point** artifact (late + deep contact
  reads lower AA — same trap as the biomech contact-point case study).
- **Cues:** **Vertical bat path** ("expect a pitch at the bottom of the zone," "swing underneath
  your shoulders," **"ferris wheel" bat path**); **Higher ball flight** ("hit the ball further out
  in front," "hit it in the air," "fly ball over the CF fence," "pull it in the air with backspin").

## How used
Turns biomech diagnosis into a **daily constraint program**. The **bat-path flaws specifically
require pairing bat-sensor AA with batted-ball outcome data** (K%/in-zone contact for high AA;
GB%/barrel/xwOBACON for low AA) — the direct Astros tie-in to attack-angle + outcome tracking. The
two attack-angle flaws are also the spot where **timing/contact-point artifacts** masquerade as path
changes (control for contact point — see the biomech case study).
