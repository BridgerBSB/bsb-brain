# Periodized Arm Care (Driveline)

> **Note on the source file:** named `bridger_armcare_1.docx` (Zac's own folder), but the
> contents are Driveline's **"Why Periodize Arm Care?"** *framework* doc — a methodology
> writeup, **not** personal ArmCare assessment numbers. If Zac's actual ArmCare data (ROM/

**Core idea:** throwing is one of the most demanding movements an athlete can perform —
extreme stress on the soft tissues of the **shoulder + elbow.** **Periodized arm care**
prepares the arm for those demands + mitigates the injury risk of rising workload across the
season + offseason. Driveline's goal: **optimize throwing performance while minimizing injury
by matching the arm-care stimulus to the skill (throwing) side.**

## The science — periodization = progressive overload
Structure training with **progressive overload** (gradually increasing demand over time →
tissues become stronger + more resilient). Applied to arm care, three commitments:
- **Build Capacity** — start with a manageable arm-care workload, **gradually increase volume
  + intensity** as the throwing arm adapts; mirrors the throwing progression across offseason →
  preseason → in-season; strengthen tissue incrementally **without overtaxing training economy.**
- **Stress and Recover** — apply a stimulus sufficient to **trigger adaptation** while allowing
  **adequate recovery** (high-intensity days — throwing/plyos/sprints/lifting — balanced with
  rest or light recovery so tissue repairs + grows; overtraining hinders progress + raises risk).
- **Match the Stimulus** — tailor arm care to **complement** the throwing program; when throwing
  intensity rises (bullpens, velo sessions), arm care adapts (recovery strategies / tissue-specific
  work) so the **arm-care workload stays proportional** to throwing demand.

## Periodization in action — the phase ladder
**Capacity before performance.** Before chasing velo/performance, ensure tendons, ligaments,
muscles can handle the workload.

1. **Capacity-Focused Arm Care** — progressive loading prepares tissue for increased throwing
   volume/intensity; build **tissue capacity, strength endurance, joint stability** = the
   foundation for the rest of the offseason.
2. **Hypertrophy-Focused Arm Care** — build **muscle size + reinforce soft tissue** to tolerate
   mechanical stress; targeted, controlled movements + **moderate-intensity throwing** refine
   strength + durability.
3. **Strength-Focused Arm Care** — shift to **max strength + handling high-intensity workloads**;
   developing **force production** here is critical to handle higher-velocity throwing.
4. **RTD-Focused Arm Care (Rate of Torque Development)** — RTD = how fast the athlete can produce
   **torque**, essential for explosive high-velocity throws. Dynamic high-intensity work
   (**plyometric + oscillation** exercises) optimizes torque production + explosiveness —
   translating strength into functional high-speed power **without compromising tissue integrity.**
5. **In-Season Arm Care** — focus shifts to **maintaining resilience + managing fatigue**; preserve
   strength + recovery without overloading. Strategies: **Workload Management** (monitor volume/
   intensity to avoid fatigue accumulation); **Tissue-Resilience Maintenance** (eccentric-focused
   work, light strength, joint stabilization); **Recovery Optimization** (mobility, soft-tissue
   care, deliberate rest). **Adaptive** — adjusts continuously to workload/feedback/recovery; if
   fatigue signals appear, shift to targeted recovery + maintaining baseline strength.

## Takeaway
Periodized arm care is the foundation of **sustainable** performance: progressing **Capacity →
Hypertrophy → Strength → RTD → In-Season** builds a resilient, high-performing arm. Each phase
builds on the last; arm care **isn't static — it evolves with the athlete**, matching workload +
performance goals with precision + intent.
