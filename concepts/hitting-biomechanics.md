# Hitting Biomechanics

Driveline captures full-swing kinematics + kinetics in **"The Launchpad"** — an
instrumented cage. **No single swing model exists**: across MVPs, future HOFers, and
All-Stars there is no identical pattern. Optimize for **function, not aesthetics**;
anatomy, joint mobility, body comp, and muscle structure dictate each hitter's own
best mechanics (a hyper-mobile hitter has different optimal mechanics than a stiff
one). Driveline will *never* cookie-cutter hitters into one stance/load/swing — the
job is helping each find his own best swing. That said, certain attributes recur among
the best and confer a mechanical advantage; this note catalogs them.

## The Launchpad technology
- **Optitrack Prime 17W** mocap cameras up to 360 Hz (**17 for hitting**) — gold-standard
  kinematic/kinetic reports.
- **Edgertronic SC1** high-speed (7), synced/genlocked — side, rear, overhead.
- **Bertec fixed force plates** (7 total, **4 for hitting**) in the boxes, **1080 Hz** —
  front/back-leg force profiles.
- **Rapsodo Pitching 2.0 + TrackMan Portable** — spin, break, trajectory, velo.
- **Blast** + **Diamond Kinetics SwingTracker** bat sensors — swing path.
- **HitTrax** — pitch location + batted-ball LA/EV in real time.

**Report reading:** full-signal plots, x = time, y = metric value (joint angle/velocity,
etc.), so you see the variable across the *whole* swing. The **shaded band = the "fast
swings"** reference: all swings with **lab peak barrel velocity > 72 mph** (mostly
pros), plotted as **mean ± 1 SD**. It shows how an individual's movement/position
compares to the elite subset — not all swings, but a strong benchmark.

## Positions & Phases framework

**Positions = static points; phases = movements between them.** Sequence:
**stance/setup → [LOAD] → loaded position → [STRIDE] → launch position → [SWING] →
impact position → [FINISH].** Each phase has a clear quantifiable start/finish plus
**swing checkpoints** (the things coached). Stance and finish are mostly *style* and
not detailed. (This is the Foundations of Hitting cert vocabulary — adopt the
*meaning*, not necessarily the words.)

### Phase 1 — Load ("the gather")
**Def:** begins when COM moves back toward the catcher; ends when COM begins forward
movement toward the pitcher. Sets up the explosive move ("a swing that starts poorly
ends poorly") — positioning + **timing** (the big "aha" is how *early* you can start;
a sound load lets you start early and "hold" if a tick early — starting late is trouble).

**Checkpoints:**
1. **COM positioning** — slight weight shift back *before* lifting the front foot.
   50/50 weight at front-foot lift → falls forward (physics). Hitting **looks bilateral
   but is largely unilateral** — almost entirely on the back leg during load, almost
   entirely on the front leg through swing/impact; time with *both* legs loading is minimal.
2. **Simultaneous controlled coil** — slight *rearward* rotation of pelvis + torso (RHH
   rotates slightly right, showing the rear/back pocket to the pitcher), at slow
   controlled velocity (no last-moment rapid coil). **Minimal hip-shoulder separation**
   here — lab hitters show only **4–10° separation at pelvis-fwd-rotation onset, better
   hitters less.**
3. **Dynamic balance & control** — subjective but observable; rhythmic, athletic.

**Loaded position checkpoints:** (1) coiled pelvis + torso; (2) athletic posture
**~20–30° torso forward bend** ("hitting posture," despite stance variance); (3)
minimal hip/shoulder separation (best seen overhead/rear).

**Load flaws/compensations:** **Torso pull** (pelvis can't coil → "pull" torso back →
early separation, sequencing issues — athletes self-organize around a constrained
pelvis); **arm compensations** ("arm bar"/"bat wrap" — back elbow adducts away,
wrists over-wrap barrel behind head). *Distal compensations for proximal mistakes are
common.*

**Coil amounts:** typically **~5° torso coil** at loaded position, **+10° during
stride**. MLB range **5°→15°** (0° = shoulders point at pitcher). **<0° = undercoil,
>15° = overcoil.**

### Phase 2 — Stride
**Def:** COM begins forward → **front-foot plant** (entire foot down; in lab = ankle
stops downward movement — important b/c many hitters set the ball of the foot down
early or "no-stride").

**Checkpoints:**
1. **Pelvis rotation timing** (two parts): hold the load-coil through the forward move;
   then **uncoil** into front-foot plant. **Pelvis rotational velocity *at foot plant*
   correlates higher to bat speed than *peak* pelvis velocity** — rotating fast *into*
   foot plant > overall peak.
2. **Increased pelvis/torso separation** — pelvis rotates, torso doesn't → stretches the
   torso rotators (initiates the **stretch-shortening cycle**). From ~2–8° at pelvis-rotation
   onset, hitters reach **max separation just over 20°**, beginning toward the end of stride.
3. **Posture** — land in **high-pitch posture** (~**25° forward torso bend**): easier to
   adjust *down* (side bend) than *up*; most top-of-zone pitches are FBs (need the most
   quickness). Maintain torso **side bend** — don't drop the back shoulder/collapse;
   in-gym avg **−10° side bend** (front shoulder slightly below back, tilted downhill to
   the pitcher) at swing-phase start.

**Launch position checkpoints:** (1) **front-knee flexion** (slight bend → adjustability;
stiff straight leg kills power + adjustability); (2) **rear-arm position** — back elbow
should have begun adduction into "the slot" (still up → timing issues / bat wrap); (3)
**early hip extension held** — keep the hip hinge so pelvis rotators work + create space
for rear elbow/hands (early extenders struggle inside-pitch — "losing space" — and have
low bat-to-ball b/c they lose torso-bend adjustability out front).

**Stride/launch flaws:** **Closed pelvis at launch** (no separation → can't generate
torso velocity → either a **push pattern** [arms/top-hand take over early] or a **gate
swing** [all segments spin together like a gate, no space]); **hanging back** (COM never
transitions forward); **early hip extension** (lose hip flexion striding → overly upright
→ "down and across" bat path).

### Phase 3 — Swing
**Def:** front-foot plant → impact. The most important phase (though prior phases set it
up).

**Checkpoints:**
1. **Stay behind the ball** — rotate around a COM that's *no longer moving* toward the
   pitcher → high end-point velocity ("whip") + correct sequencing. Continued forward
   drift → barrel drags slower.
2. **Maintain posture** — torso rotates **>800°/sec**; during it the hitter *removes*
   pelvis/torso separation while *adding* torso side bend + hip extension (strength +
   mobility + coordination). The swing is a **tilted rotational movement** — rotate around
   the launch-position spine axis without big deviation; losing it = "losing posture"
   (standing upright while rotating).
3. **Connection** — torso, lead arm, hand travel at the same rotational speed early in
   the swing (isometric contraction of posterior shoulder/arms/lats/traps).
4. **Bat lag & release** — maintain wrist lag during early torso rotation; once torso
   *decelerates*, unhinge the wrists to accelerate the barrel into contact. Proper
   wrist loading/timing = max body→bat energy transfer + adjustability (hold lag, adjust
   to slower pitches).

**Impact position:** (1) back leg **mostly/completely unweighted** (proper weight
transfer + GRF use); (2) **"palm-up / palm-down"** at impact (proper wrist engagement);
top-hand pronation should come *after* impact (early pronation = common flaw).

**Swing/impact flaws:** **Drift** (COM keeps moving linearly post-plant → negative
attack angle, can't adjust to offspeed); **losing posture** (upright while rotating →
inefficient bat path, late arm/wrist adjustments); **disconnection** — **drag** (hands
lag behind torso) or **push** (hands move out/away before torso starts); **barrel
dump** (early wrist unhinge → torso-dominant, spinny, "grooved," barrel drops below
plane → hard compensations).

## Segment Analysis

### Pelvis
Less bat-speed contribution than coaches think — **pelvis angular velocity ↔ bat speed
correlation is relatively low** (constrain the lower half by sitting/kneeling/no-stride
and you still get decent bat speed → the swing is largely upper-body). Narrow inter-swing
variability (mainly timing + small side-bend). Movement: starts slightly **anteriorly
tilted**, coils rearward in load, then **uncoils + posteriorly tilts + side bends** in
stride/swing. **What rotates it?** Likely the **core/lower-back muscles above the pelvis**
turn it into foot plant (back leg + pelvis rotate in unison → pelvis pulls the rear leg
more than the leg pushes the pelvis; we rarely see rear-hip external rotation that pushing
would cause). **What decelerates it?** (1) obliques firing concentrically (anchored to
pelvis); (2) the pelvis rotating into a stiff **extending front leg** ("lead-leg block"
like pitching) — front femur internally rotates near end-ROM (or the ankle rolls to bleed
energy). Last move = **posterior pelvic tilt / hip thrust** at end of swing → better torso
forward-bend angle for a positive attack angle + a powerful GRF release (some hitters get
airborne) as the wrists release the barrel. **Why deadlift/squat matter** — they train the
hip hinge/unhinge.

**Pelvis role in launch position:** the swing relies on rapid torso rotation; the torso
rotators need to **launch from the right length-tension** — pelvis rotates into foot plant
while the upper body stays square → **hip-shoulder separation** stretches the rotators for
max leverage. Start torso rotation from a neutral pelvis/torso → drastically less speed +
reliance on arms/hands. **Timing:** the SSC stretch can't be held too long (must contract
soon after) so the pelvis can't turn too early. **Over-rotation** exists too (over-stretches
the core or prematurely pulls the torso out of its loaded position) — but most strugglers
**under-rotate.**

### Torso
The dominant segment (despite hitters self-reporting "stay closed / don't fly open"):
similar to pelvis but **greater speed/range/variability**. From forward bend → coils →
uncoils + side bends in swing. **High inter-swing variability**, especially adjusting to
**pitch height** (less side bend / more upright for high pitch; more side bend for low). A
hitter who can't adjust to height likely can't vary side bend. **>800°/sec** rotation
creates momentum that pulls into side bend — so high-FB hitting needs high rotational speed
*while resisting* the side-bend "dip." Many hitters "feel" a downward attack angle because
they're resisting the back-shoulder dip. **Torso deceleration timing** drives spray:
**oppo** contact = decelerate torso *earlier*, contact *deeper*, more **in-to-out** swing
direction; **pull** = rotate further before decelerating + engaging arms/hands. Can't hit
oppo → can't decelerate torso early (sweepy, slices oppo); can't pull → drags arms across
body (hook/topspin). Overhead view shows: torso **more closed** oppo, **more open** pull.
Train via **random spray-angle practice** + **offset-rotation** constraint drill. The best
also have a **wider window** of torso-vs-pelvis timing → an adjustability window.

### Arms
Don't generate much speed themselves but transfer energy to wrists/hands/bat. **Pre-swing
arm movement = mostly style** (big/small movements, barrel tips, wrist flicks — ignore).
What matters at swing-phase start: **connected, aligned, ready.**
- **Connected** — arms travel with torso early ("tight"/close to rear shoulder), then
  **release** off the torso (along with torso deceleration) to send hands/barrel to the
  ball. The connect→disconnect relationship transfers body speed into usable bat speed.
- **Aligned** — ideally the bat is in the same plane as the lead arm + swing plane (clean
  rotation). Common flaw: **barrel too vertical** at launch → hand momentum pulls the barrel
  *under* the plane → late grip/hand compensations. (A few hitters launch from vertical —
  the exception.) Alignment lets hitters use **elbow flexion/extension + lead-shoulder
  horizontal abduction** ("chicken wing") to adjust to inside/outside; misalignment "fuses"
  the elbow (same angle every swing → can't adjust).

### Hands & Wrists
Last link; big contributor to **both** bat speed + bat-to-ball. Loading/unloading mirrors
the arms: wrists load into **"lag"** (~**90° between lead forearm and bat**, viewed
perpendicular to the plane); maintain lag as the torso pulls them; **don't dump the barrel
early** (forces torso-dominant swing — *imagine your wrists couldn't move*: you'd over-turn
the body, but the torso should already be *decelerating* at contact as the hands whip the
barrel through). "Over-swinging" = high torso speed not transferred through arms/hands →
higher torso speed, **lower bat speed** — exactly why a **bat sensor** is essential for
bat-speed training (find the ideal intent so you don't make changes that *feel* faster but
are slower). Hands are also the only thing to hold back on a **take** — watching takes
reveals mechanics (barrel-dumpers can't take fringe pitches, they commit too early).
**Grip pressure:** mostly **bottom-hand dominant early** (the "downswing"), top hand
increases pressure as the barrel accelerates. **Bottom-hand-dominant** hitters → more
vertical plane, "lose" the barrel under the plane/ball (low-ball hitters who struggle with
high FB). **Top-hand-dominant** → flat high barrel path → ground balls / roll-overs (early
top-hand release brings the bat around).

### Ground Force (GRF)
Newton's 3rd Law — force into the ground returns into the body. Three vectors: **vertical,
anterior/posterior, medial/lateral.**
- **Rear leg:** load to store energy while controlling the forward move. **Medial force**
  pushes toward the pitcher — too much → "jumping out of the back leg"; **high-level hitters
  ≈ 200 N medial.** As rotation starts, rear-leg force goes neutral/posterior → anterior;
  **going anterior too early** → back hip follows toward the plate → early hip extension +
  bat-path issues. Elite hitters hold neutral/slightly-posterior until first move / just
  before torso rotation. **Timing of when rear-leg force dissipates matters more than peak
  vertical force** (lose it too fast → can't control forward move; too late → can't transfer
  up the chain).
- **Lead leg:** stabilizes the lower half so the torso can accelerate. Should stay
  **posterior** through the whole swing phase; without it the pelvis slides toward the
  pitcher. Rear-anterior + lead-posterior = a **force couple** → rotational energy *without*
  translation, a stable base. The swing is *both* legs: rear-leg force *decreasing* triggers
  rotation; lead-leg posterior force stabilizes it.

## Bat Path (start analysis here)

"Cleaning up bat path is often the quickest way to improve performance."

**Key terms — movement:**
- **Attack Angle (AA):** vertical angle the **barrel** travels (barrel = point 6" from end
  cap), measured at impact. + = up, 0° = parallel to ground, − = down ("uppercut/level/down").
- **Horizontal Attack Angle / swing direction:** horizontal barrel direction ("out-to-in" /
  "in-to-out").

**Key terms — bat position:**
- **Horizontal Bat Angle (HBA):** the bat's *own* horizontal angle ("where would the ball
  go if squared perfectly?"). Usually ≈ swing direction through impact, but early in the
  swing the bat angle "opens" relative to swing direction; leaving it **open at impact** →
  excess side spin / not flush. **Lower-than-expected HBA → struggle to pull / hit in the
  air; higher-than-expected HBA → more pull at higher LA.**
- **Vertical Bat Angle (VBA):** bat's angle to the ground at impact ("flat" ≈ 0° vs
  "vertical" toward −90°). Pitch location drives it — **up in zone → flatter (near 0°),
  down in zone → more vertical (toward −90°).**

**What is a good bat path?** MLB **avg AA ≈ 7°** — by no coincidence ≈ the incoming **pitch
descent angle**. "On plane" benefits:
1. **Large margin of error** — square it up even if slightly late/early (off-plane needs
   perfect timing).
2. **Efficient bat-ball collision** — matched planes = max bat-speed → EV transfer (measured
   by **Smash Factor**); mismatch = lower EV.
3. **Optimal batted-ball distribution** — hardest-hit balls at advantageous LA. Negative AA
   → hardest balls on the ground (huge waste of bat speed; **negative AA never seen at MLB**).
   Higher AA → higher LA on hard-hit balls, but it gets **off-plane** with most pitches →
   worse bat-to-ball (a tradeoff). *Teach a lower AA with 2 strikes.*

Horizontal: MLB hitters go **"through the baseball"** (barrel near the horizontal pitch
plane); weaker amateurs deploy **"out-to-in"** ("down and across") — hinders driving the
ball, lower AA, often a result of **losing posture in stride** or launching from an
**undercoiled** position. VBA varies by hitter (posture-driven; less side/forward bend =
flatter, more = more vertical) — like pitchers' release-point variance, hitters have an
optimal postural-rotational pattern. Use drills (high-intent bat-speed training) to let the
hitter discover his own functional posture/VBA.

**Contact-point effect (a measurement trap):** AA is **dynamic** — negative on the
"downswing," "zeros out" at the low point, then progressively positive out front. **Case
study:** early Driveline hitters jumped from ~0° to 7–15° AA after 1–2 weeks; *the path
didn't change* — they were **late** off the machine (deep contact, still in downswing), and
as timing improved, contact moved out front → higher measured AA. Lesson: **control for
contact point** when measuring AA, or improved *timing* masquerades as a path change.

**Ball-flight laws (with all else constant — bat speed, contact point, offset):** bat path
at collision determines flight + spin. **Perfect/flush** contact (vertical AA matches pitch
descent + HAA matches horizontal pitch plane) → very high EV, very little spin, **LA ≈ AA**.
**Backspin** when **LA > vertical AA at contact** — avg AA ~7° → most balls hit above 7°
backspin. As hitters get out front AA rises → more **topspin** + higher-LA topspin to the
**pull** side (some high-AA hitters topspin HRs). **Topspin oppo is rare** (oppo = deeper
contact, lower AA → only low-LA "topped" grounders). **Application:** can't backspin to
pull → can't keep a usable AA out front (AA >20°, "lifting up off the ball"); "stay
through" + maintain a good AA out front = good pull hitters → use the **offset-closed**
plyo drill.

**Is more backspin better?** Backspin adds carry, but **swinging down to create it costs
EV** — the carry added < carry lost from the inefficient transfer (hence no MLB hitter
swings down). Down-swingers "clip"/over-spin fly balls → "ballooning" low-EV fly balls that
drop into a glove. **Best method:** AA ≈ incoming pitch plane + slightly **undercut** → hard
line drives + deep backspin fly balls (= most HR flight).

**Flat vs Vertical swings (batted-ball profiles):**
- **Horizontal spray:** flat paths hold AA longer (less variation through the zone, e.g.
  3–12° throughout) → flush contact across a **wider range of contact points** → hard line
  drives **line-to-line** (pinball logic: wait → oppo, early → pull). Vertical paths
  struggle on the lines (tilt + swing-direction toward middle → balls down the lines are
  mishits) → **hook to pull, slice to oppo**; do most damage **gap-to-gap/middle** and need
  more perfect out-front timing.
- **Launch angle:** vertical paths have **dynamic** vertical AA → out-front balls at higher
  AA (arc lifting the barrel) → **high % of hard-hit at high LA** → many **power hitters have
  high VBA** (slug more than bat speed predicts). But high bat-to-ball + high AA is rare.
  Flat paths maintain AA → almost all high-LA balls are **"clipped"** (5° AA hitting a 35° HR
  ball = 30° gap → low smash factor + high spin) → power hitters rarely have very flat paths
  (the bat-speed bar to HR at that mishit is very high). High bat-to-ball hitters tend to use
  a **flatter** swing.

**Different swings for different situations** — compartmentalize into two:
- **Leverage (default, non-2K):** goal = **hit a pitch hard for barrel trajectory.** Hunt a
  pitch you can barrel (selective), hit it **more out front** (higher bat speed + vertical
  AA), look to drive it **in the air pull-side** (external cue for flush at high AA), employ
  a **more vertical** path (spend more time at higher AA → hard LDs + FBs). Accept reduced
  margin for error.
- **Two-strike (~50% of MLB ABs):** goal = **put a strike in play** (barreling w/ 2K is
  unlikely — pitchers throw quality, avoid the heart). Adjustments: **hunt the top of the
  zone** (cover top-down, reduce chase on breakers down/out), **hit the ball deeper** (stay
  on pitches you're fooled on), **ground ball oppo** (external cue → catch it deeper, more
  room out front), **flatter bat path** (stay on plane with most pitch types longer). "Put a
  strike in play" framing because taking a ball moves you toward base + raises the incentive
  for a hittable pitch.
