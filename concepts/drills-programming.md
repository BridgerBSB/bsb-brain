# Warm-Up / Patterning Drills (Programming — Drills)

The daily hitting warm-up is not a way to "get loose" — it is a **sequenced set of constraint /
patterning drills** (varying bats + balls) that **primes movement and patterns proper mechanics**
so the hitter can compete in an intensely focused, **external** state without conscious body
thought. Each day starts at **low-impact intensity** (constraint drills + weighted implements) for
three reasons (see [[training-implements]]): (1) **prime** muscles/pathways for accurate high-speed
swings; (2) take **more reps without fatiguing**; (3) build **"swing fitness"** so high-quality
swings survive deep into a workout/game.

Drills are organized by the swing **phase** they pattern ([[hitting-biomechanics]]) and the
**flaw** they attack ([[swing-flaws-programming]]); the bat/ball chosen for each is the
**constraint** that forces the desired solution ([[training-implements]]); and all of it runs on
the motor-learning rails of [[meta/skill-dev-principles]] (blocked → random, external focus,
intention-action, "feed the flaw"). The worked example of a full prescribed session is
[[example-hitting-workout]] (Chase Fluke). **Drill explainer videos live in the ebook.**

## A note on the constraint logic (why these drills work)

Every drill is a **constraints-led** task: set up an **environment + an external ball-flight
intention**, and let the hitter **self-organize** the movement to solve it — rather than telling
him the body part to move. Two recurring mechanisms:
- **The cage is itself a constraint** that promotes low, center-field contact (this is partly why
  cold-weather areas develop pitchers better than hitters). Several drills (Offset Rotation) **angle
  the hitter** rather than the L-screen so the *middle of the cage* stays the target while the setup
  rotates.
- **"Feed the flaw":** pick the bat/ball/setup that makes the *wrong* movement fail and the *right*
  movement succeed (e.g., a long/overload bat forces torso-driven barrel delivery; a short bat forces
  postural side-bend; a constraint on the ground blocks a bad stride direction).

## Load-phase drills (gather → loaded position → into stride)

### Offset Rotation (Offset Open · neutral · Offset Closed)
**Purpose.** A swing-phase drill training the **different amounts of torso rotation** needed to hit
flush to varying parts of the field. The hitter cycles three setups (no stride): **Offset Open**
(simulates oppo-field torso rotation), **neutral** (middle), **Offset Closed** (pull-side), all
aiming to **drive a flush line drive up the middle of the cage**, 25–35° open/closed.
**Why it matters.** Hitting is a **random** skill needing a *wide array of movement solutions*. In
elite hitters the amount of torso rotation **varies swing-by-swing** — they "slam the brakes" and
decelerate the torso at **different times/locations** to hit flush to all fields at a range of
contact depths. A hitter who can't vary it is only effective to one part of the field (his bat path
/ swing direction won't adjust — and it all starts with the torso). Example: a hitter rotates the
torso *more* to pull a ball out front; try to hit oppo deep with those same pull kinematics and the
swing "cuts across" the zone in a slice. The drill develops **swing-by-swing variability** of torso
position + swing direction.
**Setup check / cues.** Each back edge of the plate is angled 45° off a direct line to the pitcher;
hitters should be **10–20° less** than that edge angle. RHH Offset Open faces toward SS; LHH toward
2B (and the reverse for Closed). Cycle the sequence **3–4×** per round.
**Common mistakes.** (1) **Oppo ball flight in all three positions** → trains *under*-rotation for
all three tasks (defeats the purpose); demand the middle-of-cage line drive. (2) **Over/under-shoot
the setup** when moving quickly → use the plate edges to self-check. (3) **Stride direction cheat**
(striding back toward neutral) → begin **no-stride**; use a ground constraint to block striding the
wrong way (closed during Open, open during Closed).

### Offset Open
**Purpose.** Typically the **first load-phase drill** of the warm-up; attacks **pelvis + torso
coil**. Starting open gives more ROM/momentum to coil the pelvis + torso, add knee/hip flexion, and
tilt the pelvis anteriorly → setting up powerful extension + rotation. **Doubles as a stride/launch
drill:** starting open puts the hitter in a **separated launch position** without needing independent
pelvis rotation before the stride — great for hitters with **minimal separation at launch** or a
**push pattern from early hip extension.**
**Setup.** 25–35° open (as if the pitcher throws from SS for RHH / 2B for LHH); check with the back
edge of the plate nearest the hitter; usually **no stride**. Cue: point **both segments** (pelvis +
torso) toward the pitcher to finish.
**Common mistakes.** (1) **Flares / weak oppo contact** → do it with an **overload or long bat**
(added weight/length forces the torso to turn the barrel into the zone → flatter path, more margin).
(2) **Rollovers to pull** → keep the head over the plate ("rotate around a steel rod through the
spine") to maintain spine angle. Short bat → forces **torso side-bend** to adjust to pitch heights.

### Step Backs
**Purpose.** A **staple** load/gather + rhythm/timing drill. The hitter learns to **load and coil
into the rear hip**, recruiting **posterior chain** (glutes/hamstrings) that **eccentrically
contract/stretch** to decelerate forward momentum and prevent falling over the back leg.
**Setup.** Start feet slightly closer together + about **a foot closer to the pitcher** than normal;
**step backward** into normal positioning; **load into the back hip** and begin the stride.
**Common mistakes.** (1) **Hopping back vs stepping back** → the point is to *activate posterior
chain to control forward momentum*, not to fight backward momentum; hopping over-emphasizes
decelerating with the trail leg. Cue: **"step over an imaginary sheet of paper"** behind the trail
foot (external goal to organize a stable load). (2) **Stepping without loading** → watch the COM; no
rearward shift = he's just stepping + rotating around the same center. Cue overload analogies: **"load
as if the bat weighs 100 lbs"** (extreme-overload analogy → quick adjustments + larger-muscle recruitment).

### Hook Drill
**Purpose.** Patterns the **transition** load→stride and stride→launch. A good hook setup puts the
hitter in an efficiently loaded position (proper hip hinge + knee flexion, **even coil** of pelvis +
torso). The preset position encourages a **controlled stride**, **correct hip-shoulder separation
timing**, and an **open, independently-rotated pelvis at front-foot plant.** Big wins for
**over-coilers** and hitters who **struggle to control forward momentum** in the stride.
**Setup.** Cross / "hook" the lead foot over the top of the rear foot; flex the trail knee slightly +
add hip hinge to the rear hip; from the loaded position, lift the lead foot back over the rear foot,
**stride forward while "hovering"** (lead foot close to the ground) into front-foot plant, and hit.
**Common mistakes.** **Stride length + timing** — over-stride (out of control) or under-stride (no
weight transfer), both common when hitters add a leg kick out of the hook. Cue: keep the lead foot
**close to the ground** (keeps the rear hip loaded → controls the forward move). Simple timing cues
("start earlier/later") + an external goal (contact point or ball flight) fix the rest: over-striders
→ "hit it deeper / oppo gap"; under-striders → "contact further out front / flush in the air to pull."

### Walk Throughs (and Shuffle Swing)
**Purpose.** A classic drill that **syncs the load + stride phases** and lets hitters **explore
rhythm/timing** that works for them. The **trail foot steps behind the front foot** (even pelvis +
torso coil) — flowing through a correct loaded position so the hitter can control the forward move
into launch. Because it's dynamic, it's often the **last load drill** of the warm-up (piecing
together Offset Open + Step Backs + Hook). On **bat-speed days** it's the **gateway to high-intensity
swings / the first high-intent round** — the added linear momentum raises bat + segment speeds.
**Setup.** Start **behind the batter's box**; step trail foot toward the pitcher into the box (like
stepping to throw); the front leg/foot swings back around to a normal hitting position; swing with the
intent to **move the bat as fast as possible.** *(Note: stepping behind is preferred, but in front is
ok — the key is staying athletic + starting early enough to fully load.)*
**Shuffle Swing** = the **overspeed progression** (BatSpeed B / "Body Speed" workouts): same setup,
but **shuffle** into the swing to move body + bat as fast as possible.
**Common mistakes.** **Drifting into foot strike** — after the heel lands and weight shifts forward,
the COM keeps drifting over the front side instead of rotating about an axis **behind** the front
side. The added momentum exposes this.

## Stride/launch + swing-phase drills

### Offset Closed
**Purpose.** Primarily a **swing-phase** drill to improve the **torso rotation pattern for hitting
flush to the PULL side.** The closed stance encourages aggressive upper-half rotation to deliver the
barrel → promotes **efficient upper-body sequencing + connection.** A staple for patterning the
**swing phase + bat path.**
**Setup.** 25–35° closed vs neutral (RHH face the 1B dugout; LHH the 3B dugout); drive the ball
through the middle of the cage.
**Common mistakes.** (1) **Rollovers** → often early wrist unhinge; cue **"hit the inside part of the
baseball"** to better time barrel release (but don't let it become a push pattern). A **handle-loaded
or short bat** keeps the bat close to the body through the turn; the **long/bazooka bat** patterns
upper-body **connection + barrel-release timing.** (2) **Oppo ball flight** → under-rotation; cue
**backspin a low line drive to the pull-side gap** (LCF for RHH / RCF for LHH) → forces more torso
rotation while staying connected.

### Keep it Fair
**Purpose.** Patterns the stride→launch and launch→swing transitions. The **extreme inside** pitch
location is a constraint that forces the hitter to **rotate on time + in proper sequence.** Attacks
**late pelvis rotation into launch** (hip/shoulder separation timing) and **late torso rotation /
push pattern** to start the swing.
**Setup.** Tee (or flip) **well off the inner third**, close to the body (~the chalk line of the
box). Expecting the extreme inner-half pitch, the hitter rotates the lower half **on time** to create
the space to deliver the barrel with good direction *inside* the ball → **line-drive pull-side
backspin.** Cue: **"hit it over the foul pole."**
**Common mistakes.** **Late rotation** — symptoms are hooking a ball foul or getting jammed/popping
up to the infield (no room/space to turn the barrel inside the pitch line). Late timing → compensation
(push pattern or drift after foot plant); a **closed pelvis at launch** can also mean **early hip
extension** compensating for running out of hip ROM → serious bat-path + adjustability issues. Fixes:
**contact more out front + a pull-side ball-flight cue.** Many hitters fear pulling (especially in the
cage), so they organize to cover middle/outer with a push-pattern compensation; coaches can encourage
**over-exaggerated** pull approaches (look for a pitch that "hits your front hip," "pull it into the
dugout") to explore new solutions. Learning to **correctly pull the ball in the air with backspin**
usually unlocks a more adjustable bat path with **more depth** (hit flush deeper *and* oppo).

## Bat-path / adjustability drills

### Around the World (and Low-to-High)
**Purpose.** Pattern an **adjustable bat path.** External focus = hit flush to **all parts of the
field** (cycle **oppo → middle → pull**, 3–4× per round) → organizes a bat path capable of flush
contact at **many contact points.** **Low-to-High variation** adds **launch-angle** constraints: oppo
= low (below ~5°), middle = line drive (5–15°), pull = high (15–25°). *Why:* most hitters mishit oppo
balls by **under-cutting** them.
**Setup (tee).** Shift the **horizontal target after each successful rep.** Oppo → tee on the middle/
outer half, **deeper**; middle → front edge, middle/middle; inside → **4–8" in front** of the front
edge, inner half. (Flips: request locations to match the task; if flip command is an issue, throw
everything down the middle and don't be too picky.)
**Common mistakes.** **Cheating to the oppo field** by drastically **closing the stride** + landing
closed → early hip extension (closed pelvis at launch) + a push pattern that can't hit flush deep. The
point is **adjustability**, not selling out for one field. Cue: hit it **deeper in the zone** for low
line-drive contact; a quick fix for chronic closed-/over-striders is to stride **slightly open** on
oppo (more barrel depth, lets the ball get deep, adjust with the upper half). Give time for
self-discovery before cueing.

### Depth Ladder
**Purpose.** **Contact-depth precision.** Three balls = three contact points: **deep** (middle of the
plate), **middle** (front of the plate), **out-front** (~6" in front) — each **6" apart** (a 12" zone
to be precise in). Hit a **hard line drive** at deep → middle → out-front, then back down the ladder
(12 swings).
**Common mistakes.** (1) **Weak contact deep** → a **push pattern** that only reaches positive attack
angles at middle/out-front. Fix: **stride slightly open** (pelvis rotates away earlier → space for a
direct path → positive AA at deep contact); add a **short bat** (extra inches of depth, forces the
torso to turn rather than the hands push). (2) **Rollovers / pop-ups out front** → **losing posture**
(failing to replace forward bend with side bend → bat goes up and out of the zone → too-high AA or
out-of-control out front). Fix: **long bat** (build solutions for better out-front contact); mix the
**short bat** to challenge posture/side-bend/staying-on-plane through elbow extension + scapular upward
rotation.

### Launch Angle Ladder
**Purpose.** A **bat-to-ball / barrel-precision** drill: hit at **0/10/20/30°** in 10° increments,
advancing a "rung" only when the ball is hit **within 4°** of the target (challenge: how many rungs in
8–10 reps; at 30°, work back down). Develops **barrel + ball-flight precision** and quicker
adjustments. **KEY TEACHING:** change launch angle by hitting **different parts of the ball**
(bat-ball **contact**), **NOT** by manipulating **attack angle.** *"A round bat strikes a round ball."*
Hitters who hyper-focus on ball flight tend to manipulate AA as if the bat were flat — which is why
**negative-AA down-swingers hit high, lazy pop-ups** (they swing down and clip the *bottom* of the
ball). Be wary of hitters leaning on **bat-path changes instead of contact changes.** (Best done with a
launch monitor; without one, mark targets in the cage: 0° = coach's knees, 10° = coach's head, 20° =
top bar of the L-screen, 30° = a spot in the netting.) Direct tie to the AACon work in
[[blast-metrics]] / [[swing-path-bat-tracking]].

### High, Middle, Low
**Purpose.** Pattern movement solutions to **hit flush line drives at varying pitch heights** —
training an **adjustable bat path** via **posture + joint-angle adjustments** at launch + through the
swing. Tee high (2–3" above the belly button, standing straight up), middle (6–8" lower, ~mid-thigh),
low (~kneecaps), 3 swings each. The point: hit the **same LA + spray** at each height = the hitter has
adjusted successfully (the **top-down approach**: look top-of-zone, add side bend to reach lower).
Incorporates **random practice** into tee work.
**Common mistakes.** **Losing posture** — setting up with **too much forward bend** → standing upright
on the high pitch. Quick fix: refocus on **line-drive ball flight on the high pitch**; if still
struggling, raise the tee + exaggerate **taller posture**, or aim for lower line-drive flight up.

### High Tee
**Purpose.** Isolated from High-Middle-Low for hitters who **struggle at the top of the zone.** Patterns
a **flatter bat path** + the **top-down approach.** Tee stays at the high location (2–3" above the belly
button) all round; **land at high-pitch posture.**
**Common mistakes.** (1) **High AA + high ball flight / topspin** → low (steep/negative) VBA + high-AA
swings → well-hit balls at fly-ball LA but **"flipped" topspin** that drops as lazy liners / rolls
over. Fix: land **taller**, aim for **lower backspin flight**; raise the tee + shoot lower to exaggerate
flattening at launch. (2) **Push pattern** → vertical swings with rapidly ascending AA; pair High Tee
with **Offset Closed** to emphasize pelvis/torso rotation timing + upper-body connection.

### Low Tee
**Purpose.** Isolated for adjusting to **pitches low in the zone.** Provides a controlled environment to
find solutions to get **on plane** and hit line drives low; teaches a **more vertical bat path** + flush
contact at **higher AA.** The movement solutions don't change as the pitch gets lower — they're just
**more pronounced** (more torso side-bend, hip + knee flexion). A steeper VBA reaches positive AA low
but spends **less time on-plane** near zero (so flat-swing/low-AA hitters are challenged to line-drive
off the low tee — they "zero out" then AA climbs fast).
**Common mistakes.** **Low-pitch posture** — "pre-adjusting" by starting/landing with exaggerated
forward bend + hip flexion → fails to keep high-pitch posture → makes the drill easy but negates its
utility (and leaves the hitter vulnerable up). Cue: **"set up for the high pitch"**; a **long bat** can
raise posture back to normal (length makes low-pitch adjustments feel easier).

## Drill → phase / flaw map

| Target | Drills | Pairs with implement |
|---|---|---|
| Coil / load (pelvis+torso) | Offset Open · Step Backs · Hook · Walk Through | Long/overload (separation), "100 lb" analogy |
| Separation / late rotation / push | Keep it Fair · Offset Closed · (High Tee + Offset Closed) | Handle-load / Long / Bazooka (connection, timing) |
| Adjustable bat path / spray | Around the World · Low-to-High · High-Middle-Low | Short bat (posture), Long bat (out-front) |
| Contact depth | Depth Ladder | Short bat (depth), Long bat (out front) |
| Barrel / ball-flight precision | Launch Angle Ladder | Plyo/Smash Factor balls (flush feedback) |
| Posture / pitch height | High-Middle-Low · High Tee · Low Tee | Short bat (steeper), Long bat (raise posture) |

## How used
The drill layer under the [[swing-flaws-programming]] packages — each flaw maps to specific drills +
implements + cues. A flaw-focused hitter attacks the **same focus every day** (focused reps
re-pattern movement), then **stabilizes via variability** (game-like pitch types → progressively more
specific random environments) — the blocked→random arc of [[meta/skill-dev-principles]].
[[example-hitting-workout]] (Chase Fluke) is a real sequenced session built from these drills +
implements + ball-flight intentions. Feeds the "Compensations / Drills" module (Module 6) of
[[pd-onboarding-curriculum]] and the [[hitting-quiz-bank]] assessments. *(The drills PDF transitions
