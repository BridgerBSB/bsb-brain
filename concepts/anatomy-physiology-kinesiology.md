# Anatomy, Physiology & Kinesiology (hitting crash course)

The reference layer beneath [[hitting-biomechanics]] — the terms used to describe swing
movement precisely. **Anatomy** = structure of the body; **Physiology** = functions of
the body; **Kinesiology** = study of body movements.

## Anatomical terminology

**Joint movements:**
- **Flexion / Extension** — joint angle gets smaller / larger (elbow flexion at contact;
  front-knee flexion at landing).
- **Adduction / Abduction** — toward / away from the body (rear humerus *adducts* to start
  the swing).
- **Internal / External rotation** — rotating toward / away from body midline (lead-leg
  femur **internally rotates** in the hip joint during a RHH swing).
- **Pronation / Supination** — hand rotation relative to the elbow (top hand **pronates
  *after* impact** — early pronation is a flaw).

**General movement:** **Linear** (straight line) vs **Rotational** (around an axis) — hitting
has both. **Three planes:** **sagittal** (forward bend), **frontal** (side bend), **transverse**
(rotation). **Relative position:** **proximal** (toward midline) / **distal** (away);
**anterior** (front) / **posterior** (back).

## Joints & muscle contractions
- **Hinge joint** — one plane only (flex/extend): **elbow, knee.**
- **Ball-and-socket** — all three planes, large ROM: **hip, shoulder.**
- **Concentric** — muscle contracts + **shortens** (bicep in a curl).
- **Eccentric** — muscle contracts + **lengthens** (hamstrings lowering into a squat).
- **Isometric** — contracts **without changing length** (rear-leg hamstring stabilizing
  during the load).

## Segments of the body
- **Pelvis** — bony structure between abdomen + thighs ("the hips" in hitting).
- **Torso** — trunk/upper body, excluding arms/neck/head.

**Lower half** — bones: **femur** (hip↔knee), pelvis (their interaction is key). Joints:
**hip** (femur↔pelvis), **knee** (femur↔shin), **ankle** (shin↔foot). Muscles: **posterior
chain** (glutes + hamstrings), **anterior chain** (quads). Movements: **hip hinge** (flexion
at the hip), **pelvic thrust / posterior tilt** (pelvis tilts posteriorly), and its reverse
**anterior tilt.**

**Upper half** — bones: **spine, scapula** (shoulder blade), **humerus** (shoulder↔elbow),
**forearm** (ulna + radius → wrist). Joints: **thoracic (T-spine) + lumbar spine** (where most
swing rotation happens), **shoulder, elbow, wrist.** Muscles: **obliques** (core — largely
responsible for torso rotation), **lats** (lower back → scapula/humerus), **pecs, traps**
(scapula↔neck), **triceps** (posterior arm). Movements: **posture / spine angle**, **pelvis/
torso separation (= hip/shoulder separation)**, **scap retraction** (scapula back toward spine),
**elbow extension**, **radial / ulnar deviation** (wrist toward thumb / pinky).

## Physiology

**How muscles work:** for any joint there's a **protagonist** (helps the movement),
**antagonist** (resists), and **stabilizer**. Bicep curl: bicep = protagonist, tricep =
antagonist — the tricep must **relax** to let the elbow flex. (A hitter "stuck" at a joint is
sometimes the **antagonist not letting go.**)

**Muscle elasticity & the Stretch-Shortening Cycle (SSC):** muscles are **elastic** — they
recoil when stretched, producing more force than they could alone. **EMG research: the obliques
produce >100% of their standalone force during a swing**, via the elastic properties of muscle
+ tendon. **Timing-dependent** — unlike a rubber band, a stretch held too long loses the elastic
benefit; the stretched muscle must contract relatively **quickly after.** **Plyometrics** =
movements that use the SSC (rapid stretch→contract for speed/power).

**Energy systems:** **fast-twitch** fibers → rapid movements (swing/throw/sprint, **anaerobic**);
**slow-twitch** → jogging/standing (**aerobic**). Most baseball movements are fast-twitch /
anaerobic. (→ S&C should be explosive/plyometric, not long-slow cardio — see
[[meta/skill-dev-principles]] transfer.)

**Nervous system** (brain + spinal cord + nerves) controls movement. **"Muscle memory" is
actually the nervous system** — muscles have no memory; the NS coordinates movement. So most
mechanical adjustments are an **operating-system / nervous-system** change, not a bones/muscles
change.

## Kinesiology

**Kinetic Link Principle (definition):** *body segments generate high end-point velocity by
accelerating + decelerating adjacent links, using internal + external muscle torques applied
**sequentially**, **proximal → distal**, **most-massive → least-massive**, **most-fixed →
most-free**.* Energy works **ground up** through the body like links in a chain → like a whip,
faster as it moves proximal→distal. The wrists can't move a bat 70 mph alone — they use momentum
captured from proximal segments for high end-point bat speed. Coaching = efficient creation +
transfer of energy: body → bat → ball. **Measured via the rotational speeds of pelvis, torso,
lead arm, lead hand** — looking for **speed gain at each segment** (efficient transfer) +
**correct deceleration order** across the swing.

**Interacting with the ground (GRF):** the ground is stable; pushing into it returns force up
into the body = **ground reaction force**. Directional (multi-vector), measured by **force
plates**. (Force-couple mechanics: see [[hitting-biomechanics]] GRF section.)

**Center of Mass (COM):** the balanced center point, **near the belly button.** In load, COM
moves **back toward the rear leg**, then **forward in stride** — the anchor concept for the
positions/phases framework.

## How used
Reference glossary that makes [[hitting-biomechanics]] precise; the **SSC + kinetic-link +
fast-twitch** facts also justify Driveline's strength + bat-speed training choices (deadlift/squat
for hip hinge, plyometrics for power, the 20% over/under bat rule for fast-twitch recruitment).
