# Vertical Approach Angle (VAA)

The angle at which a pitch crosses home plate *vertically* (how flat or steep it
enters the zone). **VAAAA** = VAA Above Average, adjusting for velocity, location,
pitch type, and release height — isolating the excess flatness/steepness.

## Why it matters
> **"VAA(AA) is a swinging-strike weapon"** (vs [[horizontal-approach-angle|HAA]] =
> called-strike weapon).

A **flat** four-seamer up in the zone generates whiffs (it "rises" past the
barrel); a **steep** sinker/curve down generates ground balls. Crucially, **elite
VAAAA plays up even in poor locations** — because a hitter's vertical swing margin
is narrow, a small vertical miss still whiffs/fouls. Flattest/steepest pitches
reach **1.5–2.0° excess VAA** (much bigger lever than HAA's ±0.5°).

## VAA vs HAA (the pairing)
- **VAA** → whiffs, location-robust, big magnitude, set by **release height +
  movement** (needs mechanical change to move).
- **HAA** → called strikes, location-dependent (edges only), small magnitude,
  tunable by **horizontal release point** (slide on the rubber). See
  [[horizontal-approach-angle]].
