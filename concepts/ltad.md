# Long-Term Athletic Development (LTAD)

**Module 1 of the PD onboarding curriculum** ([[pd-onboarding-curriculum]]) and the spine's
top priority. LTAD is the *macro* view of player development: the multi-year, stage-by-stage
plan for turning a kid who can run-jump-throw into a professional athlete — and then keeping
him healthy and productive for a long career. Everything else in the curriculum (assessment,
the Big 3, training environments, biomechanics, drills) is what we do **inside** a stage; LTAD
is the framework that tells us **which stage a player is in, what to emphasize there, and why
you sequence development rather than pile everything on at once.**

> **Voice note.** Unlike our hitting content (where we borrow ideas but never name the source),
> LTAD is **public sport science** — Istvan Balyi, the Canadian *Sport for Life* (S4L)
> framework, the FUNdamentals lineage. We **cite these by name** because they're the shared
> academic vocabulary of the field. What's *ours* is the synthesis: how we map LTAD onto a
> baseball acquisition-and-development pipeline, and how it locks together with our periodization
> backbone ([[meta/org-training-process]]) and our skill-development engine
> ([[meta/skill-dev-principles]]).

**The one-sentence version:** *Develop the athlete before the specialist; build broad physical
literacy and general capacity first, layer Special Physical Preparation (SPP) on top of it, and
specialize last — sequenced to the athlete's biological maturation, not his birth certificate.*

## 1. Origin & framework

### Balyi & the Canadian Sport for Life model

The modern LTAD model was popularized by **Istvan Balyi** (a sport scientist who worked across
Canadian and British high-performance systems) in the late 1990s–2000s, and institutionalized by
**Canadian Sport for Life (CS4L / now Sport for Life)**. Its founding complaint: most sport
systems were **competition-driven and short-term** — winning U10 trophies, over-racing young
kids, specializing too early — and were systematically **failing to develop athletes** to their
genetic potential while burning many of them out before they matured.

The corrective idea: lay out an **athlete-centered, development-driven, stage-based** roadmap
across the whole lifespan, where the **content of training matches the athlete's stage of
physical and motor maturation**, not the calendar or the competition schedule.

### The seven (sometimes eight) stages

The canonical Balyi/CS4L model has **seven stages**:

1. **Active Start** (≈ 0–6)
2. **FUNdamentals** (≈ 6–9 boys / 6–8 girls)
3. **Learn to Train** (≈ 9–12 boys / 8–11 girls)
4. **Train to Train** (≈ 12–16 boys / 11–15 girls)
5. **Train to Compete** (≈ 16–23 boys / 15–21 girls)
6. **Train to Win** (≈ 19+ boys / 18+ girls)
7. **Active for Life** (any age — the "exit ramp" that runs alongside everything)

Later versions add **"Awareness"** and **"First Involvement"** stages (how people find sport and
get in the door) — bringing the count to eight or nine — but the seven above are the development
spine. Note the **age bands are guidelines, not gates**; the model's whole point is that you
trigger stage transitions off *maturation*, not *chronological age* (see §3–4).

### The FUNdamentals lineage & "physical literacy"

The intellectual lineage runs FUNdamentals → **physical literacy** → **physical competence as
the foundation of all later sport**. "Physical literacy" (the **ABCs**: Agility, Balance,
Coordination, Speed — plus the throwing/catching/striking patterns) is treated like reading and
writing: a foundational competence that, if missed in childhood, is very hard to recover and
caps everything built on top of it. A kid who never developed broad movement vocabulary becomes
the prospect who's "a great baseball player but a poor *athlete*" — and who plateaus the moment
the game asks for a movement skill he was never taught.

> **Critique to keep in view.** LTAD's specific *windows-of-trainability* claims (§6) are
> evidence-informed but not all rigorously proven; some sport scientists (e.g., Ford et al.)
> argue the "windows" are softer and more continuous than the original diagrams imply. We treat
> the **stage logic and the sequencing principle as load-bearing**, and the precise window timing
> as **a strong heuristic, not a law.** Don't tell a coach a kid "missed his speed window forever."

## 2. The stages in depth (baseball lens)

For each stage: the age band (boys / girls), the focus, the key do's and don'ts, and where it
lands in a baseball pipeline. Remember the male/female split — **girls mature ~2 years earlier**,
so every transition shifts left for them.

### Stage 1 — Active Start (≈ 0–6, both)
- **Focus:** Movement *as play*. Build the brain–body wiring through unstructured active play.
- **Do:** Daily activity in short bursts; lots of different movements; fun.
- **Don't:** Sedentary screen time; organized leagues; "sport-specific" anything.
- **Baseball:** Pre-baseball. The output is a kid who shows up to T-ball already an *athlete*.

### Stage 2 — FUNdamentals (≈ 6–9 boys / 6–8 girls)
- **Focus:** **Fundamental movement skills** — the **ABCs** plus run/jump/throw/catch/kick/strike.
  This is where **physical literacy** is *built*.
- **Do:** Multi-sport exposure; develop both sides of the body; emphasize speed/agility through
  games; introduce simple rules; develop suppleness while it's free.
- **Don't:** Specialize; periodize; over-compete; one position year-round.
- **Baseball:** Throw-and-catch foundation + general athleticism. **No position. No radar gun.**

### Stage 3 — Learn to Train (≈ 9–12 boys / 8–11 girls)
- **Focus:** **The most important stage for motor-skill acquisition** — the "golden age of
  learning." The brain is primed for **sport-specific skills**; **Skill** is the headline trainable.
- **Do:** Teach proper technique in a fun, high-rep environment; develop *all* fundamental sport
  skills; keep playing 2–3 sports; keep practice:competition high on the practice side.
- **Don't:** Over-compete; heavy load; pick a position.
- **Baseball:** **Build the swing and throw correctly, now.** Patterns laid here are the ones we
  spend pro careers refining or unwinding. First place [[meta/skill-dev-principles]] applies at
  scale (more blocked/fundamentals reps first, then layered variability).

### Stage 4 — Train to Train (≈ 12–16 boys / 11–15 girls)
- **Focus:** **Building the engine** — aerobic + strength base, organized *around* **Peak Height
  Velocity (PHV)** (§3–4). Consolidate skills while developing physical capacities.
- **Do:** Develop the aerobic base (window opens at PHV); begin structured strength **after PHV**
  (boys) / around menarche & PHV (girls); keep speed + skill; introduce periodization; **monitor
  growth** to time windows.
- **Don't:** Sacrifice skill to early competition; ignore the growth spurt (coordination loss +
  injury risk); specialize prematurely.
- **Baseball:** **The critical inflection for an org.** Top end = late-HS draft radar. PHV
  coordination loss explains a young arm's command "falling apart" — biology, not a flaw. **Pitch
  count / workload caution peaks here** (growth plates + rapid loading); early-spec injury risk
  (UCL/elbow) compounds (§7).

### Stage 5 — Train to Compete (≈ 16–23 boys / 15–21 girls)
- **Focus:** **Optimizing the engine + specialization.** Commit to one sport/role; train to
  compete — position-specific, individualized, periodized year-round.
- **Do:** Individualize to strengths/position/physiology; high-intensity position-specific work;
  full periodization with recovery; introduce **SPP as the priority** (hand-off into
  [[meta/org-training-process]]); model competition demands.
- **Don't:** Over-race; neglect mobility/regeneration; equate "specialize" with "only play games."
- **Baseball:** **Most of college, the draft, the signing, lower MiLB.** The acquisition→
  development hand-off (§8). Meet the player with the full assessment battery ([[big-3-hitting]]
  in-gym assessment, force plates, biomech) and start individualized SPP + skill work.

### Stage 6 — Train to Win (≈ 19+ boys / 18+ girls)
- **Focus:** **Maximizing performance** — peak on demand for the highest level.
- **Do:** Peak for the season that matters (the **utmost** intensity tier in
  [[meta/org-training-process]]); maximize recovery/monitoring/individualization; exploit full
  motor potential.
- **Don't:** Treat development as finished; ignore workload/regeneration for short-term wins.
- **Baseball:** **Upper MiLB → MLB.** Performance optimization, in-season workload management,
  peaking, the highest-resolution rep → feedback → adjust loop.

### Stage 7 — Active for Life (any age)
- **Focus:** Staying active/healthy **for life** after (or alongside) sport — available from *any*
  stage.
- **Baseball:** Post-career health; duty-of-care tail; the reminder that **most** players we
  develop will *not* reach MLB — don't break them on the way out.

### Male / female differences (summary)
- **Girls mature ~2 years earlier** → every transition shifts earlier. PHV girls ≈ 11–12; boys ≈ 13–14.
- Windows anchor to PHV and (girls) menarche → they open at different *chronological* ages by sex.
- Strength window: girls = around menarche / immediately post-PHV; boys = 12–18 months post-PHV.

## 3. Three "ages": chronological vs developmental/biological vs training age

The cornerstone insight — and the one new coaches most often miss — is that **a player's birthday
tells you almost nothing about how to train him.** Three clocks:

| "Age" | Definition | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Chronological** | Time since birth. | Default everyone uses — and the cause of the **relative age effect** (§5). Useful only for league eligibility. |
| **Developmental / biological** | How far along **maturation** — skeletal maturity, % of adult height, PHV status, hormonal maturity. | The clock LTAD times training off. Two 14-year-olds can be **±4 years apart** biologically. |
| **Training age** | How long the athlete has trained **systematically** — total and **sport-specific**. | A 22-year-old with 2 years of structured training is a *beginner* — treat him like one. |

**Consequences for an org:** same listed age can need totally different programs (one is
biologically 2 years ahead); a **low training age** in a chronologically "old" player (converted
athlete, late international signee) = **huge upside** but you must **build the foundation he
skipped**, not jump to high-level SPP. Our intake battery captures **biological/training-age
markers** ([[youth-hp-analytics]]-style testing), not just roster age.

## 4. Peak Height Velocity (PHV) & maturation

**PHV** = the point of **fastest growth** in the adolescent spurt — the single most useful
**non-invasive landmark** for biological age, because trainability windows anchor to it.
- **Boys:** PHV ≈ **13–14**, ~9–10 cm/yr at peak. **Girls:** ≈ **11–12**, ~8 cm/yr.
- Identified by tracking **standing + sitting height** (spurt goes legs-then-trunk) + a maturity-
  offset estimate — no imaging needed.

**Why it's load-bearing:** **coordination temporarily degrades** ("adolescent awkwardness") as
limbs outgrow the nervous system's calibration (a swing/delivery can look like it's falling apart
— *the growth spurt, not a flaw*); **injury risk spikes** (growth plates/tendons) → the basis for
conservative pitch counts; the **stamina + strength windows open relative to PHV** (§6), so you
must know where a kid is vs his PHV.

### Early, average, late maturers
- **Early maturers** hit PHV first → temporarily bigger/stronger/faster → dominate youth on
  *maturity, not skill* → risk of plateau when peers catch up ("man among boys at 13, ordinary at 18").
- **Late maturers** are temporarily smaller/weaker → **overlooked/cut** despite often being **more
  skilled** (they had to develop skill to compete). Many **best long-term athletes were late
  maturers.** Losing them early is the most expensive talent-ID error in youth sport.
- **Org implication:** adjust for maturation. A late-maturing, elite-skill, small-frame HS prospect
  can have **enormous physical upside**; an early-maturing "maxed" one, less. A core acquisition
  judgment LTAD makes explicit.

## 5. Relative Age Effect (RAE)

Within any birthdate-banded cohort, kids born **just after the cutoff** are nearly a **year older**
than the youngest — a massive maturity edge at young ages.
- **Effect:** first-months-of-year birthdays **over-represented** in selected cohorts; late-in-year
  **under-represented.** Self-reinforcing — older-for-grade kids get picked, get more/better
  coaching, and the early edge compounds into a real-but-**manufactured** skill gap.
- **Cost:** the system **selects for birth month, not talent**, discarding younger-for-cohort
  (often late-maturing, often more skilled) players before they mature.
- **Org implication:** know each player's birthdate vs his cutoff; **discount the apparent edge of
  the oldest-for-age** and **hunt the talented younger-for-age / late-maturing** players everyone
  passed over. RAE + the maturer spectrum explain most "how did everyone miss this guy" and
  "can't-miss who flamed out" stories.

## 6. Windows of trainability — the 5 S's

Certain capacities have **sensitive periods** — windows of **accelerated adaptation** keyed to
maturation/PHV. The **5 S's**:

| S | Capacity | Window (relative to maturation) |
|---|---|---|
| **Stamina** | Aerobic/endurance | Opens at **onset of PHV** (the aerobic window). |
| **Strength** | Force production | **Boys: 12–18 mo post-PHV.** **Girls: immediately post-PHV / around menarche.** Before: bodyweight, med balls, hops. |
| **Speed** | Quickness / max velocity | **Two windows.** Pre-PHV (~7–9 boys / 6–8 girls): agility/CNS/short-duration. Post-PHV: max-velocity/anaerobic. |
| **Skill** | Sport-specific motor skill | The **"golden age,"** Learn-to-Train years **just before PHV** (~9–12 boys / 8–11 girls). |
| **Suppleness** | Flexibility/mobility | Early (~6–10), with special attention **during PHV** (bone outpaces soft tissue → mobility loss if neglected). |

**Read without over-claiming:** windows say *"especially responsive now — don't waste it,"* NOT
*"un-trainable otherwise"* (our MiLB S&C proves adults gain strength/speed/stamina). Honest
framing: **train all capacities at all stages, weight the emphasis to the open window.** Baseball
lens: if the skill window was used well we inherit a clean foundation; if missed, habits to
unwind; missed speed/strength windows = untrained adaptation we can still unlock (the "gainers"
upside in [[youth-hp-analytics]]).

## 7. Physical literacy, ABCs & early specialization vs sampling

### Physical literacy & ABCs
**Physical literacy** = competence/confidence/motivation to move well broadly — the movement
equivalent of reading/writing. Building blocks: the **ABCs** (Agility, Balance, Coordination,
Speed) + fundamental movement skills. Built in FUNdamentals + Learn-to-Train; the **substrate
every sport skill layers on.** Miss them → capped ceiling (the "good baseball player, poor
athlete"). Connects to our stack: ABCs are the general-movement vocabulary that makes the
**variability / differential-learning** and **constraints-led** work in [[meta/skill-dev-principles]]
possible — a movement-literate athlete is a better **movement problem-solver**, the trait that
separates elite hitters (crush a novel implement immediately) from lesser ones.

### Early specialization vs early sampling
- **Early specialization** (one sport, year-round, often one position, young) is appropriate only
  for **early-specialization sports** (gymnastics, figure skating, diving). **Baseball is a
  late-specialization sport.**
- **Early sampling/diversification** → broader athletic base, **lower overuse-injury + burnout**,
  longer careers, often **higher elite attainment.** Many MLB players were multi-sport into HS.
- **Early specialization in baseball costs:** overuse injuries (UCL/elbow, shoulder), burnout/
  dropout, narrow base. Year-round one-position ball before PHV is an LTAD red flag.

### Early-engagement nuance
**Early engagement / deliberate play** (Côté) ≠ early specialization: a future MLBer's baseball
should be **high in deliberate play (fun, varied, self-directed)** and **low in adult-style
deliberate practice + over-competition**, *and* he should also play other sports. Quality + variety
of engagement, not single-sport volume.

### 10-year / 10,000-hour rule
- **Claim** (Ericsson; Gladwell): expertise ≈ **10 yr / ~10,000 hr of deliberate practice.**
- **Critiques:** 10,000 is an **average, not a threshold**; deliberate practice explains **far less
  variance** than implied (genetics, maturation, start age, deliberate *play* all matter); for
  late-spec sports, accumulating hours via **early diversification + play** beats early grind; the
  rule ignores **transfer** (10,000 low-transfer "showcase skill" hours don't make a game hitter,
  per [[meta/skill-dev-principles]]).
- **Our synthesis:** volume is necessary, not sufficient — **quality, transfer, variety, timing**
  decide whether the hours compound.

## 8. Mapping LTAD onto a baseball PD pipeline

| Pipeline stage | LTAD stage(s) | Org emphasis |
|---|---|---|
| **Youth (pre-org)** | Active Start → Learn to Train | *Mostly outside our walls,* but the talent-ID context. Read through a **maturation + RAE lens**; value the late-maturing, high-skill, movement-literate kids the system overlooks. |
| **Amateur — HS** | Train to Train (top) | Workload/health caution (PHV injury risk); avoid early-spec traps; project **biological upside** not just present performance. |
| **Amateur — college** | Train to Compete | Closer to finished, but **training age varies wildly** — a "polished" senior may be a low-training-age beginner with upside. Assess, don't assume. |
| **Draft / sign** | Train to Compete | **Acquisition → Development hand-off.** Capture the full intake snapshot. |
| **MiLB (low→up)** | Train to Compete → Train to Win | Individualized SPP-first ([[meta/org-training-process]]); skill refinement via [[meta/skill-dev-principles]] / [[drills-programming]]; periodized to the season. |
| **MLB** | Train to Win | Performance optimization, peaking, in-season workload, highest-resolution feedback loop. |
| **Post-career** | Active for Life | Duty-of-care tail; most developed players never reach MLB. |

### The acquisition → development hand-off
The most operationally important boundary. **Acquisition** asks *"who do we get?"*; **development**
asks *"how do we make him better?"* LTAD is the shared language across both:
- **Acquisition** uses LTAD for **upside** (maturation, training age, RAE adjustment, movement
  literacy, room to grow) — a late-maturer with elite skill + low training age is a "buy."
- **Development** uses LTAD to set the **entry program** (*where in the model does this player
  actually sit?*) — a polished college bat and a toolsy low-training-age international signee get
  **different first programs** even at the same chronological age/level. Mis-reading the stage
  over-loads an unbuilt foundation or under-challenges a ready athlete.
- **Shared artifact** = the **intake snapshot** (Module 2; [[big-3-hitting]] assessment battery) —
  the biological/training-age read LTAD demands. Youth analog = [[youth-hp-analytics]].

## 9. LTAD ↔ periodization ↔ the Astros development cycle

LTAD = **macro** (which stage, multi-year). Periodization = **meso/micro** (sequencing within a
stage). They nest.

### LTAD → periodization ([[meta/org-training-process]])
At **Train to Compete / Win** (our MiLB→MLB world), training content is governed by the
periodization backbone:
- **SPP-first.** LTAD: general capacity + physical literacy before specializing. Periodization:
  within the trained athlete, build motor potential (SPP) before technique/speed/competition. Same
  **general-before-special** logic at two time-scales.
- **Concentrate loads, don't pile on.** LTAD's "sequence, don't pile on" at career scale =
  periodization's **concentrated-SPP-in-a-limited-phase** at cycle scale (avoid the complex-parallel
  "concurrent everything" plateau).
- **Long-Term Delayed Effect (LTDE).** Concentrated SPP → functional dip → **over-recuperation** to
  a higher level, expressed later — the cycle-scale echo of LTAD's thesis: **development is delayed
  and sequenced, not immediate and additive.** Don't judge a development block by its in-block
  numbers any more than you judge a 14-year-old by his U14 trophies.
- **Intensity ladder** (optimal → maximum → utmost) maps to **Train-to-Train → Compete → Win.**

### LTAD → the Astros development cycle
Both terminate in the core loop (also driving [[meta/skill-dev-principles]]):
> **rep → objective, external feedback → adjust → repeat**
LTAD says **what to develop now** (skill in Learn-to-Train; engine in Train-to-Train; SPP +
position-specific in Train-to-Compete); periodization says **how to sequence the loads**; the
development cycle says **how each rep gets better** (objective KR, external focus, tight loop). Three
zoom levels of one idea: **measure where you are, develop in the right order, give objective
external feedback, re-measure** — the assessment-to-development engine the whole curriculum rests on
(*"how does this player compare to the best, and how does he develop to get there?"*).

## 10. Practical do / don't & common pitfalls

| Stage | DO | DON'T |
|---|---|---|
| **FUNdamentals** | Multi-sport play; ABCs; both sides | Specialize; periodize; one position |
| **Learn to Train** | Teach technique in the skill window; practice-heavy | Over-compete; heavy load; pick a position |
| **Train to Train** | Aerobic + (post-PHV) strength base; **monitor PHV**; periodize | Sacrifice skill to win-now; ignore growth-spurt injury risk; specialize early |
| **Train to Compete** | Individualize; SPP-first; position-specific; full periodization | Over-race; neglect mobility/recovery; mistake "specialize" for "only play games" |
| **Train to Win** | Peak on demand; maximize recovery/monitoring | Treat development as finished; chase short-term wins at workload cost |

**The big four pitfalls:** (1) **over-competition early** (steals skill-window reps, breeds burnout/
overuse); (2) **under-developed physical literacy** (the "good player, poor athlete" plateau, hard
to recover); (3) **premature specialization** (overuse injury, burnout, narrow base, worse long-term
outcomes); (4) **ignoring maturation & RAE** (over-loading growth-spurt bodies, over-valuing early
maturers, discarding late-maturing/younger-for-cohort talent, misreading a growth dip as a flaw).

> **The meta-pitfall:** treating development as **additive and immediate** ("more reps, more games,
> more now") instead of **sequenced and delayed** ("the right stimulus, in the right order, timed to
> maturation — the payoff comes later"). LTAD, periodization, and our development cycle all push the
> same correction.

## How used (analyst lens)
LTAD is the **macro frame** under the entire onboarding curriculum (Module 1) and the bridge from
**youth development** ([[youth-hp-analytics]] strength tracking, "gainers") to the **pro pipeline**.
It supplies the *vocabulary for upside*: biological vs chronological vs training age, PHV/maturation,
RAE, windows of trainability — turning "this 18-year-old's current numbers" into "this 18-year-old's
*trajectory*." It explains, at career scale, the same sequencing logic [[meta/org-training-process]]
explains at cycle scale: **general before special, foundation before specialization, delayed-and-
sequenced over immediate-and-additive.** When draft/PD-Goals work asks "how much room does this
player have to grow," LTAD formalizes the question.
