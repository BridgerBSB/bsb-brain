# Hitting Coach Candidate — Master Question Bank

The full interviewee question set for the Hitting Coach Candidate Prompt. A **superset** —
trim to fit the deck ([[hitting-coach-candidate-prompt]]). Each question carries the
**principle it tests** and a **"listening for"** line so this doubles as the interviewer's
scoring guide.

**Voice rule (BLOCKING):** Astros principles only. **Never name Driveline / HTKCH / any
source.** Anonymous hitter = **Ethan Frey** — never shown by name ("a hitter in our system").
Source mapping (our reference only): [[big-3-hitting]] · [[hitting-biomechanics]] ·
[[swing-flaws-programming]] · [[training-implements]] · [[meta/skill-dev-principles]] ·
[[meta/org-training-process]].

## A — Core Beliefs & Philosophy  *(deck slide 2)*

**A1.** What would you rank as your **4 core hitting tenets**, and in what order would you
place their importance? Why that order?
- *Tests:* the candidate's hitting framework (Big 3 hierarchy or a defensible alternative).
- *Listening for:* a coherent priority order; do they separate **importance** from
  **trainability**? A strong answer resembles *hit it hard · square it up · swing at good
  pitches* (bat speed → bat-to-ball → swing decisions) **or** a thoughtfully argued variant.

**A2.** Which of those is **most trainable**, and does that change where you start with a
given hitter?
- *Tests:* the trainability inversion.
- *Listening for:* **swing decisions** = fastest/most malleable; **bat speed** = most
  *reliable* to move (incremental); **bat-to-ball** = hardest, least certain. Bonus: program
  economy follows the profile (a 60/40/40 hitter ≠ a 40/60/60 hitter).

**A3.** Where is the line between a hitter's **individual style** and a **non-negotiable**?
- *Tests:* function-over-aesthetics; no single swing model.
- *Listening for:* optimize for function given the athlete's anatomy/mobility; they do NOT
  cookie-cutter; non-negotiables are mechanical *advantages* (separation, on-plane path,
  staying behind the ball), not a stance/style.

**A4.** Is there one swing every hitter should have?
- *Tests:* same; this is the red-flag version.
- *Listening for:* "no" — the best hitters share no identical pattern. A "yes" is a flag.

**A5.** How do you define a **good practice**?
- *Tests:* practice-design literacy.
- *Listening for:* deliberate (clear intent + objective per-rep feedback), representative of
  the game (**high failure rate**), focused/gamified, high reps.

## B — Swing Diagnosis: the Hitter (Ethan Frey, anonymous)  *(deck slides 3–4)*

**B1.** Identify the **3–5 most significant inefficiencies** in this swing. **Tie each to a
phase** — load, stride/launch, or swing/impact.
- *Tests:* phase-anchored diagnosis (the 8 flaws).
- *Listening for:* do they name flaws by phase? **Load:** undercoil, torso pull. **Stride/
  launch:** closed pelvis (no separation), early extension. **Swing/impact:** losing posture
  ("pulling off"), push pattern, bat drag, barrel dump. Do they separate **cause (proximal)**
  from **compensation (distal)**?

**B2.** What does his **ball-flight pattern** tell you, and does it **corroborate** the video?
- *Tests:* pairing outcome data with the eyes.
- *Listening for:* they read spray/LA/EV/contact-quality and tie it back to a mechanical
  cause — not a results-based diagnosis ("popped up → he dipped"). Pop-up may just be a
  swing a touch too low; late may just be late.

**B3.** Before you call his attack angle / contact issue a **path problem**, what do you
check first?
- *Tests:* the contact-point / timing artifact.
- *Listening for:* **control for contact point + timing** — late, deep contact reads a lower
  AA; improved timing can masquerade as a path change. Check where contact is happening first.

**B4.** Which inefficiency do you fix **FIRST**, and why that one?
- *Tests:* sequencing / training economy.
- *Listening for:* attack the **biggest constraint** / a **proximal** cause before its distal
  compensations; a reason, not a checklist.

**B5.** Pick one flaw — give me the **exact drill, constraint, implement, and cue**, and how
you'd **progress** it.
- *Tests:* constraints-led design + implement→flaw map + blocked→random.
- *Listening for:* a constraint that *requires* the right move (not "tell him to fix it");
  the right tool (e.g. **push pattern → heavy/bazooka-style bat**; **postural/top-down →
  short bat**; **bat path/flush → smash bat + plyo balls**; **sequence/separation → long
  bat**); an **external** cue; **blocked first, then progressively random** to stabilize.

**B6.** How do you **communicate** this to the player to build **buy-in**? What language,
cues, and progressions ensure he understands the *why* and **owns** it?
- *Tests:* external cueing, ownership, the tight feedback loop.
- *Listening for:* external/analogous cues over internal body-part talk; objective real-time
  feedback (KR); set the **intention + environment**, let him self-organize the feel;
  preparation breeds confidence.

## C — Data / Ball-Flight Read  *(deck slide 5)*

**C1.** Reviewing his profile, which would you **prioritize first** — and why?
**a)** bat path/attack angle  **b)** bat speed/intent  **c)** swing decisions/approach
**d)** postural/sequencing (separation, posture, ground force).
- *Tests:* reading a profile → a training decision.
- *Listening for:* a *reasoned* pick tied to his actual data + time horizon, not a default.

**C2.** What would you **measure** to know it's working — and over what **window**?
- *Tests:* objective success gate + realistic timeline.
- *Listening for:* a concrete KR metric (AA, top-8th EV, in-zone contact, smash factor,
  GB%/xwOBACON) and an honest window (bat speed = weeks/incremental; decisions = fast).

**C3.** This hitter **can't pull the ball in the air**. Walk me through your diagnostic tree.
- *Tests:* horizontal bat angle / staying through.
- *Listening for:* can he keep a usable AA **out front**? horizontal bat angle / swing
  direction; "stay through + maintain AA out front"; an offset/closed constraint drill.

**C4.** He's **ground-ball-heavy with a low attack angle** — path problem, or something else?
- *Tests:* low-AA flaw vs artifact.
- *Listening for:* could be a genuine low-AA path **or** a timing/contact-point artifact
  (late + deep reads low). Verify contact point before changing the plane.

## D — Read the Tape: Early vs Late  *(deck slide 6)*

**D1.** What **changed** between these two windows?
- *Tests:* change detection across video + data.
- *Listening for:* specific, phase-anchored deltas — not vague "he looks better."

**D2.** If the earlier version was better, what's your **directive** to get it back? If later
is better, how do you **build on it**?
- *Listening for:* a clear directive + a drill/constraint path, not just an observation.

**D3.** What **missing information** would better help your process here?
- *Tests:* intellectual humility + data fluency.
- *Listening for:* names real gaps (sensor AA, contact point, pitch context, timing) rather
  than claiming certainty.

## E — Approach & Game Plan  *(deck slide 7)*

**E1.** Given this pitcher's **arsenal · usage · location**, build the hitter's plan. Which
approach fits him — **zone, target, or guess** — and why?
- *Tests:* approach archetypes matched to the hitter.
- *Listening for:* **zone** (heatmaps, shrink the swing-zone) · **target** (commit to a ball
  flight) · **guess** (sit pitch/hard-vs-soft, esp. low bat-to-ball). High bat-to-ball →
  earn selectivity; low bat-to-ball → likely a guesser.

**E2.** What exactly do you **show him**, and how do you avoid **information overload**?
- *Tests:* distill-don't-dump.
- *Listening for:* matches the visual to the hitter type; 1–2 actionable cues, not a data dump.

**E3.** How does his approach and swing change **pre-2-strike vs with 2 strikes**?
- *Tests:* leverage vs two-strike compartments.
- *Listening for:* leverage = hunt drivable, more out front, drive in the air; **2K** = cover
  the top, **flatter** path, hit it **deeper**, ground-ball oppo, "put a strike in play."

## F — Rapid-Fire Principles (the filter round)  *(deck slide 8)*

**F1.** Attack angle jumps **~2° → ~12° in two weeks**, but you see no change on video. Most
likely explanation before you credit a path change?
- *Answer:* **timing/contact-point artifact** — he's getting out front, AA reads higher.

**F2.** When do you reach for a **heavier vs lighter training bat** — and is there a **limit**?
- *Answer:* heavier to overload/feel sequencing, lighter to underload for speed; **~20%
  over/under** is the transfer ceiling — extremes don't transfer bat speed.

**F3.** **Internal vs external cues** — when do you use each?
- *Answer:* **external** once competent (faster, more powerful, better coordination);
  internal only for first-learning a concept.

**F4.** How do you know a **cage gain will transfer** to the game?
- *Answer:* transfer types (positive/neutral/negative); perception–action coupling; test it
  in game-like / overspeed reps vs a pitcher, not just on a tee/machine.

**F5.** A hitter **feels** he's "staying back" but the data shows **drift**. How do you handle
the feel-vs-real gap?
- *Answer:* perceived ≠ actual movement; trust the objective read; let the **environment/
  constraint** produce the new feel rather than arguing about it.

**F6.** **Bottom-hand vs top-hand dominant** — what shows up in ball flight?
- *Answer:* bottom-hand-dominant → more vertical plane, loses the barrel under the ball
  (high-FB trouble); top-hand-dominant → flat/high path, roll-overs/ground balls.

## G — Scaling, Staff & Communication  *(deck slide 9)*

**G1.** You validate a hitting principle and want it **system-wide** across affiliates with
coaches of different backgrounds — some skeptical. Walk me through: a **teachable framework**;
**rollout with alignment, not micromanaging**; **accountability + feedback loops**; and
**communicating** progress/barriers to leadership + S&C/ATC/analytics. What **indicators**
tell you it scaled?
- *Listening for:* a concrete, repeatable framework; respect for coach autonomy; objective
  adoption + outcome indicators; cross-functional comms.

**G2.** A coach **disagrees** with your method and has had success his own way. How do you
handle it?
- *Listening for:* evidence + humility, alignment without ego, find the shared principle.

**G3.** How do you work with **S&C, ATC, and analytics** on a hitter's plan?
- *Listening for:* the strength → bat-speed link (lead-leg force), workload/health,
  objective feedback — they treat it as one integrated plan.
