# Report Formats and Examples

Part of [[Player-Evaluator-Agent]]. The standard output shapes.

## Quick Player Report template
Player:
Role/Profile:
Carrying Traits:
Main Risk:
Development Path:
Bottom Line:

Example style: "Player B. He misses more bats across the board: higher K%, higher FB miss%, and
better best-secondary miss%. He also allows less damage while sitting 96 mph, which already
clears average MLB starter velocity. Command is a concern, but I see that as a clearer
development opportunity than trying to add whiff and impact stuff to Player A. With B, you are
starting with a higher foundation of stuff and damage prevention, then working mechanical
deficiencies and tightening command."

## Hitter Comparison Format
Player X. With (metric comparison), he pairs (strength) with (supporting metric). That tells me
(interpretation). His (risk metric) is (context), so there is a clear path to (development
outcome) through (specific adjustment). His (EV or contact metric) suggests (power or hit
foundation). I am more confident projecting (future skill) from this foundation than betting on
(other player's riskier trait) holding as competition improves.

## Pitcher Comparison Format
Player X. He misses more bats across the board: (K%), (FB miss%), and (best secondary miss%). He
also allows less damage: (SLG/Barrel/EV) while sitting (velo), which gives him a stronger
foundation. Command is a concern, but I see that as a clearer development opportunity than trying
to add whiff or impact stuff. ERA is noisy here, so I would trust the strikeout, damage-limiting,
and whiff indicators more than surface run prevention.

## Video Evaluation Format
Order: frame and athleticism first, then defense if relevant, then swing or delivery mechanics,
then current tools, then development focus, end with projection.
Structure: Player Name; frame/athleticism; defensive actions or mound operation; swing or
delivery breakdown; carrying tool; risk; development focus; overall projection.

## Questionnaire output format
Methodology: briefly explain the data, model, features, and evaluation metrics.
Findings: concise team/player-level findings with actual numbers.
Recommendation: what the team should do differently or keep doing.
Limitations: what is missing and why it matters.
Additional information wanted: 3 useful missing pieces.

## Additional info needed (list only the highest-value items)
Hitters: age, height, weight, country or level; K%, BB%, Chase%, Whiff%; IZ/OZ Swing and
Contact; P80 EV, max EV, bat speed; ball-flight and spray; performance vs velocity; defensive
run/arm/speed grades; biomechanics or bat-sensor data.
Pitchers: pitch movement profiles; pitch usage; command metrics; pitch-level results by count and
handedness; release consistency; miss distance to target; biomechanics; level of competition;
velocity trend and injury history.
